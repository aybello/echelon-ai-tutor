# Wisconsin DNR Wastewater Subclass SS: Sanitary Sewer Collection System (basic): 250-question review

250 original practice questions: 175 conceptual and 75 calculations, in US customary units, drawn from the chapters of the DNR Sanitary Sewage Collection System Study Guide (August 2018 edition). Calculation categories follow the guide's chapter 8: sampling, flow conversions and flow rate, tank areas and volumes, pounds formula, pump rate, detention time and sewer grading. Items avoid the guide's regulatory deadlines, numeric thresholds and step-by-step safety procedures, because the dossier excludes them. This is a local content package, not proof of a production import.

Correct answers are shown beneath each item. See README.md for scope, sources and limits.

### 1: Conditions and wastewater terms (easy)

Which condition is present when oxygen is available in the liquid for organisms to use?

- **A.** Anaerobic conditions with no usable oxygen present
- **B.** Stagnant conditions with no oxygen supply
- **C.** Aerobic conditions, with oxygen present in the liquid
- **D.** Anoxic conditions with oxygen locked in compounds

**Answer: C. Aerobic conditions, with oxygen present in the liquid**

Aerobic conditions have oxygen available in the liquid. The other choices describe conditions in which oxygen is missing or held in combined form.

### 2: Conditions and wastewater terms (medium)

A sample holds oxygen only inside compounds such as nitrate or sulfate. Which term describes that condition?

- **A.** Anoxic, with oxygen in a bound form
- **B.** Aerobic, with free dissolved oxygen present
- **C.** Oxic, with free oxygen in excess
- **D.** Anaerobic, with no oxygen in any form

**Answer: A. Anoxic, with oxygen in a bound form**

Anoxic conditions hold oxygen in combined forms such as nitrate and sulfate. Aerobic conditions have free dissolved oxygen, and anaerobic conditions have none.

### 3: Conditions and wastewater terms (medium)

What does biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) measure in a wastewater sample?

- **A.** Dissolved salts left behind after evaporation
- **B.** Organic strength, measured as oxygen used over a set time
- **C.** Acidity or alkalinity on a scale of zero to fourteen
- **D.** Number of disease-causing organisms in each liter

**Answer: B. Organic strength, measured as oxygen used over a set time**

BOD reflects the oxygen microorganisms use while breaking down organic matter over a fixed period. Higher BOD in mg/L means a stronger sample.

### 4: Conditions and wastewater terms (easy)

What does a DO reading for a sample tell an operator?

- **A.** The volume of air bubbled through a grit chamber
- **B.** The total oxygen the lift station pumps each day
- **C.** The amount of oxygen bound inside the sludge solids
- **D.** The oxygen content of the water, reported in mg/L

**Answer: D. The oxygen content of the water, reported in mg/L**

DO is the measure of oxygen dissolved in water and is expressed in mg/L. It describes the oxygen available to living organisms in the liquid.

### 5: Conditions and wastewater terms (easy)

On what scale is pH measured, and which value is neutral?

- **B.** 1 to 100, with 50 as neutral
- **C.** 0 to 10, with 5 as neutral
- **D.** 0 to 7, with 3.5 as neutral

**Answer: A. 0 to 14, with 7 as neutral**

pH runs from 0 to 14. Values below 7 are acidic, values above 7 are alkaline, and 7 is neutral.

### 8: Conditions and wastewater terms (medium)

### 13: Flow, strength and treatment terms (medium)
