@Collection systems|PADEP23|Pipe materials and joints used in sewers: traits, uses, common failure modes
Which pipe material is lightweight, resists corrosion from wastewater and is widely used for small gravity sewers and laterals?|PVC plastic pipe with gasketed joints|Unlined steel pipe in bolted sections|Plain concrete pipe with mortar joints|Cast iron pipe with lead and oakum joints|PVC is light and chemically resistant, comes in long lengths with gasketed joints, and is common for small-diameter gravity sewers.|e
Which material is most at risk from sulfuric acid formed in sewers with hydrogen sulfide?|Unprotected concrete pipe and manholes|PVC plastic pipe with gasketed joints in the street|Fused polyethylene pipe used for force mains|Vitrified clay pipe laid in short lengths|Cement-based materials are attacked by sulfuric acid. Plastics and clay resist it, which is why they are often chosen where sulfide is a known problem.|e
Why does acid corrosion in a concrete sewer occur mostly along the pipe crown?|Sulfide gas in the air space turns into sulfuric acid on the moist crown wall|Flow at the bottom of the pipe is acidic and burns the pipe invert|Groundwater pressing on the top of the pipe softens the concrete there|Heat from the flow rises and bakes the crown until it cracks|Bacteria on the damp crown convert hydrogen sulfide from the air space into sulfuric acid, which dissolves the cement and leaves aggregate exposed.|m
What is a key trait of HDPE sewer pipe?|Sections can be fused by heat into a continuous line without gaskets|It cracks easily under load because it is a brittle material|It needs a rigid concrete cradle along the whole length of the run|It reacts with hydrogen sulfide and loses wall thickness quickly|Heat-fused HDPE joints are leak-free and as strong as the pipe. This makes it popular for force mains and trenchless installation methods.|m
Why have gasketed push-on joints largely replaced mortar-sealed joints in new sewers?|They tolerate small movements and keep groundwater and roots out|They permit slow seepage so that the surrounding soil stays drained|They bond the pipes into one rigid line that resists any shifting|They are cheaper to install in each kind of pipe material|A rubber gasket seals while still letting the joint flex slightly, so settlement is less likely to open the joint and let in infiltration or roots.|m
What is a common failure mode of flexible plastic pipe?|Excess deflection, or an oval shape, from poor bedding|Acid attack on the crown by hydrogen sulfide gas in the air space|Brittle cracking from a load placed directly on the pipe wall|Rapid rusting of the pipe walls in areas with high groundwater|Flexible pipe depends on side support from the soil. Weak bedding or compaction lets the pipe sag and flatten, reducing flow area.|m
Which pairing correctly matches a pipe type with a typical failure mode?|Concrete with crown corrosion, and PVC with excess deflection|Concrete with excess deflection, and PVC with crown corrosion|Clay with sulfuric acid attack, and concrete with melting|PVC with brittle shattering, and clay with fatigue|Concrete is vulnerable to sulfuric acid at the crown, while flexible plastic pipe tends to deform out of round when its soil support is poor.|h
@Collection systems|PADEP23|Combined systems: regulators, diversion structures, overflows and why they matter
What is a combined sewer?|A single pipe system that carries both sanitary wastewater and stormwater|Two parallel pipes with one for sanitary flow and one for runoff, side by side|A pressure pipe that joins two separate treatment plants together under a city|A sewer that carries flow from one large industrial user to the plant|Combined sewers were built to collect wastewater and runoff in the same pipe, so flow rises sharply when it rains.|e
What is a combined sewer overflow?|A discharge of mixed wastewater and stormwater to a stream when flow exceeds capacity|A planned release of treated flow from the plant after disinfection is complete|A backup of flow into a basement through a private lateral during a storm|A leak from a force main caused by a broken joint at a pump station|When a storm sends more flow than the interceptor or plant can take, a regulator sends the excess to an overflow outlet, usually a nearby waterway.|e
What does a regulator do in a combined sewer system?|It controls how much flow goes to the interceptor and how much to the overflow|It filters grit from the flow before the wastewater enters the system|It increases the pressure so that the flow can reach the plant|It measures the strength of the wastewater for billing purposes|A regulator, such as a weir or gate, sends flow on to the interceptor up to its capacity and lets the excess leave through the overflow.|m
How does a side-overflow weir regulator work in basic terms?|Flow up to the weir height goes to the interceptor and higher flow spills over|Flow below the weir height spills over to the overflow and higher flow goes on to the interceptor|The weir holds back the flow until the downstream line has run empty|The weir forces the flow to rise and enter the pipe at a high point|The weir sets a level. Normal flows stay in the channel toward the interceptor, while storm flows rise above the crest and leave through the overflow.|m
Why can the first part of a storm bring a surge of strong wastewater in a combined system?|Storm flow scours solids that settled in the pipes during low flows|Rain washes the pipe walls and dilutes the solids already in the flow stream|Pumps at the plant send solids back up the pipes at the start of rain|Rainwater reacts with the wastewater in the pipe and creates new solids|Low flows between storms let solids settle in the pipes. The first strong flows resuspend them and move them downstream as a concentrated slug.|m
Why should crews inspect regulators on a regular schedule?|A stuck or blocked regulator can cause overflows in dry weather or overload the interceptor|A regulator needs to be recalibrated each day to stay accurate for billing and reports|Regulators attract roots that damage the sewer pipes found close to them in the street|Inspection is needed so that the regulator keeps a record of the volume it has passed|Debris, grease and failed parts can jam a regulator. The result can be a dry-weather overflow or an overload at the interceptor, so routine checks matter.|m
An overflow is observed at a regulator on a dry day. What does this most likely indicate?|A blockage or equipment failure, not a storm effect|The system is running as designed for the normal flow on a dry day in this district|The regulator has stopped working because it has been kept too clean for its design|Groundwater has risen high enough to fill the interceptor pipe past its crown|Overflows should be tied to wet weather. One in dry weather points to a mechanical problem or a blockage that is holding flow back.|h
@Collection systems|PADEP23|Infiltration and inflow: definitions, sources, effects, and methods of finding them
In a sanitary sewer, what is infiltration?|Groundwater that seeps in through cracked pipes, leaking joints and manhole walls|Rainwater that enters the sewer directly through a roof drain or a manhole cover|Wastewater that leaks out of a cracked pipe and soaks into the surrounding soil|Water that flows back from the treatment plant into the upstream sewer|Infiltration is groundwater that finds its way into the pipe through openings below the water table. It is a slower, steadier addition than direct inflow.|e
Which of these is a source of inflow rather than infiltration?|A roof downspout connected to a building sewer|A root-damaged joint under a dry hillside slope|A cracked pipe in the bottom of a deep trench|A broken lateral in wet soil near a creek bed|A downspout sends rain straight into the sewer, so the water never passes through soil. Joint and pipe defects let groundwater in, which is infiltration.|e
How does the timing of inflow usually differ from that of infiltration?|Inflow peaks quickly in a storm and drops soon after, while infiltration changes more slowly|Inflow rises slowly over several weeks of rain, while infiltration peaks during the storm itself|Both peak at the same time and then drop at the same rate once the rain stops|Inflow occurs in the summer months, while infiltration occurs during the winter months|Surface sources respond right away to rainfall. Groundwater takes longer to rise and drain, so infiltration continues after the storm has passed.|m
Why is minimum nighttime flow in dry weather used to estimate infiltration?|Household use is very low at night, so most of the remaining flow is groundwater|Industrial use is highest at night, so the flow is easier to measure in the pipe|Rain is more likely to fall at night, so the recorded flows are at their highest|Pumps run at full speed at night, so the flow readings are the most stable|In the early morning hours, little wastewater is produced. The flow that remains in the sewer is mostly infiltration, with a small base use.|m
How does smoke testing help find I/I problems?|Smoke forced into the sewer escapes at surface openings such as cracks or bad connections|Smoke settles in the pipe and shows the spots where solids have collected in the invert|Smoke that stays in the pipe shows the exact joints where groundwater is leaking in|Smoke is used to estimate the age of a pipe from the color it leaves behind|Smoke fills the sewer and escapes through defects, cross-connected drains, and missing cleanout caps, which shows entry paths for stormwater.|m
What is dye testing used for in an I/I investigation?|To confirm that a drain is connected to the sewer by watching for dye downstream|To measure how fast groundwater is moving toward a leaking joint in the pipe wall|To color the flow so that cameras can read the pipe wall more clearly|To estimate the age of a pipe by timing how long the dye takes to travel|Dye is added to a suspected source, such as a drain or downspout, and a downstream manhole is checked to see whether the colored water arrives.|m
During storms, the flow in a basin rises sharply and drops back within hours after rain ends, with little change in baseline flow. What source is most likely?|Direct inflow from roof drains or similar surface connections|Seasonal infiltration from a high water table that builds up over weeks|Normal changes in household water use during the working week|Slow leakage from a lateral that runs through a dry area of town|A quick rise and fall that follows rainfall points to direct connections to surface water, not groundwater entering through the soil.|h

What does CCTV inspection of a sewer involve?|Pulling or driving a camera through the pipe to record its interior condition|Dropping a weighted line down each manhole to check the depth of flow|Pumping air into the pipe and watching the pressure gauge fall|Walking the street above the sewer and listening for the sound of flow|A camera on a tractor or crawler travels through the line and records video, so defects can be reviewed and coded afterward.|e
What is lamping in sewer inspection?|Shining a light down a pipe from a manhole to view its condition|Placing a lamp inside the pipe so that a camera can find its position underground|Heating the pipe wall with a lamp to see where moisture is escaping into the soil|Using a lantern to test the air in a manhole for the presence of dangerous gas|Lamping is a quick visual check. Why is a sewer line usually cleaned before a CCTV inspection?|Grease, debris and standing water can hide defects from the camera|Cleaning makes the pipe wall look newer to the camera|The camera needs a minimum flow of water to move along|Cleaning sets the correct slope for the camera to follow|Heavy deposits and roots can cover cracks or joints and block the camera. What does a bellied or sagging sewer pipe look like in a CCTV inspection?|A low spot where water pools and flow depth changes along the run|A bright spot where the camera light reflects off a hole|A sharp turn in the line at a manhole connection|A band of rust along the pipe crown for a short distance|A sag appears as a stretch of standing water in the line where the pipe has settled below the grade of the pipe on each side.|m

What should be checked during a manhole inspection?|Cracked walls, leaking pipe connections, damaged castings and signs of infiltration|The brand name of the cover and the age of the paint that was last applied to the lid|Whether the pipe leading in is made of the same material as the pipe leading out of it|The number of steps in the barrel and the spacing between each of the steps|Manholes are common entry points for infiltration and inflow. An inspection finds a hairline longitudinal crack in one pipe, and a collapsed section with missing pipe wall in another. What is mechanical rodding best known for in sewer maintenance?|Cutting roots and opening a partly blocked line with a cutter on flexible rods|Washing grease off the pipe wall with a stream of hot water from a tank on the truck|Spraying a coating that seals the joints of the pipe from the inside of the line|Measuring the slope of a pipe between two manholes using a laser and a target|Rods turn a cutting head that chops roots and breaks up obstructions. What is cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining?|A resin-soaked tube pulled into the old pipe and hardened to form a new pipe|A smaller pipe pushed into the old one and grouted in place along the whole run|A new pipe pulled through the line as the old pipe is broken apart into the soil|A coating of mortar sprayed over the walls of a manhole and left to set|CIPP creates a jointless pipe within the old one, usually without digging along the whole run.|e
When is open-cut replacement generally needed for a sewer?|When a pipe has collapsed or must have its grade or alignment corrected|When just a few joints are leaking slightly in an otherwise sound pipe|When a manhole cover has worn out and needs to be replaced by the crew|When the pipe needs a routine cleaning of grease along its length|Lining follows the existing pipe path, so a collapse or a badly misaligned line needs the pipe dug up and replaced.|e
What is a drawback of sliplining?|The new pipe is smaller, so the flow area of the line is reduced|The old pipe has to be removed before the sliplining work can start|The new pipe has to be made of steel, which is hard to handle in the trench|The method requires a pumping station to move the flow after the work|A slipline pipe fits inside the old one, so the diameter is lower.
