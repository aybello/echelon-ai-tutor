What is the main purpose of a manhole on a sewer line?|To give crews access for inspection, cleaning and repair|To store extra flow during a heavy rainstorm so that pipes downstream stay dry|To lower the velocity of flow in a steep sewer line|To filter solids out before the flow moves on|Manholes provide a way into the line and a place where equipment can be lowered. Which fitting lets crews reach the upper end of a sewer line to clean it without entering a full manhole?|A cleanout|A drop connection pipe|A siphon barrel|A diversion gate|A cleanout is a short vertical or angled pipe that reaches grade, giving tools access to the line at a dead end or along a lateral.|e
What is the job of the shaped channel and bench in the base of a manhole?|To guide flow smoothly from the inlet pipe to the outlet pipe|To hold back the flow so that solids can settle out in the base|To slow the water until it no longer touches the pipe walls|To provide a clean surface where tools can rest between inspections|A smooth channel limits turbulence and solids deposits. Which pipe material is lightweight, resists corrosion from wastewater and is widely used for small gravity sewers and laterals?|PVC plastic pipe with gasketed joints|Unlined steel pipe in bolted sections|Plain concrete pipe with mortar joints|Cast iron pipe with lead and oakum joints|PVC is light and chemically resistant, comes in long lengths with gasketed joints, and is common for small-diameter gravity sewers.|e
Which material is most at risk from sulfuric acid formed in sewers with hydrogen sulfide?|Unprotected concrete pipe and manholes|PVC plastic pipe with gasketed joints in the street|Fused polyethylene pipe used for force mains|Vitrified clay pipe laid in short lengths|Cement-based materials are attacked by sulfuric acid. Plastics and clay resist it, which is why they are often chosen where sulfide is a known problem.|e
Why does acid corrosion in a concrete sewer occur mostly along the pipe crown?|Sulfide gas in the air space turns into sulfuric acid on the moist crown wall|Flow at the bottom of the pipe is acidic and burns the pipe invert|Groundwater pressing on the top of the pipe softens the concrete there|Heat from the flow rises and bakes the crown until it cracks|Bacteria on the damp crown convert hydrogen sulfide from the air space into sulfuric acid, which dissolves the cement and leaves aggregate exposed.|m
What is a key trait of HDPE sewer pipe?|Sections can be fused by heat into a continuous line without gaskets|It cracks easily under load because it is a brittle material|It needs a rigid concrete cradle along the whole length of the run|It reacts with hydrogen sulfide and loses wall thickness quickly|Heat-fused HDPE joints are leak-free and as strong as the pipe. Why have gasketed push-on joints largely replaced mortar-sealed joints in new sewers?|They tolerate small movements and keep groundwater and roots out|They permit slow seepage so that the surrounding soil stays drained|They bond the pipes into one rigid line that resists any shifting|They are cheaper to install in each kind of pipe material|A rubber gasket seals while still letting the joint flex slightly, so settlement is less likely to open the joint and let in infiltration or roots.|m
What is a common failure mode of flexible plastic pipe?|Excess deflection, or an oval shape, from poor bedding|Acid attack on the crown by hydrogen sulfide gas in the air space|Brittle cracking from a load placed directly on the pipe wall|Rapid rusting of the pipe walls in areas with high groundwater|Flexible pipe depends on side support from the soil. Which pairing correctly matches a pipe type with a typical failure mode?|Concrete with crown corrosion, and PVC with excess deflection|Concrete with excess deflection, and PVC with crown corrosion|Clay with sulfuric acid attack, and concrete with melting|PVC with brittle shattering, and clay with fatigue|Concrete is vulnerable to sulfuric acid at the crown, while flexible plastic pipe tends to deform out of round when its soil support is poor.|h
@Collection systems|PADEP23|Combined systems: regulators, diversion structures, overflows and why they matter
What is a combined sewer?|A single pipe system that carries both sanitary wastewater and stormwater|Two parallel pipes with one for sanitary flow and one for runoff, side by side|A pressure pipe that joins two separate treatment plants together under a city|A sewer that carries flow from one large industrial user to the plant|Combined sewers were built to collect wastewater and runoff in the same pipe, so flow rises sharply when it rains.|e
What is a combined sewer overflow?|A discharge of mixed wastewater and stormwater to a stream when flow exceeds capacity|A planned release of treated flow from the plant after disinfection is complete|A backup of flow into a basement through a private lateral during a storm|A leak from a force main caused by a broken joint at a pump station|When a storm sends more flow than the interceptor or plant can take, a regulator sends the excess to an overflow outlet, usually a nearby waterway.|e
What does a regulator do in a combined sewer system?|It controls how much flow goes to the interceptor and how much to the overflow|It filters grit from the flow before the wastewater enters the system|It increases the pressure so that the flow can reach the plant|It measures the strength of the wastewater for billing purposes|A regulator, such as a weir or gate, sends flow on to the interceptor up to its capacity and lets the excess leave through the overflow.|m
How does a side-overflow weir regulator work in basic terms?|Flow up to the weir height goes to the interceptor and higher flow spills over|Flow below the weir height spills over to the overflow and higher flow goes on to the interceptor|The weir holds back the flow until the downstream line has run empty|The weir forces the flow to rise and enter the pipe at a high point|The weir sets a level. Normal flows stay in the channel toward the interceptor, while storm flows rise above the crest and leave through the overflow.|m
Why can the first part of a storm bring a surge of strong wastewater in a combined system?|Storm flow scours solids that settled in the pipes during low flows|Rain washes the pipe walls and dilutes the solids already in the flow stream|Pumps at the plant send solids back up the pipes at the start of rain|Rainwater reacts with the wastewater in the pipe and creates new solids|Low flows between storms let solids settle in the pipes. The first strong flows resuspend them and move them downstream as a concentrated slug.|m
Why should crews inspect regulators on a regular schedule?|A stuck or blocked regulator can cause overflows in dry weather or overload the interceptor|A regulator needs to be recalibrated each day to stay accurate for billing and reports|Regulators attract roots that damage the sewer pipes found close to them in the street|Inspection is needed so that the regulator keeps a record of the volume it has passed|Debris, grease and failed parts can jam a regulator. The result can be a dry-weather overflow or an overload at the interceptor, so routine checks matter.|m
An overflow is observed at a regulator on a dry day. What does this most likely indicate?|A blockage or equipment failure, not a storm effect|The system is running as designed for the normal flow on a dry day in this district|The regulator has stopped working because it has been kept too clean for its design|Groundwater has risen high enough to fill the interceptor pipe past its crown|Overflows should be tied to wet weather. One in dry weather points to a mechanical problem or a blockage that is holding flow back.|h
@Collection systems|PADEP23|Infiltration and inflow: definitions, sources, effects, and methods of finding them
In a sanitary sewer, what is infiltration?|Groundwater that seeps in through defects such as cracked pipes, leaking joints and manhole walls|Rainwater that enters directly through a roof drain or a cover|Wastewater that leaks out of the pipe into the soil|Water that flows back from the plant into the sewer|Infiltration is groundwater that finds its way into the pipe through openings below the water table. It is a slower, steadier addition than direct inflow.|e
Which of these is a source of inflow rather than infiltration?|A roof downspout connected to a building sewer|A root-damaged joint under a dry hillside|A cracked pipe in the bottom of a deep trench|A broken lateral in wet soil near a creek|A downspout sends rain straight into the sewer, so the water never passes through soil. Joint and pipe defects let groundwater in, which is infiltration.|e
How does the timing of inflow usually differ from that of infiltration?|Inflow peaks quickly during a storm and drops soon after, while infiltration rises and falls more slowly|Inflow rises slowly over several weeks, while infiltration peaks during the storm itself|Both peak at the same time and drop at the same rate|Inflow occurs only in summer, while infiltration occurs only in winter|Surface sources respond right away to rainfall. Groundwater takes longer to rise and drain, so infiltration continues after the storm has passed.|m
Why is minimum nighttime flow in dry weather used to estimate infiltration?|Household use is very low at night, so most of the remaining flow is groundwater|Industrial use is highest at night, so the flow is easier to measure|Rain is more likely to fall at night, so flows are highest|Pumps run at full speed at night, so flow readings are the most stable|In the early morning hours, little wastewater is produced. The flow that remains in the sewer is mostly infiltration, with a small base use.|m
How does smoke testing help find I/I problems?|Smoke blown into the sewer comes out wherever there are openings to the surface, such as cracks or illegal connections|Smoke settles in the pipe and shows where solids have collected|Smoke that stays in the pipe shows where groundwater is leaking in|Smoke is used to find the age of the pipe by its color|Smoke fills the sewer and escapes through defects, cross-connected drains, and missing cleanout caps, which shows entry paths for stormwater.|m
What is dye testing used for in an I/I investigation?|To confirm that a suspected drain or fixture is connected to the sewer by watching for the dye downstream|To measure how fast groundwater is moving toward a leaking joint|To color the flow so that cameras can read the pipe wall|To find the age of a pipe by tracking dye travel|Dye is added to a suspected source, such as a drain or downspout, and a downstream manhole is checked to see whether the colored water arrives.|m
During storms, the flow in a basin rises sharply and drops back within hours after rain ends, with little change in baseline flow. What source is most likely?|Direct inflow from connected roof drains or similar surface sources|Seasonal infiltration from a high water table|Normal changes in household use during the week|Leakage from a lateral in a dry area|A quick rise and fall that follows rainfall points to direct connections to surface water, not groundwater entering through the soil.|h
