# Writer flags for the reviewer

Items a writer was least sure about. A reviewer should read these first. Remove an item if it depends on local practice.

## Batch 1: Collection systems (system types, components, I&I, inspection, cleaning, rehabilitation)

Conceptual:
1. "What does a regulator do in a combined sewer system?" and "How does a side-overflow weir regulator work in basic terms?" Regulator types vary.
2. "Why can the first part of a storm bring a surge of strong wastewater in a combined system?" First flush is common, not universal.
3. "Why is minimum nighttime flow in dry weather used to estimate infiltration?" The stem does not mention the base-use allowance.
4. "How does the timing of inflow usually differ from that of infiltration?" Infiltration response varies with soil and groundwater.
5. "During storms, the flow in a basin rises sharply and drops back within hours..." Inflow is the general inference.
6. "What does a risk-based cleaning schedule mean?" Programs differ.
7. "A crew is jetting a line and pushing debris toward the downstream manhole..." Borders on procedure.
8. "When is open-cut replacement generally needed for a sewer?" and "A pipe has a severe sag that holds water..." Some sags can be tolerated or spot-repaired.
9. "What is pipe bursting?" Upsizing is limited.
10. "Which problem is chemical grouting best suited to?"
11. "Why does acid corrosion in a concrete sewer occur mostly along the pipe crown?" and "Which material is most at risk from sulfuric acid..." Standard hydrogen sulfide crown corrosion.
12. "Where is ductile iron pipe... chosen" Material use varies locally.
13. "An inspection finds a hairline longitudinal crack... Which should generally be given higher priority?" Depends on the owner's defect-grading scheme.

Calculations:
- The "gpd per inch-mile" item defines the unit. The 14 gpm night flow is treated as pure infiltration, as stated in the question.
- The "percent of peak flow is I/I" item treats everything above dry-weather flow as I/I, as stated in the question.

## Batch 2: Pumping stations and industrial pretreatment

Conceptual:
1. "Why do many stations pair a transducer for normal control with float switches as a backup?" Backup floats are common, not universal.
2. "Which setting would most favor a vacuum sewer system over a conventional gravity system with pump stations?" Design judgement.
3. "Why is a suction-lift station practical only when the pump sits a limited height above the liquid in the wet well?" The lift limit is stated without a number.
4. "What does an automatic transfer switch do?" Generator-start detail varies by installation.
5. "Why is a station's stored alarm history useful?" Depends on the station's telemetry.
6. "Why does a pump that starts too often cause trouble?" The limit itself is manufacturer-specific.
7. "Which of these is a normal part of a routine station inspection?" Routine contents vary by owner.
8. "How does the flow of a positive-displacement pump respond to a rise in discharge pressure, compared with a centrifugal pump?" Ideal behaviour.
9. "What is the basic idea behind a local limit in a pretreatment program?" Programs differ.
10. "An industrial user turns in a monitoring report late once, with no harm from its discharge. Which response best fits the idea of proportional enforcement?" Enforcement response varies by authority.
11. "Why is a grab sample often used instead of a composite sample for something like oil and grease?" Depends on the parameter and program.
12. "Why must a grease trap be pumped out on a regular schedule?" and "What is the purpose of a grease interceptor or grease trap at a restaurant?" The schedule is owner-specific.
13. "Why does a pretreatment program include inspections of industrial users?" and "What does a permit or similar authorization for an industrial user typically do?"
14. "Why do industrial users sample and monitor their discharge?" Self-monitoring versus authority sampling varies.

Calculations:
- The drawdown-test item adds an estimated 40 gpm inflow, stated in the question.
- The grease trap pump-out item uses a 25 percent pump-out level, stated as an assumption.
- The energy item states 55 percent overall efficiency in the question.

## Batch 3: Work hazards

Recognition-level safety questions only. Read these with the dossier's exclusions in mind (no procedures, thresholds, rescue steps or regulation wording):
1. "How does methane differ from hydrogen sulfide in where it tends to gather in a space?" Methane is not always present. Hydrogen sulfide is heavier than air.
2. "Which statement about hydrogen sulfide gas is true?" Distractor 3 ("no effect at low levels but burns at high levels") is loosely worded.
3. "Why is smell an unreliable way to tell whether hydrogen sulfide is present?" Close to an exposure-limit topic.
4. "Why is air tested at more than one height within a space?" Some programs test at set depths.
5. "What does forced ventilation do for a space where work will take place?" The "does not replace testing" line is practice, not a rule.
6. "What is the general role of an attendant for a confined space job?" Check that no regulation wording crept in.
7. "A space tested safe when work began. Why is that not the end of concern about its air?" Close to a continuous-monitoring requirement.
8. "Why might a crew place a heavy truck between the work area and approaching traffic?" Common, not universal.
9. "What is the role of the transition area in a work zone?" Zone names vary by traffic control standard.
10. "Why do traffic control needs for a job on a busy arterial road differ from those on a quiet side street?" Distractor 3 about "wastewater flows beneath busy roads" is odd.
11. "Why does night work raise the risk of a traffic work zone?" Distractor 2 ("traffic is heavier at night") is not universally false.
12. "How does a trench shield, often called a trench box, protect workers?" Terminology varies.
13. "Why does loose, dry sand usually hold a steep wall less well than firm, sticky soil?" Distractor 2 ("heavier than clay") is loose.
14. "Rain fell overnight on a trench left open. Why should the trench be looked at again...?" Hazard recognition, not a duty.
15. "Why is a ladder or ramp placed within easy reach of workers in a trench?" Leans toward a requirement.
16. "Why are buried utility locations marked before digging begins?" Check the locate-call system is not implied.
17. "After equipment has been locked out, why is it still checked to confirm it is de-energized?" Could read as a procedural step.
18. "What does a ground-fault circuit interrupter do?" and "What is the idea behind grounding electrical equipment?" Simplified.
19. "Why does a standby generator add a hazard for people working on station equipment?" Assumes an automatic standby unit.
20. "Why must cranes and booms be kept clear of overhead power lines at a work site?" No distances are given.

Calculations:
- Trench sloping: 1.5H:1V on a 7 ft depth gives a 10.5 ft run on each side. Arithmetic only, no soil rule.
- Ventilation: 0.785 × D² area and 500 cfm give 1.5 min for one air change. Simple arithmetic only.
