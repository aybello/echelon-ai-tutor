### 1: Types of systems: sanitary, storm, combined; gravity, force main, vacuum, low-pressure (easy)

Which type of sewer is intended to carry household, business and industrial wastewater while leaving rainfall runoff to other pipes?

- **A.** Storm sewer
- **B.** Sanitary sewer
- **C.** Overflow relief sewer
- **D.** Combined sewer

**Answer: B. Sanitary sewer**

A sanitary sewer is built for wastewater from buildings. Rainfall runoff is meant to go to a separate storm drainage system.

### 2: Types of systems: sanitary, storm, combined; gravity, force main, vacuum, low-pressure (easy)

A force main is best described as which of the following?

- **A.** A small pipe connecting a house to the street main
- **B.** A pipe that carries rainfall runoff to a roadside ditch or creek
- **C.** A pressurized pipe that receives flow from a pump station
- **D.** A sloped pipe that flows by gravity to the next manhole downstream

**Answer: C. A pressurized pipe that receives flow from a pump station**

A force main carries wastewater under pressure, typically discharging to a gravity manhole or treatment plant at a higher point.

### 3: Types of systems: sanitary, storm, combined; gravity, force main, vacuum, low-pressure (medium)

Which statement best separates collection from conveyance in a sewer network?

- **A.** Collection gathers flow from many sources in small lines, and conveyance moves it in larger lines toward treatment
- **B.** Collection means pumped flow, while conveyance means gravity flow in the same lines of a district
- **C.** Collection happens inside the treatment plant, while conveyance happens in the public street
- **D.** Collection refers to stormwater pipes, while conveyance refers to the sanitary pipes serving all homes in a district

**Answer: A. Collection gathers flow from many sources in small lines, and conveyance moves it in larger lines toward treatment**

Collection systems start at buildings and small local sewers. Larger interceptors and force mains then carry the accumulated flow to the treatment works.

### 4: Types of systems: sanitary, storm, combined; gravity, force main, vacuum, low-pressure (medium)

How does a vacuum sewer system move wastewater?

- **A.** Compressed air injected at each manhole pushes the flow along the pipe toward the plant
- **B.** A tank at a high point forces flow downhill through the lines under the force of its head, so valves are not needed

**Answer: D. A central station holds the lines under partial vacuum and valves at collection points admit the flow**

Vacuum systems use differential pressure created at a central station. Normally closed valves at the collection points open when a sump fills.

### 5: Types of systems: sanitary, storm, combined; gravity, force main, vacuum, low-pressure (medium)

### 6: Types of systems: sanitary, storm, combined; gravity, force main, vacuum, low-pressure (medium)

### 7: Types of systems: sanitary, storm, combined; gravity, force main, vacuum, low-pressure (hard)

What does it mean when a gravity sewer is described as surcharged?

### 12: Components and appurtenances: laterals, mains, interceptors, manholes, cleanouts, siphons, junction structures (medium)

What is an inverted siphon (depressed sewer) used for?

**Answer: B. Carrying gravity flow under an obstacle such as a stream or a utility**

An inverted siphon dips under the obstacle and rises again, running full under pressure from the upstream head, so no pump is needed.

### 13: Components and appurtenances: laterals, mains, interceptors, manholes, cleanouts, siphons, junction structures (medium)

Where are air release valves normally placed on a force main?

### 16: Pipe materials and joints used in sewers: traits, uses, common failure modes (easy)

Which material is most at risk from sulfuric acid formed in sewers with hydrogen sulfide?

- **B.** Vitrified clay pipe laid in short lengths

Cement-based materials are attacked by sulfuric acid. Plastics and clay resist it, which is why they are often chosen where sulfide is a known problem.

### 17: Pipe materials and joints used in sewers: traits, uses, common failure modes (medium)

Why does acid corrosion in a concrete sewer occur mostly along the pipe crown?

- **A.** Heat from the flow rises and bakes the crown until it cracks
- **B.** Groundwater pressing on the top of the pipe softens the concrete there

- **D.** Sulfide gas in the air space turns into sulfuric acid on the moist crown wall

**Answer: D. Sulfide gas in the air space turns into sulfuric acid on the moist crown wall**

Bacteria on the damp crown convert hydrogen sulfide from the air space into sulfuric acid, which dissolves the cement and leaves aggregate exposed.

### 18: Pipe materials and joints used in sewers: traits, uses, common failure modes (medium)

What is a key trait of HDPE sewer pipe?

- **D.** It needs a rigid concrete cradle along the whole length of the run

**Answer: B. Sections can be fused by heat into a continuous line without gaskets**

Heat-fused HDPE joints are leak-free and as strong as the pipe. This makes it popular for force mains and trenchless installation methods.

### 19: Pipe materials and joints used in sewers: traits, uses, common failure modes (medium)

Why have gasketed push-on joints largely replaced mortar-sealed joints in new sewers?

A rubber gasket seals while still letting the joint flex slightly, so settlement is less likely to open the joint and let in infiltration or roots.

### 20: Pipe materials and joints used in sewers: traits, uses, common failure modes (medium)

What is a common failure mode of flexible plastic pipe?

Flexible pipe depends on side support from the soil. Weak bedding or compaction lets the pipe sag and flatten, reducing flow area.

### 21: Pipe materials and joints used in sewers: traits, uses, common failure modes (hard)

### 24: Combined systems: regulators, diversion structures, overflows and why they matter (medium)

What does a regulator do in a combined sewer system?

### 25: Combined systems: regulators, diversion structures, overflows and why they matter (medium)

How does a side-overflow weir regulator work in basic terms?

### 26: Combined systems: regulators, diversion structures, overflows and why they matter (medium)

Why can the first part of a storm bring a surge of strong wastewater in a combined system?

Low flows between storms let solids settle in the pipes. The first strong flows resuspend them and move them downstream as a concentrated slug.

### 28: Combined systems: regulators, diversion structures, overflows and why they matter (hard)

### 29: Infiltration and inflow: definitions, sources, effects, and methods of finding them (easy)

In a sanitary sewer, what is infiltration?

- **A.** Water that flows back from the treatment plant into the upstream sewer
- **B.** Groundwater that seeps in through cracked pipes, leaking joints and manhole walls
- **C.** Rainwater that enters the sewer directly through a roof drain or a manhole cover
- **D.** Wastewater that leaks out of a cracked pipe and soaks into the surrounding soil

**Answer: B. Groundwater that seeps in through cracked pipes, leaking joints and manhole walls**

Infiltration is groundwater that finds its way into the pipe through openings below the water table. It is a slower, steadier addition than direct inflow.

### 30: Infiltration and inflow: definitions, sources, effects, and methods of finding them (easy)

Which of these is a source of inflow rather than infiltration?

- **C.** A root-damaged joint under a dry hillside slope
- **D.** A roof downspout connected to a building sewer

**Answer: D. A roof downspout connected to a building sewer**

A downspout sends rain straight into the sewer, so the water never passes through soil. Joint and pipe defects let groundwater in, which is infiltration.

### 31: Infiltration and inflow: definitions, sources, effects, and methods of finding them (medium)

How does the timing of inflow usually differ from that of infiltration?

**Answer: A. Inflow peaks quickly in a storm and drops soon after, while infiltration changes more slowly**

Surface sources respond right away to rainfall. Groundwater takes longer to rise and drain, so infiltration continues after the storm has passed.
