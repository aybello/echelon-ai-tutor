# Utah Drinking Water Operator Certification: Water Distribution Grade I (D1): 250-question review

250 original practice questions: 175 conceptual and 75 calculations, in US customary units. Scope is the six DDW exam categories (Math, Operation and Maintenance, Pumps, Chemical Feed, Rules, Safety and Security) with the D1 category allocation, which is adjusted; see allocationNote. Rules items use only the DDW study-help and certification-page facts, plus general distribution recordkeeping practice. The R309-300 rule text could not be retrieved, so no item cites it. This is a local content package, not proof of a production import.

Correct answers are shown beneath each item. See README.md for scope, sources and limits.

### 1: Units and conversions (medium)

Roughly how many gallons per minute does one million gallons per day equal?

- **A.** About 694 gpm
- **B.** About 1,440 gpm
- **C.** About 6,944 gpm
- **D.** About 69 gpm

**Answer: A. About 694 gpm**

One million gallons spread over 1,440 minutes a day is about 694 gpm. The figure 1,440 is the number of minutes in a day, not a flow rate.

### 2: Units and conversions (easy)

Which statement correctly relates pressure and head for water?

- **A.** One psi is about 0.433 feet of water
- **B.** One psi is about 12 feet of water
- **C.** One psi is about 2.31 inches of water
- **D.** One psi is about 2.31 feet of water

**Answer: D. One psi is about 2.31 feet of water**

A column of water 2.31 feet tall exerts about 1 psi at its base. The value 0.433 psi per foot is the pressure gained per foot of head, which is the inverse relationship.

### 3: Pipe area, velocity and flow (medium)

If a pipe's diameter is doubled with the same flow, how does the velocity change?

- **A.** It rises by about eight times
- **B.** It falls to about one quarter
- **C.** It falls by half
- **D.** It stays the same

**Answer: B. It falls to about one quarter**

Full-pipe area depends on the square of diameter, so doubling it multiplies area by four. With the same flow, velocity falls to one quarter.

### 4: Pipe area, velocity and flow (medium)

A steady flow moves at 2.0 ft/s in a pipe. The same flow passes through a pipe with half the diameter. What is the new speed?

- **A.** About 2.0 ft/s, since flow is unchanged
- **B.** About 1.0 ft/s, since diameter halves
- **C.** About 8.0 ft/s, four times faster
- **D.** About 4.0 ft/s, since area halves

**Answer: C. About 8.0 ft/s, four times faster**

Half the diameter gives one quarter the area, so the same flow moves four times faster. Halving only the area would double the speed, not quadruple it.

### 5: Pressure and head (medium)

Which statement about pressure and depth in a standing column of water is correct?

- **A.** Pressure falls about 2.31 psi per foot of depth
- **B.** Pressure is the same at any depth in the column
- **C.** Pressure rises about 2.31 psi per foot of depth
- **D.** Pressure rises about 0.433 psi per foot deep

**Answer: D. Pressure rises about 0.433 psi per foot deep**

Pressure increases with depth in a standing column of water, at about 0.433 psi per foot. Depth is what drives pressure, so it cannot be ignored.

### 6: Layout and components (easy)

Which part of a distribution system carries water from a main to a customer's meter?

- **A.** The blowoff assembly
- **B.** The service line
- **C.** The air release valve
- **D.** The hydrant lead

**Answer: B. The service line**

A service line runs from the main to the customer's meter or property line. The other fittings serve fire protection, venting or draining the main.

### 7: Layout and components (easy)

Which fitting is installed at a high point in a main to vent accumulated gas?

- **A.** Blowoff valve
- **B.** Pressure reducing valve
- **C.** Air release valve
- **D.** Check valve at the pump discharge

**Answer: C. Air release valve**

Air release valves vent accumulated air at high points so it does not restrict flow. Blowoffs drain low points, and the other two control pressure or prevent reverse flow.

### 8: Layout and components (medium)

Why are blowoffs placed at low points and dead ends of a distribution main?

- **A.** To drain and flush the line
- **B.** To meter water leaving the system
- **C.** To measure end-line pressure
- **D.** To release trapped air

**Answer: A. To drain and flush the line**

A blowoff lets an operator drain sediment and water from a low point or a dead end. Air is removed at high points, not at blowoffs.

### 9: Layout and components (medium)

What is the main advantage of looping a distribution main instead of dead-ending it?

- **A.** Looping raises pressure at each hydrant served
- **B.** Looping allows a smaller pipe diameter
- **C.** Looping removes the need for valves
- **D.** Water can reach a point from two directions

**Answer: D. Water can reach a point from two directions**

A looped main can be fed from more than one direction, so an isolated section can often be supplied from the other side. Looping does not remove valves or change diameter requirements.

### 10: Layout and components (easy)

What is the purpose of a gate valve in a distribution main?

- **A.** To add disinfectant to new pipe
- **B.** To isolate a section for repair
- **C.** To hold flow at a set point
- **D.** To prevent water flowing backward

**Answer: B. To isolate a section for repair**

Gate valves are closed to shut off a section so it can be repaired. Regulating, check and chemical feed devices do different jobs.

### 11: Layout and components (easy)

A hydrant lead connects a fire hydrant to what part of the system?

- **A.** The customer's meter at the property line
- **B.** The discharge side of a booster pump
- **C.** The main, through a branch and valve
- **D.** The top of the storage tank

**Answer: C. The main, through a branch and valve**

A hydrant lead is a branch from the main with its own valve, so the hydrant can be isolated. The other points are not the source of a hydrant connection.

### 12: Layout and components (hard)

Which description best fits a dead-end main?

- **A.** A main with no second connection point
- **B.** A main carrying booster flow back to the source
- **C.** A main connected at both ends to form a loop
- **D.** A main running in the same trench as a sewer

**Answer: A. A main with no second connection point**

A dead-end main ends without a second connection, so water in it moves slowly and can age. Looped and parallel mains are connected or placed differently.

### 13: Layout and components (easy)

What is a curb stop on a water service?

- **A.** A valve on the service line
- **B.** A blowoff at a main's dead end
- **C.** A lid over a meter pit
- **D.** A valve at the curb for a hydrant

**Answer: A. A valve on the service line**

A curb stop is a valve on the service line that isolates the customer from the main. It is not a hydrant or a meter component.

### 14: Layout and components (medium)

Why do distribution systems use pressure zones?

- **A.** To remove the need for service meters
- **B.** To separate drinking and reclaimed water mains
- **C.** To send the full flow through one pump
- **D.** To keep pressure usable at varied elevations

**Answer: D. To keep pressure usable at varied elevations**

Pressure zones keep customers at high and low elevations within workable pressure ranges. A single zone at a high elevation would over-pressure low customers.

### 15: Layout and components (hard)

Which factor most directly sets how high a pressure zone can be served from a tank?

- **A.** The diameter of the plant's main
- **B.** The tank's water surface elevation
- **C.** The material of the service lines
- **D.** The number of hydrants in the zone

**Answer: B. The tank's water surface elevation**

A tank's water surface elevation provides the static pressure for the zone, so it sets the highest elevation that can be served at usable pressure. Pipe size and hydrant count affect flow, not static head.

### 16: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (easy)

What does isolating a section of main accomplish during a break repair?

- **A.** It raises pressure at the break
- **B.** It drains the entire zone
- **C.** It limits flow into the break
- **D.** It adds disinfectant to the leak

**Answer: C. It limits flow into the break**

Closing valves around a break limits the water that can escape, which lets the crew work on the pipe. The other choices would worsen the leak or affect areas that do not need isolation.

### 17: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (medium)

Why is a main usually flushed after a repair?

- **A.** To test strength of new joints
- **B.** To raise pH in the new section
- **C.** To add a fixed chlorine dose
- **D.** To remove sediment and debris

**Answer: D. To remove sediment and debris**

Repair work can let debris and sediment into the pipe, and flushing clears them before the line is fully used. Flushing does not test joints or set pH.

### 18: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (medium)

What is the main reason to disinfect new or repaired pipe before service?

- **A.** Chlorine replaces the pressure test
- **B.** Disinfection avoids the valve check
- **C.** Work can introduce contamination
- **D.** Disinfectant strengthens the pipe wall

**Answer: C. Work can introduce contamination**

New or repaired pipe can pick up soil, water or debris during work, so it must be disinfected before use. Disinfection does not change the pipe's strength or replace other checks.

### 19: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (medium)

After a repair, what does turbidity that rises during flushing usually indicate?

- **A.** The service meter is faulty
- **B.** Sediment is being cleared from the pipe
- **C.** The new pipe has failed under line pressure
- **D.** The chlorine residual is used up

**Answer: B. Sediment is being cleared from the pipe**

During flushing, loose sediment is pushed out and turbidity can rise briefly, then fall as the line clears. A rise alone does not show pipe failure or a faulty meter.

### 20: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (medium)

Which condition is a common cause of main breaks in older cast iron pipe?

- **A.** Corrosion thinning the wall and ground movement
- **B.** Too many hydrants on one main line
- **C.** Low winter water temperature
- **D.** Excess chlorine corroding the pipe wall within days

**Answer: A. Corrosion thinning the wall and ground movement**

Corrosion thins the wall and ground movement adds stress, which can cause breaks in older iron pipe. A high chlorine residual is not a usual cause of break.

### 21: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (medium)

Why can discolored water appear after a main is returned to service?

- **A.** Changed flow stirs loose sediment in the pipe
- **B.** The pump reduced hardness throughout the new section
- **C.** Chlorine reacted with the pipe coating
- **D.** Fluoride was added to the new section

**Answer: A. Changed flow stirs loose sediment in the pipe**

A sudden change in flow direction or speed can lift sediment off the pipe wall and cause discoloration. Chlorine and fluoride do not normally color the water this way.

### 22: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (medium)

Why do operators open a valve slowly to refill a newly repaired main?

- **A.** To keep trapped air from escaping
- **B.** To give chlorine time to dissolve
- **C.** To avoid a pressure surge in the pipe
- **D.** To measure the pressure drop at the valve

**Answer: C. To avoid a pressure surge in the pipe**

Opening slowly lets air move out and water fill the pipe gradually, which prevents surge pressure. Fast refilling can stress joints and fittings.

### 23: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (easy)

What is the main goal of a distribution flushing program?

- **A.** Removing scale from the tank roof
- **B.** Removing sediment, biofilm and old water
- **C.** Finding leaks at service connections
- **D.** Reducing excess pressure in the top zone

**Answer: B. Removing sediment, biofilm and old water**

Flushing moves older water and loose deposits out of the system so fresher water can take their place. It is aimed at the pipe contents, not at pressure or leak detection.

### 24: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (hard)

Why is a dead-end main flushed more often than a looped main?

- **A.** Dead ends collect the most storage flow
- **B.** Dead ends have the highest pressure
- **C.** Dead ends are where hydrants are placed
- **D.** Water in it ages and loses residual

**Answer: D. Water in it ages and loses residual**

Water in a dead end is replaced slowly, so the disinfectant residual can fall and sediment can settle. Flushing restores movement and freshness to that water.

### 25: Main breaks, repairs and flushing (medium)

What is the purpose of a temporary bypass during a main repair?

- **A.** To deliver chemicals to the break
- **B.** To raise pressure in the isolated pipe section
- **C.** To drain the zone for a pressure test
- **D.** To keep customers supplied during isolation

**Answer: D. To keep customers supplied during isolation**

A temporary bypass maintains service to customers when a section is shut off for repair. It is not a way to raise pressure or deliver chemicals.

### 26: Valves and isolation (easy)

Why do utilities exercise distribution valves on a regular schedule?

- **A.** To measure residual at each valve
- **B.** To keep valves working for isolation
- **C.** To increase flow in the main
- **D.** To lower zone pressure by a fixed amount

**Answer: B. To keep valves working for isolation**

Valves that are not operated can seize or fill with debris and then fail when they are needed. Exercising keeps them working for isolation.

### 27: Valves and isolation (medium)

What is a risk of keeping a valve partly closed for long periods?

- **A.** The water becomes harder downstream of it
- **B.** The valve raises the chlorine dose
- **C.** Sediment can build up and seat poorly
- **D.** The flow reverses in the main

**Answer: C. Sediment can build up and seat poorly**

A partly closed valve lets sediment settle around its seat and can make it hard to seat later. It does not change chemistry or reverse flow.

### 28: Valves and isolation (medium)

Why should a valve on a pressurized main be closed slowly?

- **A.** Sudden closure can cause a pressure surge
- **B.** Slow closure keeps the residual from dropping
- **C.** Slow closure lets air escape the gland
- **D.** Slow closure keeps the stem from freezing

**Answer: A. Sudden closure can cause a pressure surge**

Closing a valve quickly stops moving water abruptly, which can create a pressure surge. Slow closure protects pipe and fittings from that surge.

### 29: Valves and isolation (medium)

What is a common use of a butterfly valve on a large main?

- **A.** To measure flow through a meter pit
- **B.** To throttle or isolate flow with a disk
- **C.** To add air to a dead end
- **D.** To hold service pressure above the main

**Answer: B. To throttle or isolate flow with a disk**

A butterfly valve uses a rotating disk in the flow path and is compact for larger sizes. It throttles or isolates flow but does not measure flow or hold pressure in a service.

### 30: Valves and isolation (hard)

Two gate valves isolate a section, and one leaks past its seat. What is the best practice before work?

- **A.** Ignore the leak if residual is steady
- **B.** Start work, since the other valve blocks it
- **C.** Drain the section through the leaking valve
- **D.** Confirm isolation with another valve first

**Answer: D. Confirm isolation with another valve first**

A leaking valve can let water back into the section, so isolation must be confirmed before work. Draining through the leaking valve or ignoring it creates new risks.

### 31: Valves and isolation (easy)

What is the purpose of a check valve in a pipeline?

- **A.** It holds downstream pressure at a fixed set value
- **B.** It releases trapped air at a high point
- **C.** It lets flow pass one way and blocks reversal
- **D.** It shuts off flow when a meter reads zero

**Answer: C. It lets flow pass one way and blocks reversal**

A check valve opens for forward flow and closes to stop reverse flow. Pressure control and air release are done by other valve types.

### 32: Valves and isolation (easy)

What is the usual purpose of a pressure reducing valve?

- **A.** To hold a lower downstream pressure
- **B.** To drain sediment from the low points
- **C.** To remove air from high points
- **D.** To raise pressure for fire flow

**Answer: A. To hold a lower downstream pressure**

A pressure reducing valve holds a set lower pressure downstream when upstream pressure is higher. It does not raise pressure or perform air release or draining.

### 33: Valves and isolation (medium)

A valve is buried under pavement and cannot be found by its box. What is the best first action?

- **A.** Remove pavement across the whole block
- **B.** Assume the valve is open and close elsewhere
- **C.** Excavate at random around the main
- **D.** Use valve records and locating tools

**Answer: D. Use valve records and locating tools**

Valve records and locating equipment can narrow the search and avoid damage. Random excavation and assumptions about valve status are unsafe and can damage utilities.

### 34: Valves and isolation (medium)

After operating a valve, why check pressure at nearby points?

- **A.** To confirm the intended section is isolated
- **B.** To check the handwheel's paint color
- **C.** To measure residual in the isolated pipe section
- **D.** To test the pump's efficiency

**Answer: A. To confirm the intended section is isolated**

Checking nearby pressures confirms the valve did what was intended and that no unexpected flow is entering the section. Paint, residual and pump efficiency do not confirm isolation.

### 35: Hydrants and flushing (easy)

What is the main purpose of a fire hydrant on a distribution main?

- **A.** A point to add disinfectant
- **B.** A connection for a firefighting supply
- **C.** A meter for measuring customer water use
- **D.** A vent that releases air

**Answer: B. A connection for a firefighting supply**

A hydrant gives firefighters a connection to the main so they can draw water. It does not meter use, add chemicals or vent air.

### 36: Hydrants and flushing (medium)

Why is a hydrant's barrel drained after use in cold weather?

- **A.** To increase flow for the next user
- **B.** To keep chlorine residual stable in the barrel
- **C.** To keep water from freezing and cracking it
- **D.** To keep pressure within a set range

**Answer: C. To keep water from freezing and cracking it**

Water left in the barrel can freeze and crack the hydrant, so drain valves are used after use. Residual and pressure are not controlled this way.

### 37: Hydrants and flushing (medium)

What does the open flow from a flushing hydrant mainly do?

- **A.** Adds chlorine to each service line
- **B.** Lowers hardness of water in the main
- **C.** Replaces old water with fresher source water
- **D.** Raises total dissolved solids throughout the main

**Answer: C. Replaces old water with fresher source water**

Flow through the hydrant moves older water out and draws fresher water in from the source. It does not change chlorine in service lines or dissolved solids.

### 38: Hydrants and flushing (medium)

Why must a flushing crew check the discharge point first?

- **A.** To measure pH at the discharge
- **B.** To confirm the hydrant's serial number
- **C.** To avoid heating water in the hydrant
- **D.** To avoid erosion and property damage

**Answer: D. To avoid erosion and property damage**

Flushing can scour soil or flood property, so the discharge path must be checked first. Serial numbers and pH checks do not decide where water can go.

### 39: Hydrants and flushing (hard)

During flushing, water stays cloudy for 10 minutes. What is the most reasonable reading?

- **A.** Chlorine is depleted across the zone
- **B.** Sediment may still be clearing
- **C.** The hydrant leaks into a drain
- **D.** Pressure in the main has risen

**Answer: B. Sediment may still be clearing**

Cloudy water can persist while loose sediment is still moving, so continued flushing and a recheck are the usual response. A zone-wide chlorine loss is not shown by one hydrant.

### 40: Hydrants and flushing (medium)

Why is hydrant flow rate important in a flushing program?

- **A.** It sets the velocity that scours sediment
- **B.** It sets the pressure at the storage tank
- **C.** It sets the meter reading at the service
- **D.** It sets the chlorine dose applied at the hydrant

**Answer: A. It sets the velocity that scours sediment**

Flow rate determines the velocity in the main, and velocity is what scours loose deposits. Dose, meter reading and tank pressure are set elsewhere.

### 41: Hydrants and flushing (easy)

What is a common reason a hydrant does not flow well?

- **A.** A partly closed valve or restricted lead
- **B.** High chlorine residual throughout the main line
- **C.** Low pH in the source water
- **D.** Large diameter of the main

**Answer: A. A partly closed valve or restricted lead**

A closed or partly closed valve, or a blocked lead, reduces the flow a hydrant can deliver. Chlorine, pH and main diameter do not restrict a hydrant's outlet.

### 42: Hydrants and flushing (hard)

Which description best fits a hydrant flow test?

- **A.** Confirms pump discharge pressure at the plant
- **B.** Measures water available at set pressure
- **C.** Verifies a service meter's accuracy
- **D.** Counts customers on a branch

**Answer: B. Measures water available at set pressure**

A flow test records the flow a hydrant can deliver while pressure is measured nearby. It is used to judge available fire flow, not customer counts, plant pressure or meters.

### 43: Water quality in the distribution system (easy)

What is chlorine residual in a distribution system?

- **A.** Chlorine added at the pump station
- **B.** Chlorine removed by filters before the plant
- **C.** Disinfectant left after reacting with demand
- **D.** Chlorine lost to the air at the tank

**Answer: C. Disinfectant left after reacting with demand**

Chlorine residual is the disinfectant left over after reactions with organic matter and other demand. It is measured in the distribution system, not only at the pump station.

### 44: Water quality in the distribution system (medium)

Why does chlorine residual usually decline with distance and time in a distribution system?

- **A.** Faster water dilutes the chlorine
- **B.** Sunlight breaks down chlorine in the main
- **C.** Hardness precipitates chlorine as a solid
- **D.** Reactions with organics and biofilm

**Answer: D. Reactions with organics and biofilm**

Chlorine is consumed by reactions with organic matter, biofilm and pipe wall materials as water travels. Dilution and sunlight do not drive the decline in buried mains.

### 45: Water quality in the distribution system (medium)

Water age in a distribution system increases when which condition occurs?

- **A.** Flow through the main is raised
- **B.** Water sits in storage or dead ends
- **C.** More customers connect to the zone
- **D.** Pump speed is raised for a long time

**Answer: B. Water sits in storage or dead ends**

Water age rises where water is not replaced, such as in storage or dead ends. Higher pump speed and flow rates move water faster, which lowers age.

### 46: Water quality in the distribution system (medium)

A zone's far end shows low residual, but the plant reads normal. What is the most likely reason?

- **A.** The plant pump raised the water's pH
- **B.** The far-end water is harder and masks the residual
- **C.** Long travel and demand have used the residual
- **D.** The far-end meter reads free, not total

**Answer: C. Long travel and demand have used the residual**

When water travels a long way through the system, the residual is used up by demand along the route. This is a common pattern in long or lightly used lines.

### 47: Water quality in the distribution system (medium)

What is biofilm in a water main?

- **A.** Sediment settling from the tank roof
- **B.** Calcium carbonate deposited on the pipe at high pH
- **C.** Protective coating applied to new pipe
- **D.** Microbes and organic matter on the pipe wall

**Answer: D. Microbes and organic matter on the pipe wall**

Biofilm is a film of microorganisms and organic material that grows on pipe surfaces. It can consume disinfectant and harbor organisms, which is why it matters in distribution.

### 48: Water quality in the distribution system (medium)

Why can stagnant water in a service line cause a taste or odor complaint?

- **A.** Aged water loses residual and picks up odors
- **B.** Minerals drop out and it tastes flat
- **C.** The water warms and tastes sweeter
- **D.** Excess chlorine builds up in the line and smells

**Answer: A. Aged water loses residual and picks up odors**

Water that sits in a service line can lose disinfectant and pick up odors from pipe materials or biofilm. Excess chlorine and warmth are not the usual causes.

### 49: Water quality in the distribution system (easy)

What does a color or turbidity complaint often point to?

- **A.** Low pH in the source water
- **B.** High chlorine residual in the water supply
- **C.** Sediment disturbed by a change in flow
- **D.** A failed meter at the service

**Answer: C. Sediment disturbed by a change in flow**

Flow changes can stir up sediment and iron or manganese deposits, which show up as color or turbidity. Chlorine level and meters do not usually produce these complaints.

### 50: Water quality in the distribution system (hard)

A zone has chloramine decay and nitrification. What operational response reduces that risk?

- **A.** Increase storage time to rebuild residual
- **B.** Stop the chloramine feed for a week
- **C.** Add pump capacity to raise zone pressure
- **D.** Reduce water age and flush dead ends

**Answer: D. Reduce water age and flush dead ends**

Nitrification risk rises with water age and low residual, so keeping water moving and flushing dead ends helps. Longer storage and shutting off the feed make the problem worse.

### 51: Water quality in the distribution system (medium)

Why do sampling taps on the distribution system matter?

- **A.** They sample water at representative points
- **B.** They measure flow in the main
- **C.** They raise pressure delivered to the meter
- **D.** They release trapped air from the main

**Answer: A. They sample water at representative points**

A sampling tap gives a representative sample of water at a known point. It is not a pressure, flow or air-release device.

### 52: Water quality in the distribution system (easy)

Which condition favors bacterial regrowth in a distribution system?

- **A.** Cold water with a high chlorine residual
- **B.** Long water age, low residual and warm water
- **C.** Fast flow through a short clean main
- **D.** High pressure and turbulent flow in large mains

**Answer: B. Long water age, low residual and warm water**

Regrowth is favored when water sits, disinfectant is low and temperatures are warm. Turbulent flow and high residuals limit regrowth rather than promote it.

### 53: Meters and water loss (easy)

What is non-revenue water in a distribution system?

- **A.** Water stored in tanks for fire demand
- **B.** Water sent to the plant for recycling
- **C.** Treated water not pumped to the system
- **D.** Produced water that is not billed

**Answer: D. Produced water that is not billed**

Non-revenue water is produced water that earns no bill, including leaks, unmetered use and meter error. Water stored for fire demand is not counted as lost.

### 54: Meters and water loss (medium)

Why do utilities check meters for accuracy?

- **A.** Under-registration means water goes unbilled
- **B.** Checks determine the storage tank size
- **C.** Over-registration lowers the chlorine residual
- **D.** Checks replace the need for leak surveys

**Answer: A. Under-registration means water goes unbilled**

A meter that reads low lets delivered water go unbilled, which increases apparent loss. Meter checks do not replace leak surveys or set tank size.

### 55: Centrifugal pumps and curves (easy)

What does a pump curve show for a given pump at one speed?

- **A.** Chlorine feed rate at each flow
- **B.** Head and efficiency across flow rates
- **C.** Hydrant pressure across the zone
- **D.** Yearly electricity cost of the pump motor

**Answer: B. Head and efficiency across flow rates**

A pump curve plots head, efficiency and power against flow for one impeller and speed. It does not show chemical feed, hydrant pressure or cost.

### 56: Centrifugal pumps and curves (medium)

On a centrifugal pump curve, what generally happens to head as flow increases?

- **A.** Head stays fixed at each flow rate
- **B.** Head rises steadily
- **C.** Head generally falls
- **D.** Head doubles with each flow step

**Answer: C. Head generally falls**

For a centrifugal pump, head falls as flow rises along the curve. Holding head fixed or doubling it with flow does not match the curve shape.

### 57: Centrifugal pumps and curves (medium)

What does shutoff head mean for a centrifugal pump?

- **A.** Head at the best efficiency point
- **B.** Head produced with the motor off
- **C.** Head lost in the pipe when a check valve closes
- **D.** Head developed with the discharge closed

**Answer: D. Head developed with the discharge closed**

Shutoff head is the maximum head at zero flow. It is measured with the discharge closed, not at the best efficiency point or with the motor off.

### 58: Centrifugal pumps and curves (hard)

A pump runs far to the right of its best efficiency point. What is the likely effect?

- **A.** Efficiency improves and power draw drops
- **B.** Flow falls to zero and the pump stops
- **C.** Head drops and motor power draw can rise
- **D.** Head rises above the shutoff head

**Answer: C. Head drops and motor power draw can rise**

Far to the right of the best efficiency point, head falls well below design and power draw can rise. Running this way can overload a motor that was not sized for it.

### 59: Motors and power (easy)

What is the usual purpose of a motor starter?

- **A.** Measures flow through the discharge
- **B.** Switches power to the motor safely
- **C.** Adds lubricant to the bearings
- **D.** Converts motor speed to a fixed head

**Answer: B. Switches power to the motor safely**

A starter switches power to the motor and includes overload protection. It does not set head, measure flow or lubricate bearings.

### 60: Motors and power (medium)

Why does a motor run hot when it is undersized for its pump?

- **A.** It carries more current than its rating
- **B.** It idles while drawing less current
- **C.** It runs faster and friction heats it
- **D.** Pumped water cools it through circulation

**Answer: A. It carries more current than its rating**

An undersized motor is loaded beyond its rating, so current and internal losses rise and heat the windings. Speed and idling are not the cause of the heat.

### 61: Motors and power (medium)

A three-phase motor runs on unbalanced supply voltage. What is the most likely result?

- **A.** Overheating and shorter motor life
- **B.** Automatic speed increase to balance
- **C.** Higher head from extra torque
- **D.** Better efficiency from less current
