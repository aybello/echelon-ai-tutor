# California Collection System Maintenance, Grade 1 (CWEA): 295-question review

295 original practice questions: 250 conceptual and 45 calculations, in US customary units, following the six domains of the CWEA CSM Grade 1 outline (operations and maintenance 28%, tools and equipment 20%, mapping 5%, safety and regulations 26%, administration 11%, math 10%). The first 250 were written to that outline's weights; a fifth batch of 45 was added after the bank was compared with the official Grade 1 exam content outline (handbook v2025.01) and found to be missing several sub-domains. More questions can be added later at the end of the source files. This is a local content package, not proof of a production import.

Correct answers are shown beneath each item. See README.md for scope, sources and limits.

### 1: Types of collection systems and their parts (easy)

Which pipe connects one building to the sewer in the street?

- **A.** Trunk sewer
- **B.** Lateral
- **C.** Force main
- **D.** Interceptor

**Answer: B. Lateral**

A lateral, also called a service connection, carries a single building's wastewater out to the sewer main in the street.

### 2: Types of collection systems and their parts (easy)

A separate sanitary sewer is designed to carry which kind of flow?

- **A.** Treated water headed to a river
- **B.** Street runoff from storms
- **C.** Household and business wastewater
- **D.** Creek water diverted from low areas

**Answer: C. Household and business wastewater**

Sanitary sewers are built for wastewater from buildings. Rain runoff is meant to go into a separate storm drain system.

### 3: Types of collection systems and their parts (easy)

Which part of a collection system gathers flow from several mains and carries it toward the treatment plant in a large pipe?

- **A.** Lateral
- **B.** Air release valve
- **C.** Cleanout
- **D.** Interceptor

**Answer: D. Interceptor**

Interceptors are large sewers that receive flow from many smaller lines and convey it to the treatment plant or a pump station.

### 4: Types of collection systems and their parts (medium)

How does a trunk sewer differ from a typical collector main on a residential street?

- **A.** It is larger and receives flow from several smaller mains
- **B.** It carries stormwater while the collector main carries wastewater
- **C.** It is a pressure pipe while the collector main is gravity
- **D.** It is smaller and serves a single block of homes

**Answer: A. It is larger and receives flow from several smaller mains**

Trunk sewers sit further downstream in the network. They gather flow from many collector mains and are generally bigger than the lines feeding them.

### 5: Types of collection systems and their parts (medium)

Wastewater leaves a pump station through a pressurized pipe. Where does that pipe usually deliver the flow?

- **A.** To a gravity manhole or to the treatment plant
- **B.** To the nearest storm drain inlet
- **C.** Back to the wet well of the same station
- **D.** Into a drinking water main to dilute it

**Answer: A. To a gravity manhole or to the treatment plant**

A force main lifts flow to a higher point. It normally ends at a manhole where gravity flow resumes, or at the plant headworks.

### 6: Types of collection systems and their parts (hard)

A manhole is nearly overflowing upstream, while the next manhole downstream is almost empty. What is the most reasonable conclusion?

- **A.** Infiltration is entering the downstream structure
- **B.** The slope between the manholes is too steep
- **C.** A blockage or restriction sits between the two manholes
- **D.** The upstream manhole has a bigger pipe than the downstream one

**Answer: C. A blockage or restriction sits between the two manholes**

Flow stacking up on one side of a point, with little arriving on the other side, points to a blockage between the two structures.

### 7: Gravity flow basics (easy)

What does the slope of a gravity sewer describe?

- **A.** The number of gallons passing a point each minute
- **B.** The depth of the pipe below the street surface
- **C.** The width of the pipe compared with its length
- **D.** The drop in pipe elevation for each length of pipe

**Answer: D. The drop in pipe elevation for each length of pipe**

Slope is rise or fall divided by run, and it is often shown as a percent or as feet per foot. It is what drives the flow downhill.

### 8: Gravity flow basics (easy)

What is the invert of a sewer pipe?

- **A.** The ground elevation above the pipe
- **B.** The lowest point on the inside of the pipe
- **C.** The highest point on the inside of the pipe
- **D.** The outside surface at a pipe joint

**Answer: B. The lowest point on the inside of the pipe**

The invert is the bottom inside surface where the flow runs. Invert elevations are used to figure out slope between manholes.

### 9: Gravity flow basics (medium)

What is the springline of a pipe?

- **A.** The line where two pipe sections meet at a joint
- **B.** The horizontal line at the widest part of the pipe
- **C.** The lowest line along the bottom of the pipe
- **D.** The line along the very top of the pipe wall

**Answer: B. The horizontal line at the widest part of the pipe**

The springline runs along the sides of a round pipe at its midheight, halfway between the invert and the crown.

### 10: Gravity flow basics (medium)

What is a likely result if a sewer is laid at too flat a slope?

- **A.** Flow reverses toward the upstream manhole
- **B.** Flow speeds up and wears the pipe wall quickly
- **C.** The pipe fills with groundwater from below
- **D.** Flow slows down and solids settle in the pipe

**Answer: D. Flow slows down and solids settle in the pipe**

Flat sewers lack the velocity needed to carry solids. Deposits build up and can lead to blockages and odors.

### 11: Gravity flow basics (medium)

Why can a very steep sewer also have maintenance problems?

- **A.** Gases fail to rise to the manhole openings
- **B.** The pipe is more likely to flood from below during storms
- **C.** Fast flow can wear the pipe and cause turbulence
- **D.** The flow becomes too slow to move toilet paper along

**Answer: C. Fast flow can wear the pipe and cause turbulence**

On steep grades the liquid moves quickly, which can wear the pipe wall and cause turbulence and scour at the next manhole.

### 12: Gravity flow basics (hard)

Two pipes of the same size and roughness carry the same steady, partly full flow. One is laid at 0.4 percent slope and the other at 1.0 percent. Which statement is most reasonable?

- **A.** The steeper pipe moves flow faster and is less prone to settling
- **B.** The steeper pipe moves flow slower because it is deeper
- **C.** Both pipes move flow at the same speed because the size matches
- **D.** The flatter pipe moves flow faster since it holds more water

**Answer: A. The steeper pipe moves flow faster and is less prone to settling**

Velocity rises with slope when the pipe size and roughness are the same. A faster flow is better at keeping solids moving.

### 13: Manholes and structures (easy)

Which part of a manhole sits at street level and is lifted off to get in?

- **A.** The cover
- **B.** The drop pipe
- **C.** The bench
- **D.** The cone section

**Answer: A. The cover**

The removable cover closes the opening at the surface. The cast iron frame stays set into the pavement or a riser.

### 14: Manholes and structures (easy)

What is the job of the cone section of a precast manhole?

- **A.** To store extra flow during heavy storms for later release
- **B.** To carry the flow from the inlet pipe to the outlet pipe
- **C.** To slow the flow down as it passes through the structure
- **D.** To narrow the structure from the barrel up to the frame

**Answer: D. To narrow the structure from the barrel up to the frame**

The cone tapers from the wide barrel to the small opening at the top where the frame and cover sit.

### 15: Manholes and structures (medium)

Why are grade adjustment rings placed between the cone and the frame?

- **A.** To seal the base of the manhole against groundwater
- **B.** To set the frame height to match the road surface
- **C.** To hold the steps in place along the wall
- **D.** To guide the flow toward the outlet pipe

**Answer: B. To set the frame height to match the road surface**

Adjustment rings let crews bring the frame to the finished grade after paving or settling, so the cover sits level with the road.

### 16: Manholes and structures (medium)

A manhole base has a rough, flat floor with no shaped channel. What problem is likely to develop?

- **A.** Higher pressure on the outgoing pipe
- **B.** Faster velocity through the manhole
- **C.** Solids deposits and turbulence in the base
- **D.** Better mixing of flow from the incoming pipes

**Answer: C. Solids deposits and turbulence in the base**

A smooth channel guides flow from the inlet to the outlet. Without one, flow slows and spreads and solids settle in the base.

### 17: Manholes and structures (medium)

Why is a damaged or missing manhole cover a problem in a roadway?

- **A.** It raises the flow speed in the pipe below the opening
- **B.** It lowers the pressure in the pipe below the opening
- **C.** It changes the slope of the pipe below the opening
- **D.** It endangers people and vehicles and lets debris in

**Answer: D. It endangers people and vehicles and lets debris in**

An open or broken cover is a public hazard and a path for stormwater, trash and soil to enter the sewer.

### 18: Manholes and structures (hard)

A manhole frame has settled below the pavement in a low spot, and rain runs into the pick holes of the cover. What concern does this raise?

- **A.** Inflow of surface water into the sewer
- **B.** Backflow of treated water into the line
- **C.** Loss of slope in the pipe below
- **D.** Infiltration of groundwater at pipe joints

**Answer: A. Inflow of surface water into the sewer**

Water entering from the surface through a cover or frame is inflow. Raising the frame or sealing the cover can reduce it.

### 19: Pipe materials, joints and common defects (easy)

Which pipe material is known for strong resistance to chemicals and acid, and is often used in gravity sewers?

- **A.** Unprotected concrete
- **B.** Unlined steel
- **C.** Vitrified clay
- **D.** Plain cast iron

**Answer: C. Vitrified clay**

Vitrified clay is hard and chemically resistant, though it is brittle and comes in short sections that have many joints.

### 20: Pipe materials, joints and common defects (easy)

Which common sewer pipe material is lightweight plastic with gasketed push-on joints?

- **A.** Vitrified clay
- **B.** PVC
- **C.** Cast iron
- **D.** Reinforced concrete

**Answer: B. PVC**

PVC is easy to handle and resistant to corrosion, and it is widely used for small gravity sewers and laterals.

### 21: Pipe materials, joints and common defects (medium)

What does an offset joint look like on a video inspection?

- **A.** A uniform wall color along the whole inspected section
- **B.** A step where one pipe end sits lower than the next
- **C.** A smooth continuous surface through the joint with no seam
- **D.** A sudden increase in the pipe diameter at the joint

**Answer: B. A step where one pipe end sits lower than the next**

Settling or poor bedding can leave adjoining pipes at different elevations, creating a lip that catches solids and can let soil in.

### 22: Pipe materials, joints and common defects (medium)

How is a longitudinal crack different from a circumferential crack?

- **A.** It runs along the length of the pipe
- **B.** It is a hole broken through the crown of the pipe
- **C.** It runs around the circumference of the pipe wall
- **D.** It is a joint that has pulled apart in the line

**Answer: A. It runs along the length of the pipe**

Longitudinal cracks follow the pipe's axis, while circumferential ones wrap around the barrel. Both are recorded as structural defects.

### 23: Pipe materials, joints and common defects (medium)

What does a sag, or belly, in a sewer cause?

- **A.** A spot where rain ponds on the street above it
- **B.** A section where flow speeds up and scours the pipe
- **C.** A section that runs under much higher pressure
- **D.** A low spot that holds standing water

**Answer: D. A low spot that holds standing water**

A sag is a section that has settled below the intended grade. Flow slows there and debris collects, so it often needs repeated cleaning.

### 24: Pipe materials, joints and common defects (medium)

Why does poor pipe bedding matter during installation?

- **A.** Bedding decides which direction the flow moves
- **B.** Bedding material reacts chemically with wastewater
- **C.** Uneven support can lead to sags, cracks and offset joints
- **D.** Extra bedding raises flow velocity above the design speed

**Answer: C. Uneven support can lead to sags, cracks and offset joints**

Pipe depends on a firm, even base. Voids or soft spots can allow it to settle, crack or deflect once soil load is on it.

### 25: Pipe materials, joints and common defects (hard)

A camera shows the top of a pipe wall eaten away and rounded stones exposed. Which pipe and cause fit best?

- **A.** Concrete attacked by acid from hydrogen sulfide
- **B.** Clay worn smooth by flow at high velocity
- **C.** PVC weakened by ultraviolet light in the sunlight
- **D.** HDPE softened by the temperature of the wastewater

**Answer: A. Concrete attacked by acid from hydrogen sulfide**

Sulfide gas in the air space is turned into sulfuric acid on the damp crown, which dissolves the cement and leaves aggregate showing.

### 26: Force mains and pump station basics (easy)

What is a wet well?

- **A.** A pipe that carries pumped flow uphill to the next manhole
- **B.** A manhole where two gravity sewers join into one pipe
- **C.** A structure that stores treated water before it is released
- **D.** A chamber where wastewater collects before it is pumped

**Answer: D. A chamber where wastewater collects before it is pumped**

The wet well is the storage chamber at the pump station. Pumps start and stop as the level rises and falls in it.

### 27: Force mains and pump station basics (easy)

What does a check valve on a pump discharge do?

- **A.** Lets air into the force main to relieve pressure
- **B.** Sets the speed of the pump motor to match the incoming flow
- **C.** Keeps pumped flow from running back into the pump
- **D.** Measures the rate of flow leaving the station

**Answer: C. Keeps pumped flow from running back into the pump**

The check valve closes when the pump stops. It stops the column of water in the force main from draining back into the wet well.

### 28: Force mains and pump station basics (easy)

Which device usually tells a pump station when to start and stop the pumps?

- **A.** A discharge pressure gauge at the station
- **B.** A float switch or level sensor
- **C.** An air release valve at the high point of the main
- **D.** A check valve on the pump discharge line

**Answer: B. A float switch or level sensor**

Level controls monitor the wet well and call pumps on and off at set levels so the well does not run dry or overflow.

### 29: Force mains and pump station basics (medium)

Why do most pump stations have more than one pump?

- **A.** One can keep running while another is down for service
- **B.** The extra pump keeps the wet well empty between storms
- **C.** Two pumps run together to raise the pressure in the main
- **D.** The extra pump lets the station take in stormwater

**Answer: A. One can keep running while another is down for service**

Redundancy keeps the station working when a pump fails or is pulled for maintenance, which protects against overflows.

### 30: Force mains and pump station basics (medium)

What problem can trapped air at a high point cause in a force main?

- **A.** Corrosion at the low points of the line
- **B.** Faster flow and lower pressure in the pipe
- **C.** Less flow area and more resistance for the pumps
- **D.** Backflow of wastewater into the wet well

**Answer: C. Less flow area and more resistance for the pumps**

Air pockets act like a partial blockage. They raise pumping head and reduce the flow that the main can deliver.

### 31: Force mains and pump station basics (hard)

Pumps at a station run much more often during storms than in dry weather. What does this most likely indicate?

- **A.** Normal daily changes in water use
- **B.** A failing check valve at the pump discharge
- **C.** A plugged air release valve on the force main
- **D.** Extra flow from inflow and infiltration upstream

**Answer: D. Extra flow from inflow and infiltration upstream**

Rain-related runs point to stormwater and rain-driven groundwater getting into the sewer system. A dry-weather pattern would reflect household use instead.

### 32: Infiltration and inflow awareness (easy)

Which statement best describes inflow?

- **A.** Wastewater that leaks out of a cracked pipe into soil
- **B.** Stormwater entering through direct connections
- **C.** Groundwater that seeps in through cracks in the pipe
- **D.** Flow that returns from the treatment plant

**Answer: B. Stormwater entering through direct connections**

Inflow is surface water that enters quickly, for example through roof drains, yard drains or uncapped cleanouts, and it peaks during storms.

### 33: Infiltration and inflow awareness (easy)

Why is excess I/I a concern for a collection system?

- **A.** It strengthens the wastewater and speeds up treatment
- **B.** It warms the flow so that odors are reduced
- **C.** It lowers pumping costs at lift stations
- **D.** It uses capacity meant for wastewater in pipes and pumps

**Answer: D. It uses capacity meant for wastewater in pipes and pumps**

Clear water takes up room in pipes, pumps and the plant. In wet weather it can lead to surcharges and overflows.

### 34: Infiltration and inflow awareness (medium)

Which is an example of infiltration?

- **A.** A roof downspout piped into a building sewer
- **B.** Groundwater entering through a cracked joint
- **C.** Rainwater running into a cleanout that has no cap on it
- **D.** A catch basin tied into the sanitary sewer line

**Answer: B. Groundwater entering through a cracked joint**

Infiltration is groundwater that enters through defects such as cracks, bad joints, and leaking manhole walls.

### 35: Infiltration and inflow awareness (medium)

Which is a common private-side source of inflow?

- **A.** A yard drain or gutter downspout piped to the sewer
- **B.** A leaking joint in a main deep under the street
- **C.** A crack in a pipe lying in wet soil
- **D.** A seep through the wall of a manhole set in a creek bed

**Answer: A. A yard drain or gutter downspout piped to the sewer**

Private-side sources include downspouts, area drains and sump pumps tied to the lateral. These send storm runoff directly into the sewer.

### 36: Infiltration and inflow awareness (medium)

Why do operators compare flow data from wet and dry weather?

- **A.** It sets the rate charged for sewer service
- **B.** It predicts how many customers will connect next year
- **C.** The difference shows how much flow is rain-related
- **D.** It tells the age of the pipe in the basin

**Answer: C. The difference shows how much flow is rain-related**

Dry-weather flow gives the baseline, so extra flow during or after storms can be tied to I/I sources in that area.

### 37: Infiltration and inflow awareness (hard)

Flow in a basin rises slowly over several days after a storm, stays high, and then slowly falls. Which source fits best?

- **A.** Pump cycling at a station downstream of the basin
- **B.** Inflow from roof drains tied into the sewer system
- **C.** Normal morning and evening water use patterns
- **D.** Infiltration, as groundwater rises and drains slowly

**Answer: D. Infiltration, as groundwater rises and drains slowly**

Quick spikes point to inflow. A slow rise and slow decline match groundwater entering through pipe defects.

### 38: Preventive and corrective maintenance (easy)

Which task is an example of preventive maintenance?

- **A.** Replacing a pump after it has failed
- **B.** Cleaning a line on a set schedule before it blocks
- **C.** Clearing a line after a customer reports a backup
- **D.** Digging up and replacing a pipe that has already collapsed

**Answer: B. Cleaning a line on a set schedule before it blocks**

Preventive work is planned in advance to keep problems from developing. The other choices respond after something has gone wrong.

### 39: Preventive and corrective maintenance (easy)

What is corrective maintenance?

- **A.** Work done to fix a problem after it has occurred
- **B.** A planned replacement of parts before they wear out
- **C.** A routine inspection with no repairs
- **D.** Scheduled work meant to head off future problems

**Answer: A. Work done to fix a problem after it has occurred**

Corrective, or reactive, maintenance responds to failures or complaints such as a stoppage or a pipe defect.

### 40: Preventive and corrective maintenance (medium)

Why is preventive maintenance generally favored over waiting for failures?

- **A.** It removes the need to inspect the pipes at all
- **B.** It makes old pipes behave like new pipes
- **C.** It tends to reduce overflows and emergency repairs
- **D.** It makes keeping maintenance records unnecessary

**Answer: C. It tends to reduce overflows and emergency repairs**

Planned cleaning and inspection find trouble early. This usually costs less than emergency response and lowers the risk of overflows.

### 41: Preventive and corrective maintenance (medium)

What is a hot spot in a cleaning program?

- **A.** A pump station that has a standby generator
- **B.** A manhole where the air temperature is high
- **C.** A location with repeated problems that is cleaned often
- **D.** A point where flow is measured for billing purposes

**Answer: C. A location with repeated problems that is cleaned often**

Some lines have recurring grease, roots or low spots. They are placed on a more frequent cleaning list than the rest of the system.

### 42: Preventive and corrective maintenance (medium)

Why should crews record what they find while cleaning, such as roots or grease?

- **A.** The records help set future cleaning frequency
- **B.** They show how old the pipe in that section is
- **C.** They are used to size the cleaning truck
- **D.** They take the place of the system maps used by the crews

**Answer: A. The records help set future cleaning frequency**

Notes on conditions let the agency spot trends and adjust schedules. Lines with heavy buildup can be revisited sooner.

### 43: Preventive and corrective maintenance (hard)

A line has been cleaned three times this year after backups, and each time the debris was mostly grease. What does this pattern most suggest?

- **A.** A normal result of any cleaning in the system
- **B.** A recurring cause, such as a nearby grease source
- **C.** A pipe that is much too large for the flow it carries
- **D.** A random event that has no pattern behind it

**Answer: B. A recurring cause, such as a nearby grease source**

Repeated stoppages with the same material point to a source. Reporting it lets staff look into the cause instead of only cleaning.

### 44: Cleaning methods (easy)

Which cleaning method uses high-pressure water sprayed from a nozzle?

- **A.** Bucket machine pulled through
- **B.** Chemical foaming of the walls
- **C.** Rodding with cutting heads
- **D.** Hydraulic jetting

**Answer: D. Hydraulic jetting**

Jetting pushes water through a nozzle on a hose to cut debris loose and flush it toward a manhole.

### 45: Cleaning methods (medium)

How does a rodding machine clear a line?

- **A.** It sprays foam to coat the pipe walls and hold roots back
- **B.** It pulls a camera along the pipe invert to record it
- **C.** It sprays high-pressure water from a nozzle on a hose
- **D.** It turns rods fitted with cutting heads in the pipe

**Answer: D. It turns rods fitted with cutting heads in the pipe**

Rodding uses sectional or continuous rods with a tool on the end. It is often chosen for roots and hard blockages.

### 46: Cleaning methods (medium)

What is a combination truck used for?

- **A.** Digging up and replacing a failed pipe section
- **B.** Testing the sewer for leaks by forcing smoke into it
- **C.** Jetting and removing the loosened debris by suction
- **D.** Recording video of the inside of the sewer line

**Answer: C. Jetting and removing the loosened debris by suction**

A combination unit carries a water tank and a vacuum system, so one crew can wash a line and remove the material at the manhole.

### 47: Cleaning methods (medium)

Which situation is a bucket machine most suited for?

- **A.** Checking the joints of a pipe for leaks
- **B.** Pulling heavy sand and gravel out of a line
- **C.** Measuring the flow rate passing through the pipe
- **D.** Applying a root control treatment to the pipe joints

**Answer: B. Pulling heavy sand and gravel out of a line**

A bucket is dragged through the pipe to scoop heavy material. It works where jetting would only push debris downstream.

### 48: Cleaning methods (medium)

Which risk comes with careless use of a jetting nozzle?

- **A.** Damage to weak pipe or water forced toward buildings
- **B.** Collapse of a manhole cover in the roadway above
- **C.** Loss of slope in the pipe that is being cleaned
- **D.** A rise in the level of the groundwater around it

**Answer: A. Damage to weak pipe or water forced toward buildings**

High pressure can harm old or damaged pipe, and water can flow back through a lateral if the crew is not careful.

### 49: Cleaning methods (medium)

What does a nozzle with rear-facing jets do?

- **A.** Pushes debris into the pipe wall and packs it there
- **B.** Pulls itself and the hose forward while washing debris back
- **C.** Reverses the direction of flow in the line being cleaned
- **D.** Cuts through roots in the line with sharp blades

**Answer: B. Pulls itself and the hose forward while washing debris back**

The jets shoot backward, so the thrust moves the nozzle forward. The water then washes debris back toward the manhole where the hose is set.

### 50: Cleaning methods (hard)

A line has heavy roots growing at the joints. Which cleaning option is most suitable?

- **A.** A routine video inspection by itself
- **B.** A grease emulsifier poured into the line
- **C.** Simple hose flushing from the manhole above
- **D.** A rod machine or a jet with a root-cutting head

**Answer: D. A rod machine or a jet with a root-cutting head**

Roots need a cutting tool. Flushing or a grease product will not remove roots, and a camera only looks at the problem.

### 51: Inspection methods and what defects look like (easy)

What does a CCTV inspection provide?

- **B.** The age of the pipe estimated from its material
- **C.** A measurement of the flow rate moving through the pipe
- **D.** A reading of the air quality in the manhole opening

**Answer: A. A video record of the inside condition of the pipe**

A camera on a tractor or float travels the pipe and records defects and features, which can be reviewed later.

### 52: Inspection methods and what defects look like (easy)

What is lamping?

- **A.** Forcing smoke into the line to find the openings

- **C.** Viewing a line from a manhole with a light or mirror
- **D.** Pumping water through the line to test it for leaks

**Answer: C. Viewing a line from a manhole with a light or mirror**

Lamping is a quick visual check. A light is placed at one manhole and the line is observed from the next one.

### 53: Inspection methods and what defects look like (medium)

Why does a CCTV report record the distance from the manhole for each defect?

- **A.** So the slope of the pipe can be measured directly
- **B.** So the flow rate in the pipe can be calculated
- **C.** So the defect can be found again for repair
- **D.** So the age of the pipe can be estimated later

**Answer: C. So the defect can be found again for repair**

A location along the pipe lets crews return to the exact spot to repair it or check it later.

### 54: Inspection methods and what defects look like (medium)

Why is smoke testing usually avoided when the ground is saturated?

- **A.** Rain turns the smoke into a harmful acid
- **B.** Smoke becomes heavier in the rain and sinks
- **C.** Wet weather raises the flow in the pipe
- **D.** Wet soil can keep smoke from showing at defects

**Answer: D. Wet soil can keep smoke from showing at defects**

Smoke needs a path to the surface. Saturated soil or water in the pipe can block it and hide defects.

### 55: Inspection methods and what defects look like (medium)

Which finding on a video suggests root intrusion?

- **A.** A fibrous mass entering at a joint
- **B.** A smooth dark stain on the pipe wall
- **C.** A bright reflection on the invert
- **D.** A rounded wrinkle in a lining

**Answer: A. A fibrous mass entering at a joint**

Roots enter at joints and cracks and often look like hair or rope. They collect solids and can block the line.

### 56: Inspection methods and what defects look like (hard)

Video shows water standing in a section while flow upstream is low. What is the most reasonable finding?

- **A.** Normal flow at that location in the line
- **B.** A sag holding water in the pipe
- **C.** A pipe laid too steeply for the flow
- **D.** A new connection joined to the line

**Answer: B. A sag holding water in the pipe**

Standing water in a low-flow line usually means the pipe sits lower than the sections around it, a belly or sag. A restriction downstream can look similar, so crews also check the line downstream.

### 57: Roots, grease, FOG and overflow awareness (easy)

Why do tree roots enter sewers?

- **A.** They are attracted to vibration from the flow
- **B.** They are drawn by the warm surface of the pipe
- **C.** They grow along the pipe to follow its slope
- **D.** They seek moisture and nutrients at cracks

**Answer: D. They seek moisture and nutrients at cracks**

Wastewater and moist soil around leaks offer water and nutrients. Roots enter through small openings and then grow.

### 58: Roots, grease, FOG and overflow awareness (easy)

What does FOG stand for in a sewer maintenance program?

- **A.** Flow, overflow and grit
- **B.** Filters, outfalls and gates
- **C.** Fats, oils and grease
- **D.** Force, odor and gas

**Answer: C. Fats, oils and grease**

FOG is the material from cooking and food preparation that cools and sticks in sewer pipes.

### 59: Roots, grease, FOG and overflow awareness (medium)

Why does grease lead to sewer blockages?

- **A.** It cools, hardens and sticks to pipe walls
- **B.** It dissolves into the flow and thickens the wastewater
- **C.** It boils off in the pipe and leaves a residue behind
- **D.** It reacts with PVC and swells the pipe

**Answer: A. It cools, hardens and sticks to pipe walls**

Warm grease from a kitchen cools in the sewer. It forms a layer that traps other debris and narrows the pipe.

### 60: Roots, grease, FOG and overflow awareness (easy)

Which type of business is the usual focus of a FOG control program?

- **A.** Parking garages without any floor drains
- **B.** Restaurants and other food service kitchens
- **C.** Retail clothing stores and boutiques in malls
