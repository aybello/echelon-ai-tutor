What can happen if a pipe rests on a rock in the trench bottom?|Stress concentrates at that spot and the pipe may crack|The pipe corrodes faster along its whole length|The gasket is squeezed out of its groove|The pipe carries less flow because of the bend|A hard spot creates point loading that can break or damage the pipe wall, which is why a smooth bedding layer is placed first.|m
Why is backfill placed in thin layers and compacted?|Each layer can be packed to uniform density, limiting later settling|The pipe can be inspected again after each layer goes in and is covered|Less soil is needed to fill the trench once it is placed in layers|Moisture drains out of the soil much more quickly through the layers|Thin lifts let the compaction equipment reach the whole layer, so the fill does not sink or leave voids around the pipe later.|m
Which equipment is used to compact soil placed around pipe?|A mechanical tamper or vibratory plate compactor|A tapping machine bolted to the main under pressure|A pipe locator with a transmitter and receiver|A trench box set in the excavation as protection|Tampers and plate compactors pack soil. The other tools cut taps, find pipe or protect the trench, not pack soil.|e
Why should large rocks and frozen lumps be kept out of the backfill next to the pipe?|They can damage the pipe and leave voids that settle later|They drain too well and wash the bedding away|They are too light and let the pipe float|They make the soil harder to dig when repaired later|Hard lumps scratch or load the pipe unevenly and leave air gaps that collapse later. Fine, compactable soil is used next to the pipe.|h
@Water mains and piping|CAD1|Connecting pipe, joints and fittings
What should be done to the pipe end and gasket before a push-on joint is assembled?|Clean them and apply approved lubricant|Sand the spigot smooth so the gasket grips tighter than before|Heat the bell so it expands easily over the spigot end|Coat the gasket in pipe dope so it seals better|A clean surface and the proper lubricant let the gasket seat without rolling or tearing. Dope and heat can damage it.|m
Why are the bolts on a mechanical joint tightened in a criss-cross pattern?|To seat the gland evenly against the gasket|To make the bolts easier to remove later|To stretch the pipe wall for added strength|To keep the bolts from rusting in the soil|Alternating across the joint draws the gland down evenly, which compresses the gasket uniformly and prevents leaks.|m
Which fitting connects a branch line to the side of a main?|A tee|An elbow|A reducer|A plug|A tee has an outlet at right angles to the run. An elbow turns a line, a reducer changes size and a plug closes an opening.|m
A newly made joint leaks when the line is first pressurized. Which is the most sensible first check?|Whether the gasket sits properly and the pipe was fully inserted|Whether the trench above the joint has been compacted|Whether the nearby meter is the correct size|Whether the lubricant was wiped from the bell|Leaks at a new push-on joint usually come from a misplaced gasket or a pipe not pushed home. Compaction and meters are not the cause.|h
@Water mains and piping|CAD1|Pipe locating methods
Which tool is used to find a metal pipe buried underground?|An electronic pipe locator|A pitot gauge|A chlorine comparator|A sounding tape|A locator picks up a signal carried by the pipe or sent to it. A pitot gauge measures flow and the other tools serve other jobs.|e
Why can a plastic main be hard to find with an electronic locator?|The pipe does not conduct, so a tracer wire or other aid is needed|The pipe is normally laid much deeper than iron pipe|The pipe reflects the locator signal back to the operator|The pipe has no joints for the locator to pick up|Locators depend on a conductive path. Nonmetallic pipe needs a tracer wire or similar aid so a signal can be applied.|m
Which source is usually checked first to learn where a main was installed?|Utility maps and record drawings|Customer billing history for the street|Water quality sampling reports|Hydrant flow test sheets from the zone|Record drawings show installed locations and depths. The other documents do not show where the pipe runs.|m
@Water mains and piping|CAD1|Pipe materials and applications
Which pipe material is widely used for large water mains and holds up well under high pressure?|Ductile iron|Vitrified clay|Thin-wall electrical conduit|Corrugated drain pipe|Ductile iron is strong and tough, so it is a common choice for mains. The other materials are not used for pressurized drinking water.|e
Where is copper tubing most often used in a water system?|Small service lines and plumbing|Large transmission mains across town|Hydrant barrels and bonnets|Reservoir roof decks and hatches|Copper is easy to bend and joins well in small sizes, so it is common for service lines. It is too costly for large mains.|m
Which statement is true of PVC water pipe?|It does not rust and is light to handle|It breaks down quickly in wet soil|It carries a locating signal as well as steel does|It is made of iron and needs a mortar lining|PVC is a plastic that resists corrosion and weighs little, which makes it easy to install. It does not conduct a locating signal.|m
What should an operator recognize about asbestos-cement pipe?|It is an older material, and dust from cutting it must be controlled|It is a modern plastic pipe made with a smooth inner liner|It is a steel pipe coated inside and out with cement mortar|It is a flexible pipe that bends a long way without breaking|AC pipe is common in older systems and can release fibers when cut or broken, so special handling is required.|m
@Water mains and piping|CAD1|Material compatibility
What can happen when two dissimilar metals touch in the presence of water?|One metal corrodes faster as galvanic action begins|Both metals corrode at the same rate|The metals bond and become stronger|The water becomes harder and scales the pipe|Different metals form a small cell in water, and the less noble one wears away. This is why mixing metals needs care.|e
What is the purpose of an insulating (dielectric) fitting between copper and galvanized steel pipe?|To block the current that causes corrosion|To raise the pressure rating of the joint|To let the pipes expand freely|To filter rust from the water|An insulating fitting breaks the electrical path between unlike metals so galvanic corrosion cannot take place at the joint.|m
Which requirement applies to a pipe, fitting or gasket that touches drinking water?|It must be approved for use with potable water|It must be new rather than previously used|It must be made of metal|It must come from a local supplier|Materials in contact with drinking water must be suitable and approved for that use. Where it comes from or its metal content is irrelevant.|e
Why is it a concern to attach copper fittings to bare buried steel pipe?|The steel is the less noble metal and corrodes faster|The copper acts as the sacrificial metal and wears away first|The steel becomes harder and the joint cracks|The copper threads strip because they are harder|In a galvanic pair the less noble metal is attacked. Steel gives up metal to copper, so the steel next to the fitting deteriorates.|h
@Water mains and piping|CAD1|Service line installation
Why is a new service line flushed before the customer uses it?|To clear out dirt and cuttings left from the work|To test the new meter for accuracy|To raise the pressure in the nearby main|To bring the water to room temperature|Installing a line leaves soil, shavings and debris inside. Flushing moves them out so they do not reach the customer.|e
A service has a 1-inch tap at the main and a 5/8-inch meter. What does this show?|Tap size and meter size are chosen separately and may differ|The meter must be changed to 1 inch to match the tap|The service line must be reduced to 5/8 inch at the main|The installation is wrong and must be redone|The tap and the line size are picked for the supply needed, while the meter is sized for the flow range. They do not have to match.|m
How is the size of a water meter described?|By its nominal connection size, such as 5/8 inch or 1 inch|By the pressure in psi it is built to hold|By the number of gallons it has recorded so far|By the length of the meter box it sits in|Meters are named by nominal size, for example 5/8 or 1 inch. Pressure rating and recorded volume do not name the size.|e
Which part supports soft polyethylene tubing inside a compression fitting so it does not collapse?|An internal stiffener insert|A rubber washer on the outside|A threaded sleeve glued to the tube|A lead seal around the tubing|The compression nut squeezes the tubing, so an insert inside the bore keeps the wall from collapsing and lets the fitting seal.|h
