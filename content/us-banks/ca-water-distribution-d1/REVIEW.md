### 1: Purpose of disinfection, chlorine demand, residual and total chlorine (easy)

What is the main goal of disinfecting drinking water?

- **A.** To clear suspended particles so the water looks bright
- **B.** To destroy or inactivate organisms that can cause illness
- **C.** To bring the water temperature down before delivery
- **D.** To remove the dissolved minerals and salts that cause hardness

**Answer: B. To destroy or inactivate organisms that can cause illness**

Disinfection protects public health by killing or inactivating pathogens. Removing minerals, cooling and clarifying are separate jobs.

### 2: Purpose of disinfection, chlorine demand, residual and total chlorine (easy)

What does the term chlorine demand describe?

- **A.** The amount a feed pump can deliver during one day
- **B.** The amount of chlorine left in the water after contact time
- **C.** The amount of chlorine kept in storage at the plant
- **D.** The amount of chlorine used up by reactions in the water

**Answer: D. The amount of chlorine used up by reactions in the water**

Demand is the chlorine consumed by substances in the water. What remains afterward is the residual.

### 3: Purpose of disinfection, chlorine demand, residual and total chlorine (easy)

How is chlorine demand figured from the test results at a sample point?

- **A.** Dose fed minus the residual measured
- **B.** Dose fed plus the residual measured
- **C.** Dose fed divided by the residual measured
- **D.** Residual measured minus the dose fed

**Answer: A. Dose fed minus the residual measured**

Whatever chlorine was added and not found afterward was consumed, so demand equals dose minus residual.

### 4: Purpose of disinfection, chlorine demand, residual and total chlorine (medium)

Which condition commonly raises the chlorine demand of water?

- **A.** A lower reading on the chlorine feed pump gauge
- **B.** Cooler air around the pipe at the sample station
- **C.** Dissolved iron and organic matter reacting with chlorine
- **D.** A fall in the demand for water at the tap during the night hours

**Answer: C. Dissolved iron and organic matter reacting with chlorine**

Iron, manganese, hydrogen sulfide, ammonia and organic matter react with chlorine and use some of it up before a residual can form.

### 5: Purpose of disinfection, chlorine demand, residual and total chlorine (medium)

An operator finds a much lower chlorine residual at a tap far from the source than at the entry point. What is the most likely reason?

- **A.** The tap sample sat too long before it was tested
- **B.** Chlorine was consumed over time by the water and pipe wall
- **C.** The entry point sample was drawn before the chlorine had mixed in
- **D.** A different supply with less chlorine blended in near that tap

**Answer: B. Chlorine was consumed over time by the water and pipe wall**

Residual decays as chlorine reacts with organics, biofilm and the pipe surface during the time water spends in the system.

### 6: Purpose of disinfection, chlorine demand, residual and total chlorine (medium)

Why does contact time matter when water is disinfected?

- **A.** Longer exposure lets the residual rise above the dose that was fed
- **B.** Time is needed to bring the pH to the level chlorine requires
- **C.** A strong chlorine level kills organisms the instant it touches them
- **D.** Organisms need time in the disinfectant to be inactivated

**Answer: D. Organisms need time in the disinfectant to be inactivated**

Kill depends on both the disinfectant level and how long organisms are exposed to it, so a short exposure may leave pathogens alive.

### 7: Purpose of disinfection, chlorine demand, residual and total chlorine (hard)

A test kit reads zero free chlorine, yet the total chlorine reading is clearly above zero. What does this suggest?

- **A.** The water contains a high level of dissolved oxygen
- **B.** The water holds no chlorine and the total reading is merely instrument noise
- **C.** The chlorine present is bound with ammonia or organics
- **D.** The sample holds strong free chlorine beyond what the kit can display

**Answer: C. The chlorine present is bound with ammonia or organics**

Total chlorine is free plus combined chlorine. A total reading with no free reading means the chlorine is in combined forms, which disinfect far more weakly.

### 8: Water main disinfection and dechlorination (easy)

Why is a newly laid main usually flushed before the disinfectant is added?

- **A.** To wash out dirt and debris that would use up chlorine
- **B.** To fill the pipe with enough water to cool it
- **C.** To bring the main up to its full working pressure before chlorine goes in
- **D.** To check the new pipe wall for hidden cracks before it is filled

**Answer: A. To wash out dirt and debris that would use up chlorine**

Preliminary flushing clears soil and construction debris, so less chlorine is lost to demand and the disinfectant contacts clean pipe walls.

### 9: Water main disinfection and dechlorination (easy)

What is the purpose of dechlorinating water that is discharged from a flushed main?

- **A.** To make the discharged water taste better to nearby residents
- **B.** To increase the amount of water that reaches the storm drain
- **C.** To protect streams and aquatic life from the chlorine
- **D.** To raise the chlorine residual in the main that remains

**Answer: C. To protect streams and aquatic life from the chlorine**

Strongly chlorinated discharge can harm fish and other aquatic organisms, so the chlorine is neutralized before it reaches a creek or storm drain.

### 10: Water main disinfection and dechlorination (medium)

Which chemical is commonly used to neutralize chlorine in discharged water?

- **A.** Sodium bisulfite or a similar sulfite compound
- **B.** Lime slurry
- **C.** Aluminum sulfate solution
- **D.** Calcium hypochlorite in a granular form fed to the discharge

**Answer: A. Sodium bisulfite or a similar sulfite compound**

Sulfur-based compounds such as sodium bisulfite react with chlorine and remove it. Hypochlorite adds chlorine, and the others do not neutralize it.

### 11: Water main disinfection and dechlorination (medium)

In the slug method of disinfecting a main, what is done?

- **A.** The main is filled and held for a day with one steady low dose
- **B.** The main is drained and sprayed with strong chlorine
- **C.** Tablets are placed in each section as it is laid
- **D.** A band of highly chlorinated water is moved along the pipe

**Answer: D. A band of highly chlorinated water is moved along the pipe**

The slug method passes a measured length of strong chlorine solution through the main at a slow rate so it contacts every wall surface for the needed time.

### 12: Water main disinfection and dechlorination (medium)

What does the AWWA standard for disinfecting water mains cover?

**Answer: B. Procedures for disinfecting new and repaired mains**

AWWA C651 sets out accepted methods for disinfecting new and repaired mains and for checking the result before returning them to service.

### 13: Water main disinfection and dechlorination (medium)

Which result is used to show that a newly disinfected main may go into service?

- **A.** A chlorine residual that stayed strong during the contact period
- **B.** A pressure test that holds steady for several hours after filling
- **C.** Passing bacteriological samples taken after the flush
- **D.** A clear color in the water leaving the hydrant

**Answer: C. Passing bacteriological samples taken after the flush**

After the disinfectant is flushed out, lab samples must show no coliform. Clear water or a strong residual during contact does not prove the water is safe.

### 14: Water main disinfection and dechlorination (hard)

During a main fill, an air pocket is trapped at a high point in the pipe. What is the likely problem for disinfection?

- **A.** The pipe surface under the pocket is left untreated
- **B.** Chlorine bleeds out through the pocket and lowers the dose downstream
- **C.** Air in the pocket uses up chlorine faster than the water does
- **D.** Water speeds up around the pocket and cuts contact time everywhere

**Answer: A. The pipe surface under the pocket is left untreated**

Where air holds the water back, the pipe wall stays dry and out of contact with the chlorine, so organisms there can survive. Venting at high points prevents this.

### 15: Well disinfection: contamination sources, depth, volume and dose (easy)

An open or damaged casing top lets rain, runoff, insects and debris reach the water. A sound pad and a raised casing help keep them out.

### 16: Well disinfection: contamination sources, depth, volume and dose (easy)

Which tool is commonly used to find how far down the water surface lies from the top of a well?

An electric sounder signals when its probe touches the water, and the marked cable shows the distance from the reference point down to the water surface.

### 17: Well disinfection: contamination sources, depth, volume and dose (medium)

Why must an operator know the volume of water in a well before disinfecting it?

- **A.** To choose the chlorine solution strength the supplier must stock
- **B.** To work out how much chlorine to add for the target dose

**Answer: B. To work out how much chlorine to add for the target dose**

The chlorine amount needed depends on the water volume that must be treated, so the casing diameter and water depth are needed first.

### 18: Well disinfection: contamination sources, depth, volume and dose (medium)

Which describes a typical way of disinfecting a well after repair work?

- **A.** Run the pump briefly, then take a sample for the laboratory
- **B.** Add a small chlorine dose, take a sample and return the well to service

**Answer: D. Add a strong chlorine solution, circulate it, hold, then flush**

A strong chlorine solution is added and mixed through the casing and pump column, held for the contact time, and then flushed out of the well.

### 19: Well disinfection: contamination sources, depth, volume and dose (medium)

A septic drain field is located close to a well. How does this affect the well?

**Answer: C. It is a possible source of contamination that can reach the water**

Wastewater from a nearby septic system can travel through the soil to a well, so such systems are a recognized contamination source.

### 20: Well disinfection: contamination sources, depth, volume and dose (hard)

A pump is pulled from a well for repair and then reinstalled. Why is the well usually disinfected before it returns to service?

Any equipment lowered into the well may carry bacteria from the surface. Disinfecting afterward treats that possible contamination.

### 21: Storage reservoir disinfection and contamination sources (easy)

Open hatches, torn vent screens and roof openings let animals, droppings, insects and runoff reach the stored water.

### 22: Storage reservoir disinfection and contamination sources (easy)

What is the purpose of a screen on a tank vent?

- **B.** To measure the water level in the tank

A tank must breathe as the level rises and falls, so the vent is screened to keep animals and insects from entering.

### 23: Storage reservoir disinfection and contamination sources (medium)

After a storage tank is cleaned, which method is often used to disinfect its interior?

**Answer: C. Swabbing or spraying walls and floor with strong chlorine solution**

Applying a strong chlorine solution to cleaned surfaces gives direct contact. Contact, filling, residual checks and bacteriological acceptance then follow the applicable method.

### 24: Storage reservoir disinfection and contamination sources (medium)

Volume of a cylinder depends on its diameter and the water depth, and the chlorine needed for a stated dose follows from that volume.

### 26: Storage reservoir disinfection and contamination sources (hard)

Why is a tank cleaned of sediment before it is disinfected?

**Answer: A. Sediment uses up chlorine and can shelter organisms**

Deposits exert chlorine demand and shield organisms from the disinfectant, so surfaces are cleaned first for the chlorine to reach them.

### 27: Coliform group, pathogens and test results (easy)

What is a pathogenic organism?

- **C.** A microbe that helps break down organic waste

Pathogens such as bacteria, viruses and protozoa can cause illness. Many microbes are harmless or helpful.

### 28: Coliform group, pathogens and test results (easy)

Why is the coliform group tested for in drinking water?

- **C.** Their absence proves the water holds no viruses or parasites

**Answer: D. They indicate that contamination could have reached the water**

Coliforms are easy to test for and live in the same places as many pathogens, so their presence signals a possible pathway for contamination.

### 29: Coliform group, pathogens and test results (medium)

Which coliform test result points most strongly to fecal contamination?

- **A.** A high reading for heterotrophic bacteria
- **B.** A positive test for E. coli
- **C.** A low reading for chlorine residual
- **D.** A positive test for total coliform

**Answer: B. A positive test for E. coli**

E. coli lives in the intestines of warm-blooded animals, so finding it means fecal matter has probably reached the water.

### 30: Coliform group, pathogens and test results (medium)

Which is a common laboratory method for testing for coliform bacteria?

- **A.** Spectrophotometer reading of the sample's color against a standard

**Answer: C. Presence-absence testing of the sample in a growth medium**

Presence-absence, membrane filter and multiple-tube methods grow the organisms and report whether, or how many, coliforms are found.

### 31: Coliform group, pathogens and test results (hard)

A routine distribution sample is positive for total coliform but negative for E. coli. How should the result be viewed?

**Answer: A. As a warning of a possible pathway that calls for follow-up**

Total coliform presence shows that contamination may be entering. It calls for a repeat check and a search for the cause, even without E. coli.

### 32: Turbidity, pH and nitrate (easy)

Turbidity is the cloudiness caused by fine suspended solids such as silt, clay and organic particles.

### 33: Turbidity, pH and nitrate (easy)

Finished drinking water is usually near neutral. A reading far outside that range suggests a problem that should be investigated.

### 34: Turbidity, pH and nitrate (medium)

Solids can surround and protect microorganisms and also use up chlorine, so cloudy water may be less well disinfected.

### 35: Turbidity, pH and nitrate (medium)

**Answer: A. Flow stirred up sediment from the pipe walls**

Sudden flow changes can scour loose deposits from the pipe, which raises turbidity until the sediment clears.

### 36: Turbidity, pH and nitrate (medium)

### 37: Turbidity, pH and nitrate (hard)

At a tap, turbidity rises while the chlorine residual falls. Which explanation fits best?

Particles exert chlorine demand and may protect organisms, so a rise in turbidity with a falling residual is a warning sign.

### 38: Unidirectional flushing (easy)

What is the idea behind unidirectional flushing?

- **A.** Valves are set so water moves one way through the mains
- **B.** Each main is isolated and left standing for a day
- **C.** Water is pushed back toward the source at high speed
- **D.** Hydrants are opened at random to relieve pressure

**Answer: A. Valves are set so water moves one way through the mains**

Closing selected valves directs clean water through the chosen main segment and out the hydrant being opened, so the pipes are scoured in an orderly path.

### 39: Unidirectional flushing (medium)

Why does flushing velocity matter?

- **A.** Faster flow adds chlorine to the water
- **B.** Slower flow removes air from the water
- **C.** Faster flow scours deposits from the pipe wall
- **D.** Slower flow raises the pressure in the main

**Answer: C. Faster flow scours deposits from the pipe wall**

Deposits are loosened only when the water moves fast enough. If the flow is too slow, sediment stays on the walls.

### 40: Unidirectional flushing (medium)

Which equipment is commonly used with a hydrant during flushing?

- **B.** A diffuser and a flow-measuring gauge
- **C.** A chlorine feed pump and day tank
- **D.** A jar test machine

**Answer: B. A diffuser and a flow-measuring gauge**

A diffuser slows and spreads the discharge to avoid damage, and a pitot or meter shows the flow so velocity can be checked.

### 41: Unidirectional flushing (hard)

Which is an expected impact of flushing on the system?

- **A.** Loosened sediment is pushed to the dead ends and stays there for good
- **B.** Pressure drops and water briefly turns cloudy at nearby taps
- **C.** Chlorine demand is removed from the whole system
- **D.** Pressure rises at nearby taps and the water turns clear at once

**Answer: B. Pressure drops and water briefly turns cloudy at nearby taps**

Flushing pulls water from other parts of the system and stirs loosened deposits, so customers may see low pressure and discolored water for a time.

### 42: Hydrologic cycle, well depth and sanitary survey (easy)

### 47: Unit conversions for volume, pressure and time (easy)

### 49: Backflow conditions and cross-connections (easy)

What is meant by backflow in a distribution system?

- **A.** A rise in pressure that speeds flow toward customers
- **B.** Water leaving a hydrant faster than the main supplies it
- **C.** Slow flow caused by buildup on the pipe wall
- **D.** Flow moving opposite to its normal direction

**Answer: D. Flow moving opposite to its normal direction**

Backflow is a reversal of flow, which can carry used or polluted water back into the public supply.

### 50: Backflow conditions and cross-connections (medium)

Which condition produces back-siphonage?

- **A.** A valve closing quickly in the main and sending a pressure surge
- **B.** Trapped air collecting at a high point and blocking the flow
- **C.** Supply pressure falling below atmospheric, drawing liquid from an open vessel
- **D.** Customer-side pressure rising above the supply pressure in the line

**Answer: C. Supply pressure falling below atmospheric, drawing liquid from an open vessel**

Back-siphonage is a vacuum or negative-pressure effect: when supply pressure drops below atmospheric, liquid at an atmospheric vessel is drawn backward into the supply.

### 51: Backflow conditions and cross-connections (medium)

Which situation is an example of back-pressure?

- **A.** A main break draining the line and lowering the supply pressure
- **B.** A boiler on the customer side pushing water above supply pressure
- **C.** A hydrant opened nearby, pulling the pressure down in the main
- **D.** A valve closed quickly, sending a surge along the pipe

**Answer: B. A boiler on the customer side pushing water above supply pressure**

Back-pressure occurs when pressure on the user side is higher than the public supply, as with a pump, boiler or elevated tank, and pushes water backward.

### 52: Backflow conditions and cross-connections (medium)

A large main break lowers the pressure in a neighborhood. Why does this raise the risk of contamination at some connections?

- **A.** Water can be drawn backward from a connected source
- **B.** The meters in the area stop registering flow
- **C.** Chlorine is forced out of the water by the low pressure
- **D.** Water speed rises and scours the pipe walls clean

**Answer: A. Water can be drawn backward from a connected source**

Lost supply pressure can fall below that at a connected hose or tank, allowing back-siphonage of whatever the connection touches.

### 53: Backflow conditions and cross-connections (easy)

Which of these is a cross-connection?

- **A.** A pressure gauge fitted to a pump discharge pipe
- **B.** A meter set on a service line ahead of the building
- **C.** A hose on a faucet with its end under chemical in a bucket
- **D.** A hydrant joined to a main through a gate valve

**Answer: C. A hose on a faucet with its end under chemical in a bucket**

A cross-connection links the drinking water to a possible source of pollution. A submerged hose end can draw that liquid back into the plumbing.

### 54: Backflow prevention methods and hazards (easy)

Which protection depends on a vertical open space between the supply outlet and the rim of a receiving vessel?

- **B.** An air gap
- **C.** A reduced pressure backflow assembly
- **D.** A pressure vacuum breaker

**Answer: B. An air gap**

An air gap is a physical separation with open space that water cannot cross backward, so it is the simplest dependable form of protection.

### 55: Backflow prevention methods and hazards (medium)

Why is one ordinary check valve not trusted as protection against a serious hazard?

A single check can foul or wear and then pass water backward. High hazards call for tested assemblies or an air gap.

### 56: Backflow prevention methods and hazards (hard)
