Which statement correctly relates total, free and combined chlorine?|Total chlorine equals free chlorine plus combined chlorine|Free chlorine equals total chlorine plus combined chlorine|Combined chlorine equals total chlorine plus free chlorine|Total chlorine equals free chlorine minus combined chlorine|Total chlorine is the sum of the free and combined portions, so free chlorine is total minus combined.|e
Two samples receive the same chlorine dose. Sample A ends with a residual of 1.2 mg/L and sample B ends with 0.3 mg/L. What does this show?|Sample B has the higher chlorine demand|Sample A has the higher chlorine demand|Sample B was held at a lower temperature|Sample A holds more iron and organic matter|Demand is dose minus residual. Why do operators value a free chlorine residual more than a combined residual of the same reading?|Free chlorine inactivates organisms at a faster rate|Free chlorine persists longer before it breaks down|Free chlorine adds less taste and odor to the water|Free chlorine is easier for a test kit to measure|Free chlorine is a much stronger and faster disinfectant than combined forms such as chloramines, even though combined forms may last longer.|m
As pH rises in water that holds a free chlorine residual, what happens to its disinfecting power?|It drops because more of the chlorine is the weaker hypochlorite form|It rises because more of the chlorine is the stronger hypochlorite form|It stays the same because the amount of residual sets the effect|It rises because the chlorine breaks down more slowly|At higher pH more free chlorine is hypochlorite ion, which disinfects less well than hypochlorous acid, so the same residual does less.|h

Why is a storage tank disinfected after it has been drained for cleaning or repair?|Tools and crews may leave contamination on the tank surfaces|Chlorine hardens the interior coating so that it cures faster|The treated surfaces keep the stored water from warming|Chlorine removes the dissolved air that speeds corrosion|Workers, equipment and debris can leave organisms on tank surfaces, and disinfection prevents them from entering the water supply.|m

A crew will flush chlorinated water from a new main near a creek. Why must liquid hypochlorite be kept away from acids?|The mixture can release toxic chlorine gas|The mixture can turn cloudy and clog a feed line|The mixture can form a harmless salt that settles out|The mixture can raise the pH of the treated water|Acids added to hypochlorite can release chlorine gas, which is a serious inhalation hazard, so the products are stored and handled apart.|e

What does a thrust block on a hydrant or fitting do?|Passes pressure force from the fitting to firm soil|Slows the water moving through the hydrant barrel|Seals the joint between the hydrant and the main|Keeps the hydrant upright against wind and traffic loads|Water pressure pushes outward at bends, tees and hydrant branches. On a typical service line, which fitting is connected directly to the water main?|Corporation stop|Curb stop|Meter setter|Backflow preventer|The corporation stop is the first valve on the service, installed at the main. The curb stop sits farther along, near the property line.|e

What does a meter setter provide?|Fixed connections so a meter can be swapped easily|A built-in strainer that protects the meter from debris|A pressure-reducing section placed ahead of the meter|A tap through the wall of the main for the service|A setter holds the meter at the correct height and spacing with valves or couplings on each side. @Install equipment|FLDS|Pipe laying, backfill and testing a new main
Why is pipe laid on a prepared, uniform bedding in the trench?|To avoid point loads that can crack the pipe|To let the pipe slide freely as pressure changes|To keep groundwater from reaching the pipe joints|To remove the need for restraint at fittings|Even support along the barrel spreads soil loads. Why is tracer wire laid along a buried plastic main?|So a locator can find the pipe later|To ground the pipe against lightning strikes|To keep the pipe from floating in a wet trench|To carry meter signals back to the office|Plastic pipe cannot be located by a metal detector, so wire laid with it gives a conductive path for a locator signal.|m
Why is backfill placed and compacted in thin layers around a new main?|Each thin layer compacts evenly, so support is uniform|Thin layers keep the trench walls from collapsing as it fills|Thin layers allow larger rocks to be mixed in near the pipe|Thin layers let water drain from the soil as the trench is filled|Compaction energy only reaches a limited depth. Why is a newly laid main flushed before it is placed in service?|To remove dirt and debris left inside the pipe|To check the joints for leaks under pressure|To settle the backfill around the fittings|To measure the flow capacity of the new pipe|Flushing carries away soil, stones and other debris from construction. Why is a newly laid main pressure tested before it is accepted?|To confirm joints and fittings hold without leaking|To measure how much chlorine the pipe walls absorb|To settle the backfill around the pipe|To check that hydrants will flow at the expected rate|The pressure test checks the integrity of joints, fittings and restraints.
