# Florida Water Distribution System Operator, Level 3 (DEP): 150-question review

150 original practice questions: 114 conceptual and 36 calculations, in US customary units. DEP's published outline for the Level 3 distribution exam lists subject areas only (administrative duties, drinking water regulations, install equipment, maintain equipment, disinfection, operate equipment, system design, system inspection, water quality, safety and security, math), with no weights and no objectives, so the questions are spread across those areas by judgement and kept at general entry-level practice. No Florida rule numbers, limits or deadlines are tested. This is the first release size; more questions can be added later at the end of the source files. This is a local content package, not proof of a production import.

Correct answers are shown beneath each item. See README.md for scope, sources and limits.

### 1: Residuals, free and total chlorine, and demand (easy)

Why is a disinfectant residual kept in the water after it leaves the plant?

- **A.** It prevents scale from forming on the pipe wall
- **B.** It gives lasting protection against microbes in the pipes
- **C.** It lowers friction losses in long water mains
- **D.** It removes hardness from water before delivery

**Answer: B. It gives lasting protection against microbes in the pipes**

The residual gives continuing protection if organisms get into the pipes or regrow. It does not clear water, remove minerals or support pressure.

### 2: Residuals, free and total chlorine, and demand (easy)

Which statement correctly relates total, free and combined chlorine?

- **A.** Total chlorine equals free chlorine plus combined chlorine
- **B.** Combined chlorine equals total chlorine plus free chlorine
- **C.** Free chlorine equals total chlorine plus combined chlorine
- **D.** Total chlorine equals free chlorine minus combined chlorine

**Answer: A. Total chlorine equals free chlorine plus combined chlorine**

Total chlorine is the sum of the free and combined portions, so free chlorine is total minus combined.

### 3: Residuals, free and total chlorine, and demand (medium)

Two samples receive the same chlorine dose. Sample A ends with a residual of 1.2 mg/L and sample B ends with 0.3 mg/L. What does this show?

- **A.** Sample B was held at a lower temperature
- **B.** Sample A holds more iron and organic matter
- **C.** Sample B has the higher chlorine demand
- **D.** Sample A has the higher chlorine demand

**Answer: C. Sample B has the higher chlorine demand**

Demand is dose minus residual. The sample with the lower residual after the same dose used up more chlorine.

### 4: Residuals, free and total chlorine, and demand (medium)

Why do operators value a free chlorine residual more than a combined residual of the same reading?

- **A.** Free chlorine persists longer before it breaks down
- **B.** Free chlorine is easier for a test kit to measure
- **C.** Free chlorine adds less taste and odor to the water
- **D.** Free chlorine inactivates organisms at a faster rate

**Answer: D. Free chlorine inactivates organisms at a faster rate**

Free chlorine is a much stronger and faster disinfectant than combined forms such as chloramines, even though combined forms may last longer.

### 5: Residuals, free and total chlorine, and demand (hard)

As pH rises in water that holds a free chlorine residual, what happens to its disinfecting power?

- **A.** It rises because more of the chlorine is the stronger hypochlorite form
- **B.** It drops because more of the chlorine is the weaker hypochlorite form
- **C.** It rises because the chlorine breaks down more slowly
- **D.** It stays the same because the amount of residual sets the effect

**Answer: B. It drops because more of the chlorine is the weaker hypochlorite form**

At higher pH more free chlorine is hypochlorite ion, which disinfects less well than hypochlorous acid, so the same residual does less.

### 6: Disinfecting mains, tanks and wells; chlorine hazards (medium)

Which risk is the main reason a repaired section of main is disinfected before it carries drinking water again?

- **A.** The pipe wall may have lost its protective layer of scale
- **B.** The section may have lowered the pressure in the system
- **C.** The new fittings may have changed the hardness of the water
- **D.** Soil and trench water may have brought in harmful organisms

**Answer: D. Soil and trench water may have brought in harmful organisms**

Repairs expose the inside of the pipe to soil, groundwater and tools, so organisms can enter. Disinfection treats that contamination.

### 7: Disinfecting mains, tanks and wells; chlorine hazards (medium)

Why is a storage tank disinfected after it has been drained for cleaning or repair?

- **A.** Chlorine hardens the interior coating so that it cures faster
- **B.** The treated surfaces keep the stored water from warming
- **C.** Tools and crews may leave contamination on the tank surfaces
- **D.** Chlorine removes the dissolved air that speeds corrosion

**Answer: C. Tools and crews may leave contamination on the tank surfaces**

Workers, equipment and debris can leave organisms on tank surfaces, and disinfection prevents them from entering the water supply.

### 8: Disinfecting mains, tanks and wells; chlorine hazards (medium)

After a well pump is pulled for service, why is the well disinfected before it returns to service?

- **A.** The pump, drop pipe and tools can carry organisms down the well
- **B.** The work lowers the water table and changes how microbes grow
- **C.** Removing the pump lets aquifer water mix with surface water
- **D.** The pump motor runs better in a housing filled with chlorine

**Answer: A. The pump, drop pipe and tools can carry organisms down the well**

Equipment lowered into a well can introduce organisms, so the well is disinfected before the water is delivered.

### 9: Disinfecting mains, tanks and wells; chlorine hazards (medium)

A crew will flush chlorinated water from a new main near a creek. Which action best protects the creek?

- **A.** Test the water for cloudiness and then release it
- **B.** Remove the chlorine from the water before it leaves the site
- **C.** Send the water straight to a storm drain so the pavement filters it
- **D.** Release it faster so the creek dilutes it quickly

**Answer: B. Remove the chlorine from the water before it leaves the site**

Strongly chlorinated water can harm fish and other aquatic life, so the chlorine is neutralized first. Storm drains lead to creeks and filter nothing.

### 10: Disinfecting mains, tanks and wells; chlorine hazards (easy)

Why must liquid hypochlorite be kept away from acids?

- **A.** The mixture can raise the pH of the treated water
- **B.** The mixture can turn cloudy and clog a feed line
- **C.** The mixture can release toxic chlorine gas
- **D.** The mixture can form a harmless salt that settles out

**Answer: C. The mixture can release toxic chlorine gas**

Acids added to hypochlorite can release chlorine gas, which is a serious inhalation hazard, so the products are stored and handled apart.

### 11: Valves, hydrants and service connections (medium)

What is the purpose of a valve box placed over a buried gate valve?

- **A.** To give a key a clear path down to the operating nut
- **B.** To hold the valve disc at a fixed position
- **C.** To keep groundwater away from the valve body
- **D.** To support the weight of the valve and the attached pipe

**Answer: A. To give a key a clear path down to the operating nut**

A valve box is a sleeve that lets a key reach the operating nut from grade. It should not carry the load of the valve or pipe, and it does not seal the valve.

### 12: Valves, hydrants and service connections (easy)

What does a thrust block on a hydrant or fitting do?

- **A.** Keeps the hydrant upright against wind and traffic loads
- **B.** Seals the joint between the hydrant and the main
- **C.** Slows the water moving through the hydrant barrel
- **D.** Passes pressure force from the fitting to firm soil

**Answer: D. Passes pressure force from the fitting to firm soil**

Water pressure pushes outward at bends, tees and hydrant branches. A thrust block spreads that force across firm soil so joints are not pushed apart.

### 13: Valves, hydrants and service connections (medium)

Why is crushed stone placed around the base of a dry-barrel hydrant?

- **A.** To raise the operating nut closer to ground level
- **B.** To let the barrel empty through its drain port
- **C.** To filter sediment out of the water entering the shoe
- **D.** To anchor the hydrant against pressure thrust forces

**Answer: B. To let the barrel empty through its drain port**

Stone gives the drain port a path to dissipate water into the surrounding soil so the barrel does not stay full. Restraint comes from thrust blocks or restrained joints, not from stone.

### 14: Valves, hydrants and service connections (easy)

On a typical service line, which fitting is connected directly to the water main?

- **A.** Meter setter
- **B.** Curb stop
- **C.** Corporation stop
- **D.** Backflow preventer

**Answer: C. Corporation stop**

The corporation stop is the first valve on the service, installed at the main. The curb stop sits farther along, near the property line.

### 15: Valves, hydrants and service connections (medium)

What is the main purpose of a curb stop on a service line?

- **A.** To regulate the pressure delivered to the customer
- **B.** To prevent customer water from flowing back into the main
- **C.** To record the volume the customer uses each month
- **D.** To let the utility isolate one customer's service

**Answer: D. To let the utility isolate one customer's service**

A curb stop is a service shutoff the utility can operate, usually in a small box near the property line. Pressure control, backflow protection and measurement are done by other devices.

### 16: Valves, hydrants and service connections (medium)

What does a meter setter provide?

- **A.** Fixed connections so a meter can be swapped easily
- **B.** A pressure-reducing section placed ahead of the meter
- **C.** A built-in strainer that protects the meter from debris
- **D.** A tap through the wall of the main for the service

**Answer: A. Fixed connections so a meter can be swapped easily**

A setter holds the meter at the correct height and spacing with valves or couplings on each side. It is not a tap, a strainer or a regulator.

### 17: Pipe laying, backfill and testing a new main (medium)

Why is pipe laid on a prepared, uniform bedding in the trench?

- **A.** To avoid point loads that can crack the pipe
- **B.** To remove the need for restraint at fittings
- **C.** To let the pipe slide freely as pressure changes
- **D.** To keep groundwater from reaching the pipe joints

**Answer: A. To avoid point loads that can crack the pipe**

Even support along the barrel spreads soil loads. Bedding does not seal joints, allow free movement, or replace thrust restraint.

### 18: Pipe laying, backfill and testing a new main (medium)

Why is tracer wire laid along a buried plastic main?

- **A.** To keep the pipe from floating in a wet trench
- **B.** So a locator can find the pipe later
- **C.** To carry meter signals back to the office
- **D.** To ground the pipe against lightning strikes

**Answer: B. So a locator can find the pipe later**

Plastic pipe cannot be located by a metal detector, so wire laid with it gives a conductive path for a locator signal.

### 19: Pipe laying, backfill and testing a new main (hard)

Why is backfill placed and compacted in thin layers around a new main?

- **A.** Thin layers keep the trench walls from collapsing as it fills
- **B.** Thin layers allow larger rocks to be mixed in near the pipe
- **C.** Thin layers let water drain from the soil as the trench is filled
- **D.** Each thin layer compacts evenly, so support is uniform

**Answer: D. Each thin layer compacts evenly, so support is uniform**

Compaction energy only reaches a limited depth. Thick lifts leave loose pockets that settle later and can leave the pipe unevenly supported. Large rocks near the pipe can damage it.

### 20: Pipe laying, backfill and testing a new main (easy)

Why is a newly laid main flushed before it is placed in service?

- **A.** To check the joints for leaks under pressure
- **B.** To settle the backfill around the fittings
- **C.** To remove dirt and debris left inside the pipe
- **D.** To measure the flow capacity of the new pipe

**Answer: C. To remove dirt and debris left inside the pipe**

Flushing carries away soil, stones and other debris from construction. Leak checking is the job of the pressure test.

### 21: Pipe laying, backfill and testing a new main (medium)

Why is a newly laid main pressure tested before it is accepted?

- **A.** To check that hydrants will flow at the expected rate
- **B.** To measure how much chlorine the pipe walls absorb
- **C.** To confirm joints and fittings hold without leaking
- **D.** To settle the backfill around the pipe

**Answer: C. To confirm joints and fittings hold without leaking**

The pressure test checks the integrity of joints, fittings and restraints. Disinfection and flushing are separate steps with other purposes.

### 22: Valves and hydrants (easy)

Why do crews exercise distribution valves on a regular schedule?

- **A.** To flush sediment from the main and valves
- **B.** To lubricate the pipe joints beneath the valve
- **C.** To calibrate the position indicator on the valve
- **D.** To confirm they move and keep them from seizing

**Answer: D. To confirm they move and keep them from seizing**

Valves left untouched for years can stick from corrosion and deposits. Exercising proves they will work when needed.

### 23: Valves and hydrants (hard)

A valve closes in far fewer turns than its record shows. What does this most likely suggest?

- **A.** The main pressure is higher than normal
- **B.** Debris or a broken part has stopped the disc
- **C.** The packing has been tightened too much
- **D.** The valve is in good condition and needs no follow-up

**Answer: B. Debris or a broken part has stopped the disc**

Each valve has a typical turn count recorded. A much lower count means the disc is blocked or the stem is damaged, so it should be reported for follow-up.

### 24: Valves and hydrants (easy)

What is a common reason for a routine hydrant flushing program?

- **A.** To clear sediment and stale water from mains
- **B.** To make fire flow testing unnecessary at the site
- **C.** To reduce demand on the elevated storage tank
- **D.** To wear in the seats of newly installed hydrants

**Answer: A. To clear sediment and stale water from mains**

Regular flushing moves out sediment and aged water, which helps keep water quality in the system healthy.

### 25: Valves and hydrants (hard)

After a dry-barrel hydrant is shut down, its barrel stays full. What does this suggest?

- **A.** The drain port is plugged or has failed
- **B.** The nozzle caps were left off after the test
- **C.** An isolation valve on the hydrant branch is closed
- **D.** The operating nut has become worn

**Answer: A. The drain port is plugged or has failed**

A dry-barrel hydrant should empty through its drain port after shutdown. A barrel that stays full points to a plugged or failed drain.

### 26: Meters, pumps and records (easy)

Why are customer meters tested or replaced on a schedule?

- **A.** Testing removes scale from inside the register
- **B.** Wear makes them record less water than passes
- **C.** Corrosion of the case is the main source of water loss
- **D.** Aging makes them record more water than passes

**Answer: B. Wear makes them record less water than passes**

Moving parts wear with use, and meters tend to under-register, which means lost revenue. Testing finds meters that no longer meet accuracy standards.

### 27: Meters, pumps and records (medium)

A bench test shows a meter at 97 percent accuracy. What does that mean?

- **A.** About 3 percent of the flow bypasses the register
- **B.** It has a 97 percent chance of lasting another year
- **C.** It registers about 3 percent less water than actually flows
- **D.** It registers about 3 percent more water than actually flows

**Answer: C. It registers about 3 percent less water than actually flows**

Accuracy is registered volume divided by actual volume. A reading of 97 percent means the meter records slightly less than the true amount.

### 28: Meters, pumps and records (medium)

Which routine check helps catch early bearing trouble on a pump and motor?

- **A.** Checking the color of the water at the discharge
- **B.** Counting days since the last hydrant flush
- **C.** Reading the customer meters near the station
- **D.** Feeling and listening for heat, noise and vibration

**Answer: D. Feeling and listening for heat, noise and vibration**

Rising temperature, new noise or vibration are early signs of a failing bearing. Regular rounds let operators report them before a breakdown.

### 29: Meters, pumps and records (medium)

Why should a packing-type pump show a slight drip at the gland?

- **A.** It lets air escape from the casing
- **B.** It shows that the impeller is wearing
- **C.** The drip lubricates and cools the shaft and packing
- **D.** It keeps a vortex from forming at the suction

**Answer: C. The drip lubricates and cools the shaft and packing**

Packing needs a little leakage to carry away heat and reduce wear on the shaft. A gland tightened until it stops dripping can burn the packing and score the shaft.

### 30: Meters, pumps and records (easy)

Which task is an example of preventive maintenance?

- **A.** Rewinding a motor that has burned out
- **B.** Greasing a motor bearing on a set schedule
- **C.** Repairing a main that has broken
- **D.** Replacing a valve that has seized

**Answer: B. Greasing a motor bearing on a set schedule**

Preventive maintenance is planned work done before failure. The other tasks fix equipment after it has already failed.

### 31: Meters, pumps and records (medium)

What is the main benefit of keeping maintenance records for each piece of equipment?

- **A.** They show repair history and help plan future service
- **B.** They replace the need for the manufacturer manual
- **C.** They let operators skip checks on newer equipment
- **D.** They set the price customers pay for water

**Answer: A. They show repair history and help plan future service**

A history of inspections, repairs and parts shows patterns, such as repeated failures, and helps schedule work and budget for replacement.

### 32: Chemical feed, tools and cathodic protection (medium)

Which task helps a chemical feed pump keep delivering its set rate?

- **A.** Keeping the day tank full to the brim
- **B.** Raising the stroke setting to its maximum position
- **C.** Running the pump dry to clear air from the head
- **D.** Cleaning or replacing clogged check valves and tubing

**Answer: D. Cleaning or replacing clogged check valves and tubing**

Scaled or fouled check valves and tubing change the amount a pump delivers. A pump should not be run dry, and a higher stroke setting changes dose rather than restoring accuracy.

### 33: Chemical feed, tools and cathodic protection (medium)

How should hand tools such as valve keys and wrenches be cared for?

- **A.** Kept in the truck bed between jobs to stay ready
- **B.** Repaired in the field with tape when handles loosen
- **C.** Reshaped by the user to fit different nuts
- **D.** Cleaned, inspected for damage and stored dry after use

**Answer: D. Cleaned, inspected for damage and stored dry after use**

Damaged or dirty tools can fail or slip. Cleaning and inspecting them keeps them safe to use and ready for the next job.

### 34: Chemical feed, tools and cathodic protection (medium)

What is the purpose of cathodic protection on a buried metal pipe or tank?

- **A.** To seal pinholes in the metal with a mineral coating
- **B.** To warm the metal so moisture does not collect on it
- **C.** To slow corrosion by supplying protective current
- **D.** To block electrical grounding of the whole system

**Answer: C. To slow corrosion by supplying protective current**

Cathodic protection makes the structure the protected part of a corrosion cell, using a sacrificial anode or an applied current.

### 35: Chemical feed, tools and cathodic protection (medium)

Why is a buried sacrificial anode checked and replaced on a schedule?

- **A.** It is consumed over time as it protects the metal
- **B.** It loses its charge when the surrounding soil dries
- **C.** It builds up scale that narrows the pipe
- **D.** It wears away the valve it is attached to

**Answer: A. It is consumed over time as it protects the metal**

A sacrificial anode corrodes in place of the structure. When it is used up, protection ends unless it is replaced.

### 36: Judging normal and abnormal operation (hard)

A pump motor draws well above its normal amperage while flow stays the same. What is the best interpretation?

- **A.** The water level at the suction has risen
- **B.** Drag or an electrical fault is loading the motor
- **C.** The supply voltage to the station has improved
- **D.** The pump is running more efficiently than before

**Answer: B. Drag or an electrical fault is loading the motor**

Higher current at the same output means extra load or a problem in the motor or pump, such as binding or bearing trouble. It should be reported.

### 37: Judging normal and abnormal operation (medium)

A pressure gauge on a vented, isolated line reads well above zero. What does this suggest?

- **A.** The line is holding its normal working pressure
- **B.** The check valve is leaking forward
- **C.** The gauge is out of calibration or damaged
- **D.** Water hammer is occurring in the line

**Answer: C. The gauge is out of calibration or damaged**

With the line open to the atmosphere, a correct gauge reads zero. A reading above zero points to a faulty or uncalibrated gauge.

### 38: Judging normal and abnormal operation (easy)

When should an operator report an abnormal reading or unusual noise from equipment?

- **A.** After the equipment actually stops working
- **B.** Once it has repeated for about a full week
- **C.** At the next routine scheduled inspection
- **D.** Soon after noticing it, before the fault can grow

**Answer: D. Soon after noticing it, before the fault can grow**

Early reports let the cause be found while it is still a small problem. Waiting can turn a minor fault into a failure or an outage.

### 39: Judging normal and abnormal operation (hard)

At a booster station, discharge pressure rises while flow drops. Which cause is most reasonable?

- **A.** A downstream valve is partly closed
- **B.** A leak has opened on the suction line
- **C.** The impeller has worn
- **D.** The pump is cavitating

**Answer: A. A downstream valve is partly closed**

Closing a valve on the discharge side raises pressure and cuts flow. A worn impeller or cavitation lowers pressure.

### 40: Valves, hydrants, pumps and booster stations (medium)

Why is a large gate valve closed slowly?

- **A.** To allow the disc to bed into its seat
- **B.** To avoid surge pressure in the line
- **C.** To protect the packing from wear
- **D.** To let air enter the pipe

**Answer: B. To avoid surge pressure in the line**

Fast closure stops moving water suddenly and can create a pressure surge. Slow closure lets the momentum of the water dissipate.

### 41: Valves, hydrants, pumps and booster stations (medium)

Why should a dry-barrel hydrant main valve be opened completely while flowing, instead of throttled?

- **A.** Air can be drawn into the main and the barrel
- **B.** Pitot readings become impossible to take
- **C.** The barrel can overheat from the friction
- **D.** The seat can wear and the drain can stay open

**Answer: D. The seat can wear and the drain can stay open**

The drain port closes as the main valve opens and can stay open at small openings. Throttling lets water erode the seat and drain out through the port.

### 42: Valves, hydrants, pumps and booster stations (easy)

What does a booster pump station do?

- **A.** Stores water to meet peak demand and fire flow needs
- **B.** Adds pressure to serve higher ground or distant areas
- **C.** Reduces pressure where it is too high
- **D.** Disinfects water before it enters the zone

**Answer: B. Adds pressure to serve higher ground or distant areas**

A booster pump raises pressure in the pressure zone it serves. Storage, disinfection and pressure reduction are done by other facilities.

### 43: Valves, hydrants, pumps and booster stations (easy)

What does the totalizer on a flow meter display?

- **A.** The volume passed since the meter started or reset
- **B.** The highest flow reached since installation
- **C.** The present flow rate in gallons per minute
- **D.** The average pressure recorded over the last day

**Answer: A. The volume passed since the meter started or reset**

A totalizer is like an odometer, adding up volume over time. The rate display shows the flow right now.

### 44: Backflow, SCADA and emergency power (medium)

What does a reduced-pressure principle backflow assembly protect against?

- **A.** Pressure loss caused by friction in long pipes
- **B.** Surge from the rapid closing of a valve
- **C.** Reverse flow from customer plumbing into the supply
- **D.** Low disinfectant residual at dead ends

**Answer: C. Reverse flow from customer plumbing into the supply**

A backflow assembly keeps water from reversing and entering the public system. It does not reduce friction loss, surges or dead-end water age.

### 45: Backflow, SCADA and emergency power (medium)

What is the purpose of SCADA at a tank or booster station?

- **A.** To replace the need for equipment in the field
- **B.** To treat the water leaving the site
- **C.** To supply backup power when utility service fails
- **D.** To monitor and control remote equipment centrally

**Answer: D. To monitor and control remote equipment centrally**

SCADA collects data such as levels, pressures and pump status, and sends alarms and commands over communication links.

### 46: Backflow, SCADA and emergency power (easy)

Why are emergency generators run on a regular exercise schedule?

- **A.** To confirm they start and carry load in an outage
- **B.** To keep fuel levels from running too low in the tank
- **C.** To lower the station's monthly electric bill
- **D.** To remove the need for an automatic transfer switch

**Answer: A. To confirm they start and carry load in an outage**

A generator that sits idle may not start when needed. Periodic running finds weak batteries, fuel problems and other faults.

### 47: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (easy)

Which distribution layout has mains tied together in closed circuits so water can reach a point from more than one direction?

- **A.** Linear layout
- **B.** Grid layout
- **C.** Radial layout
- **D.** Tree layout

**Answer: B. Grid layout**

A grid ties mains into loops, so a break or shutdown on one pipe can be bypassed. A tree layout feeds each branch from a single path.

### 48: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (medium)

Why do dead-end mains usually produce more water quality complaints than looped mains?

- **A.** Pressure runs high there, so joints leak and let in soil
- **B.** Pipe is small there, so it corrodes faster than larger mains
- **C.** Water moves slowly there, so it ages and loses its residual
- **D.** Water moves quickly there, so sediment gets stirred up constantly

**Answer: C. Water moves slowly there, so it ages and loses its residual**

Little demand at a dead end means long travel times and stagnant water. Looping gives water a path to keep moving.

### 49: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (easy)

What is the main reason a distribution system is divided into pressure zones?

- **A.** Meters read accurately at one fixed pressure setting across the system
- **B.** Ground elevation differences would give too much or too little pressure
- **C.** Disinfectant strength has to be adjusted separately for each area
- **D.** Different pipe materials must be kept apart by neighborhood and by pipe age

**Answer: B. Ground elevation differences would give too much or too little pressure**

Water pressure rises as ground gets lower and falls as ground gets higher. Zones keep service pressure within a workable range for each area.

### 50: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (medium)

What does an elevated storage tank do for the system during the hours of highest demand?

- **A.** Raises the chlorine residual as water sits in the tank
- **B.** Lets the pumps shut down until the tank is empty
- **C.** Supplies water and helps hold pressure while pumps catch up
- **D.** Lowers system pressure so that leakage is reduced

**Answer: C. Supplies water and helps hold pressure while pumps catch up**

Storage floats on the system and releases water when demand exceeds pump output, which steadies pressure. Standing water does not increase the residual.

### 51: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (medium)

What happens when a larger pipe diameter replaces a smaller one carrying the same flow?

- **A.** Velocity falls and friction loss falls
- **B.** Velocity rises and friction loss falls
- **C.** Velocity rises and friction loss rises
- **D.** Velocity falls but friction loss rises

**Answer: A. Velocity falls and friction loss falls**

The same flow spread over a bigger cross-section moves slower, and slower water loses less energy to pipe friction.

### 52: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (medium)

Why are isolation valves placed at intervals along a main instead of only at the ends?

- **A.** The valves let each section use a different disinfectant
- **B.** Pressure drops in steps so the pipe wall in each section stays thinner
- **C.** Each valve measures the flow in its own section of pipe
- **D.** A break can be shut off with fewer customers out of service

**Answer: D. A break can be shut off with fewer customers out of service**

Closely spaced valves shrink the part of the system shut down during a repair, so fewer customers lose water.

### 53: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (hard)

Why should a hydrant be connected to a main sized to deliver fire flow, rather than to a small line?

- **A.** A small fitting does not accept the threads of the hydrant branch and valve
- **B.** A small main loses too much pressure when the hydrant draws heavy flow
- **C.** A small main is pulled loose by the weight of the hydrant barrel above it
- **D.** A small main corrodes more quickly once the hydrant is opened and used

**Answer: B. A small main loses too much pressure when the hydrant draws heavy flow**

Large flows through a small pipe cause high friction loss and a sharp pressure drop at the hydrant and for nearby customers.

### 54: Layouts, zones, storage, pipe and valves (medium)

Which feature gives the most dependable physical protection against backflow at a point where a supply line fills a tank or vessel?

- **A.** A closed gate valve kept on the fill line
- **B.** A single swing check valve on the supply line
- **C.** An air gap between the outlet and the receiving rim
- **D.** A strainer placed ahead of the outlet

**Answer: C. An air gap between the outlet and the receiving rim**

A vertical open space cannot be bridged by reversed flow, unlike a check valve that can foul or stick. Strainers and closed valves do not provide backflow protection.

### 55: Hydrants, valves, meters, tanks, backflow and records (easy)

Which condition found during a hydrant inspection should be recorded as a defect?

- **A.** A barrel that is clear of weeds
- **B.** A cap that turns snugly by hand
- **C.** A bonnet painted in the standard color
- **D.** Damaged threads on an outlet nozzle

**Answer: D. Damaged threads on an outlet nozzle**

Bad threads can leak or prevent hose connections during a flow. The other findings are normal for a hydrant in good condition.

### 56: Hydrants, valves, meters, tanks, backflow and records (easy)

Which finding at a valve box should be reported for follow-up?

- **A.** Dirt or asphalt packed down on top of the operating nut
- **B.** A lid that sits level with the surrounding pavement and moves freely
- **C.** A cover marked with a label for the water system
- **D.** A box centered over the valve stem
