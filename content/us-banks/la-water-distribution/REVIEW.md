### 1: Sources and characteristics of water (easy)

A distribution system's supply is drawn from wells, not from a river intake. What type of source is this?

- **A.** Groundwater from an aquifer
- **B.** Rainwater collected in roof cisterns
- **C.** Treated wastewater reused after advanced treatment
- **D.** Surface water stored in a reservoir

**Answer: A. Groundwater from an aquifer**

Wells draw groundwater from an aquifer. Reservoirs and river intakes hold or take surface water, and reuse is a separate supply option.

### 2: Sources and characteristics of water (easy)

What causes most of the hardness in a water supply?

- **A.** Sodium and potassium chloride, which mainly add salinity
- **B.** Lead and copper particles released from older plumbing
- **C.** Free chlorine and chloramine residuals in the network
- **D.** Calcium and magnesium salts dissolved in the water

**Answer: D. Calcium and magnesium salts dissolved in the water**

Hardness comes from dissolved calcium and magnesium, which form scale and reduce soap lathering. Sodium and potassium add salinity rather than classic hardness.

### 3: Sources and characteristics of water (easy)

Turbidity in a water sample is best described as

- **A.** Total dissolved solids that pass through a fine filter
- **B.** Cloudiness caused by suspended particles that scatter light
- **C.** Free chlorine left over in the pipe after disinfection
- **D.** Concentration of dissolved oxygen that remains after treatment

**Answer: B. Cloudiness caused by suspended particles that scatter light**

Turbidity measures how much suspended material scatters light, which makes water look cloudy. Dissolved solids, chlorine and oxygen are measured separately.

### 4: Sources and characteristics of water (medium)

Water with a pH well below 7 is classed as

- **A.** Alkaline, which speeds the decay of disinfectant in the pipes
- **B.** Hard, which means the water will form scale on pipe walls
- **C.** Acidic, so corrosion of metal pipe becomes more likely
- **D.** Neutral, so corrosion of metal pipe is unlikely to occur

**Answer: C. Acidic, so corrosion of metal pipe becomes more likely**

A pH below 7 is acidic. Acidic water tends to corrode metal pipe, which is why pH is monitored in distribution systems.

### 5: Sources and characteristics of water (medium)

Dissolved ferrous iron in groundwater that is exposed to air is most likely to

- **A.** Join the calcium fraction of hardness and settle out in the tank
- **B.** Oxidize and form reddish-brown particles that stain fixtures
- **C.** Lower the water temperature through evaporation at the tap
- **D.** Turn into a clear gas that escapes from the water and is lost

**Answer: B. Oxidize and form reddish-brown particles that stain fixtures**

Dissolved ferrous iron oxidizes on contact with air and forms rust-colored particles. These particles stain laundry and fixtures.

### 6: Sources and characteristics of water (medium)

A utility is choosing between a well supply and a river supply for a new zone. Which property usually favors the well supply?

- **A.** Higher turbidity from runoff after storms
- **B.** Greater exposure to sunlight, which encourages algae
- **C.** Lower turbidity and steadier temperature
- **D.** Faster recovery after a spill upstream of the intake point

**Answer: C. Lower turbidity and steadier temperature**

Soil and rock filter groundwater, so it is often clear and temperature-stable. Surface water responds quickly to rain and runoff.

### 7: Sources and characteristics of water (medium)

Why does the temperature of source water matter to a distribution operator?

- **A.** Warmer water lowers the chlorine demand of the source water
- **B.** Warmer water increases the flow a main can carry at a given head
- **C.** Warmer water holds more free chlorine, so the feed can be cut
- **D.** Warmer water speeds disinfectant decay and microbial growth

**Answer: D. Warmer water speeds disinfectant decay and microbial growth**

Warmer water speeds chlorine decay and microbial growth in the system. Warmer water holds less dissolved gas, so the other options do not follow.

### 8: Sources and characteristics of water (medium)

Algae in the source water most often cause which problem in the finished water?

- **A.** Taste and odor complaints from customers at the tap
- **B.** Lower hardness that raises the corrosion rate of pipe
- **C.** Higher turbidity that must be removed in the distribution mains
- **D.** Lower chlorine demand that lets microbes grow in storage tanks

**Answer: A. Taste and odor complaints from customers at the tap**

Algae release taste and odor compounds that can reach the tap. Turbidity removal is a treatment step, and algae do not lower hardness.

### 9: Potable water quality concepts (medium)

What makes a water supply potable in general terms?

- **A.** Water with a high mineral content, which gives it a stronger taste
- **B.** Water safe to drink, free of harmful microbes and chemicals
- **C.** Water that has passed a visual check at the source and the plant
- **D.** Water softened for use in boiler feed and process equipment

**Answer: B. Water safe to drink, free of harmful microbes and chemicals**

Potable water is safe for drinking, which depends on microbial, chemical and physical quality. A visual check alone does not establish it.

### 10: Potable water quality concepts (medium)

Coliform bacteria are used as an indicator in drinking water mainly because

- **A.** Easy to test for and suggest that contamination may have entered
- **B.** They are the main source of taste in the finished water
- **C.** They remove chlorine residual from the inside wall of the pipe
- **D.** They cause disease at very low counts in most people who drink it

**Answer: A. Easy to test for and suggest that contamination may have entered**

Coliforms are easy and inexpensive to test for, and a positive result signals a possible contamination pathway. They are an indicator rather than the main health hazard.

### 11: Potable water quality concepts (easy)

Which measure of finished water is most closely linked to its resistance to microbial regrowth in pipes?

- **A.** Specific conductance, which reflects how many dissolved ions the water carries
- **B.** Odor threshold measured by a panel of trained testers
- **C.** Disinfectant residual left in the water as it moves through pipes
- **D.** Color units read against a standard platinum-cobalt scale

**Answer: C. Disinfectant residual left in the water as it moves through pipes**

A disinfectant residual holds back regrowth as water moves through the network. The other measures do not represent that protection.

### 12: Potable water quality concepts (medium)

Taste and odor complaints in a distribution system are most often traced to

- **A.** Excessive pipe pressure that forces air into the water supply
- **B.** Excess chlorine residual reacting with pipe linings in the zone
- **C.** High static pressure that expands the pipe walls and joints
- **D.** Chemical or biological compounds present at low levels in the water

**Answer: D. Chemical or biological compounds present at low levels in the water**

Taste and odor usually come from compounds in the water, including some from biological activity. Pressure and pipe linings do not directly cause the common complaints.

### 13: Potable water quality concepts (medium)

A lead service line is a concern for water quality mainly because

- **A.** Lead increases the flow capacity of the service connection itself
- **B.** Lead can dissolve into water that stands in contact with it
- **C.** Lead raises hardness above the level the treatment target allows
- **D.** Lead stops chlorine from reacting with organic matter in the pipe

**Answer: B. Lead can dissolve into water that stands in contact with it**

Lead dissolves into water that stays in contact with the line, so standing water is the main concern. The other options misstate the effect.

### 14: Potable water quality concepts (easy)

Nitrification in a chloraminated system is most often linked to which condition?

- **A.** High iron content in the source water entering the plant
- **B.** Cold water temperature during the winter months across the district
- **C.** Long water age with a low chloramine residual in the network
- **D.** Frequent hydrant use during construction work in the zone

**Answer: C. Long water age with a low chloramine residual in the network**

Stagnant water with a low chloramine residual lets nitrifying bacteria grow. Water age is the main driver, not iron or construction use.

### 15: Potable water quality concepts (easy)

The term water age in a distribution system refers to

- **A.** Time since the last meter reading at a customer's property
- **B.** Age of the source reservoir before water enters the treatment plant
- **C.** Number of years a pipe has been in service in the district
- **D.** Average time water has spent in the system since leaving the plant

**Answer: D. Average time water has spent in the system since leaving the plant**

Water age is how long treated water has been in the network. Longer ages can reduce disinfectant residual.

### 16: Potable water quality concepts (medium)

Why do distribution operators check turbidity in water entering a zone?

- **A.** High turbidity can shelter microbes from disinfectant
- **B.** High turbidity lowers the pH of the water entering the zone
- **C.** High turbidity shows that the zone's pipes need new lining
- **D.** High turbidity can clog pipe joints and reduce the flow of the mains

**Answer: A. High turbidity can shelter microbes from disinfectant**

High turbidity can shield microbes from disinfectant, so it is watched closely. Turbidity does not show joint condition, pH or the condition of the lining.

### 17: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (medium)

Ductile iron pipe is often chosen for water mains mainly because it

- **A.** Needs no lining because the iron is naturally corrosion-proof
- **B.** Needs no joint restraint at any operating pressure
- **C.** Weighs less than PVC pipe of the same diameter and wall thickness
- **D.** Resists breakage better than brittle cast iron under impact and load

**Answer: D. Resists breakage better than brittle cast iron under impact and load**

Ductile iron is tougher than older gray cast iron and handles impact and load well. Bends and thrust still need restraint or thrust blocks, and the pipe still needs corrosion protection.

### 18: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (medium)

Which valve type controls flow with a quarter-turn motion and a rotating element?

- **A.** Pressure reducing valve, which holds a set downstream pressure in the zone
- **B.** Butterfly valve, which uses a disc on a central shaft to throttle flow
- **C.** Check valve, which closes to stop reverse flow through the line
- **D.** Gate valve, which uses a sliding wedge that rises and falls in the body

**Answer: B. Butterfly valve, which uses a disc on a central shaft to throttle flow**

A butterfly valve uses a disc on a central shaft to throttle or isolate flow. Gate valves use a sliding wedge, and check valves are one-way.

### 19: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (easy)

The purpose of a fire hydrant on a water main is to

- **A.** Control of the flow to customers during a planned shutdown
- **B.** Measurement of the pressure delivered to each customer meter

**Answer: C. Connection for firefighting and for flushing the main**

Hydrants supply water for fire protection and are also used for flushing. Pressure measurement and flow control are done with other fittings.

### 20: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (easy)

Which pipe material is commonly used for small-diameter service lines because it is flexible and lightweight?

- **A.** Polyethylene tubing, which bends easily and resists rust
- **B.** Galvanized steel pipe, which is rigid and joined by threads

**Answer: A. Polyethylene tubing, which bends easily and resists rust**

Polyethylene tubing is flexible, corrosion resistant and easy to install in service lines. The other materials are rigid or unsuited to small service connections.

### 21: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (easy)

A restrained joint on a water main is used to

- **A.** Allows the pipe to move freely during soil settlement and frost
- **B.** Increases the flow capacity through the joint at high velocity
- **C.** Stops the joint from leaking when the trench is backfilled with sand
- **D.** Keeps the pipe from separating under thrust at bends and fittings

**Answer: D. Keeps the pipe from separating under thrust at bends and fittings**

A restrained joint resists the thrust that builds at bends, tees and dead ends. Flexible joints serve a different purpose.

### 22: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (medium)

A corporation stop is installed on a water main to

- **A.** Connects a service line to the main and allows it to be shut off
- **B.** Vents trapped air from the top of the main to the surface
- **C.** Measures flow in the service line continuously for billing
- **D.** Reduces pressure to the customer's fixtures before the water passes the meter

**Answer: A. Connects a service line to the main and allows it to be shut off**

A corporation stop is the tap on the main where a service line connects. It lets the service be shut off at the main.

### 23: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (medium)

Air release valves are placed at high points on a water main to

- **A.** Let air into the main so that the pipe drains fully during repairs
- **B.** Remove trapped air that reduces flow capacity and causes surges
- **C.** Raise the pressure at the lowest point of the line in the zone
- **D.** Keep the main full during freezing weather by holding water

**Answer: B. Remove trapped air that reduces flow capacity and causes surges**

Trapped air at high points reduces the effective area of flow and can cause pressure surges. Air release valves vent it.

### 24: Pipe materials, joints and appurtenances (medium)

Which factor matters most when choosing a pipe material for a main that runs through wet, aggressive soil?

- **A.** Joint type, regardless of the pressure rating
- **B.** Whether the pipe carries hot or cold water as a design rule
- **C.** Soil corrosivity and the corrosion protection it needs
- **D.** Pipe length, since longer lengths need thicker walls

**Answer: C. Soil corrosivity and the corrosion protection it needs**

Material choice weighs soil corrosivity, pressure rating, installation conditions and cost. Length and joint type alone do not decide the material.

### 25: Installation and construction methods (easy)

Bedding under a water main is placed mainly to

- **A.** Keeps the trench dry during construction while the pipe is laid
- **B.** Holds the pipe at a fixed grade so that flow can be metered
- **C.** Supports the pipe evenly and prevents point loads on the barrel
- **D.** Adds strength to the pipe wall so thinner pipe can be used

**Answer: C. Supports the pipe evenly and prevents point loads on the barrel**

Uniform bedding spreads the load along the pipe. Point loads from rock or uneven support can crack the barrel.

### 26: Installation and construction methods (medium)

Minimum cover over a water main protects it from

- **A.** Corrosion caused by high chlorine residual in the water main
- **B.** Flow restriction at the service connection to the customer
- **C.** Pressure surges caused by valve operation during routine work
- **D.** Frost, surface loads and accidental damage from digging

**Answer: D. Frost, surface loads and accidental damage from digging**

Cover depth protects the pipe from freezing, traffic loads and digging damage. Surge and corrosion are managed by other means.

### 27: Installation and construction methods (easy)

A concrete block placed behind a bend in a water main works by

- **A.** Carrying the weight of the valve box and its cover securely
- **B.** Transferring the force at the bend into surrounding soil
- **C.** Sealing the joint so that no leakage occurs at the fitting
- **D.** Anchoring the pipe so that it can expand with temperature changes

**Answer: B. Transferring the force at the bend into surrounding soil**

A thrust block bears against undisturbed soil so the force at the bend does not push the joint apart. Joint sealing and temperature expansion are separate concerns.

### 28: Installation and construction methods (medium)

Trench backfill is placed in lifts and compacted mainly to

- **A.** Prevents later settlement that can damage the pipe and surface
- **B.** Keep groundwater from rising into the trench during the work
- **C.** Removes the need for bedding material under the pipe barrel
- **D.** Lowers the groundwater table near the pipe during the work

**Answer: A. Prevents later settlement that can damage the pipe and surface**

Compacted lifts limit settlement that can stress joints and damage the surface. Poorly compacted backfill can also disturb the bedding under the pipe.

### 29: Installation and construction methods (medium)

Why are pipes laid with a separation from sewer lines?

Separation and crossing rules reduce contamination risk when a water main or sewer breaks. The other options are not reasons for separation.

### 30: Installation and construction methods (medium)

Horizontal directional drilling is used for water main installation mainly to

- **A.** Installs pipe under a road or waterway with little surface disruption

**Answer: A. Installs pipe under a road or waterway with little surface disruption**

### 31: Installation and construction methods (easy)

A main is installed deeper than its design cover. Which effect is most directly a concern for the pipe?

- **A.** Lower internal pressure in the pipe at each connection point
- **B.** Greater external load on the pipe and its fittings
- **C.** Higher risk that the pipe will freeze, due to the extra depth
- **D.** Less need for bedding material along the barrel

**Answer: B. Greater external load on the pipe and its fittings**

Greater cover adds load on the pipe and fittings, which the design must account for. Depth does not lower internal pressure, and extra depth reduces freezing risk.

### 32: Installation and construction methods (medium)

Which step helps prevent a newly installed main from carrying construction debris into service?

- **A.** Adding extra hydrants along the run during construction
- **B.** Pressure-testing the joints and then leaving the main to stand full
- **C.** Flushing and disinfecting the main before it goes into service
- **D.** Filling the main and letting it stand for a week before flushing

**Answer: C. Flushing and disinfecting the main before it goes into service**

New mains are flushed along their full length to remove debris and then disinfected before service. Standing water and a pressure test do not clean the main.

### 33: Disinfection and residual chlorination (easy)

Free chlorine residual in distribution water is the chlorine that is still available to

- **A.** Raise the pH of the water by a fixed amount at each booster
- **B.** Lower the hardness of the water at the booster station
- **C.** Bind hardness minerals into a solid that settles in storage tanks
- **D.** Kill microbes and react with contaminants in the network

**Answer: D. Kill microbes and react with contaminants in the network**

Free chlorine is the active form that keeps disinfecting and reacting in the network. Hardness binding and pH control are not its role.

### 34: Disinfection and residual chlorination (medium)

Chlorine demand is best described as

- **A.** Chlorine used up by reactions before a residual remains
- **B.** Number of feed pumps needed at a booster station in the zone
- **C.** Total chlorine stored on site at the plant for emergencies
- **D.** Flow rate at which chlorine is pumped into the main line

**Answer: A. Chlorine used up by reactions before a residual remains**

Chlorine demand is the chlorine used up by organic matter, iron, manganese and microbes. A residual remains only after demand is met.

### 35: Disinfection and residual chlorination (easy)

Operators want the disinfectant to act on microbes in the water before it reaches the first customer. What term describes that period?

- **A.** Detention time, the average storage in a tank that is not in service

- **C.** Water age, the average time since the water left the plant
- **D.** Travel time, the time a pump takes to start after a call

**Answer: B. Contact time, the duration of exposure before water is used**

Contact time is the period the disinfectant has to act before use. Water age measures time in the network, which is a different quantity.

### 36: Disinfection and residual chlorination (medium)

A chlorine residual that falls steadily along the network is most often a sign of

- **A.** Clogged hydrant drain at the far end of the zone
- **B.** Pressure surge in the main at the plant after a pump start

- **D.** Broken service line at a customer's meter during the night

**Answer: C. Demand from water quality changes and long travel time**

Residual decay along the network typically reflects demand and water age. Pressure surges and hydrant drains do not directly reduce chlorine.

### 37: Disinfection and residual chlorination (easy)

Free chlorine and combined chlorine differ mainly in that combined chlorine

Combined chlorine forms from reactions with ammonia or nitrogen compounds. It is generally a weaker disinfectant than free chlorine.

### 38: Disinfection and residual chlorination (medium)

Why is a disinfectant residual measured at points far from the treatment plant?

Residuals at distant points show whether protection survives the trip through the network. Plant pump speed and meter readings are measured at the plant.

### 39: Disinfection and residual chlorination (easy)

A chlorine feed at the plant is underdosing. Which pattern would operators most likely see?

An underdosed feed produces low residuals throughout the network. Far taps often show the lowest levels because demand and water age are greatest there.

### 40: Disinfection and residual chlorination (easy)

The breakpoint in chlorination is the point at which

- **A.** Added chlorine has met the demand so free residual begins to rise

**Answer: A. Added chlorine has met the demand so free residual begins to rise**

Breakpoint chlorination oxidizes ammonia and other demand so that additional chlorine produces free residual. It is a chemistry point, not a pump or pH state.

### 41: Sampling, flushing and dead ends (easy)

### 44: Sampling, flushing and dead ends (medium)

### 45: Sampling, flushing and dead ends (easy)

Water standing in a hydrant barrel is not representative. Flushing the outlet first gives a sample of the main's water.

### 46: Sampling, flushing and dead ends (medium)

Water age in a system is most likely to increase when

Oversized storage and low demand let water sit longer before use. Higher flow through the network, such as from a booster pump, tends to shorten water age.

### 47: Sampling, flushing and dead ends (medium)

Which sign at a sample tap most suggests stagnant water rather than current flow?

### 54: Bacteriological sampling and test meaning (medium)

- **A.** Measure the chlorine dose needed at the plant
