# New Mexico Wastewater Collection System Level 1 (CS1): 250-question review

250 original practice questions: 195 conceptual (including 8 Regulations items from 20.7.4 NMAC) and 55 calculations, in US customary units. Scope is the technical content of the March 2026 CS1 Operator Guidebook. No 20.6.2 NMAC or NPDES items: the guidebook names those rules but no clauses within them. This is a local content package, not proof of a production import.

Correct answers are shown beneath each item. See README.md for scope, sources and limits.

### 1: Layout, components and service connections (medium)

Which part of a sewer network carries flow from a single building to the collection main in the street?

- **A.** Interceptor sewer that collects flow from many mains across a district
- **B.** Service lateral from one building to the main
- **C.** Drop manhole that joins two lines at different elevations
- **D.** Siphon that carries flow under an obstruction

**Answer: B. Service lateral from one building to the main**

A service lateral is the line from one property to the collection main. Interceptors collect from many mains and are much larger.

### 2: Layout, components and service connections (medium)

What is the usual purpose of a cleanout placed on a lateral near the property line?

- **A.** Measuring the flow entering the public main
- **B.** Pumping wastewater up to the street main when the ground is flat
- **C.** Access for rodding and cleaning the lateral
- **D.** Relief of pressure during peak storm flows in the street main

**Answer: C. Access for rodding and cleaning the lateral**

A cleanout is an access fitting for clearing blockages in the lateral. It does not pump or meter flow.

### 3: Layout, components and service connections (medium)

Manholes are usually placed at which locations on a gravity sewer?

- **B.** Only where the pipe crosses a major highway under a bridge
- **C.** Midway between every service connection along the street
- **D.** The far end of each dead-end main, and nowhere else along the run

**Answer: A. Changes in direction, size or grade, and at junctions**

Manholes give access where cleaning and inspection are needed, including changes in alignment, size or grade and junctions.

### 4: Layout, components and service connections (hard)

Which collection system layout relies on natural ground slope to move wastewater toward the treatment plant?

- **A.** Pressure network in which a grinder pump at each house lifts its own flow
- **B.** Vacuum network with sealed valve pits at each customer connection
- **C.** Dead-end network with no outlet point at the far end of each main
- **D.** Gravity sewer network with manholes at intervals

**Answer: D. Gravity sewer network with manholes at intervals**

Gravity networks depend on a sustained pipe slope, which is set in design and need not match the ground. Pressure and vacuum systems are used where ground conditions prevent gravity flow.

### 8: Layout, components and service connections (medium)

A dead-end main is closed at one end, so flow reaching that end has no way on and solids can collect there. That end needs planned cleaning or flushing.

### 13: Gravity sewers, force mains and manhole concepts (medium)

### 14: Gravity sewers, force mains and manhole concepts (hard)

### 16: Gravity sewers, force mains and manhole concepts (medium)

### 18: Gravity sewers, force mains and manhole concepts (medium)

### 19: Gravity sewers, force mains and manhole concepts (medium)

### 21: Gravity sewers, force mains and manhole concepts (hard)

### 22: Gravity sewers, force mains and manhole concepts (hard)

A slope too flat for the flow lets solids settle. Over time deposits reduce capacity and raise the water surface.
