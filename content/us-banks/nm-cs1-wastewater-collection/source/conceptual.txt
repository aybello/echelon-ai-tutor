Under 20.7.4 NMAC, what may the department restrict a certification to?|Distribution systems or collection systems|Treatment plants or lift stations|Laboratory systems or sampling networks|Individual pumps and valves|20.7.4.20 D NMAC says the department may issue certifications restricted to distribution systems or collection systems. Under the definitions in 20.7.4 NMAC, which description fits a collection system?|Pipelines or conduits, pumping stations, force mains and related facilities used to collect and convey waste|Pipelines and appurtenances that carry potable water under pressure to each consumer in the service area|Tanks, clarifiers and aeration basins that treat waste before it is discharged to a receiving water body|Pipes that carry treated effluent from the plant to the receiving water body for final release|20.7.4.7 D NMAC defines a collection system as pipelines or conduits, pumping stations, force mains and other devices, appurtenances and facilities used to collect and convey waste to a point of treatment and disposal. The potable water description is the distribution system definition in 20.7.4.7 G.|m
Under 20.7.4 NMAC, how does meeting its certification rules affect a person's other legal obligations?|It does not relieve the person of duties under other state and federal rules|It fully satisfies the state and federal rules that apply to the same work|It replaces federal rules for collection systems within New Mexico|A valid operator certificate waives other rules for the work it covers|20.7.4.9 NMAC says compliance with this part does not relieve a person from the obligation to comply with other applicable state and federal regulations. Certification is one layer of duty, not a substitute for the rest.|m
Under 20.7.4 NMAC, what standard governs the number of certified operators at a public wastewater facility?|The minimum needed to operate the facility to protect human health, public welfare or the environment|The number the owner's insurer specifies for liability coverage of the facility and its staff|The number of operators holding the highest wastewater certificate that the state issues to any operator|Whatever number the facility's staffing budget allows for its crew and shifts|20.7.4.15 A NMAC requires a public wastewater facility to provide the minimum number of certified operators needed to operate it to protect human health, public welfare or the environment. Under Subsection B the department may set that number in writing after a violation finding.|m
What is listed first among a certified operator's duties under the code of professional conduct in 20.7.4 NMAC?|Protecting the safety, health and welfare of the public in the operator's work|Protecting the employer's reputation and standing ahead of other operator duties|Keeping the facility's operating records confidential from the department itself|Reporting conduct to the owner first and not to the department or other authority|20.7.4.16 C(1) NMAC says the certified operator shall protect the safety, health and welfare of the public in performing the operator's duties. Under 20.7.4 NMAC, can one operator certificate cover operating both a drinking water system and a wastewater facility?|Separate certification is required for each type of operation, so one certificate does not cover both|One certificate covers both types of facility once the operator passes a combined exam|A water certificate of level 4 or higher covers wastewater work without any further certification|A wastewater laboratory certificate also covers plant operation under the same rules|20.7.4.20 B NMAC requires separate certification for the operation of public water supply systems and public wastewater facilities.
