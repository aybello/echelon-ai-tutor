Why must a centrifugal pump with a suction lift be primed before startup?|Air in the casing is unable to create enough suction to move the liquid, so the casing must be full first|Liquid in the pump lubricates the bearings during startup, and the bearings need it to turn|Priming cools the motor windings during the first minutes of the startup, which prevents overheating|Priming stops the solids from settling in the pump casing during the time that the pump is idle|A centrifugal pump creates suction by moving liquid. Air in the casing prevents that, so priming removes the air.|m
In typical turbulent pipe flow, what happens to friction head loss when the flow rate increases?|It increases, and it grows faster than the flow rate does because velocity enters the calculation|It falls, because faster water slides more easily along the pipe wall and loses less energy|It stays the same, because friction depends mainly on the length of the pipe and not on flow|It falls to zero once the pipe is full, because the water no longer touches the pipe wall|Friction head loss rises with velocity, roughly with the square of velocity in turbulent flow. That is faster than the flow rate.|h
What is the purpose of a bearing lubrication schedule in a maintenance program?|Lubricant reduces friction and heat in bearings, and a schedule helps prevent wear and early failure|Oil acts as the coolant that keeps the motor from overheating during a power failure on the site|Oil prevents corrosion inside the pump casing that is caused by the gases in the sewer system|Oil makes the impeller turn faster at the same speed so that the pump delivers a higher flow|Bearings wear faster without lubrication. A schedule makes sure lubrication happens before the problem develops.|m
@Flow measurement|PADEPM25|Flow measurement
Why does a treatment plant measure its flow?|Flow data are needed to run the plant, to size chemical doses and to report what has been treated|Flow readings replace the need for any laboratory testing of the wastewater samples at the plant|Flow readings decide the color of the treated water that is discharged to the receiving stream|Flow data are used to set the price of the water supplied to the customers on the public system|Flow is used to control chemical feed, to judge hydraulic loading and to track volumes treated.|e
What does a weir measure?|The upstream depth above the crest, which is converted to flow with a head-discharge relationship|The weight of solids that settle out of the flow as they pass over the crest of the structure|The speed of the water as it passes over the crest at the downstream end of the channel|The volume of air trapped above the water surface in the channel upstream of the crest|A weir is a hydraulic control. The depth of water upstream of the weir gives flow through a known relationship.|m
What is the main advantage of a Parshall flume?|It measures flow with a small head loss and a stable, known relationship between depth and flow|It needs no calibration because its throat dimensions set the flow exactly for every installation|It keeps the velocity steady at any depth, so grit stays suspended in the channel at any flow|It weighs the water collected in the throat so the total volume is recorded directly by the operator|A flume is a calibrated open-channel device. Its head loss is small, and under free-flow conditions the upstream depth gives a well-established flow relationship.|m
How does a magnetic flowmeter measure flow?|It measures the voltage induced in a conductive liquid moving through a magnetic field|It counts the revolutions of a paddle wheel that is turned by the moving liquid in the pipe|It times how long a dye pulse takes to travel between two sensors placed along the pipe|It measures the pressure drop across an orifice plate installed in the pipe at the meter|Faraday's law of induction gives a voltage proportional to the velocity of the conductive liquid moving through the field.|m
What is the difference between a flow rate and a totalized flow?|A flow rate is the amount per unit time, and a totalized flow is the accumulated volume over a period|A flow rate is the volume held in one tank, and a totalized flow is the sum of the pipe diameters|A flow rate and a totalized flow are the same measurement, expressed in different units on the display|A flow rate counts the number of pump starts, and a totalized flow counts the number of alarms|Flow rate is volume per time, such as gpm or MGD. Totalizing adds the volume over time to give the total gallons.|m
Why are flow meters checked and calibrated on a schedule?|Readings drift with wear and fouling, so meters must be checked against a known standard|Meters are checked only when the process data look wrong, so routine calibration is unnecessary|Calibration resets the totalizer to zero each shift so that the daily volume starts from zero|Calibration is needed mainly so the pump start logs match the flow meter's daily totals|Flow instruments drift over time and can foul or wear. Checking them against a known reference keeps the readings reliable.|m
@Laboratory and administration|PADEPM12|Laboratory overview
What is the main purpose of routine laboratory testing at a treatment plant?|To give operators water and solids data so they can judge treatment performance|To confirm that the flow meter readings match the pump run hours recorded on the logs|To decide which chemicals the plant buys each month from the supplier's catalog|To verify that the discharge permit fees have been paid to the state agency each year|Lab results tell operators how the process is performing and support decisions about operation.|e
Why must a wastewater sample be representative?|A representative sample reflects the whole flow, so the result describes the process and not one spot|A representative sample is the largest sample that can be collected, so the lab can split it into tests|A representative sample must be taken from the top of the tank so that the solids are excluded from it|A representative sample is one that has been chilled and preserved so that it stays fresh for testing|Sampling technique matters because a sample that does not reflect the flow gives a misleading result.|m
Why do plants keep complete laboratory records?|Records show trends over time and document how the plant was operated and tested|Records serve mainly to show the plant's annual payroll and staffing costs|Records let the lab skip a routine test whenever the previous month's result was acceptable|Records set the number of samples the lab can analyze in a given week|Trend data are useful only when records are kept, and records document the work done.|e
Why might an operator run a duplicate analysis on a sample?|It checks the precision of the test, so a bad result can be spotted before it is reported|It doubles the number of results that are reported to the state agency each reporting period|It makes the sample safe to pour down the drain after the testing is complete in the lab|It replaces the need for a standard solution in the test procedure used by the lab|Duplicate analyses show how closely repeated measurements agree, which helps identify faulty results.|m
@Laboratory and administration|PADEPM13|Plant administration
What is a key responsibility of plant administration?|Keeping records, budgets, staffing and reports that support safe and reliable operation|Setting the chemical feed rate for each pump every hour during the shift by hand|Repairing motors in the field every day for the crew that is assigned to the work|Designing new tanks and piping for a plant expansion project that is being planned|Administration supports operations through records, budgets, staffing and reporting.|e
Why is a maintenance budget important for a treatment plant?|It lets the plant plan for repairs, replacements and spare parts, so equipment is not run to failure|It limits the number of replacement parts that the plant can order in a year, to keep stock low|It sets the maximum number of overtime hours each operator may work in a week under the plan|It determines the minimum number of samples the lab must run each month for the process data|Planned budgets allow timely replacement and repair, which reduces the risk of failures.|m
Why is record keeping of plant operations important?|Records document what was done and what the results were, so the plant can show how it operated|Records replace the daily flow log, so the plant can stop reading the flow meters on each shift|Records are passed to the town council each quarter so that rates can be set for each customer class|Records show the state inspectors the staff payroll for each shift|Operating records provide evidence of performance and support decisions about the process.|m
Why do plants train their staff on procedures?|Trained staff apply procedures consistently, which reduces errors and improves reliability|Training reduces the number of operators the plant must have on staff at each shift|Training lets a shift skip the written logbook when the workload is heavy|Training makes the plant's written procedures unnecessary for experienced staff|Consistent procedures and training reduce human error in daily operations.|m
@General chemistry for wastewater|PADEPM29|General chemistry for wastewater
What does a pH value of 7 indicate at 25 degrees Celsius?|The solution is neutral, with equal concentrations of hydrogen and hydroxide ions|The solution is strongly acidic and will corrode metal pipes and tanks in contact with it|The solution is strongly basic and will react with the grease and solids in the flow|The solution is saturated with dissolved oxygen so that aerobic organisms are not limited|At 25 degrees Celsius, pure water has pH 7, which is neutral. Values below 7 are acidic and values above 7 are basic.|e
In chemistry, what does oxidation involve?|The loss of electrons by a substance during a chemical reaction|The gain of electrons by a substance during a chemical reaction in the solution|The addition of water molecules to a compound to form a hydrate in solution|The dissolving of a solid into a liquid so that the solid no longer appears in it|Oxidation is loss of electrons. Reduction is the gain of electrons, and the two occur together in a reaction.|e
In the Bronsted sense, what is an acid?|A substance that donates a proton to another substance during a reaction|A substance that accepts a proton from water and forms a base during the reaction|A substance that releases electrons into the solution when it dissolves in the water|A solid that does not dissolve in water and so stays in the bottom of the container|An acid is a proton donor. A base accepts protons.|m
What does the unit mg/L describe?|Mass of a substance per volume of water, about the same as ppm when dilute|A percent by weight of solids in the sample, since 1 mg/L is equal to 1 percent|The parts per billion of the substance, since mg/L and ppb are interchangeable|The chlorine that one liter of water can absorb at the standard temperature|Milligrams per liter is a mass-per-volume concentration. For dilute water samples it is numerically close to ppm.|m
Why does warmer water hold less dissolved oxygen?|Gases are less soluble in warm water, so less oxygen dissolves when the temperature rises|Warm water has more oxygen atoms in each molecule of water, so the molecules become heavier|Warm water makes oxygen lighter, so the gas rises out of the water and escapes to the air|Oxygen forms bubbles in the water only when the water is at low temperature and high pressure|Solubility of gases falls as temperature rises at a given gas pressure. This is why warm water holds less dissolved oxygen.|m
