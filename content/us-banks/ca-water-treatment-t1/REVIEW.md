### 1: Flow measurement and water level devices (easy)

Which flow device uses a narrowed throat to create a pressure difference that indicates flow?

- **A.** Magnetic flow meter
- **B.** Propeller meter
- **C.** Venturi meter
- **D.** Weir plate

**Answer: C. Venturi meter**

A venturi meter squeezes the flow through a throat and measures the pressure difference between the pipe and the throat. That difference is related to flow.

### 2: Flow measurement and water level devices (medium)

What does a flow totalizer display?

- **A.** The water speed at the center of the pipe
- **B.** The pressure loss across the meter
- **C.** The flow rate at the instant of reading
- **D.** The running volume that has passed the meter

**Answer: D. The running volume that has passed the meter**

A totalizer adds up the volume over time, like an odometer. A rate indicator shows the flow at a single moment.

### 3: Flow measurement and water level devices (medium)

In open-channel flow measurement, what does a weir use to indicate how much water is passing?

- **A.** The height of water above the weir crest
- **B.** The pressure inside a closed pipe
- **C.** The voltage induced as water moves through a field
- **D.** The speed of a rotor turned by the water

**Answer: A. The height of water above the weir crest**

Water flowing over a weir rises in proportion to the flow, so a measured head above the crest can be converted to a flow rate.

### 4: Flow measurement and water level devices (medium)

A pressure sensor near the bottom of a tank reports the water level. Pressure at that point depends on what?

- **A.** The temperature of the stored water
- **B.** The depth of water above the sensor
- **C.** The width of the tank floor
- **D.** The rate of flow entering the tank

**Answer: B. The depth of water above the sensor**

Pressure from water rises with the height of the water column above the sensor, about 0.433 psi for each foot, so pressure can be read as level.

### 5: Flow measurement and water level devices (hard)

Why does a venturi meter cause less lasting pressure loss than a sharp orifice plate?

- **A.** Its throat holds water at a higher speed past the meter
- **B.** Its pressure taps sit farther apart so the drop is spread out
- **C.** It has moving parts that give way to the flow
- **D.** Its smooth outlet cone lets most of the pressure recover

**Answer: D. Its smooth outlet cone lets most of the pressure recover**

The gradual widening after the throat slows the water smoothly and returns much of the pressure. A sharp plate wastes more energy in turbulence.

### 6: Well components and depth measurement (easy)

What is the main purpose of a well casing?

- **A.** To record the water level in the well at all times
- **B.** To support the borehole wall and keep surface water out
- **C.** To meter the volume pumped from the well each day
- **D.** To lift water from the aquifer up to the surface for use

**Answer: B. To support the borehole wall and keep surface water out**

Casing is the pipe lining the borehole. It keeps the hole from caving in and blocks poor-quality water from entering above the screen.

### 7: Well components and depth measurement (easy)

What is the job of a well screen?

- **A.** It lets groundwater flow in while holding sand back
- **B.** It seals the gap between the casing and borehole wall
- **C.** It raises water from the aquifer to the surface at pressure
- **D.** It keeps the pump motor cool during long pumping runs

**Answer: A. It lets groundwater flow in while holding sand back**

The screen is the slotted section placed in the water-bearing zone. Openings are sized to pass water but hold back formation material.

### 8: Well components and depth measurement (medium)

Why is cement grout placed in the space around the upper part of a well casing?

- **A.** To stop the casing from rusting on its inside surface
- **B.** To raise the amount of water the aquifer gives up to the well
- **C.** To block surface water from traveling down outside the casing
- **D.** To hold the screen at the correct depth in the borehole

**Answer: C. To block surface water from traveling down outside the casing**

A sealed annular space prevents runoff and shallow water from following the outside of the casing down to the aquifer.

### 9: Well components and depth measurement (medium)

An electric sounder lowered into a well gives its signal at what moment?

- **A.** The probe senses the motor running
- **B.** The probe tip reaches the bottom of the screen
- **C.** The cable has passed the pump intake
- **D.** The probe tip contacts the water surface

**Answer: D. The probe tip contacts the water surface**

The probe closes a small circuit when it touches water. The marked cable is then read at the measuring point to give depth to water.

### 10: Well components and depth measurement (medium)

Why should depth-to-water readings be taken from the same marked measuring point each time?

- **A.** It removes the need to record the date of each reading
- **B.** Changes in level then reflect the well, not a shifting start
- **C.** It cancels out any stretch in the measuring cable
- **D.** It makes the reading independent of whether the pump is running

**Answer: B. Changes in level then reflect the well, not a shifting start**

A fixed reference point lets readings from different dates be compared. A moving starting point would look like a change in water level.

### 11: Drawdown, specific capacity and abnormal well operation (easy)

What is the static water level in a well?

- **A.** The level after the pump has been off long enough to recover
- **B.** The level at which the pump intake is set in the well
- **C.** The average of the levels recorded over a full year
- **D.** The level while the pump is running at its normal rate

**Answer: A. The level after the pump has been off long enough to recover**

Static level is the undisturbed level with no pumping effect. Drawdown is measured from that level to the pumping level.

### 12: Drawdown, specific capacity and abnormal well operation (medium)

What is meant by well drawdown?

- **A.** The time the level takes to rise again after pumping stops
- **B.** The total volume of water pumped from the well in one day
- **C.** How far the water level falls because the pump is running
- **D.** The distance from ground level down to the pump intake

**Answer: C. How far the water level falls because the pump is running**

Drawdown equals the pumping level minus the static level. A larger drawdown at the same rate means the well is working harder.

### 13: Drawdown, specific capacity and abnormal well operation (medium)

How is specific capacity of a well expressed?

- **A.** Pounds of pressure for each foot of well depth drilled
- **B.** Gallons stored for each foot of casing length in the well
- **C.** Gallons per minute of yield for each foot of drawdown
- **D.** Feet of drawdown for each gallon per minute pumped out

**Answer: C. Gallons per minute of yield for each foot of drawdown**

Specific capacity is yield divided by drawdown, in gpm per ft. A higher number shows a more productive well.

### 14: Drawdown, specific capacity and abnormal well operation (medium)

At the same pumping rate, a well's specific capacity slowly falls over several months. What does this suggest?

- **A.** The well is becoming more productive with each month
- **B.** The measuring point has moved down the casing by mistake
- **C.** Aquifer recharge has begun to run faster than in the past
- **D.** The screen may be plugging or aquifer conditions are declining

**Answer: D. The screen may be plugging or aquifer conditions are declining**

Lower yield per foot of drawdown points to lost well efficiency, such as screen plugging, or to a weakening water supply, and it calls for investigation.

### 15: Drawdown, specific capacity and abnormal well operation (hard)

Sand begins to appear in water from a well that ran clear for years. Which cause is most likely?

- **A.** A failing screen or filter pack that passes formation material
- **B.** Pumping at a rate slightly below the usual setting
- **C.** A small rise in the aquifer static level over the season
- **D.** A pump set a little deeper in the casing than before

**Answer: A. A failing screen or filter pack that passes formation material**

Sand entering a long-clear well suggests the screen or gravel pack has deteriorated so that formation sand gets through with the water.

### 16: Aquifers, the hydrologic cycle and surface water influence (easy)

Which process returns water vapor to the air from plant leaves?

- **A.** Infiltration
- **B.** Transpiration
- **C.** Percolation
- **D.** Runoff

**Answer: B. Transpiration**

Plants draw up soil water and release vapor from their leaves. Infiltration and percolation move water downward, and runoff moves it over the surface.

### 17: Aquifers, the hydrologic cycle and surface water influence (medium)

What describes an unconfined aquifer?

- **A.** A layer that feeds a spring and is not tapped by wells
- **B.** A zone of saturated rock too tight to yield useful water
- **C.** A water-bearing layer whose top is the water table
- **D.** A layer held under pressure between layers of clay

**Answer: C. A water-bearing layer whose top is the water table**

The upper surface of an unconfined aquifer is the water table, and it is recharged from rain and surface water seeping down from above.

### 18: Aquifers, the hydrologic cycle and surface water influence (easy)

Which aquifer material generally gives water to a well most readily?

- **A.** Shale
- **B.** Coarse sand and gravel
- **C.** Clay
- **D.** Dense unfractured granite

**Answer: B. Coarse sand and gravel**

Coarse, clean sand and gravel have large connected pores and pass water easily. Clay holds water but releases it very slowly.

### 19: Aquifers, the hydrologic cycle and surface water influence (hard)

A shallow well near a river shows cloudier water and a temperature change soon after the river rises. What does this suggest?

- **A.** River water is moving into the aquifer toward the well
- **B.** Seasonal air temperature is warming the casing
- **C.** The well has begun drawing from a deeper confined layer
- **D.** The pump is cycling more often because demand is low

**Answer: A. River water is moving into the aquifer toward the well**

Fast changes that follow the river show a link between the stream and the well, so surface contaminants may reach the well water.

### 20: Chemical makeup of groundwater and surface water (easy)

Compared with surface water, groundwater typically carries what?

- **A.** More algae and floating debris
- **B.** Higher levels of suspended silt
- **C.** Wider daily swings in temperature
- **D.** Higher levels of dissolved minerals

**Answer: D. Higher levels of dissolved minerals**

Groundwater spends a long time in contact with rock and soil, so it picks up dissolved minerals. Surface water usually carries more particles and organisms.

### 21: Chemical makeup of groundwater and surface water (medium)

Which pair of dissolved ions is mainly responsible for water hardness?

- **A.** Chloride and sulfate
- **B.** Calcium and magnesium
- **C.** Nitrate and phosphate
- **D.** Sodium and potassium

**Answer: B. Calcium and magnesium**

Hardness is caused mainly by dissolved calcium and magnesium picked up from rock such as limestone and dolomite.

### 22: Chemical makeup of groundwater and surface water (medium)

Which underground condition often leads to dissolved iron and manganese in well water?

- **A.** Oxygen-poor water in contact with the rock
- **B.** A heavy load of suspended silt from storm runoff
- **C.** A high level of free chlorine in the supply
- **D.** Oxygen-rich water in rapid movement through the rock

**Answer: A. Oxygen-poor water in contact with the rock**

With little dissolved oxygen, iron and manganese in soil and rock dissolve. They turn to solid particles and color the water after they meet air.

### 23: Chemical makeup of groundwater and surface water (medium)

After a heavy storm, which change is most expected in a river source?

- **A.** A fall in suspended solids from dilution
- **B.** A steady rise in dissolved mineral content
- **C.** A rise in hardness from deeper rock contact
- **D.** A rise in turbidity and organic matter

**Answer: D. A rise in turbidity and organic matter**

Storm runoff washes soil, debris and organic matter into rivers, so cloudiness and organic content climb while mineral content often falls.

### 24: Alkalinity, hardness and hard water problems (easy)

What does the alkalinity of water measure?

- **A.** Its tendency to carry floating particles
- **B.** Its content of dissolved oxygen at the surface
- **C.** Its ability to resist a drop in pH when acid is added
- **D.** Its ability to kill microbes at a given dose

**Answer: C. Its ability to resist a drop in pH when acid is added**

Alkalinity is the buffering capacity, mostly from bicarbonate, carbonate and hydroxide. It slows pH change when acids enter the water.

### 25: Alkalinity, hardness and hard water problems (medium)

Which chemical is commonly added to raise alkalinity?

- **A.** Carbon dioxide gas
- **B.** Sulfuric acid
- **C.** Soda ash
- **D.** Chlorine gas

**Answer: C. Soda ash**

Soda ash is sodium carbonate and adds carbonate alkalinity. Chlorine gas, carbon dioxide and sulfuric acid all lower pH and use up alkalinity.

### 26: Alkalinity, hardness and hard water problems (medium)

What do lime and limestone add to water as they dissolve?

- **A.** Fluoride and sodium
- **B.** Acidity and turbidity
- **C.** Chlorine demand and color
- **D.** Alkalinity and calcium hardness

**Answer: D. Alkalinity and calcium hardness**

Both contain calcium compounds that dissolve to supply calcium, which adds hardness, and carbonate or hydroxide, which adds alkalinity.

### 27: Alkalinity, hardness and hard water problems (easy)

Which problem is caused by hard water in a water heater?

- **A.** Rapid pitting of the copper supply pipes
- **B.** Mineral scale that coats the heating surfaces
- **C.** Brown stains on the tank exterior
- **D.** Excess soap lather that is hard to rinse away

**Answer: B. Mineral scale that coats the heating surfaces**

Heating drives calcium and magnesium out of solution as scale, which insulates the heating surfaces and wastes energy.

### 28: Alkalinity, hardness and hard water problems (hard)

Which statement about alkalinity and hardness is correct?

- **A.** Water rich in sodium bicarbonate can have high alkalinity and low hardness
- **B.** Hardness and alkalinity come from the same laboratory test, so they match
- **C.** High alkalinity is a sure sign of high hardness in the water
- **D.** Alkalinity is a measure of how bitter the water tastes to a drinker

**Answer: A. Water rich in sodium bicarbonate can have high alkalinity and low hardness**

The two properties are separate. Hardness comes from calcium and magnesium, while alkalinity comes from carbonate species that sodium can also supply.

### 29: Raw water contaminants and their sources (easy)

Which of these is a microbial contaminant of raw water?

- **A.** Arsenic from certain rocks
- **B.** Lead from old service pipes
- **C.** Giardia cysts
- **D.** Nitrate dissolved from fertilizer

**Answer: C. Giardia cysts**

Giardia is a protozoan parasite. Nitrate, arsenic and lead are chemical contaminants rather than living organisms.

### 30: Raw water contaminants and their sources (medium)

Which is a common source of nitrate in groundwater?

- **A.** Dissolved oxygen carried in rainfall to the soil
- **B.** Limestone dissolving in the aquifer rock
- **C.** Corrosion of pipes inside the treatment plant
- **D.** Fertilizer, manure and septic system leachate

**Answer: D. Fertilizer, manure and septic system leachate**

Nitrogen from fertilizer and wastes moves down through soil to the aquifer, so farms and septic systems near wells are common sources.

### 31: Raw water contaminants and their sources (medium)

How does arsenic commonly get into groundwater?

- **A.** By dissolving from certain rock and sediment
- **B.** By chlorine reacting with organic matter in the well
- **C.** By effluent from a nearby septic tank or field
- **D.** By runoff of road salt into shallow wells nearby

**Answer: A. By dissolving from certain rock and sediment**

In some regions arsenic is naturally present in rock and sediment and dissolves into the water, so it can occur without any human source.

### 32: Raw water contaminants and their sources (hard)

Why is Cryptosporidium of special concern in surface water sources?

- **A.** It dissolves in raw water and slips through treatment steps
- **B.** Its oocysts resist chlorine and enter with animal waste
- **C.** It forms harmful by-products when it meets chlorine
- **D.** It grows rapidly once water is stored in warm tanks

**Answer: B. Its oocysts resist chlorine and enter with animal waste**

Cryptosporidium oocysts are tough and are carried into streams from animal and human waste, so a chlorine dose alone does not reliably inactivate them.

### 33: Abnormal water conditions and reservoir warning signs (easy)

Which sight on a reservoir is a visual sign of possible contamination?

- **A.** Light ripples across the surface on a breezy day
- **B.** A green mat of algae spreading across the surface
- **C.** Clear water over the intake structure
- **D.** A gradual rise in level after snowmelt

**Answer: B. A green mat of algae spreading across the surface**

Spreading algal growth and scum point to nutrient loading and can bring toxins, taste and odor. Ripples, snowmelt and clear water are normal.

### 34: Abnormal water conditions and reservoir warning signs (easy)

A rotten-egg odor from raw well water points to which compound?

- **A.** Fluoride
- **B.** Calcium carbonate
- **C.** Hydrogen sulfide
- **D.** Free chlorine

**Answer: C. Hydrogen sulfide**

Hydrogen sulfide gas dissolved in groundwater gives a distinct rotten-egg smell, often from low-oxygen conditions or sulfur bacteria.

### 35: Abnormal water conditions and reservoir warning signs (medium)

A well's water turns reddish brown soon after the pump starts. What is the most likely cause?

- **A.** Dissolved iron turning to rust particles in air
- **B.** Foam from entrained air in the discharge column
- **C.** Heavy chlorine overfeed at the wellhead pump
- **D.** Algae growing on the inside of the casing

**Answer: A. Dissolved iron turning to rust particles in air**

Dissolved iron is clear in the ground but oxidizes to reddish solids after reaching oxygen, which gives a rust color.

### 36: Abnormal water conditions and reservoir warning signs (medium)

Which reading is a sign of abnormal well operation?

- **A.** Discharge pressure steady at its usual value for the well
- **B.** Pumping level and flow that match last month's logs
- **C.** Motor amperage close to its recorded average for the pump

A deeper pumping level at an unchanged rate means more drawdown, which can show screen plugging or a falling water supply.

### 37: Abnormal water conditions and reservoir warning signs (hard)

Why can an algal bloom in a source reservoir be a problem beyond its looks?

- **A.** Some blooms release toxins and taste-and-odor compounds
- **B.** It improves clarity by settling solids to the bottom
- **C.** It lowers the hardness of the water in the lake
- **D.** It consumes the microbes that cause disease in people

**Answer: A. Some blooms release toxins and taste-and-odor compounds**

Certain algae produce toxins, and many produce compounds with earthy or musty tastes. Blooms also raise treatment difficulty.

### 38: Security risks and storage disinfection (medium)

Damaged locks, gates and fences let unauthorized people reach equipment, so they should be reported and repaired quickly.

### 39: Security risks and storage disinfection (medium)

Which observation at a storage tank suggests possible tampering?

- **C.** Fresh cuts in the fence and pry marks on the hatch

**Answer: C. Fresh cuts in the fence and pry marks on the hatch**

Cut fences and pry marks on hatches show forced entry. Those signs should be reported and the site checked for contamination.

### 40: Security risks and storage disinfection (medium)

Why is a storage tank disinfected after cleaning or repair before it returns to service?

- **A.** Disinfection removes scale from the interior walls
- **B.** Disinfection tests the tank for leaks
- **C.** Disinfection lowers the dissolved iron in the water
- **D.** Workers and tools can leave bacteria behind

**Answer: D. Workers and tools can leave bacteria behind**

People, equipment and debris bring organisms into a tank. A chlorine contact step treats the surfaces before the water is delivered.

### 41: Security risks and storage disinfection (hard)

After a well is disinfected and flushed, why is a bacteriological sample collected?

- **A.** The sample finds the drawdown at the new pumping rate
- **B.** The result shows how much chlorine remains in the casing
- **C.** Lab results confirm the water is free of coliform before service
- **D.** The test sets the chlorine dose for the next disinfection cycle

**Answer: C. Lab results confirm the water is free of coliform before service**

Disinfection is not accepted on trust. A passing bacteriological sample shows that the treatment worked before water goes into the system.

### 42: What causes turbidity (easy)

Turbidity is a measure of which property of water?

- **A.** The ability of the water to resist pH change
- **B.** The concentration of dissolved minerals
- **C.** The oxygen demand of organic matter
- **D.** Cloudiness from suspended particles

**Answer: D. Cloudiness from suspended particles**

Turbidity describes how cloudy water looks because particles scatter light. It does not measure dissolved minerals, buffering or oxygen demand.

### 43: What causes turbidity (medium)

After a heavy rain, a river source turns muddy and its turbidity jumps. What is the most likely cause?

- **A.** Runoff carrying silt and clay
- **B.** A drop in the dissolved oxygen level of the stream
- **C.** Hardness released from the rock minerals nearby
- **D.** Slower microbial growth in the colder water

**Answer: A. Runoff carrying silt and clay**

Storm runoff washes soil particles into surface water and raises turbidity quickly. Dissolved oxygen and hardness changes do not make water cloudy.

### 44: What causes turbidity (medium)

Besides clay and silt, which of these can add to turbidity?

- **A.** Dissolved sodium chloride from the source
- **B.** Algae and other microscopic organisms
- **C.** Dissolved carbon dioxide gas in the water
- **D.** Dissolved calcium bicarbonate from limestone

**Answer: B. Algae and other microscopic organisms**

Living and dead microorganisms are suspended particles that scatter light. Dissolved salts and gases stay in solution and do not cloud the water.

### 45: What causes turbidity (medium)

Why does high turbidity matter for disinfection, apart from how the water looks?

- **A.** Particles can shield microorganisms from it
- **B.** Particles raise the pH enough to weaken the disinfectant
- **C.** Particles make microorganisms grow faster during contact
- **D.** Particles convert free chlorine into chloramines in the water

**Answer: A. Particles can shield microorganisms from it**

Microorganisms can hide in or on particles, where disinfectant reaches them poorly. That is why particles are removed before disinfection.

### 46: Interpreting turbidity information (easy)

In what unit is turbidity commonly reported?

- **A.** Pounds per million gallons
- **B.** Nephelometric turbidity units (NTU)
- **C.** Milligrams per liter of solids
- **D.** Standard color units

**Answer: B. Nephelometric turbidity units (NTU)**

Turbidimeters report scattered light in NTU. Color units and mg/L describe other water properties.

### 47: Interpreting turbidity information (medium)

How does a nephelometric turbidimeter measure turbidity?

- **A.** The weight of the residue left after a sample is dried
- **B.** The time particles take to settle in a measuring tube
- **C.** By sensing light scattered by the particles
- **D.** The amount of light that passes straight through the sample

**Answer: C. By sensing light scattered by the particles**

The instrument shines a light and detects the part scattered sideways. More particles scatter more light and give a higher reading.

### 48: Interpreting turbidity information (medium)

Filter effluent turbidity creeps upward over several hours while the flow is steady. What does this trend most likely signal?

- **A.** The raw water has become softer, so less is needed
- **B.** The turbidimeter has drifted and the readings can be ignored
- **C.** The disinfectant demand has dropped in the meantime
- **D.** The filter may be losing its ability to hold back particles

**Answer: D. The filter may be losing its ability to hold back particles**

A steady rise at constant flow points to declining filter performance, so the trend should be investigated. Assuming instrument drift without checking could miss a real problem.

### 49: Interpreting turbidity information (hard)

Why is monitoring each filter separately more informative than watching only the blended effluent?

- **A.** One poorly performing filter can be hidden by better ones in the blend
- **B.** Blended water is warmer, which distorts the reading from the meter
- **C.** Individual meters read low values more precisely than a combined meter does
- **D.** Individual readings are needed to set the chlorine dose at the plant

**Answer: A. One poorly performing filter can be hidden by better ones in the blend**

Mixing dilutes a bad filter's output with clean water from the others. Separate readings show which filter is failing.

### 50: Chlorine compounds and forms (easy)

In a gas chlorine cylinder, in what form is the chlorine stored?

- **A.** A granular solid that dissolves in the feed water
- **B.** A tablet compound that erodes slowly in a feeder
- **C.** A liquid held under pressure in the cylinder
- **D.** A weak solution that is ready to be pumped directly

**Answer: C. A liquid held under pressure in the cylinder**

Chlorine gas is compressed into a liquid in the cylinder and vaporizes as gas is withdrawn. Dry and liquid hypochlorites are separate products.

### 51: Chlorine compounds and forms (medium)

Which common chlorine product is a dry solid sold as granules or tablets with a high available chlorine content?

- **A.** Sodium hypochlorite
- **B.** Chloramine
- **C.** Chlorine dioxide
- **D.** Calcium hypochlorite

**Answer: D. Calcium hypochlorite**

Calcium hypochlorite is the dry form of chlorine, usually around two-thirds available chlorine. Sodium hypochlorite is a liquid.

### 52: Chlorine compounds and forms (medium)

Why does a stored sodium hypochlorite solution lose strength with time?

- **A.** Its sodium settles out of the liquid as a sludge
- **B.** It slowly breaks down, faster in heat and sunlight
- **C.** It picks up moisture from the air and becomes dilute
- **D.** It soaks into the walls of the storage tank

**Answer: B. It slowly breaks down, faster in heat and sunlight**

Hypochlorite solution decomposes gradually, and warm temperature and light speed the loss. Fresh deliveries and cool, shaded storage help keep strength.

### 53: Chlorine compounds and forms (hard)

How does adding sodium hypochlorite solution typically change pH, compared with adding chlorine gas?

- **A.** It leaves pH unchanged, while the gas raises it
- **B.** It pushes pH up, while the gas pushes pH down
- **C.** It lowers pH to the same degree as the gas
- **D.** It pushes pH down, even more than the gas does

**Answer: B. It pushes pH up, while the gas pushes pH down**

The hypochlorite solution is alkaline, while chlorine gas forms acids in water. That difference matters when pH control is a concern.

### 54: Chlorine chemistry and disinfectant properties (easy)

When chlorine is added to water, which form is the strongest disinfectant?

- **A.** Monochloramine formed with ammonia
- **B.** The chloride ion left after the reaction
- **C.** Hypochlorous acid (HOCl)
- **D.** The hypochlorite ion in solution

**Answer: C. Hypochlorous acid (HOCl)**

Hypochlorous acid is the most effective form of free chlorine. The hypochlorite ion is weaker and chloride has no disinfecting power.

### 55: Chlorine chemistry and disinfectant properties (medium)

What happens to free chlorine disinfection as pH rises?

- **A.** Disinfection slows as more shifts to the weaker hypochlorite ion
- **B.** Free chlorine converts to chloramine on its own as pH climbs
- **C.** The residual rises because less chlorine is consumed by demand
- **D.** More shifts to the stronger acid form, so disinfection speeds up

**Answer: A. Disinfection slows as more shifts to the weaker hypochlorite ion**

Higher pH favors the hypochlorite ion over hypochlorous acid, which makes chlorine a slower disinfectant at the same residual.

### 56: Chlorine chemistry and disinfectant properties (medium)

Which description fits free chlorine in a water sample?

- **A.** Chlorine still sitting in the cylinder awaiting feed
- **B.** Chlorine attached to ammonia or to organic nitrogen
- **C.** Chlorine that has reacted with the water and been used up
- **D.** Chlorine as HOCl and hypochlorite ion

**Answer: D. Chlorine as HOCl and hypochlorite ion**

Free chlorine is the unreacted, active portion. Chlorine bound to ammonia is combined chlorine, and used-up chlorine is part of the demand.

### 57: Chlorine chemistry and disinfectant properties (hard)

Why does a chloramine residual last longer in a distribution system but disinfect more slowly than free chlorine?

- **A.** It holds more oxidizing power for each unit that is fed
- **B.** It dissolves less in water and settles in the pipes
- **C.** It reacts less readily, so it persists but acts slowly
- **D.** It reacts more readily, so it acts quickly and then fades

**Answer: C. It reacts less readily, so it persists but acts slowly**

Chloramines are weaker oxidizers. They survive farther through the system but need more time to inactivate microorganisms.

### 58: Breakpoint chlorination (easy)

What is the goal of breakpoint chlorination?

- **A.** To raise the chlorine dose until turbidity drops below a target
- **B.** To stop feeding once a combined residual first appears
- **C.** To hold a combined residual at the lowest detectable level
- **D.** To destroy chloramines and ammonia so free chlorine builds

**Answer: D. To destroy chloramines and ammonia so free chlorine builds**

At the breakpoint, the added chlorine has oxidized the ammonia and chloramines, and further chlorine stays as free residual.

### 59: Breakpoint chlorination (medium)

On a breakpoint curve, why does the measured residual dip after its first rise?

- **A.** Chlorine oxidizes chloramines
