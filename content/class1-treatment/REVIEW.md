# Class 1 Treatment — 500 original practice candidates

These supplement the existing banks. They are not official exam questions or an official weighted exam. Correct answers are shown for review. No production import is performed.

## Water Treatment

### 2001 — Source assessment
A reservoir intake has ports at several depths. What evidence best supports selecting a port after summer stratification develops?

A. The shallowest port's distance from shore
B. The previous winter's average raw-water pH
C. The deepest port's pipe diameter alone
D. Temperature and quality profiles at each depth

Answer: D. Temperature and quality profiles at each depth
Depth profiles reveal changes such as low oxygen, algae or dissolved metals; depth alone does not identify the best water.

### 2002 — Source assessment
A raw-water turbidity spike follows heavy rain. What operational comparison is most useful?

A. Average annual raw turbidity versus the current dose
B. Current jar-test results versus current plant settings
C. Current filter wash volume versus the stock-tank level
D. Yesterday’s jar doses versus the current chemical delivery

Answer: B. Current jar-test results versus current plant settings
Storm runoff can change treatability rapidly. Current tests support adjustments to coagulation rather than relying on stale settings.

### 2003 — Source assessment

B. Filtration has already removed its dissolved carbon
C. The water contains no disinfection by-product precursors
D. The water has a low disinfectant demand by definition

Answer: A. Clear appearance does not exclude dissolved contaminants
Dissolved substances may not scatter much light. Visual clarity cannot establish chemical quality or treatment needs.
