Q1 [easy] (Source Water: Raw water quality and seasonal change)
Which raw water source usually shows the biggest jump in turbidity after a heavy rainstorm?
A. Water from a deep confined aquifer protected by clay layers
B. Water held in a covered finished-water tank
C. Water from a well with a long steel casing
D. A river or stream draining open farmland
Key: D
Explanation: Surface runoff carries soil and debris into rivers quickly, while deep groundwater is filtered by the ground and changes slowly.

Q2 [easy] (Source Water: Raw water quality and seasonal change)
What does turbidity tell an operator about a raw water sample?
A. How well it resists a change in pH when acid is added
B. How much dissolved gas it carries at the sample temperature
C. How cloudy it is from suspended particles
D. How much dissolved calcium and magnesium it holds in solution
Key: C
Explanation: Turbidity is a measure of light scattered by suspended particles such as clay, silt and organisms. Dissolved minerals and buffering are different properties.

Q3 [easy] (Source Water: Raw water quality and seasonal change)
In water treatment, what does alkalinity describe?
A. Resistance to a drop in pH when acid is added to it
B. The amount of suspended clay and silt carried by the water
C. The total of dissolved salts that leave scale on heaters
D. The quantity of oxygen available to aquatic life in the source
Key: A
Explanation: Alkalinity is the buffering capacity of the water, mostly from bicarbonate, carbonate and hydroxide. It matters because coagulants consume it.

Q4 [medium] (Source Water: Raw water quality and seasonal change)
Which pair of dissolved minerals is mainly responsible for the hardness of a water supply?
A. Chloride and sulfate from road salt and rock
B. Calcium and magnesium dissolved from rock
C. Sodium and potassium found in many brines
D. Iron and manganese from deep wells
Key: B
Explanation: Hardness is the sum of the calcium and magnesium concentrations, usually expressed as mg/L as calcium carbonate.

Q5 [medium] (Source Water: Raw water quality and seasonal change)
A cold winter raw water supply is treated with the same coagulant dose as in summer. What is the usual effect of the low temperature on settling?
A. Particles settle more slowly because the water is more viscous
B. Particles settle at the same speed because their own weight sets the rate
C. Particles settle faster because the water is denser than in summer
D. Floc forms faster so the basins need less time to do the job
Key: A
Explanation: Cold water is more viscous, so it resists the fall of particles and slows both floc formation and settling. Plants often adjust dose or mixing in winter.

Q6 [medium] (Source Water: Raw water quality and seasonal change)
A river intake receives a large rainfall runoff. Which change in the raw water is most likely?

C. Turbidity falls and hardness climbs because of dilution of solids
D. Turbidity climbs and alkalinity often drops from dilution

Explanation: Runoff brings in solids and dilutes the mineral content that gives alkalinity and hardness, so coagulant demand can change quickly.

Q7 [hard] (Source Water: Raw water quality and seasonal change)

Q8 [easy] (Aeration: Gas transfer and oxidation)

Explanation: Hydrogen sulfide is a dissolved gas that escapes to the air during aeration, which reduces the odor. Oxygen and nitrogen are gases that normally dissolve into the water.

Q11 [medium] (Aeration: Gas transfer and oxidation)

Q18 [medium] (Coagulation and Flocculation: Coagulants, mixing, jar testing and chemistry)

Q20 [medium] (Coagulation and Flocculation: Coagulants, mixing, jar testing and chemistry)

Q21 [hard] (Coagulation and Flocculation: Coagulants, mixing, jar testing and chemistry)

Q30 [easy] (Filtration: Rapid sand, backwash, slow sand and membranes)

Key: C
Explanation: Solids lodged between the grains raise head loss. Reversing the flow lifts and scrubs them away so the filter can run again.

Q31 [medium] (Filtration: Rapid sand, backwash, slow sand and membranes)

Explanation: As solids collect in the pore spaces, water meets more resistance and more pressure is lost. A rising head loss is a signal that a wash is coming due.

Q32 [medium] (Filtration: Rapid sand, backwash, slow sand and membranes)

Q34 [medium] (Filtration: Rapid sand, backwash, slow sand and membranes)

Explanation: Water age rises where water sits in low-demand dead ends and tanks. Moving fresh water through often helps more than a higher plant dose, which does not move any water.

Q62 [easy] (Equipment Evaluation and Operation: Pump and equipment performance)
What is the main job of a pump in a water treatment plant?

Key: D
Explanation: A pump adds head so water can be moved through piping and process units. Mixing, gas removal and flow measurement are done by other equipment.

Q63 [medium] (Equipment Evaluation and Operation: Pump and equipment performance)

B. The impeller speeds up to hold flow at any suction lift

D. The suction valve closes on its own and stops the motor
Key: A
Explanation: When pressure at the pump inlet drops below vapor pressure, bubbles form and collapse on the impeller. That causes noise, vibration, pitting and loss of head and flow.

Key: C
Explanation: A drive changes motor speed so the pump follows changing demand and uses less energy than throttling a valve. The other options describe full speed or voltage changes that do not match that purpose.

Q65 [hard] (Equipment Evaluation and Operation: Pump and equipment performance)

C. Assume the flow meter is wrong and wait for the next reading to confirm

Key: B
Explanation: A fall in flow at a fixed speed and pressure usually points to a suction restriction or impeller wear. Comparing with the curve identifies the likely cause before any part is replaced or the speed is changed.

Q66 [medium] (Equipment Evaluation and Operation: Pump and equipment performance)

Q68 [medium] (Facility Operation and Maintenance: Preventive maintenance and chemical feed reliability)

Explanation: Feed shortfall often comes from air or a blockage on the suction side, leaking valves or a pump out of calibration. Changing the chemical strength or the set point does not fix a mechanical cause.

Q70 [hard] (Facility Operation and Maintenance: Preventive maintenance and chemical feed reliability)

Q72 [easy] (Iron and Manganese Control: Oxidation, filtration and sequestering of metals)

Q74 [medium] (Iron and Manganese Control: Oxidation, filtration and sequestering of metals)

Q75 [medium] (Iron and Manganese Control: Oxidation, filtration and sequestering of metals)
What is a sequestering agent used for in iron control?

B. To turn dissolved iron into solid particles that the filters can remove
C. To raise alkalinity and buffer the pH of the water
D. To hold dissolved iron in solution so it does not stain fixtures
Key: D
Explanation: A sequestrant binds iron so it stays dissolved and does not precipitate. It is the opposite of oxidation and filtration, which remove iron as a solid.
