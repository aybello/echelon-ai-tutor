## Ontario regulatory sources

| Source | Verified finding used for repair |
|---|---|
| [O. Reg. 128/04](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040128) | This is the drinking-water operator and water-quality analyst regulation. Section 9 governs operator-in-training certificates; Schedule 2, s. 1 requires Grade 12 (or Director-accepted equivalent) and a satisfactory mark in a Director-approved OIT examination for a municipal residential subsystem OIT certificate. |
| [O. Reg. 129/04](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040129) | This is the Licensing of Sewage Works Operators regulation. Section 6 establishes OIT licences for each wastewater facility type, so a question that presents water and wastewater OIT credentials cannot cite O. Reg. 128/04 as the sole authority. |
| [O. Reg. 170/03](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/030170) | Schedule 7 requires continuous monitoring equipment to be checked and calibrated in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. If the instructions do not state a frequency, paragraphs 9–10 prescribe at least monthly checks for specified small or seasonal systems and, for other systems, checks as often as necessary to maintain prescribed margins of error. For an immediate report under section 18 of the Safe Drinking Water Act, Schedule 16, s. 16-6(3) requires speaking in person or by telephone with the Ministry’s Spills Action Centre and with the Medical Officer of Health or health-unit on-call system, as applicable; s. 16-7(1) requires the corresponding written notice within 24 hours. |
| [O. Reg. 128/04](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040128) | Section 28 requires the owner or operating authority of a drinking-water subsystem to ensure that operators and maintenance personnel have ready access to comprehensive operations and maintenance manuals sufficient for safe and efficient operation. |
| [O. Reg. 129/04](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040129) | Section 20 similarly requires a wastewater-facility owner to ensure that operators and maintenance personnel have ready access to comprehensive operations and maintenance manuals with plans, drawings, and process descriptions sufficient for safe and efficient operation. |
| [Ontario: Report pollution and spills](https://www.ontario.ca/page/report-pollution-and-spills) | A person required to report a spill must call the Spills Action Centre. The page identifies immediate reports to the local municipality, substance owner, and person in control where applicable, and refers to O. Reg. 675/98 for more detail. The owner or controller must do everything practicable to prevent and eliminate negative effects and restore the natural environment. |
| [O. Reg. 675/98](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980675) | Section 13 identifies persons subject to spill or discharge notification, requires telephone notice to the Spills Action Centre, and requires report information including the pollutant, quantity, location, causes, actual or potential adverse effects, and actions taken or planned. It also establishes specified spill classifications and exemptions. |

| [AESSEAL mechanical-seal primer](https://www.aesseal.com/en/resources/academy/what-is-a-mechanical-seal/) | A mechanical seal contains pumped fluid at a rotating shaft through the interaction of stationary and rotating seal faces and a lubricating fluid film. Packing remains a common alternative but typically requires adjustment and can involve visible leakage. |

## Repair constraints

2. Any safety scenario must preserve appropriate first actions and must not normalize delaying notification, energy isolation, assessment, or corrective response.
3. Any changed item remains `in_review` until an independent external semantic review confirms accuracy, safety, a single defensible key, and acceptable distractors.
