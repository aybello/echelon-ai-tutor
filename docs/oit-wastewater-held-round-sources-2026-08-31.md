## Safety and jurisdiction constraints

The Class A/Class B item must not represent U.S. EPA Part 503 terminology as an Ontario classification. The permit-to-work item must frame entry permits as a confined-space safety control rather than a blanket legal condition for all plant work. All revised technical items must avoid unsourced universal performance claims, precise figures, and operational instructions not required to answer the learning objective.

## OIT-level classification

OntarioLearn describes its Ontario OIT wastewater preparation course as covering **basics** of math, chemistry, hydraulics, electricity, pumps and motors, wastewater collection, and wastewater treatment. OWWCO separately identifies OIT as an Ontario-content examination rather than one of the standardized Class 1–4 wastewater examinations.[1][2]

| Classification | Question numbers | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational OIT candidates | 144, 404, 466, 480 | Narrow to basic headworks purpose, confined-space permit documentation, BOD meaning, and representative composite sampling. Each can be assessed without detailed process-design, laboratory-method, or jurisdictional assertions. |
| Defer from OIT remediation | 132, 219, 477, 526, 532, 534 | Retain unchanged. These depend on activated-sludge configuration comparison, U.S.-specific biosolids classifications, detailed BOD method setup, EBPR failure modes, MLE configuration, or tertiary-treatment design choices that exceed the foundational scope selected for this bank. |
