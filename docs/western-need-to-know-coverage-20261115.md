# Western content against the ABC Need to Know outlines

Written 2026-11-15. Checks the 16 western (WPI) course tables, drafts only, against the published Need to Know outlines.

## Short result

- Coverage is broad. Of 190 topic checks across the four systems, 15 were flagged as thin (Class 1 under 5 items, or Class 3 under 10).
- The thin topics are mostly equipment and administration (generators, electrical controls, security, maintenance management), plus a few specific processes (swabbing, temporary service, dechlorination, blowers).
- No exam topic is missing outright, except blowers and compressors in water treatment (0 items) and generators and engines in Class 1 distribution (0 items).
- This is a keyword count, not a judgement of quality. It shows where to look, not whether a topic is taught well.

## Sources

The outlines are on `owwco.ca`, the Ontario Water Wastewater Certification Office, and are the Association of Boards of Certification (ABC) documents. ABC is now Water Professionals International (WPI).

- Water Distribution Operator Need-To-Know (July 2004), and the Water Distribution and Supply version.
- Water Treatment Need To Know Guide.
- Need-to-Know Criteria for Wastewater Treatment Operators and for Collection Operators (2008).
- Canadian Water and Wastewater Operator Certification Best Practices (copy on owwco.ca).

Limits to know about:
- These are Ontario editions of ABC documents, dated 2004 and 2008. The current WPI exam outlines may differ. Please check against the current outlines from WPI or a provincial certifier before treating any of this as final.
- The collection guide says Ontario uses the Canadian Standardized Exams, set by Canadian subject matter experts from the Need-to-Know criteria, with minor differences from ABC's own exams.
- The guides are about what the exams cover. They contain no exam questions, and none were copied.

## What the outlines say that matters here

- Exams are cumulative. Class IV must know all Class I to III topics, with harder questions on the same topic.
- Distribution Class I has 80 questions: 6 general math, 9 safety, 12 applied science, 16 support systems, 34 processes, 3 administration, 0 management. Processes grow to 44 to 49 at higher classes.
- Distribution priority topics at Class I are conveyance, compliance, then excavation and repair.
- Distribution rates pipes, joints, valves, fittings, cross-connection and backflow, safety procedures and units of expression as Advanced even at Class I.
- Class I and II exams have 5 to 10 calculation questions. Class III and IV have 10 to 15, and they are harder problem-solving questions.

## Not checked

- Compliance and regulation questions. They differ by province, so the shared tables avoid them. Distribution lists compliance as a priority topic. That content needs a province-aware design, not shared rows.
- Whether the depth and difficulty at each class match the rating (Basic, Intermediate, Advanced) for each topic.
- Calculation question counts per class.

## Suggested follow-up

Four small tables of about 40 to 60 items each, one per system, covering the thin topics above at the right class level. About 200 items in all, so a small job. Distribution first (generators and engines, electrical controls, swabbing, temporary service, fire flow, pipe materials), then water treatment (blowers, dechlorination, cross-connection, security).
