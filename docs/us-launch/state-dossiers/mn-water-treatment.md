## Credential and examination boundary

Minnesota has a state-administered **Drinking Water / Water Supply System Operator** certification program, not an official water-treatment-only operator credential. The Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) issues a water-operator certificate for community and nontransient noncommunity water systems; current MDH program material identifies five water-operator certificate classes, A through E. [1] [2]

The current, official boundary is therefore the **Minnesota Water Supply System Operator Class A, B, C, D, or E written examination**, selected by the system classification and applicant qualifications. Minnesota Rules require separate examinations for each designated *system* class. The exams may cover water treatment and also other water-system topics, including water-distribution systems; they are not designated as a treatment-only exam. [3] [4]

System class is likewise not a treatment specialty. Rule 9400.0400 scores a whole water supply system using source, treatment processes, storage, wells, and population, then assigns Class A through E from the accumulated value. It lists treatment processes in that system-classification calculation, but does not create a distinct treatment certificate or treatment examination. [5]

Current MDH public guidance confirms the written-exam format for the A–E water program: 100 multiple-choice questions, closed book, three hours, a formula sheet, and a 70/100 passing score. [2] The governing rule establishes a 70-percent passing grade and a closed-book examination, which aligns with MDH’s current guidance. [4]

**Resulting boundary finding:** The assigned stream label “water treatment” does not map to one unique Minnesota credential or examination. A future package cannot be named, scoped, or reviewed as a Minnesota water-treatment-only exam product without an authoritative MDH mapping to a specific class and a class-specific content boundary.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The central program and exam pages were updated by MDH on 2026-01-11, and the training/exam schedule was updated on 2026-10-08; both were current at retrieval. The Revisor pages are the official current publication of the governing rule, although the displayed electronic-publication history for the relevant parts is 2007. Their content is consistent with the current MDH pages on A–E classification, class-specific exams, closed-book administration, and a 70-percent passing standard. [2] [4] [6]

There is no material conflict between the current official sources. The material limitation is a lack of public specificity: MDH says class-specific subject lists exist, but requires contact with MDH to obtain them, and does not publish a treatment-only exam outline, a public class-by-class content outline, or a mapping that turns the assigned water-treatment stream into a single A–E examination. [7] This prevents a defensible candidate-package scope even though the overall credential is current and legitimate.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The potentially relevant learner population is an eligible Minnesota drinking-water-system operator seeking one named Water Supply System Operator class (A, B, C, D, or E). It is not a generic entry-level water-treatment learner group. Eligibility and appropriate level vary materially by certificate class and operating experience. [2] [6]

A future scoped package must exclude all of the following unless a new official source makes the boundary explicit:

- water-distribution content presented as if it were excluded from a Minnesota class exam, because the rule includes water-distribution systems within the potential domains of class examinations; [4]
- higher- or lower-class material where the selected A–E class has not been verified;
- site-specific treatment operation, process-control setpoints, chemical-dose calculations, emergency actions, equipment procedures, and any safety-critical operating instruction not supported by a current official class-specific scope;
- wastewater credentials and wastewater-treatment facility requirements, which have a different certifying authority under Minnesota Rule 9400.0350. [3]

No high-level learning objectives are proposed. This dossier is **Defer**, not Suitable or Narrow, because a learning objective cannot be tied to one exact official Minnesota water-treatment examination or class-specific blueprint.

## Readiness decision

**Decision: Defer.** Minnesota’s official sources establish a real, current, state-specific water-supply operator certification program and a generic class-exam domain. They do not establish the assigned “water treatment” stream as an independent credential/exam boundary. They also do not make a current treatment-only or class-specific public examination outline available. The evidence therefore does not meet the required threshold for a limited future candidate dossier for this route.

This is a source-specific deferral, not a finding that Minnesota certification is unavailable. Reconsider only after obtaining current official MDH material that both: (1) selects one named A–E Water Supply System Operator examination as the intended credential level, and (2) provides or confirms an authoritative class-specific subject list/outline with a defensible treatment-content boundary.

## Bank recommendation

**No unique bank proposed.** Do not create a bank named for `mn-water-treatment`, and do not treat the matrix stream label as proof of a separate Minnesota treatment examination.
