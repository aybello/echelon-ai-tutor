## Decision

**Narrow.** North Carolina’s broad matrix stream, “water treatment,” is not one examination. The current official program distinguishes multiple surface-water and well-water grade examinations. The official NCDEQ candidate page actively lists the Well & Surface needs-to-know manual, current 2026–2027 examination dates, and separate results for A-, B-, and C-Surface and A-, B-, C-, and D-Well examinations. The live official outline provides a specific examination blueprint for **C-Well**, and the current rule identifies Grade C-Well as a distinct certificate class with defined eligibility and a separate exam requirement. [1] [2] [4]

The route is therefore ready only for a limited future, review-only candidate dossier at the **North Carolina Grade C-Well** boundary. It is not ready as a combined “North Carolina water-treatment” bank because that label would improperly combine separately classified surface and well credentials, different grades, and different outlines. [1] [2] [4]

## Credential and examination boundary

The only eligible future candidate-package boundary is:

> **North Carolina Water Treatment Facility Operators Certification Board — Grade C-Well drinking-water treatment operator certificate examination, as represented by the Well & Surface Needs-to-Know manual currently linked from NCDEQ’s active Drinking Water Operator Certification: Exams page.**

Under the current official rule, a Grade C-Well applicant must be at least 18 years old, have a high-school diploma or GED, complete a Board-approved C-Well school, and meet one of the rule’s defined well-facility experience or qualifying-certification pathways. The applicant must also pass an examination designed for the class sought. [4]

The active official C-Well outline specifies a 45-question examination. Its identified content categories are disinfection; distribution-system and cross-control awareness; hydrology; laboratory-process bacteriology; Board and public-water-system rules; safety; small-water-system operation; treatment; well construction, hydrology, and pumps; and designated mathematics categories. [2]

This dossier evaluates the written examination boundary and its published outline. It does not evaluate school completion, education verification, work-experience verification, supervisor certification, application processing, certificate issuance, renewal, operator-in-responsible-charge designation, or hands-on competence. Passing an examination does not by itself establish full certification; the rule and current application process require other qualification elements. [3] [4] [5]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The intended learner is an applicant preparing for the specific **Grade C-Well** examination: an entry-to-intermediate treated-groundwater operator candidate who is pursuing a defined North Carolina certificate class, not a generic water-treatment learner. The official rule ties acceptable well experience to active on-site operational duties for public water systems with chemical treatment and one or more wells. [4]

A future review-only candidate dossier may use only these high-level, source-bound objectives:

- Recognize the C-Well outline’s foundational treated-well-water disinfection, treatment, and small-water-system concepts.
- Recognize the outline’s treated-well context for bacteriological laboratory processes, well construction, well hydrology, and well pumps.
- Recognize the C-Well outline’s North Carolina Board-rule and public-water-system-rule subject categories without representing an outline topic as a legal conclusion beyond the current cited rule.
- Recognize the safety and distribution-system/cross-control awareness domains identified for C-Well.
- Apply only the high-level mathematics categories named in the C-Well outline: conversions, disinfection, dose/feed rate, and volume.

The following remain excluded:

- All other North Carolina treatment examinations and certificates: A-, B-, and C-Surface; A-, B-, and D-Well; apprenticeships; distribution; and cross-connection control. The Grade C-Surface on-premises treatment requirement is not a Grade C-Well requirement and must not be transferred into this package. [1] [4] [5]
- A statewide generic treatment bank, multi-grade treatment bank, or a single package combining surface- and well-water treatment. The official source system separates their qualifications and examination outlines. [1] [2] [4]
- Board-approved school material beyond the outline, proprietary textbooks, external preparation content, site-specific standard operating procedures, equipment-specific instructions, treatment design, laboratory procedures, emergency response, legal advice, or operating judgments.
- Regulatory thresholds, reporting timeframes, local permit conditions, safety-critical procedures, chemical-feed instructions, water-quality decisions, or other claims that would require a separately verified, current primary source and independent qualified review.
- Any learner-visible item, answer key, explanation, assessment delivery, certificate representation, or claim that a candidate will qualify for, pass, receive, or maintain a North Carolina credential.

## Source freshness and conflict assessment

The decisive sources are official and live at retrieval. NCDEQ’s active examinations page displays future 2026–2027 exam dates and links the well-and-surface outline. The current program report, submitted in May 2025, describes the program’s continuing administration and periodic review of the well and surface curriculum. Together, they support limited future candidate-dossier planning, not learner release. [1] [2] [6]

The outline file itself is named “Needs to Knows 2021 Well and Surface.” Its date is older than the active examination page, but it remains the outline NCDEQ currently directs candidates to download. It is adequate for this **Narrow** decision because the route is confined to one named examination and its live-posted official outline. Before any candidate drafting, the owner must reconfirm that NCDEQ still posts this manual or obtain and assess a superseding official outline. A change to the C-Well categories, question count, formula sheet, rule, or referenced regulatory materials requires a new readiness review. [1] [2] [6]

A material conflict was found in two regulator-hosted rule access points. NCDEQ’s rules page points candidates to the OAH live 15A NCAC 18D repository. An older DEQ-downloadable rule compilation differs in several qualification details from that OAH text. The OAH live rule, which records a September 1, 2018 readoption and is the source linked by NCDEQ’s Rules page, is controlling for this dossier. The older DEQ compilation is not used to set eligibility, objectives, or a credential boundary. [4]

No source supports treating “North Carolina water treatment” as a single combined written examination. The distinction between the official surface and well certificate classes, their outlined examinations, and the separate Grade C-Surface operational requirement rules out a general all-treatment package. [1] [2] [4] [5]
