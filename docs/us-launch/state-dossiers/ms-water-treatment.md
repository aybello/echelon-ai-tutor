## Credential and examination boundary

Mississippi’s water-treatment route is administered by the Mississippi State Department of Health (MSDH), Bureau of Public Water Supply, under the state’s waterworks-operator certification programme. It is **not one undifferentiated water-treatment examination**. The controlling state rule establishes Class A through Class E water-system classifications; Classes A through D are treatment classifications, while Class E covers purchased-water systems and distribution-only operator duties. A certificate of competency requires the applicant to pass the appropriate Bureau examination and meet the class requirements. [1]

The live PSI programme sponsored by MSDH currently lists four distinct, authorization-required treatment exams: **Mississippi Water Treatment Class A**, **Class B**, **Class C**, and **Class D**. The live catalogue therefore supplies a current, specific exam identity for each of these four levels. [5] The separate test pages identify the same four test titles and point candidates to level-linked bulletins, but do not themselves publish content domains or an exam blueprint. [6] [7] [8] [9]

For this dossier, the route boundary is therefore:

- **In scope:** a future Mississippi-specific candidate package for one named treatment exam only: Class A, Class B, Class C, or Class D.
- **Not a supported boundary:** a combined “Mississippi water treatment” bank across Classes A–D, because the official programme presents them as separate exams and the classes represent materially different treatment complexity.
- **Excluded:** Class E/distribution-only certification, backflow assembly testing, wastewater treatment or collection certification, site-specific operating instructions, plant design, and legal/compliance content that is not tied to a verified current exam outline.

The class descriptions establish the following credential-level distinctions, not tested content allocations: Class D is limited to chlorination, fluoridation, or direct chemical feed; Class C includes aeration, pH adjustment, corrosion control, and specified closed pressure filtration; Class B includes combinations of Class C treatment and additional processes such as membrane filtration or ion exchange; and Class A includes surface-water treatment, groundwater under the direct influence of surface water, lime softening, or certain coagulation-and-filtration treatment. [1] [3]

The regulation also says that a prospective examinee must have attended a Bureau-sponsored waterworks-operator short course within the preceding 12 months to be eligible for a written examination. That eligibility requirement is not evidence of the examination’s content coverage. [1]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The legal classification and certification requirements are current enough for route definition: the official Secretary of State rule was modified in October 2024, the MSDH certification page was reviewed in March 2025, and the PSI catalogue is live and offered current test dates at retrieval. [1] [2] [5]

However, the content evidence is not current and reliable enough for exam-targeted authoring. MSDH’s current certification page continues to link an exam handbook and a May 2024 certification-requirements document, but the current `page/resources` PDF endpoints returned 404 in direct retrieval. The May 2024 requirements document remained available through MSDH’s legacy official URL and supports classification only; it does not supply an exam blueprint. [2] [3]

The material conflict is more significant: every live PSI Class A–D exam page links a purported candidate bulletin, yet all four linked bulletin URLs returned the same current MSDEQ wastewater bulletin. That bulletin explicitly concerns wastewater exams and cannot be treated as evidence for MSDH drinking-water treatment examinations. [6] [7] [8] [9] [10] No current official, class-specific treatment examination outline, domain breakdown, reference list, question allocation, or official programme statement identifying tested objectives was located in the official sources reviewed.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The intended learners are prospective Mississippi water-treatment operator candidates at a **single specified MSDH examination class**. The credential level must be fixed before any candidate development because the state’s Class D, C, B, and A classifications correspond to different treatment configurations and progressively different experience requirements. [1] [3]

This dossier does not authorize learner objectives. Under a defer decision, it does not infer the content of Class A, B, C, or D examinations from the rule’s facility-classification descriptions, the operator manual, historic administrative material, national water-operator conventions, or content from another Mississippi programme. It also excludes actual plant operational directions, emergency procedures, design calculations, individualized legal-compliance advice, and any content sourced from the erroneously linked wastewater bulletin.

## Readiness decision: Defer

**Decision: Defer.**

The exact jurisdiction, credential family, provider, and four current exam names are verified. Nevertheless, this route does not satisfy the readiness contract for a future review-only question-bank candidate package because no current official class-specific blueprint or examination outline is available, and the active Class A–D bulletin links resolve to a different programme. The available classification definitions establish what types of systems fall in each certification class, but they do not establish what concepts, weighting, or cognitive tasks are actually examined. [1] [5] [10]

This is a **Defer**, rather than a Hold, because the underlying programme and exam identities are active and unambiguous; the blocker is an official content-evidence gap and contradictory linked programme documentation, which may be resolved by a corrected MSDH or PSI publication.

## Bank recommendation

**No unique bank is proposed.** A route-level `ms-water-treatment` bank would wrongly merge four separately named exams, and a Class A, B, C, or D bank cannot be evidence-supported until MSDH or its authorized testing provider publishes or directly supplies a current, class-specific examination outline, tested-objective list, or corrected candidate bulletin.

A later reassessment may consider one class-specific bank only after it verifies: the exact live exam title; a current sponsor-approved content outline tied to that title; source-bound, class-appropriate objectives; and a separate independent review record. Any future candidate material must remain `in_review` and inaccessible to learners unless and until those controls are completed.
