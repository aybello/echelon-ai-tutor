## Credential and examination boundary

This route concerns the **North Dakota Department of Environmental Quality (NDDEQ) water treatment facility operator certificate**. North Dakota law defines a water treatment plant as the portion of a water-supply system that alters the physical, chemical, or bacteriological quality of water. The Department classifies facilities and certifies a competent operator by grade for the facility or system to be supervised. An operator in direct responsible charge must hold a certificate at least equal to the facility classification. [1] [2]

The current administrative rule establishes five water-treatment grades—IA, I, II, III, and IV—and requires an applicant to meet the applicable education and experience requirements and pass the Department-developed examination for the facility or system class on the application. It also requires separate examinations for each facility or system classification. [2] The official 2026 program report confirms that treatment and distribution certificates are separate, and that candidates must pass an examination appropriate to the system classification they seek. [5]

The only official treatment examination outline located for the lower grades is NDDEQ's **Study Guide for Grade 1A, 1 & 2: Water Treatment Plant Operators**. It expressly describes itself as covering “primary skill and knowledge levels” and says its outline topics are the general subjects on which examination questions are based. [6] The exact supported boundary is therefore:

> **North Dakota water-treatment primary-level common-outline subset for Grades IA, I, and II**, limited to concepts explicitly listed in the official Grade 1A/I/II guide. It is not an individual Grade IA, I, or II examination blueprint, and it must not be represented as one.

The guide does not distinguish which listed subject is tested at Grade IA versus Grade I versus Grade II. Because the rule requires separate exams, a bank cannot validly claim complete coverage of, or readiness for, any one of those three grade examinations from this grouped guide alone. [2] [6]

## Supported learner-level scope

The supported audience is an applicant who is otherwise eligible under North Dakota's education-and-experience requirements and is preparing for the **shared primary-skill-and-knowledge scope** published for water-treatment Grades IA, I, and II. Eligibility itself remains a Department determination; the dossier does not advise on an individual's eligibility. [2] [6]

For a future limited candidate dossier, the high-level objectives may be confined to the guide's common outline:

- Recognize fundamental physical, chemical, and radiological characteristics of source water.
- Distinguish groundwater and surface-water characteristics and foundational well concepts.
- Identify the purposes and basic concepts of potable-water treatment processes listed by NDDEQ, including sedimentation, coagulation/flocculation, softening, filtration, disinfection, fluoridation, aeration, and corrosion control.
- Apply foundational operational concepts for filtration, pumping, storage, valves, cross-connection control, hydraulics, safety, and recordkeeping.
- Recognize the guide's high-level treatment-related regulatory, sampling, reporting, and public-notification subject areas without embedding unverified or outdated numerical obligations. [6]

### Exclusions

This dossier excludes the following from any future candidate work under this decision:

- A claim of complete readiness for a discrete Grade IA, Grade I, or Grade II examination; the official guide is grouped and does not allocate topics by grade. [2] [6]
- Grade III and Grade IV material, which NDDEQ labels as advanced skill-and-knowledge content in a separate guide. [7]
- Water distribution certification, wastewater credentials, reciprocity, temporary permits, and certification-administration procedures, except where needed to state this boundary. [1] [2]
- Individual eligibility decisions, direct-responsible-charge determinations, facility classification determinations, site-specific procedures, equipment settings, and safety-critical operating instructions.
- Numeric regulatory thresholds, sampling frequencies, fee amounts, passing scores, renewal deadlines, or other legal/administrative details unless rechecked against the current controlling legal source immediately before authoring. The 2020 outline includes time-sensitive references, while the official 2025 amendment notice and 2026 annual report show rule updates were proposed or in progress. [5] [6] [9]
- Anything drawn from the guide's sample questions, answer material, or worked calculations. No questions, options, answers, explanations, or bank data are contained in this dossier.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The credential boundary is current enough to establish a **narrow** route: the Legislative Council's current Administrative Code index continues to list Chapter 33.1-19-01, the statute authorizes the Department's examination/certification program, and the live NDDEQ training page schedules 2026 examinations and links the treatment study guides. [1] [3] [7]

The examination-topic source is nevertheless dated December 15, 2020. It remains linked on the live 2026 NDDEQ page, and the 2026 annual report says no examination changes were made. That combination supports use of the guide for a tightly constrained common-outline scope, not a claim that it is a current per-grade blueprint. [5] [6] [7]

A material administrative freshness issue remains. NDDEQ's 2025 notice describes amendments as proposed; the annual report reproduces draft language with a blank effective date and says Administrative Code updates were in progress. The current code text accessed from the Legislative Council's index shows rule history through January 1, 2022. The proposal therefore cannot be treated as current law or incorporated into candidate content. Before any future drafting, recheck the rule for final adoption and revalidate the grouped guide with NDDEQ or an official replacement outline. [2] [3] [5] [9]

## Readiness decision and bank recommendation

**Decision: Narrow.** North Dakota has a current, official state credential and a state-administered examination program. The official lower-grade guide supports a finite set of primary-level water-treatment objectives for Grades IA, I, and II, and the 2026 report indicates the examinations did not change. [2] [5] [6]

The route is not **Suitable** for a discrete exam bank because North Dakota requires separate examinations by classification but publishes only one combined lower-grade guide; it does not identify the knowledge allocation, weight, or unique boundary for Grade IA, I, or II. In addition, the underlying guide is dated 2020 and administrative-code revisions were still unsettled in 2026. [2] [5] [6] [9]

**Bank recommendation:** A unique, prospective state-specific bank identity is supportable only at the documented shared-outline level: **North Dakota Water Treatment — Grades IA/I/II Common Primary Outline**. It must remain explicitly limited to common guide-supported concepts and must not be marketed, labelled, or reviewed as a complete preparation product for any individual grade. Do not create a Grade IA-only, Grade I-only, Grade II-only, Grade III, or Grade IV bank from this dossier.

Under the readiness contract, only **Suitable** dossiers may generate review-only JSON candidate batches. This **Narrow** dossier authorizes no such package now. If later promoted after NDDEQ confirms a current grade-specific blueprint or explicitly validates the shared-guide use, any resulting work remains **candidate-only / no learner access** pending structural validation and independent review.
