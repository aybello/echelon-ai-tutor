## Credential and examination boundary

The supported route is the **New Mexico Water Distribution System – Level 1 (DS1) operator certification examination**, administered through the New Mexico Environment Department (NMED) Utility Operator Certification Program. The official DS1 guidebook identifies a 100-item multiple-choice certification examination with a 70% passing score and ten defined content areas. It was issued in March 2026 and states that the New Mexico Utility Operators Certification Advisory Board reviewed it in January and February 2026. [1]

This is a distinct **distribution-system** credential boundary. The current governing rule assigns DS1 to the operation of unchlorinated-groundwater distribution systems serving 501–5,000 people; it assigns the Small Water credential to the 25–500 category and DS2 or DS3 to the higher listed categories. The guidebook notes that DS1 also covers lower categories. [1] [3]

The applicant and credential-administration requirements are not the proposed instructional target. They confirm the boundary only: the current rule requires a written examination for the certification level sought and, for DS1, high-school graduation or GED, one year of experience, and ten training credits, subject to the rule’s substitution provisions. [3]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The substantive blueprint source is recent: NMED’s DS1 guidebook is dated March 2026 and was reviewed by the state advisory board in early 2026. The NMED program page currently links that guidebook. The codified rule page is older in page-modification metadata, but it is the official, permanent governing rule and remains consistent with the 2026 guidebook on the material DS1 points: a distribution-specific credential, written examination, DS1 eligibility baseline, and classification framework. [1] [2] [3]

No material conflict was found. The guidebook’s detailed need-to-know heading contains “WD1” once, while the document title, credential discussion, examination content, and NMED program page consistently identify **DS1**. Treat “WD1” as a non-substantive labeling inconsistency; do not use it as a credential name in future candidate materials. [1] [2]

The current official materials do not support treating DS1 as interchangeable with New Mexico Small Water, DS2, DS3, Water Supply, sampling, wastewater, or wastewater-collection credentials. [1] [3]

## Learner-level scope for a future limited candidate dossier

The supported learner is an applicant preparing for the **New Mexico DS1 written certification examination**. The future candidate dossier must remain limited to the official DS1 blueprint, using one source-traceable objective at a time. The following are high-level objectives only, not assessment content:

- Recognize the function, common components, and basic operation-and-maintenance concepts of drinking-water distribution infrastructure, including hydrants, meters, piping and joints, valves, maps, pressure, and water-quality considerations. [1]
- Apply the DS1-level foundational concepts of cross-connection control, disinfection, storage, and water characteristics within the topic boundaries stated by NMED. [1]
- Use the blueprint’s foundational measurement, flow, pressure, volume, dosage, efficiency, hydraulics, unit, and terminology concepts. [1]
- Recognize DS1-level operation-and-maintenance concepts for chemical feeders, instrumentation and metering equipment, motors, pumps, and valves. [1]
- Identify the high-level regulatory subject areas named in the DS1 blueprint: EPA Safe Drinking Water Act regulations, New Mexico utility-operator certification regulations, and New Mexico drinking-water regulations. [1]
- Recognize the safety topic domains specified by the DS1 blueprint, including chemical, confined-space, electrical, excavation/shoring, hazardous-gas, rotating-machinery, traffic/street-work, and related safety-documentation topics. [1]

### Explicit exclusions

A future candidate package for this route must exclude:

- DS2, DS3, Small Water, Water Supply, Water Sampling Technician, wastewater, wastewater-collection, and other state or national credential scopes.
- Treatment-process design or operation beyond the DS1 distribution-system blueprint, advanced engineering design, local utility policies, and site-specific standard operating procedures.
- Examination application eligibility, fee, renewal, equivalency, and scheduling administration as instructional targets, except where a later official examination outline expressly includes the concept.
- Operational recipes, site-specific emergency directions, or safety-critical procedures not directly and unambiguously supported by the current DS1 blueprint and an appropriate current authority.
- Assertions that a national or external study reference creates a New Mexico legal or certification requirement. The guidebook’s suggested references are study resources, not substitutes for New Mexico’s credential boundary. [1]

## Readiness decision

**Suitable.** NMED currently provides a state-issued, credential-specific DS1 guidebook with a granular examination blueprint, question allocation by content area, named topics, and a stated passing threshold. The current New Mexico rule independently confirms the DS1 classification and written-examination requirement. This is enough official evidence to support a **limited future review-only candidate dossier** for the exact DS1 route. [1] [3]

This decision does not validate individual future items, create a question bank, or authorize production. Each future candidate must still be traceable to the current official source record, structurally checked, and independently reviewed.
