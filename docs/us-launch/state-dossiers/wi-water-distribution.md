## Credential and examination boundary

The supported target is the **Wisconsin Municipal Waterworks Operator Certification, Subclass D — Distribution**. Wisconsin Administrative Code ch. NR 114 classifies a waterworks containing a distribution system as Subclass D. Grade T certification requires passing the examination for the given subclass; Grade 1 requires the Grade T requirements plus one year of satisfactory, subclass-specific experience. The certification is therefore bounded to the **Subclass D examination at the Grade T entry point**, not to a separate Grade 1 examination or to experience assessment. [1]

The current DNR examination information identifies Municipal Waterworks — Distribution as a standalone **40-question**, multiple-choice, closed-book examination. The official 2027 municipal-waterworks application separately lists “Distribution” as an examination choice, which confirms that the Subclass D route remains independently administered. [2] [3]

This route does not cover Wisconsin small-water-system certification, privately administered water licensing, wastewater credentials, or Municipal Waterworks subclasses G, I, L, S, V, or Z. It also does not cover operator-in-charge experience, renewal/continuing-education administration, reciprocity, or facility-specific operating authority. [1] [4]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The credential boundary is current and specific. The Legislature’s online administrative-code record states that the code is updated monthly and identifies the current qualification rule; the qualification language reflects the May 2022 amendment history. Current DNR programme and examination pages continue to administer the Municipal Waterworks route, and the DNR’s October 2026-revised application provides a 2027 examination schedule. [1] [2] [3] [4]

The objective-level source is official but dated: the Distribution Study Guide is a December 2006 edition revised February 2016. DNR’s current waterworks page still designates that guide as the Subclass D study guide and says study guides provide the objectives covered on examinations. [4] [5] The guide itself flags updates that were tied to 2013–2016 drinking-water rule changes. Its regulatory values, sampling frequencies, laboratory-method details, and other time-sensitive compliance statements cannot be treated as current merely because the guide remains posted. [5]

No source conflicts on the existence, subclass, grades, or standalone Distribution examination were found. The DNR examination page describes 2026 administration, while the later-revised official application lists 2027 sessions; this is an administrative-date difference, not a credential or scope conflict. The 2027 application is the controlling source for its listed 2027 session information. [2] [3]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The future candidate audience is an applicant preparing for the **Grade T, Municipal Waterworks Subclass D** examination. Grade 1 experience is a credential prerequisite after the Grade T requirements, not a separate learner level for this examination boundary. [1]

Because of the dated objective guide, any future candidate dossier must be limited to source-stable foundational concepts and must independently verify every factual or numerical assertion against current Wisconsin primary authority before review. It must exclude:

- current Wisconsin compliance values, monitoring frequencies, sample handling rules, reporting or notification duties, and laboratory-method requirements unless separately supported by current official Wisconsin authority;
- safety-critical work methods, construction specifications, isolation and disinfection procedures, and site-specific operating instructions unless a current official source supports the exact bounded claim;
- Grade 1 experience determinations, operator-in-charge duties, renewal, reciprocity, enforcement, or other administrative topics that are not an examination objective for the Subclass D entry examination;
- water-treatment subclasses, small-water-system certification, wastewater, and material from other jurisdictions.

## High-level objectives supported for a limited future candidate dossier

Only the following high-level objective areas are supportable at this stage. They are scope headings, not question content and not approval to author candidate items.

1. Recognize the basic purpose, principal components, and functional relationships of a potable-water distribution system, including storage, mains, valves, pumps, hydrants, meters, and service connections.
2. Apply foundational concepts for routine distribution-system operation and maintenance, including equipment functions, records, meter use, valve and hydrant maintenance, and cross-connection concepts.
3. Interpret non-regulatory distribution-system monitoring and troubleshooting concepts, such as meter readings, loss of pressure, leaks, pump symptoms, and common water-quality symptoms.
4. Perform the basic conversion, storage-pressure, arithmetic-average, and disinfectant-quantity calculation concepts named in the official guide, subject to current-source validation of every formula, unit, and applied condition. [5]

## Readiness decision

**Narrow.** Wisconsin has a current, official, state-specific Subclass D credential/exam boundary: DNR administers a standalone 40-question Municipal Waterworks — Distribution examination, and the current rule distinguishes Grade T examination qualification from Grade 1 experience. [1] [2] The current DNR waterworks page also maintains an official Subclass D study guide that supplies a substantial objective framework. [4] [5]

The route is not Suitable for the guide’s full scope because that framework was last revised in February 2016 and contains time-sensitive regulatory, monitoring, laboratory, and operational material. Under the readiness contract, this Narrow finding does **not** authorize generation of a review-only candidate package. Reassess this route only after current Wisconsin primary authority verifies the proposed limited objectives and any later claim; any such later material must remain in review-only status.

## Bank recommendation

A single future unique state-specific bank may be proposed for **Wisconsin Municipal Waterworks — Subclass D (Distribution)** because the official evidence identifies a discrete Wisconsin credential/exam family rather than a generic distribution route. The proposal must remain restricted to the Grade T Subclass D examination boundary, must not imply Grade 1 experience qualification, and must first complete the programme-required read-only recovery and overlap comparison against any prior U.S. Class I distribution package. No bank is created by this dossier.
