## Decision

**Suitable** for a future, limited candidate dossier only. New Mexico Environment Department (NMED) currently identifies WW2 as a named Public Wastewater Facility certification and provides a March 2026, Advisory-Board-reviewed guidebook that defines the related written examination: 100 multiple-choice questions, a 70 percent passing score, and a 16-area need-to-know outline with question counts. The controlling state rule separately requires an applicant to pass the written examination for the certification level sought and fixes the WW2 facility-classification and eligibility context. This is a specific, current state credential/exam boundary, not a generic wastewater-treatment topic. [1] [3]

The result supports a distinct state-specific identity because the verified examination is expressly New Mexico WW2, has its own NMED blueprint, and is not shown as a shared WPI or previously identified U.S. Class I package. The proposed identity is only a future review container; it is not a direction to create records or candidate content.

## Credential and examination boundary

The in-scope credential is **Public Wastewater Facility – Level 2 (WW2)** administered by the NMED Utility Operator Certification Program. WW2 is one of New Mexico’s general public-wastewater-facility certification levels. For initial certification, the rule requires the applicant to meet the applicable education, experience, and training conditions and to pass the written examination for the level sought. The standard WW2 minimums are a high school diploma or GED, two years of experience, and 30 training credits, subject to the rule’s specified substitutions. [3] [4]

The legal facility boundary is narrower than “all wastewater treatment.” Under 20.7.4.13(A) NMAC, WW2 applies to the listed population categories for aerated lagoons, primary treatment, and primary treatment plus oxidation ponds; it also applies to secondary treatment by trickling filter only for the 501–5,000 population-served category. The rule assigns other credential levels to other process/population combinations. [3]

The NMED WW2 guidebook states that the examination has 100 multiple-choice questions across 16 specified areas, with a minimum passing score of 70 percent. That official outline is the only examination content boundary for any future candidate-only package under this dossier. [1]

## High-level source-bound objectives

Any future candidate material must remain limited to the WW2 guidebook’s named examination areas. At a high level, a candidate package could assess recognition and application of the following source-bound objective groups, without expanding into unlisted procedures or facility-specific mandates:

- Collection-system, pretreatment, mechanical-system, pond, sedimentation, solids-handling, and trickling-filter concepts within the WW2 outline.
- Disinfection, dechlorination, effluent-polishing, cross-connection-control, and associated operational principles listed for WW2.
- Foundational wastewater characteristics, calculations, hydraulics, flow, volume, dosage, efficiency, and removal concepts identified under the guidebook’s general area.
- The expressly listed WW2 laboratory, sampling, reporting, regulatory, and safety areas, limited to the guidebook’s stated topics and the controlling New Mexico certification context.

These are objectives, not drafted questions, answers, rationales, operating instructions, or a content release.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The intended learner is an eligible or prospective New Mexico **WW2** operator-certification examinee, not an entry-level wastewater learner and not an operator seeking a different credential. The baseline eligibility indicates an experienced learner: WW2 normally requires two years of relevant experience and 30 approved training credits, though NMED may apply documented rule-based substitutions. [1] [3] [4]

The following exclusions are mandatory:

- Do not extend this dossier to WW1, WW3, WW4, Small Wastewater (SWW), Small Wastewater Advanced (SWWA), wastewater laboratory technician levels, or any drinking-water credential. Those are separately named credentials or levels, and no source here establishes a shared examination blueprint.
- Do not treat WW2’s lesser-included-certification effect as authority to create a combined WW2/WWLT1/CS1/CS2 examination bank. The rule describes what a WW2 certificate holder may perform; the guidebook defines the WW2 examination. Standalone lower-level examinations require their own source verification. [1] [3]
- Do not cover treatment/process combinations that the legal classification table assigns to SWW, SWWA, WW3, or WW4, including aeration-based secondary treatment, physical-chemical treatment, advanced waste treatment, and phosphorus/nitrogen removal, except to the limited extent a topic is explicitly inside the WW2 guidebook outline and does not imply eligibility for another level. [1] [3]
- Do not state site-specific permit limits, local reporting protocols, operating setpoints, emergency actions, engineering design, or equipment-specific instructions. The WW2 guidebook names NPDES, regulations, sampling/reporting, and safety as examination areas, but it does not supply a universal facility permit or site procedure.
- Do not use the listed third-party study references as final authority for New Mexico certification, regulatory, safety, or exam-blueprint claims. They are suggested study resources, whereas this dossier relies on the NMED guidebook, NMED program information, NMED eligibility supplement, and the official New Mexico rule. [1]

## Source freshness and conflict check

The decisive programme materials are current 2026 NMED materials: the March WW2 guidebook and the April eligibility pamphlet. NMED’s currently available certification page links both documents and presents WW2 as an active certification route. [1] [2] [4]

No material conflict was found. The state rule’s section histories show earlier amendments, including relevant updates in 2011, but the 2026 NMED guidebook and 2026 eligibility pamphlet expressly apply and cite the same rule provisions for WW2. The rule therefore remains the source for legal classification and eligibility; the newer NMED guidebook is the source for the current examination blueprint, question count, and passing score. [1] [3] [4]

One source limitation does not change the decision: NMED’s general programme page says candidate guidebooks are available for each certification type/level while its table marks several other wastewater guidebooks as “in development.” That inconsistency concerns other credentials, not WW2; WW2 is specifically listed with an active March 2026 guidebook and April 2026 supplement. [2]
