# Wisconsin wastewater-treatment operator certification readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `wi-wastewater-treatment`

**Existing matrix label:** state-specific
**Research retrieval date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Suitable**

## Decision and credential / examination boundary

Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources (DNR) administers a current wastewater-treatment-operator certification program under Subchapter IV of Wisconsin Administrative Code ch. NR 114. The rule establishes two operator levels and 13 wastewater-treatment subclasses. It requires both the **basic general wastewater examination** and a specified subclass examination for operator-in-training status in that subclass; basic certification also requires one year of satisfactory subclass-specific experience. [1]

The evidence supports one limited, distinct examination boundary for future review-only candidates: **Wisconsin DNR Wastewater–General examination**. This is the 50-question, closed-book general examination offered by the DNR. It is a required common examination component for the operator-in-training and basic certification path in wastewater-treatment subclasses, except the rule’s stated exclusions for the Unique Treatment Systems (U) and Sanitary Sewage Collection System (SS) basic certifications. It is **not itself a complete operator credential**. Passing it alone does not establish operator-in-training or basic certification. [1] [2]

The boundary is sufficiently specific because the current DNR wastewater-certification page states that a person seeking certified wastewater-treatment-plant operator status must pass the Wastewater–General examination and at least one applicable subclass examination. The same live DNR page identifies the separate treatment process subclasses, while the current 2027 DNR examination application separately lists “Wastewater – General” among the available examinations. [2] [5]

This decision is **Suitable** only for the discrete Wastewater–General examination’s foundational scope. It does not support a general Wisconsin wastewater-treatment bank across all credentials, a bank for any individual subclass, or a combined general-and-subclass bank.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The supported learner is an applicant preparing for the common Wastewater–General examination as part of the Wisconsin operator-in-training or basic wastewater-treatment certification path. DNR states that prior education or training is not required before taking examinations, although it recommends training. Certification progression remains conditional on the relevant subclass examination and, for basic certification, documented subclass-specific experience. [1] [2]

For a future limited candidate dossier, high-level objectives may address only the current DNR Basic General Wastewater Study Guide’s stated content areas:

- wastewater terminology, flows, pollutants, sewer-system concepts, basic processes, and foundational safety/regulatory terminology;
- influent sources, conveyance, wastewater characteristics, flow monitoring, and sampling concepts;
- high-level preliminary, primary, secondary, final clarification, tertiary, disinfection, pond/lagoon, equipment, and maintenance concepts;
- biosolids/sludge thickening, treatment, dewatering, land-application, sampling, and reporting concepts;
- effluent flow monitoring, sampling, permitting/reporting awareness, and general wastewater-treatment-plant management topics;
- personal and chemical safety concepts; and
- the guide’s identified foundational calculations: sampling, flow conversion and flow rate, tank area and volume, pounds formula, pump rate, detention time, and percent removal. [4]

The future candidate dossier must exclude:

- all process-specific subclass examination content: A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, B, C, D, L, N, P, SS, and U;
- the wastewater advanced examination and all advanced-certification point, education, experience, and application content;
- claims that passing the general examination alone awards an operator-in-training, basic, or advanced credential;
- sanitary sewage collection-system content and Unique Treatment Systems content, which the current rule expressly treats as exceptions to the general-examination requirement for basic certification;
- Wisconsin credential eligibility determinations, reciprocal certification, renewal, fees, enforcement, or continuing-education requirements, except where a future candidate has a current, direct official source for the exact claim;
- site-specific plant operations, WPDES permit limits, facility-specific reporting duties, engineering/design decisions, emergency response procedures, and any legal or safety-critical operational direction; and
- non-Wisconsin rules, commercial preparation material, blogs, test recollections, or inferred examination weighting.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The governing administrative-code page identifies the code as current and updated monthly. The current rule history shows the examination-administration provisions were amended effective May 1, 2022, while the certification structure and examination boundary remain stated in the live rule. [1] The DNR wastewater-certification and examination pages were retrieved live on October 29, 2026. The DNR’s current application is revised October 2026 and schedules 2027 examination sessions, providing recent official evidence that Wastewater–General remains an active, separately selectable examination. [2] [3] [5]

The study guide has an older named edition date, revised June 2016. That is a material freshness limitation for any time-sensitive regulatory, permit, safety, reporting, fee, or administrative assertion. It is nevertheless an actively linked official DNR preparation resource on the current certification page, and the current 2027 application directs candidates to that DNR wastewater page for study guides. It is therefore sufficient for the bounded foundational examination domains above, but not for assuming current legal thresholds, unverified fixed examination weighting, or facility-specific directions. [2] [4] [5]

No material conflict prevents the decision. The live DNR program page and the current rule agree that the general examination is paired with one or more subclass examinations for the normal wastewater-treatment pathway. The sources also consistently distinguish the general examination from subclass examinations and from the advanced examination. [1] [2] [3] [5]

## Bank recommendation and release boundary

A unique future bank identity is evidence-supported only for the discrete **Wisconsin Wastewater–General examination**, for example: `wi-wastewater-general`. It must be limited to source-traceable foundational knowledge from the current DNR-hosted Basic General Wastewater Study Guide and must preserve the stated credential boundary: it prepares only for the general examination component, not for a subclass examination or a complete certification.

This dossier does **not** authorize a Wisconsin all-subclass wastewater-treatment bank, an advanced wastewater bank, a credential claim, or authoring beyond the stated general-examination boundary. It contains no questions, options, explanations, code, bank data, or release.

**Release boundary:** future candidates only, in review-only status after structural validation and independent review; no learner access, publication, activation, database import, entitlement, pricing, checkout, approval, or other release action.
