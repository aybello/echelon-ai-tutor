## Decision

**Hold.** Nebraska has a current, official water-operator licensure program and current Grade I–VI examination administration materials. The reviewed official materials do not establish a separately named Nebraska water-distribution credential or examination, nor do they publish an official grade-specific examination outline that isolates a bounded water-distribution content domain. The available evidence therefore does not support an exact credential/exam boundary for the requested stream. [1] [2] [3]

## Credential and examination boundary

Nebraska Title 179 governs licensure of public water-system operators. A licensed operator may make process-control or system-integrity decisions affecting water quality or quantity in public water systems, and an operator in responsible charge may make daily operational decisions for a public water system, water-treatment facility, and/or distribution system. This is a public-water-system operator credential framework, not a distribution-only credential. [1]

The current rule offers Grade I through Grade V licensure to public-water-system operators and Grade VI licensure to authorized backflow-preventer testing and repair technicians. Its examination provisions are grade-based: Grades I–IV use validated examinations at the respective grade, Grade V addresses water-sample collection, biological-result interpretation, and records, and Grade VI addresses cross connections and backflow prevention. [1]

The current examination application asks the applicant to select Grade I, II, III, IV, V, or VI. It does not offer a water-distribution examination selection. It lists Grade III and IV training-course materials that include a distribution-system textbook, but identifies those books only as required course books and does not provide an official examination blueprint, domain list, weighting, or a distribution-only grade boundary. [3]

**Determination:** the defensible boundary is **Nebraska grade-based public-water-system operator licensure (Grades I–V, with Grade VI specialized backflow licensure), not a Nebraska water-distribution operator examination.** This boundary is incompatible with identifying the requested stream as a distinct candidate package.

## Source freshness and conflicts

Title 179 is the controlling rule in the reviewed record and states an effective date of 2025-03-18. The DWEE application page identifies WAT283 as revised 2025-07-08. The live program page confirms that DWEE currently administers and scores water-operator license examinations. [1] [2] [3]

The three reviewed sources are consistent on grade-based operator licensure. No direct conflict was found. The material limitation is an absence of a current official Nebraska distribution-only examination name, candidate handbook, content outline, or official blueprint. The Grade III/IV textbook reference in WAT283 is not treated as an examination outline and cannot establish what a Nebraska distribution examination tests. [3]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

No learner-level content scope is approved. The sources support only the following administrative boundary: an applicant for a Nebraska public-water-system operator license at a grade appropriate to the system classification, rather than a candidate for a separately identified distribution credential. [1] [3]

Excluded from any future work under this route are: Grade VI backflow-prevention testing and repair; Grade V sample-collection, biological-result interpretation, and recordkeeping licensure; generic Grade I–IV treatment or whole-system content that cannot be attributed to a distribution-specific official exam domain; non-Nebraska examination frameworks; and course-book material that has not been tied to an official Nebraska test specification. [1] [3]

## Bank recommendation

**No unique bank proposed.** Do not create, name, or stage a Nebraska water-distribution bank from the current evidence. A future reconsideration requires a current official Nebraska source that identifies either:

- a distinct water-distribution credential/examination; or
- an expressly identified, grade-specific distribution examination domain with enough published scope to limit future candidate material accurately.

The current grade-based licensure scheme is not evidence that a standalone distribution bank exists or is appropriate. [1] [3]
