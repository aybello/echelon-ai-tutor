## Exact state and stream

- **State:** New Hampshire (NH), United States.
- **Stream:** Wastewater collection.
- **Verified credential route:** New England Water Environment Association (NEWEA) **Collection Systems Certification Program**, a voluntary regional certification program available to collection-systems personnel across the New England states, including New Hampshire.[1] [2]

The route is therefore not a New Hampshire-only state credential. NEWEA's New Hampshire resource identifies collection-system certification as a voluntary program administered through NEWEA, while the current NEWEA program document defines the regional credential and its examination series.[1] [2]

## Credential and examination boundary

The supported future candidate boundary is **NEWEA Collection Systems Certification Program — Grade I examination**. Grade I-OIT is a status available within the Grade I route for candidates who have met the examination and education requirements but do not yet have the full qualifying experience; it is not a separate collection-system blueprint in the official program document.[1]

The Grade I credential route is limited to personnel whose fieldwork is focused on **sewer maintenance or construction** or **pumping stations**, within systems that collect and convey wastewater through gravity sewers, force mains, manholes, pumping equipment, and related facilities.[1] The current program has four grades. A Grade I-certified person is qualified to maintain operation of a Grade I collection system; Grade I system classification is a total point value of 1–20 under NEWEA's system-classification framework.[1]

The examination boundary is specific: each certification examination is a 100-question, multiple-choice examination, with a 70% minimum passing grade. The official Grade I outline assigns the exam's subject-matter distribution as follows: Equipment Operation, Evaluation & Maintenance (28%); Collection System Operation, Maintenance & Restoration (20%); Lift Station Operation & Maintenance (14%); Collection System Monitoring, Evaluation & Adjustment (19%); and Applied Math, Safety and Administrative Procedures (19%).[1]

This route must not be represented as the NHDES wastewater treatment-plant operator certificate. Current NHDES certification information and Env-Wq 304 apply to wastewater **treatment plant** operators and owners of permitted treatment plants. The cited NHDES materials do not establish a separate state-mandated New Hampshire collection-system operator examination or collection-only state credential.[3] [4]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The controlling examination source is NEWEA's official program document revised **June 2026**, which is current relative to this dossier date and provides the credential-specific Grade I exam boundary.[1] The New Hampshire NEWEA resource page is undated; it is used only to connect the voluntary regional NEWEA collection credential to New Hampshire, not for detailed eligibility, fees, dates, or an exam blueprint.[2]

The current state rule was readopted with amendment effective September 27, 2023, with the source record showing an expiration date of September 27, 2033.[4] NHDES's current program page is consistent with that rule's treatment-plant-only scope.[3] No conflict was found: the sources describe different credential boundaries. NHDES regulates and certifies wastewater treatment-plant operators, while NEWEA administers the voluntary regional collection-system certification.[1] [2] [3] [4]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The supported learner level is an entry-level collection-systems candidate seeking **NEWEA Grade I**, including the Grade I-OIT pathway where experience affects credential status. Any future candidate material must remain at the published Grade I boundary and use only the five current official outline domains.[1]

High-level, source-bound objectives for a future limited candidate dossier are:

1. Recognize foundational equipment operation, evaluation, and maintenance responsibilities in wastewater collection systems.
2. Recognize foundational collection-system operation, maintenance, and restoration concepts.
3. Recognize foundational lift-station operation and maintenance concepts.
4. Recognize foundational collection-system monitoring, evaluation, and adjustment concepts.
5. Apply the Grade I-level outline's high-level applied-math, safety, and administrative-procedure domains only where the current NEWEA source provides adequate, unambiguous support.[1]

The following are excluded from this route: NHDES wastewater treatment-plant operator certification and its Grades I–IV; treatment-process control, laboratory analysis, permit-compliance and effluent-limit content; New Hampshire-specific legal, reporting, licensing, or facility-owner duties; higher NEWEA Grades II–IV; facility-specific procedures; and any claim that NEWEA collection certification is mandatory in New Hampshire.[1] [3] [4]

## Decision and bank recommendation

**Decision: Suitable.** A current official certification-body program document provides a precise, active Grade I credential and examination boundary, together with a published exam format and domain-weight outline sufficient for a limited, future review-only candidate dossier.[1]

**Bank recommendation:** Do **not** create an NH-only bank. The evidence supports one shared regional credential identity, proposed only for future duplicate/overlap validation as **`newea-collection-systems-grade-1`**. Its scope must be the NEWEA Grade I Collection Systems Certification examination, not state-specific New Hampshire treatment-operator content. A Grade II–IV bank, state-law addendum, or collection-system claim beyond the official Grade I outline is not supported by this dossier.
