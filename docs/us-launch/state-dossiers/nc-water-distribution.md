## Credential and examination boundary

North Carolina’s drinking-water distribution certifications are administered through the North Carolina Water Treatment Facility Operators Certification Board (NCWTFOCB) within the North Carolina Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ). State law authorizes the Board to establish certification programs for personnel operating the distribution portion of a water treatment facility. [1]

The live DEQ examination page currently identifies separate **A-Distribution, B-Distribution, C-Distribution, and D-Distribution** examinations. It also links a “Distribution A, B and C” Needs To Know manual. [2] The current rule separately establishes eligibility requirements for Grade D-, C-, B-, and A-Distribution certification, and requires an examination designed for the certification class sought. [3] [4]

The supported boundary is therefore limited to the **North Carolina Grade A-, B-, and C-Distribution examination family**, with each future objective and candidate explicitly tagged to the applicable grade marker in DEQ’s official A/B/C Distribution Needs To Know manual. The official manual is not an outline for Grade D-Distribution. Because DEQ currently administers D-Distribution as a separate examination but the reviewed official scope document does not cover it, this dossier does **not** support the whole NC water-distribution route as a single examination package. [2]

This boundary does not establish candidate eligibility, certification, apprenticeship status, work-experience credit, school completion, trench-shoring training, Operator in Responsible Charge authority, or facility-specific operating authority. It does not replace Board-approved training or current governing requirements.

## Readiness determination

**Narrow.** DEQ’s live October 2026 examination page, the active Administrative Code text, and the Board’s current program report establish a genuine state-administered distribution credential family, separate grade examinations, and an official A/B/C content reference. [2] [3] [4] [6] The active Needs To Know manual offers substantial, grade-marked topical coverage for the A/B/C subset. [5]

The route is not **Suitable** as a state-wide water-distribution examination family because the evidence does not furnish an official Grade D-Distribution content outline, and the current program explicitly recognizes D-Distribution as a separately administered examination. The A/B/C manual cannot be assumed to represent that D examination. [2] The Narrow decision permits retention of this bounded source record and objective map only. Under the readiness contract, a Narrow dossier does not authorize generation of a candidate batch; only a Suitable dossier can do so.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The most current program-operation evidence is DEQ’s live examination page, which publishes 2026–2027 examination administration information and August 2026 results, and DEQ’s program report submitted in May 2025. [2] [6] The rule pages are hosted by the North Carolina Office of Administrative Hearings, which is the authoritative current-code source linked by DEQ’s rules page. [3] [4] [7]

The only official content outline found for this stream is dated May 21, 2019. That date is a material limitation, not a conflict. DEQ still links the document as the current “Distribution A, B and C” Needs To Know manual on its live examination page, while the 2024 annual report says the distribution curriculum is regularly reviewed and revised by the program and the NCAWWA Board of Education and Examiners. [2] [5] [6] The record therefore supports use of the live-linked A/B/C manual for a narrow research dossier, but it does not justify assuming that the 2019 document is a complete, current blueprint for D-Distribution or that it discloses exam weights, item counts, cognitive levels, reference-material permissions, calculator policy, exact scoring, or test-form design.

No conflict was found about the existence of distinct distribution grades. The limiting issue is coverage: the current rule and live examinations page recognize D-Distribution, whereas the official Needs To Know manual expressly covers A, B, and C only. [2] [3] [5]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

**Learner level.** Candidates pursuing the North Carolina Grade C-, B-, or A-Distribution examination, or the corresponding B/C apprentice examination pathway where allowed by the current rule. Future material must distinguish the grade and remain traceable to the outline’s grade markers. Grade C is not treated as interchangeable with Grade B or Grade A, and no A/B/C topic is presumed to be a Grade D topic. [3] [5]

**Supported narrow scope.** This dossier supports only conceptual, source-bound coverage of subjects identified in the A/B/C outline, including distribution-system planning and layout; basic water-use, hydraulics, and operator mathematics; pipe, appurtenances, water-main installation, testing, disinfection, and safety; services, valves, hydrants, storage, pumps, metering, operational practices, water-quality testing, cross-connection concepts, information management, and high-level safety, security, and emergency-response concepts. Grade tagging must follow the manual’s explicit C/B/A markers. [5]

**Excluded.** The following remain outside this dossier:

- Water treatment, well, surface-water, cross-connection-control, wastewater, and non-North Carolina credential content, except where a current official A/B/C Distribution outline expressly includes a bounded conceptual reference.
- Certification eligibility, experience verification, training-school approval, apprenticeship conversion, trench-shoring qualification, reciprocity, fees, renewal, scheduling, accommodation, or application procedures as learner content. These are administrative or legal matters and must be revalidated from contemporaneous official sources if ever needed. [2] [3] [4]
- Legal thresholds, compliance timelines, reporting obligations, or a restatement of regulatory text drawn from the 2019 outline without separate contemporaneous verification against current official law or rule.
- Site-specific system operation, engineering design, troubleshooting, emergency-response directions, excavation practice, live-system work, chemical-handling instructions, or safety-critical procedures.
- Claims about test weights, question counts, score prediction, examination security, permitted aids, or a complete examination blueprint.

## Decision and bank recommendation

**Decision: Narrow.** North Carolina has an active, state-administered water-distribution certification family with class-specific examination requirements. The record supports an A/B/C-only source map, but it lacks an official D-Distribution outline even though D-Distribution is actively examined. [2] [3] [4] [5] A route-wide water-distribution package would therefore overstate the official scope.

**Bank recommendation: No unique bank identity proposed.** Do not create, name, or populate a new North Carolina distribution bank from this dossier. The available official scope supports only a constrained A/B/C research record and does not support a single all-grade NC distribution bank. In addition, the readiness contract requires recovery and comparison of any earlier dedicated U.S. Class I water-distribution package before a potentially overlapping identity is proposed. If later evidence supplies a current official D outline and that comparison proves a state-specific gap, the program may reassess whether separate grade-specific or otherwise bounded identities are warranted.
