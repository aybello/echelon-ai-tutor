# Pennsylvania water treatment — state-specific readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `pa-water-treatment`
**Exact state / stream:** Pennsylvania (PA) / drinking-water treatment operator certification
**Matrix label:** state-specific
**Assessment date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Narrow**

## Decision

Pennsylvania has an active, state-administered drinking-water operator certification pathway with an identifiable two-part examination structure. It is not a single, generic “water-treatment” examination. For treatment operators, the relevant certification combination is the **Drinking Water Part I General Examination** plus the applicable **Part II Technology Specific Examination(s)**; treatment technologies are separately classified. The governing rule is current through August 8, 2026, and DEP’s active 2026 examination schedule and September 2026 fact sheet confirm that this structure remains in use. [1] [2] [5] [6]

The full `pa-water-treatment` route is therefore too broad for one candidate package. It spans 14 treatment-technology subclasses, with the applicable Part II examination determined by the system’s technologies. The evidence supports only a narrow future component dossier for **Part II Drinking Water Subclassification 1 — Conventional Filtration**. DEP’s current training-module index expressly maps Module 14 to that examination, and DEP’s current preparation materials still direct candidates to the handbook’s examination knowledge, skills, and abilities (KSAs). [3] [7] [8] [11] [12]

This classification authorizes no drafting now. It identifies a future **review-only** candidate-package boundary only. No questions, answers, explanations, item bank, database records, activation, publication, or learner-facing material are created by this dossier.

## Credential and exam boundary

### State credential

The credentialing authority is the **Pennsylvania State Board for Certification of Water and Wastewater Systems Operators**, administered through the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP), Bureau of Safe Drinking Water. A certificate is awarded only after the applicant completes the applicable examinations and meets the separate education, qualifying experience, application, criminal-history, and Board-approval requirements. Passing examinations without the education and experience requirements can result in operator-in-training status rather than independent certification. [2] [6]

### Exam family

For drinking-water treatment, Pennsylvania requires a **Part I General Examination** and the applicable **Part II Treatment Technology Specific Examination(s)** for Class A, B, or C certification. The Part I examination measures knowledge, skills, and abilities common to water systems, while Part II measures those needed for the applicable treatment technology. Class D treatment requirements are conditional under the rule: a Class D certificate does not require a subclassification unless the operator makes process-control decisions involving a specified treatment technology. [1] [2] [4]

### Narrow future component boundary

**Included examination component:** Pennsylvania Drinking Water **Part II, Subclassification 1 — Conventional Filtration**.

The current regulation identifies Subclassification 1 as Conventional Filtration, and DEP identifies it as a distinct water-treatment technology examination. The regulatory definition describes conventional filtration as a series of processes for substantial particulate removal consisting of coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, and filtration. This is an examination component, not a standalone operator credential; an applicant’s certificate remains dependent on the appropriate Part I result and the other statutory/regulatory certification conditions. [2] [3] [5] [8]

**Excluded from this narrow component:**

- Part I General Examination content, because it is a separate examination family and may overlap the existing dedicated U.S. Class I water package identified in the readiness contract.
- Every other Pennsylvania drinking-water treatment subclass: direct filtration; diatomaceous earth filtration; slow sand filtration; cartridge or bag filtration; membrane filtration; corrosion control and sequestering; chemical addition; ion exchange and greensand; aeration and activated carbon adsorption; gaseous chlorine disinfection; nongaseous chemical disinfection; ultraviolet disinfection; and ozonation. [3]
- Laboratory supervisor qualification, which is separately regulated and requires both an existing operator certificate and a separate Part II laboratory-supervisor examination. [4]
- Class E distribution/consecutive-system, Dc/Dn small-system, wastewater, collection-system, master-certificate, site-specific permit, design, and independent process-control topics. [2] [3] [5]
- Plant-specific operating directions, chemical-dose or process setpoints, emergency response, manufacturer instructions, legal advice, and local permit compliance determinations.

## Supported learner level and scope

The appropriate future learner is an adult Pennsylvania drinking-water operator applicant preparing for the **Conventional Filtration Subclass 1 Part II examination**, not an untrained general audience and not a learner seeking authority to operate a plant independently.

If a later gated review authorizes a candidate-only package, its high-level objectives may be limited to the following source-supported areas:

1. Identify the Pennsylvania certification boundary between the common drinking-water Part I examination and the technology-specific Conventional Filtration Part II examination. [1] [2] [5]
2. Recognize the role of conventional filtration in a drinking-water treatment train, including the high-level sequence of coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation/clarification, and filtration. [3] [11]
3. Relate process-monitoring, measurement, chemical-handling, calculation, equipment, maintenance, and safety concepts to the KSA categories identified for the Conventional Filtration examination, without substituting generic operations advice for a facility’s approved procedures. [12]
4. Distinguish high-level normal versus abnormal process-performance concepts within the scope of the official conventional-filtration training module, without stating site-specific corrective actions, dosages, or compliance conclusions. [11] [12]

These are objective boundaries only. They are not questions, answers, explanations, instructional procedures, or an exam blueprint.

## Bank recommendation

**Do not propose or create a unique question-bank identity at this time.** The evidence supports a distinct Pennsylvania Part II Conventional Filtration examination component, but it does not support a comprehensive `pa-water-treatment` bank because that label would conflate 14 separate treatment technologies. It also does not establish that a new Part I/general-water package would be non-duplicative of the existing `us-class1-water` package referenced in the readiness contract.

Before any future candidate-only work, the program must first recover and compare the immutable package, checksum, and authoritative inventory for the existing U.S. Class I water release. Only after that read-only overlap check and an independent source review may the team consider whether a narrow component label is warranted for the Pennsylvania **Part II Conventional Filtration** examination. This dossier itself neither recommends that bank identity nor authorizes its creation. [13]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The legal and program-boundary evidence is current: Pennsylvania Code reports changes effective through August 8, 2026; DEP publishes a 2026 examination schedule; and DEP’s operator-certification fact sheet is revised September 2026. These sources agree that the program uses a Part I / Part II structure and that treatment technology determines the applicable Part II examination. [1] [2] [5] [6]

DEP’s current preparation page says that examination KSAs are in Appendix A of the linked Operator Certification Program Handbook. That handbook is dated January 26, 2013. The current DEP training-module index maps Module 14 to Subclass 1, but the underlying Module 14 workbook is dated February 2014. These materials are official and currently linked or listed by DEP, but their internal publication dates make them unsuitable as sole evidence for a broad or unqualified statewide treatment bank. They support the narrow component only, subject to a fresh independent review at candidate stage. [7] [8] [11] [12]

There is no substantive conflict requiring a hold within the narrow boundary. Apparent differences are resolved by source hierarchy: the current regulation lists 14 treatment technology subclasses, while DEP’s definitions page also lists laboratory supervisor as Subclassification 15. The latter is a separate qualification with separate eligibility and examination conditions, not a fifteenth treatment technology for the proposed conventional-filtration component. [3] [4]

No current, separately dated public candidate handbook or item-weighted examination outline for the Conventional Filtration Part II examination was identified among the official materials retrieved. DEP directs candidates to the current preparation page, training modules, and staff for study material. That limitation is why the decision is **Narrow**, rather than Suitable for a full route. [7] [8] [9]
