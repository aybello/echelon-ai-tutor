# Indiana Water Distribution — State-Specific Readiness Dossier

**Decision:** **Narrow**

## Credential and examination boundary

Indiana’s state-administered drinking-water distribution route consists of three separate certification grades: **DSS** (Distribution System Small), **DSM** (Distribution System Medium), and **DSL** (Distribution System Large). IDEM’s current application form requires an applicant to select one grade per application, and the current programme page states that Indiana has three distribution-type examinations with corresponding certifications. [1] [2]

The current rule makes the grades operationally cumulative but distinct: DSS authorizes operation of a Class DSS system; DSM authorizes Class DSS and DSM systems; DSL authorizes Class DSS, DSM, and DSL systems. The class boundary is based on population and distribution-system components. [3]

The present dossier boundary is therefore **Indiana drinking-water distribution foundations that are expressly common across the current, official IDEM distribution manual and the current Rule 12 framework**. It is not an assertion that all manual chapters, practice material, or any particular objective appears on all three examinations. No current official grade-specific examination outline, content-weighting document, candidate handbook, or explicit examination-to-manual mapping was located in the official materials reviewed.

## Readiness determination

**Narrow.** Current official sources establish a real, administered Indiana credential family and three discrete distribution examination grades. IDEM also publishes a current, state-specific transmission-and-distribution manual with bounded foundational subject areas. That is sufficient to preserve a limited source-bound dossier for later review. [1] [2] [4]

It is **not** sufficient to represent a comprehensive DSS, DSM, or DSL examination blueprint, or to infer that a shared manual topic is tested at every grade. The rule defines credential eligibility and class authority, while the manual expressly describes itself as a basic overview and states that it cannot replace experience, institutional knowledge, or a system’s internal documentation and standard operating procedures. [3] [4]

## Bank recommendation

**No unique bank identity proposed.** Do not create or name a distinct Indiana DSS, DSM, or DSL question bank from the reviewed record. The current official materials do not publish a grade-specific content outline or a documented equivalence among the three examinations.

The only evidence-supported future scope is a narrowly controlled, review-only **Indiana water-distribution foundations** dossier. It must remain separate from the three grade-specific examinations and must not be described as a complete preparation product for DSS, DSM, or DSL. Under the readiness contract, this Narrow dossier does not authorize generation of a review-only candidate batch; only a Suitable dossier may do so.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

**Learner level.** Experienced or supervised drinking-water distribution personnel pursuing an Indiana distribution certification grade. State eligibility includes foundational numeracy, reading a linear scale, calculation of simple volumes, recordkeeping, English-language interpretation of service materials and work orders, and basic sanitation and science; the rule also requires qualifying experience under the stated framework. [3]

**Supported narrow scope.** If later elevated through evidence, high-level objectives may be limited to the following official, cross-grade foundations:

- Distinguish the Rule 12 distribution-system classifications DSS, DSM, and DSL from their published population and system-component criteria. [3]
- Identify the state programme’s foundational distribution domains named in the January 2025 IDEM manual: source water and regulation; sampling, monitoring, recordkeeping, and corrosion control; hydraulics and transmission; storage and system maintenance; system protection and instrumentation; and pumps. [4]
- Recognize that operator certification is grade-specific and requires examination and qualifications under the Indiana Rule 12 framework. [1] [3]

**Excluded.** No grade-specific test claim, score prediction, examination weighting, passing standard attribution to a particular grade, site-specific operating instruction, system standard operating procedure, emergency response procedure, treatment-plant content, wastewater content, facility-specific operator content, or inferred DSS/DSM/DSL equivalency is within this dossier. The official manual’s included practice exams and answer key are not a published IDEM examination blueprint and are excluded as a basis for an examination-content representation. [4]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The operative source for certification and grade authority is the **current** Indiana Administrative Code page for Rule 12. Its text records a Rule 12 readoption filed October 18, 2024. [3] The June 2026 IDEM application form and live IDEM certification and exam-administration pages corroborate that DSS, DSM, and DSL remain active, separately selected certification grades. [1] [2] [5]

The January 2025 IDEM manual is the newest official instructional resource found. It is useful for a limited foundational-topic record but is not an examination outline and explicitly limits its own operational role. [4] The exam-administration page remains live and states that computer-based examinations are offered, but its listed annual May 2026 paper session is already past as of retrieval; it is not used as evidence of a future paper-exam date. [5]

No conflict was found among the current rule, the 2026 form, the live programme pages, and the 2025 manual about the existence of DSS, DSM, and DSL. The material limitation is not a conflict: the current official record reviewed does not publish grade-specific content delineations or examination weights. This prevents a Suitable finding and blocks a grade-specific bank recommendation.
