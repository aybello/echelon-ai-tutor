## Credential and examination boundary

The credential route is the North Dakota Department of Environmental Quality (NDDEQ), Division of Municipal Facilities, **Water Distribution and Storage System Operator** certification route under North Dakota Administrative Code (NDAC) chapter 33.1-19-01. The regulated system boundary is the portion of a water supply system that obtains, stores, and conveys water from a treatment facility or other supply point to the consumer’s premises. It is not the water-treatment, wastewater-treatment, or wastewater-collection credential route. [1]

The current rule establishes five water-distribution and storage system classes and corresponding operator grades: **IA, I, II, III, and IV**. The distribution-system population bands are under 500; 500 to 1,500; 1,500 to 15,000; 15,000 to 50,000; and 50,000 or more, respectively. NDDEQ may classify a system based on special or complex features, volume and flow conditions, or a combination of conditions. A direct-responsible-charge operator must hold a certificate at least equal to the classification of the system. [1]

For this route, applicants must pass a certification examination for the system class for which they apply; the rule requires separate examinations for each facility or system classification. The current official examination page publishes two water-distribution study guides: one for Grades IA, I, and II, and one for Grades III and IV. [1] [2] The guides themselves state that their outlined subjects form the basis of the examination questions; the first describes primary skill and knowledge levels, and the second describes advanced skill and knowledge levels. [3] [4]

This dossier covers the **state-specific water-distribution examination family only**. It does not treat the route as one undifferentiated national water-operator exam. Any future package must retain the applicable North Dakota grade label and distinguish the IA–II primary-level material from the III–IV advanced-level material.

## Readiness decision

**Suitable.** NDDEQ’s current 2026 programme materials establish a specific North Dakota credential family, separate distribution certificates, distribution exam activity by grade, and current publication of grade-banded official study guides. The current rule fixes the credential, grade, system, and examination boundaries; the grade-banded guides provide a bounded official subject scope for a limited future candidate dossier. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

The decision is limited by the dated study guides. They are adequate as official, currently hosted examination-scope materials when read with the 2026 programme report, which says no exam changes were made during the reporting year. They are not sufficient by themselves to establish the current text of state or federal regulatory duties, numerical limits, fees, renewal rules, or facility-design requirements. Those claims must be verified against a current primary authority for each future review candidate. [1] [5]

## Learner-level scope

The learner is an eligible North Dakota water-distribution and storage operator candidate at the relevant certification grade. The learner-level split is:

- **Grades IA, I, and II:** primary skill and knowledge level for the lower three distribution grades. [3]
- **Grades III and IV:** advanced skill and knowledge level for the upper two distribution grades. [4]

The official grade-banded guides support only the following high-level objective areas for a future review-only candidate dossier:

1. Relate public-health protection and water-quality fundamentals to water-distribution responsibilities.
2. Recognize the function and basic operation of distribution-system infrastructure, including mains, valves, hydrants, service connections, cross-connection controls, pumping, storage, and metering.
3. Apply grade-appropriate distribution measurements and calculations involving flow, velocity, pressure, volume, storage, and related operational data.
4. Recognize the role of distribution-system disinfection, flushing, integrity protection, and routine maintenance within the official guide’s subject scope.
5. Recognize safety, sampling, records, reporting, and public-notification subject areas as identified in the official study-guide scope; any future regulatory claim must be independently mapped to a current authority. [3] [4]

### Exclusions

The following remain outside this route and must not be inferred from this dossier:

- Water-treatment processes, source-water treatment, wastewater treatment, and wastewater collection/transfer content.
- A generic U.S., multi-state, or WPI-equivalent bank; North Dakota’s grade labels, system boundary, and separate examinations are controlling.
- Credential-administration content such as eligibility adjudication, reciprocity, fees, scheduling, renewal, and portal use, except where a future candidate has a separately verified and current official source.
- The restricted small-system Water Distribution 1A alternative booklet. The 2026 annual report describes it as a one-time, open-book alternative for qualifying systems under 500 people and as restricted to the operator’s system; it is not evidence for the standard IA–IV examination family or a general bank scope. [5]
- Site-specific operating procedures, engineering design, emergency response, and safety-critical actions that are not directly supported by current authoritative North Dakota materials.
- Reproduction of official examination content, sample items, answer keys, or protected guide material.

## Source freshness and conflict controls

The assignment’s legacy rule URL points to a `.bak` file that reflects pre-amendment text. It must not be used as the controlling rule. The current North Dakota Legislative Branch rule is amended effective **April 1, 2026**, and controls the credential, classification, and examination-boundary statements in this dossier. [1]

The current NDDEQ training-and-examination page actively links the distribution study guides, but the guides display a **December 15, 2020** date. The same NDDEQ page warns that it may contain legacy documents published before April 24, 2026. The 2026 annual report reports that no changes were made to the examinations during the reporting year, which supports using the guides for high-level exam scope only. It does not make every dated regulatory reference, numerical value, or cited external publication in those guides current. [2] [3] [4] [5]

Where the current rule and older guide language could diverge, the current rule prevails. In particular, future review work must not use a guide’s dated references as proof of a present legal, compliance, construction, sampling, reporting, or public-notification requirement. The NDDEQ system-requirements document can corroborate the operating-system and certification context but does not replace the current codified rule or a current claim-level source. [1] [6]
