## Credential and examination boundary

The supported target is the **Pennsylvania Drinking Water Class E — Distribution and Consecutive Water Systems** credential route, bounded to the **stand-alone Distribution Examination**. Pennsylvania Code identifies Class E systems as distribution and consecutive water systems without treatment, and requires a drinking-water applicant to pass the Distribution Examination as one of the listed examination routes. [1] [3]

This is not the Part I General Examination, a Class A–D treatment credential, or a Part II treatment-technology examination. Current DEP scheduling material independently identifies Drinking Water Class E as a stand-alone exam and describes it as applicable to distribution and consecutive water systems; it may be held with subclasses 7–14, but those treatment subclasses are not within this route’s core examination boundary. [5] The current rule also treats water distribution as a separate stand-alone examination rather than a Part I/Part II combination. [2]

The intended learner level is an applicant preparing for the Pennsylvania Class E Distribution Examination and, after satisfying separate statutory and administrative requirements, eligibility for Class E certification. Passing the examination alone does not create certification: the Board’s requirements also include application, education, and qualifying experience. [4]

## Readiness decision

**Suitable.** Pennsylvania provides a current, official, credential-specific boundary: the Class E Distribution Examination is identified in the current Pennsylvania Code and on DEP’s 2026 exam schedule. [1] [2] [5] DEP’s current preparation page directs candidates to DEP training modules, and the current DEP module catalogue expressly associates **Module 8: Distribution Systems** with the **Class E** exam. [6] [7] The Module 8 student manual, revised January 2023, supplies an official, bounded topical structure adequate for a limited future review-only candidate package. [7]

This finding supports only controlled future candidate-dossier work. It does not authorize drafting, staging, importing, activating, publishing, pricing, or learner delivery of questions or other content.

## Bank recommendation

A unique future identity is evidence-supported: **`pa-class-e-water-distribution`**, limited to the Pennsylvania Drinking Water Class E stand-alone Distribution Examination.

The identity is justified because the Commonwealth defines Class E as the distribution/consecutive-system class and separately recognizes a Distribution Examination for drinking-water certification. [1] [3] It must remain distinct from: (1) Pennsylvania Class A–D drinking-water treatment examinations; (2) treatment technology subclasses 1–14; (3) Class Dc and Dn small-system examinations; and (4) any pre-existing shared U.S. distribution package until the required read-only recovery and overlap check is completed.

This is a recommendation only. No bank is created, populated, approved, or released by this dossier.

## High-level source-supported objectives

For a future limited candidate dossier, the objectives may be confined to the following high-level areas drawn from DEP’s Class E-associated module:

1. Identify the principal components and functions of a drinking-water distribution network, including transmission and distribution mains, storage, pumping, valves, meters, hydrants, and backflow prevention. [7]
2. Apply foundational distribution-system hydraulics concepts to interpret pressure, head, energy loss, customer demand, and the relationship of pressures and flows to system performance. [7]
3. Describe the purpose, types, operation, and maintenance considerations of distribution storage, including basic volume and water-level management concepts. [7]
4. Recognize distribution-system water-quality and monitoring considerations, including disinfection, monitoring, flushing, cross-connection control, main cleaning or lining, and management of dead ends and residence time. [7]

These are topic boundaries, not drafted learning content or item specifications.

## Scope exclusions and safeguards

The future candidate scope must exclude the following unless later added through current, objective-specific official evidence and independent review:

- Part I General Examination material and all Class A, B, C, and D drinking-water treatment examination scope.
- All Part II treatment-technology subclass examinations, including subclasses 7–14 even though DEP permits them to be held with Class E. [5]
- Class Dc and Dn small-system examination scope, wastewater collection or treatment scope, laboratory-supervisor scope, and credential-reciprocity determinations.
- Site-specific standard operating procedures, permit conditions, engineering design, worksite safety instructions, emergency-response actions, exact regulatory thresholds, reporting deadlines, and localized construction or maintenance requirements.
- Any claim that passing an exam itself certifies an operator; Board certification additionally depends on the applicable application, education, and experience requirements. [4]

Any future work must stay at the Class E distribution-examination level and must not generalize this Pennsylvania boundary to another jurisdiction.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The legal boundary is current: the official Pennsylvania Code pages used here state that the Code reflects changes effective through August 8, 2026. [1] [2] [3] [4] DEP’s current program pages include a 2026 exam schedule and link to the training-module catalogue. [5] [6] The substantive Class E learning reference, Module 8, is explicitly revised January 2023 but remains linked from that current DEP catalogue and is specifically mapped to the Class E exam. [7]

No material conflict was found among the current Code, DEP examination schedule, preparation page, and module catalogue. The schedule’s statement that Class E may be combined with subclasses 7–14 is not a conflict: it permits co-held treatment credentials but does not expand the stand-alone Class E distribution-exam boundary. [5] The docket should nevertheless treat treatment-subclass content as excluded from this distribution-only route.

A current public DEP candidate handbook or a separately dated Class E test-weighting blueprint was not identified in the official sources reviewed. DEP instead points to its training modules, while the official Module 8 topical outline provides sufficient bounded scope for a limited future candidate dossier. [6] [7] This limitation bars claims about current item counts, score thresholds, domain weights, or unlisted examinable content unless confirmed by a newer official source at the time of any future work.
