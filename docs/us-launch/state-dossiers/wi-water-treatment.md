# Wisconsin water-treatment route readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `wi-water-treatment`
**Existing matrix label:** state-specific
**State and stream:** Wisconsin — municipal waterworks, water treatment
**Research status:** official-source review only; no question content, question-bank data, activation, or learner-facing material

## Decision: Narrow

Wisconsin has a current, state-administered municipal waterworks certification program, but the assigned water-treatment stream is not one unified treatment examination. It is divided into separately examined subclasses. The official sources support one bounded future target: the **Municipal Waterworks — Surface Water, Subclass S examination**, which is an 80-question, closed-book examination. The resulting certificate is a Wisconsin waterworks operator certificate with the Subclass S designation. A candidate who passes the subclass examination qualifies at Grade T; Grade 1 additionally requires one year of satisfactory subclass-specific experience. [1] [2] [3]

This is therefore **Narrow**, not Suitable for the whole assigned stream. The official program separately identifies groundwater, iron removal, lime softening, VOC removal, and zeolite softening subclasses, each with a distinct examination and study guide. It would be inaccurate to represent a Surface Water outline as coverage of all Wisconsin water-treatment subclasses. [1] [3]

## Credential and examination boundary

The supported future review boundary is exactly:

> **Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources Municipal Waterworks Operator Certification — Surface Water, Subclass S; Grade T examination boundary.**

The credential is not a generic “Wisconsin water treatment operator” credential. Wisconsin Administrative Code NR 114 establishes seven waterworks operator subclasses and two grades. Subclass S applies to waterworks using a surface-water source; Grade T requires passing the given subclass examination, while Grade 1 requires completion of Grade T requirements plus one year of satisfactory experience in that subclass. The current rule sets 75 percent as the passing score for each examination. [3]

The DNR’s current 2026 examination page identifies the Municipal Waterworks — Surface Water examination as multiple-choice, 80 questions, with municipal waterworks examinations administered closed book. The current 2026 application separately lists Surface Water among the selectable municipal waterworks exams. [2] [4]

**Boundary exclusions:** This dossier does not cover the Distribution subclass; Groundwater; Iron Removal; Lime Softening; VOC Removal; Zeolite Softening; small-water-system certification; wastewater certification; reciprocity; continuing-education renewal; or the Grade 1 experience determination. It also does not cover system-specific design, operating instructions, emergency actions, chemical-handling procedures, legal reporting duties, or current numeric regulatory thresholds.

## Learner level and scope

The appropriate learner is an applicant preparing for the Wisconsin **Grade T Surface Water, Subclass S** knowledge examination. The DNR states that a person seeking certification in the subclasses must have a high school diploma or GED, subject to the legacy experience alternative stated in the rule. Passing the exam alone does not establish Grade 1 eligibility or authorize an applicant to act as operator-in-charge. [3] [4]

The currently linked DNR Surface Water Study Guide says that its objectives are covered on the examination and organizes the scope into principles, structure and function, operation and maintenance, monitoring and troubleshooting, safety, and calculations. Its current DNR hosting page identifies it as the Subclass S guide. [1] [5]

### High-level future objectives

If a later review-only package is approved at this narrow boundary, its objectives should remain at the following high level:

- Explain foundational surface-water source and treatment-process concepts, including treatment terminology and water-quality considerations.
- Identify the functions of common treatment-plant equipment, controls, chemical-feed equipment, disinfection equipment, and filtration components.
- Describe source-supported operational and maintenance concepts for surface-water treatment processes.
- Interpret source-supported monitoring and troubleshooting concepts relevant to treatment performance.
- Apply source-supported safety concepts and basic water-treatment calculations.

These are content domains only. They are not questions, answer keys, operating procedures, or a substitute for supervised facility training.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The governing rule is presented by the Wisconsin Legislature as the current administrative code and states that the code is updated on the first day of each month. The DNR program, exam schedule, and application are current 2026 materials, and they align on the existence of separately administered Municipal Waterworks subclass examinations. [2] [3] [4]

The only material freshness limitation is the Subclass S guide itself: it is dated February 2016 and includes objectives that refer to then-current drinking-water rules and numeric requirements. The DNR’s current waterworks page still identifies this guide as the Surface Water study guide and says its study-guide objectives are covered on the exams, so it is sufficient as an official exam-scope source. [1] [5] It is not sufficient by itself for a future candidate claim about current numeric limits, sampling frequency, reporting, public notification, chemical handling, or any changed federal or Wisconsin drinking-water requirement. Each such claim would need a separate current official Wisconsin source at the individual-candidate review stage.

No conflict was found between the current DNR pages, the 2026 application, and NR 114 about the existence and separate identity of the Surface Water examination. The sources do not publish item weights or a more recent Subclass S outline, so any future content must not infer a domain weighting or test-form composition beyond the official 80-question examination length.
