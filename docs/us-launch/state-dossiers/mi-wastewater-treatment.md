# Michigan wastewater-treatment operator certification readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `mi-wastewater-treatment`
**State / stream:** Michigan (MI) — wastewater-treatment
**Existing matrix label:** state-specific
**Research retrieval date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Narrow**

## Decision and credential / examination boundary

Michigan Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) operates a current **Municipal Wastewater Treatment Plant Operator** certification program under Part 41 and the current Sewerage Systems Administrative Rules. The program has distinct treatment-facility certifications, not a single undifferentiated statewide wastewater-treatment examination. The governing rule requires a written examination and separately prepared examinations for each class. [1] [2]

The only boundary supported for a limited future candidate dossier in this route is the common, official **Municipal Wastewater Treatment Operator Class C and Class D certification examination** scope. EGLE publishes one current, combined Class C-and-D study guide for this examination family, revised April 2026. It identifies a treatment facility serving or designed to serve fewer than 10,000 people and provides the current C/D examination content scope. [3]

This is a **shared examination-scope boundary, not a claim that Class C and Class D are the same credential.** Under the current rule, Class C is assigned to facilities serving or designed to serve 2,000 to fewer than 10,000 people and Class D to facilities serving fewer than 2,000 people; EGLE may assign a higher class based on facility complexity, waste characteristics, or receiving-water conditions. [1] The rule also requires separate examinations for each class. [1]

The route is therefore Narrow rather than Suitable for the full stream: Michigan has separate Class A, B, C, D, L1, L2, and SC municipal treatment certifications, with different eligibility and, in several cases, distinct current study guides. [2] A general Michigan wastewater-treatment bank would blur those boundaries.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The supported audience is an experienced municipal wastewater-treatment operator who is eligible, or preparing to become eligible, for Michigan Class C or Class D examination. Class D eligibility requires a high-school education or equivalent and one year of acceptable operation experience in a Class D or higher treatment facility. Class C eligibility requires the same education, a Class D certificate, and two years of acceptable experience in a Class D or higher treatment facility. [1] EGLE’s January 2026 Board policy further describes the operational-experience expectations for these classes. [5]

The proposed candidate dossier scope is limited to high-level, common Class C/D treatment-operator knowledge stated in the current EGLE guide:

- treatment-process operation, monitoring, control, and troubleshooting across preliminary/primary and secondary/advanced treatment;
- foundational safety, regulatory-awareness, supervisory, and emergency-program themes within the C/D guide;
- laboratory analysis, sampling, preservation, water-quality interpretation, and related terminology;
- maintenance fundamentals for pumps, valves, motors, electrical components, and preventive-maintenance programs; and
- treatment-operation calculations expressly identified by the C/D guide. [4]

The dossier must exclude the following:

- Class A and Class B credentials, their management-heavy and essay-style examination content, and their higher education and supervisory prerequisites;
- Class L1 and L2 lagoon treatment credentials, including lagoon-specific scope;
- Class SC minimal-operation facility credentials;
- collection-system Classes C1–C4 and retention-treatment-basin Class RTB certifications;
- industrial wastewater, drinking water, stormwater, reciprocity, renewal, and site-specific restricted credentials;
- facility-specific operating instructions, permit limits, process design, construction, engineering decisions, or emergency response procedures;
- legal-duty, permit-condition, or reporting-detail candidates unless a future dossier adds a current primary official source that supports the exact proposition; and
- any statement that a Class C candidate is eligible for, or may act under, a Class D credential solely because the study guide is shared.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The controlling rule is current and explicitly effective April 29, 2026. [1] The Class C/D study guide is revised April 2026, and the detailed Board policy is revised January 2026. [4] [5] EGLE’s live program and examination pages were retrieved on October 29, 2026 and direct candidates to these study materials. [2] [3]

No material conflict prevents the narrow decision. The sources distinguish separate Class C and Class D credentials and require separate class examinations, while EGLE’s April 2026 guide gives them a shared study scope. [1] [4] This is resolved by treating the guide as evidence for only common foundational candidate material, not for a merged credential or claims about class-specific eligibility. The guide also says its subject areas may include, but are not limited to, those listed and that exam style and question counts may change without notice. [4] It is sufficient for a limited dossier but not for asserted fixed weighting, exhaustive specifications, or a statewide all-class bank.

EGLE continues to link older Class A, B, and SC guides, but those documents are outside the approved C/D boundary and do not reduce the freshness of the April 2026 C/D guide or the 2026 rule. [3]

## Bank recommendation and release boundary

A unique future identity is evidence-supported only for a **Michigan Municipal Wastewater Treatment — Class C/D common-foundations** candidate dossier, for example: `mi-municipal-wastewater-treatment-class-c-d`. It must contain only material traceable to the current shared C/D guide and the governing Michigan rule, with class-specific eligibility and legal claims excluded unless independently sourced and reviewed.

This dossier does **not** authorize a general Michigan wastewater-treatment bank, any all-class bank, candidate authoring beyond the stated narrow boundary, or any credential representation. It contains no questions, options, explanations, code, bank data, or release.

**Release boundary:** candidate-only and review-only in a future controlled workflow; no learner access, publication, activation, database import, entitlement, pricing, checkout, or other release action.
