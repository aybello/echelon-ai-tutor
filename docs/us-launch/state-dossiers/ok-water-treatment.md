# Oklahoma water-treatment operator route — readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `ok-water-treatment`
**State / stream:** Oklahoma (OK) / water treatment
**Existing matrix label:** state-specific
**Research retrieval date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Defer**

## Exact credential and examination boundary

Oklahoma does not publish a separately named **water-treatment operator** credential or a separately identified water-treatment examination. The current state route that contains water-treatment work is the Oklahoma Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) **Waterworks/Wastewater Works Operator** certification, available at Classes A, B, C, and D. A certified waterworks operator may perform work relating to water-treatment plants, groundwater systems, storage, distribution systems, purchased-water systems, and non-transient non-community systems. The water-treatment stream is therefore only one component of the broader **waterworks** operator role; it is not an independently bounded examination product. [1]

The current rule requires an applicant to pass the appropriate DEQ validated examination for the classification sought. A validated examination is an examination independently reviewed by subject-matter experts, based on a job analysis, and related to the classification of the system or facility. The rule also requires applications to satisfy the applicable certification requirements before examination. Examination passage is thus a certification requirement, not by itself a complete certification or authority to operate a facility. [1]

The only detailed DEQ material located that connects water-system subject areas to operator examinations is the *Water System Operations State of Oklahoma Certification Study Guide*. It describes a cumulative Class D-to-A water-system examination framework and 12 competency areas, including basic water treatment, coagulation/flocculation/sedimentation, filtration, disinfection, supplemental treatment, and groundwater systems. It also includes distribution, maintenance, safety, general management, and other water-system content. It is not a water-treatment-only outline, does not delimit a current Class A, B, C, or D blueprint, and expressly says that its study guidelines do not address every item that an operator may need to know on an examination or in work. [4]

Accordingly, no current official source establishes a specific **Oklahoma water-treatment-only credential, examination, level, blueprint, weighting, or candidate handbook**. The only defensible present credential boundary is the broad Oklahoma **Waterworks/Wastewater Works Operator Class A–D validated-examination family**, which is too broad to treat as a water-treatment examination route and too undifferentiated to support a future state-specific question-bank candidate package. [1] [4]

## Decision and bank recommendation

**Decision: Defer.** The Oklahoma program itself is active and its legal certification/examination framework is current. However, the official evidence does not supply a current, water-treatment-specific examination boundary. The linked water-system guide was issued in May 2008, even though DEQ still links it from a program-information page last modified in February 2026. Its integrated, all-level, waterworks scope and stated incompleteness mean it cannot safely serve as a current examination blueprint. [2] [3] [4]

**Bank recommendation: none.** Do not propose a unique Oklahoma water-treatment bank, a combined Oklahoma waterworks bank, or a Class A–D bank. A proposed identity such as `ok-water-treatment-*` would falsely suggest that Oklahoma offers a separately delimited treatment examination. A waterworks Class D candidate scope could be reconsidered only if DEQ publishes or directly designates a current, class-specific examination outline, candidate handbook, task list, content-domain specification, or other current study material that confirms both the selected level and the content boundary.

Because the decision is Defer, this dossier intentionally provides **no high-level objectives**. Creating objectives from the dated guide would infer that its topics, priorities, and older regulatory references remain current tested content.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The potential learner is not a general water-treatment learner. Any future candidate would need to be an applicant for one explicitly identified Oklahoma DEQ **Waterworks/Wastewater Works Operator** class and examination. The presently available sources do not identify which one class maps to this matrix’s water-treatment stream, nor do they establish a treatment-only examination. [1] [4]

The following are excluded from this route unless a current official Oklahoma source expressly identifies them for the selected examination level:

- a combined Class A–D question bank, cross-level progression content, or an assumed entry-level Class D package;
- wastewater-operator, laboratory-operator, distribution-and-collection operator, distribution-and-collection technician, temporary, or special non-operational certifications;
- content that is exclusively distribution, storage, groundwater, laboratory, wastewater, collection, engineering-design, construction, or renewal-training material;
- examination item counts, timing, delivery method, fees, application steps, pass-rate statements beyond the current rule, eligibility thresholds, or retake procedures taken from the 2008 guide;
- detailed compliance, monitoring, reporting, treatment-process, chemical, formula, safety, emergency, site-operation, or operator-action claims unless a current official source supports the exact claim and the selected examination level; and
- non-Oklahoma rules, commercial preparation materials, blogs, third-party practice tests, and any learner-visible material.

The current rule does distinguish waterworks operating authority from laboratory and distribution/collection credentials, but that credential distinction is not a current water-treatment examination outline. It must not be used to infer a test domain, item weighting, operational procedure, or competency depth. [1]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The legal certification and examination framework is current. DEQ’s Chapter 710 PDF states that the relevant provisions were amended effective September 15, 2025. DEQ’s current rules page, last modified October 13, 2026, links Chapter 710 but warns that DEQ’s downloadable rule copies are a convenience and that the Oklahoma Administrative Code maintained by the Secretary of State is controlling if there is a discrepancy. The official code portal is therefore the legal authority; the DEQ chapter copy is used here as DEQ’s current published text. [1] [5]

DEQ’s Operator Certification overview was last modified May 27, 2026, and its Program Information page was last modified February 17, 2026. Together, those current program pages show that DEQ continues to administer the operator-certification program, provide testing and certification, direct applicants to the operator portal, and intentionally retain a link to the water-system study guide. They do not state that the guide was revised, adopted as a current exam blueprint, or limited to any particular waterworks class. [2] [3]

The study guide’s own issue statement says it was produced in May 2008. Its printed content includes superseded operational details, including a $40 examination fee, while the current Chapter 710 rule lists a $62 certification-application fee subject to the rule’s adjustment provision. The guide also refers to historical program channels and older regulatory context. These discrepancies are material freshness defects. The guide remains an official DEQ-hosted legacy study resource and supports the finding that DEQ once organized water-system exam preparation around broad subject areas, but it is not used to establish current legal requirements, current exam administration, or a current blueprint. [1] [3] [4]

No source conflict changes the decision. The controlling problem is the absence of a current, class-specific and water-treatment-specific content boundary. The currently linked legacy guide cannot resolve that absence.
