**Retrieval date:** 2026-10-29
**Decision:** **Hold**

## Credential and examination boundary

Kentucky’s route is not one undifferentiated “water treatment” credential or one demonstrated single exam. It is the Kentucky Energy and Environment Cabinet (EEC), Certification and Licensing Branch water-treatment operator certification framework. The framework contains Limited, Class IA-D, Class IB-D, Class IIA, Class IIB-D, Class IIIA, Class IIIB, Class IVA, and Class IVB treatment certifications. The class establishes the plant-size authority, while the treatment subclass differentiates Subclass A from Subclass B. [1] [2]

Subclass A covers surface water, groundwater under the direct influence of surface water, and groundwater using gravity filtration. Subclass B covers groundwater not under the direct influence of surface water that either uses filtration other than gravity filtration or no filtration. Kentucky’s current 2026 programme separates **Surface Water Treatment** training/exams from **Groundwater Treatment**; it does not establish a generic, all-treatment examination boundary. [2] [6]

For certification, an applicant must meet the applicable education and experience requirements and earn at least 70 percent on a Cabinet-administered examination. The Cabinet issues the certificate for the classification in which competency was demonstrated. [1] [3] Kentucky’s drinking-water fact sheet further describes the treatment pathways as the A-series water-treatment levels and the B-series groundwater-treatment levels. [4]

**Boundary that is sufficiently evidenced:** Kentucky Cabinet water-treatment operator certification, divided at minimum by Subclass A/surface-water treatment and Subclass B/groundwater treatment, with distinct certificate classes.
**Boundary that is not sufficiently evidenced for authoring:** a current, stable, class-specific official examination blueprint or candidate handbook that identifies the testable domains, weighting, and separation of Class I/II versus Class III/IV examinations.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The rule pages supplied by the Kentucky General Assembly were generated on August 28, 2026 and show the current rule text available at retrieval. However, each controlling treatment rule directly relevant here—401 KAR 8:030, 401 KAR 11:040, and 401 KAR 11:050—shows a **7-Year Expiration of November 1, 2026**. That date is three days after this review. The rule pages also show that amendments are due April 21, 2028, but the reviewed materials do not establish the operative post-expiration text or a replacement examination framework. [1] [2] [3]

EEC’s 2026 schedule and current programme pages show that treatment examinations remain active and that Surface Water Treatment and Groundwater Treatment remain distinct programme categories. [5] [6] This supports the existence of a live route, but not a single generic water-treatment bank.

The regulator currently hosts both treatment manuals, and the current test-preparation page directs candidates to them. Yet the Surface Water manual is revised April 2018 and the Groundwater manual is revised December 2014. Both manuals include a disclaimer that the governing rule or statute prevails if it conflicts with the manual. [5] [7] [8] The reviewed current EEC pages do not provide a newer official candidate handbook, test blueprint, content-weighting document, or other level-specific examination outline. The manuals can therefore describe legacy preparatory coverage, but cannot safely establish a stable future exam-specification boundary by themselves.

There is no contradiction between the live 2026 schedule and the treatment manuals. The material defect is freshness and specificity: the course manuals are dated, while the controlling rules are at their stated expiration point and do not themselves describe the examination content.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

No learner-level candidate scope is authorized now. A future reassessment must first select one specific Kentucky certificate/exam family, at least distinguishing A-series/surface-water treatment from B-series/groundwater treatment, and then identify the applicable class or classes from a current official source.

Excluded from this route dossier are water distribution, wastewater, bottled-water certification, Limited certification for schools or semipublic supplies, reciprocity, renewal, and facility-specific operating instructions. Also excluded are unverified assumptions that all Kentucky water-treatment classes use one exam, that the historic manuals are current blueprints, or that an exam-only event establishes the content of every level’s examination. No safety-critical operating procedure, regulatory threshold, or legacy manual claim may be converted into a candidate item while the controlling rule status and current test boundary are unresolved.

Because the decision is **Hold**, no high-level candidate objectives are proposed.

## Readiness decision

**Decision: Hold.** Kentucky has a current official programme and a clearly state-specific treatment certification framework, but it does not presently meet the readiness threshold for a future review-only candidate package. The route is split into A- and B-subclass treatment pathways and multiple certificate classes, while no current official source reviewed provides a class-specific examination outline or candidate handbook. More importantly, the controlling rules displayed by the state carry an imminent November 1, 2026 expiration date. This creates a legal and source-freshness defect that cannot be resolved by relying on the dated training manuals. [1] [2] [3] [5] [6]

**Bank recommendation:** **No unique bank proposed.** Do not create a Kentucky generic water-treatment bank, an A-series/surface-water bank, or a B-series/groundwater bank from this dossier. Reconsider only after Kentucky publishes or confirms the operative post-November 1, 2026 certification rules and supplies a current official, class-specific examination scope or candidate guide. A future dossier may then assess a narrowly named surface-water/A-series or groundwater/B-series credential family separately.
