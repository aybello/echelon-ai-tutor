## Credential and examination boundary

The regulated credential is the **New Hampshire Wastewater Treatment Plant Operator Certification** administered by the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services (NHDES). NHDES has four operator grades, I through IV, that correspond to plant classifications. Each grade has an operator-in-training (OIT) status. The current rule requires a written examination for each grade and a passing score of at least 70 percent. [1]

This dossier is limited to the **Grade I written certification examination and the associated Grade I-OIT pathway**. Grade I is the entry examination boundary: no operating experience is required to take the Grade I exam, while full Grade I certification requires one year of operating experience and a high-school diploma or high-school-equivalency credential. An applicant who passes the Grade I written examination but lacks the experience requirement can hold the corresponding OIT status subject to the rule’s conditions. [1]

NHDES’s current FAQ states that Grade 1 and Grade 1-OIT written examinations consist of multiple-choice and matching content plus math word problems; it identifies 70 points for multiple-choice and matching and 30 points for six of eight math word problems. [3] The FAQ also links NHDES’s formula sheet as the agency-developed resource for common formulas needed for the wastewater operator exam. [3] [4]

This is not a boundary for Grade II, III, or IV; for plant-classification calculations; for collection-system certification; or for water-treatment or water-distribution certification.

## Readiness finding

**Narrow.** New Hampshire has a current, state-administered wastewater treatment operator credential and a specific Grade I written-exam boundary. The current rule and official FAQ establish that Grade I/I-OIT candidates take a written examination with a defined math component. The official formula sheet provides a bounded calculation domain that is plausibly relevant to that component, including fundamental unit conversions, flow/volume relationships, daily loading, percent removal, detention time, tank-volume, chemical-dose, and dosage relationships. [1] [3] [4]

The official materials do **not** provide a current Grade I examination blueprint, candidate handbook, topic weighting, or grade-mapped list of non-math learning objectives. The formula sheet is marked 2022 and is not mapped by grade or question type. It therefore cannot support an inference that the full list of formulas, advanced process formulas, or a broader treatment-operations curriculum belongs on the current Grade I examination. The evidence supports only a limited, calculation-focused future scope; it does not support a complete New Hampshire wastewater treatment exam-preparation bank. [4]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The future bounded audience would be an applicant for the **New Hampshire Grade I written wastewater-treatment operator examination**, including a Grade I-OIT applicant. The only source-supported topic band is foundational wastewater-operator calculations explicitly represented in the current official FAQ-linked formula sheet. Any later candidate content would need each objective and claim mapped directly to that formula sheet and independently reviewed against the then-current NHDES materials.

The following are excluded:

- Grade II, III, and IV examination content, advanced plant-operation judgment, and grade-specific supervisory responsibilities.
- Any formula or process not explicitly supported by the official formula sheet.
- Claims that an individual formula is tested at Grade I, appears in a particular number of questions, or carries a particular weight. NHDES does not publish that mapping in the reviewed sources.
- Site-specific operating instructions, permit limits, laboratory methods, emergency-response procedures, chemical handling procedures, process-control setpoints, equipment troubleshooting, and plant-design content.
- The separate New Hampshire wastewater-collection route, drinking-water credentials, reciprocity-only pathways, continuing-education course material, and post-certification ethics/interview material.
- Any treatment-process content that depends on a facility’s permit, equipment, influent, receiving water, or operating manual.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The controlling source is NHDES’s adopted Env-Wq 304 rule, effective August 25, 2026. It supersedes older rule versions for this dossier. [1]

The NHDES program page and FAQ are live, official pages retrieved on October 29, 2026. Neither page displays a publication or revision date. They are useful for current administration and exam format, but the adopted rule controls if an inconsistency arises. [2] [3]

The formula sheet is an official NHDES document but bears a **2022** date. The current FAQ continues to link it for formulas needed for the wastewater operator exam, which supports a narrow calculation-domain finding. It is not a current blueprint and cannot establish grade-specific coverage or question weighting. [3] [4]

There is an administrative metadata inconsistency: the active online application page identifies itself as Version 2.22 and September 2023, whereas the 2026 adopted rule refers to the application form as August 2026. This does not alter the credential boundary because the rule governs; it is a material freshness flag for any later application-process content. No application-process objective should be authored without reconfirming the live form and rule. [1] [5]

No current official candidate handbook or official Grade I examination outline/blueprint was located in the reviewed NHDES program, FAQ, rule repository, current rule, formula-sheet, and official certification-committee material. The absence of that material is the limiting evidence for the decision.

## High-level objectives for the bounded future scope

These are **objective labels only**, not questions or candidate content. They are allowed solely as a future source-mapping frame for the Narrow calculation domain.

1. Apply the official wastewater formula sheet’s fundamental unit conversions and flow/volume relationships.
2. Calculate wastewater daily loading and percent removal using the relationships represented on the official formula sheet.
3. Calculate detention time and simple rectangular or circular tank volume using the official formula sheet.
4. Apply the official formula-sheet relationships for chlorine dose and chemical dosage.
5. Select the formula-sheet relationship that corresponds to a stated foundational wastewater calculation task, without implying Grade I weighting or coverage beyond the official source.

## Bank recommendation

**Do not propose a unique bank identity.** The evidence supports only a narrow Grade I/I-OIT calculation subset, not a full state-specific examination family or a complete, grade-mapped bank. A separate bank would risk representing unsupported content as New Hampshire Grade I examination scope.

If NHDES later publishes a current Grade I blueprint, candidate handbook, or grade-specific exam outline, reassess whether that evidence supports a complete Grade I bank or whether the formula-supported calculation subset can be incorporated into an established, non-duplicative verified exam family. Until then, retain this as a dossier only.
