## Decision

**Suitable** for a limited, future **review-only** candidate package. Utah has a current state-administered Drinking Water Operator Certification route with a distinct **Distribution** discipline, five defined distribution grades, an official rule defining the credential and examination framework, and a current Utah Division of Drinking Water (DDW) page that publishes the 100-question, grade-specific examination category allocations. The evidence supports a bounded future candidate dossier, not a claim of complete preparation coverage for every grade. [1] [2] [3]

## Credential and examination boundary

The credential route is the **Utah Drinking Water Operator Certification — Water Distribution Operator** discipline. Utah Rule R309-300 defines a distribution system as spring or well sources, distribution pipelines, appurtenances, and facilities that carry potable water to consumers through a public water supply; groundwater systems that chlorinate are within this discipline. The rule distinguishes Distribution from Treatment and specifies five distribution steps: **Small System (SS), Distribution Grade I (D1), Distribution Grade II (D2), Distribution Grade III (D3), and Distribution Grade IV (D4)**. [1]

This route is not a single generic water-operator examination. A candidate selects a distribution grade; DDW states that the distribution exams are 100 multiple-choice questions, proctored and timed for three hours, with a 70% minimum passing score. The official rule requires a 70% passing grade, identifies five water-distribution examination grades, and provides for restricted certification when the examination is passed before the required experience is met. Passing an examination is therefore the knowledge boundary; unrestricted certification and Direct Responsible Charge status also depend on the rule’s experience and qualification requirements. [1] [2] [3]

The official DDW study page supplies the current high-level exam outline by category and grade. The category allocations below total 100 questions for each distribution grade:

- **SS:** Math 12; Operation and Maintenance 24; Rules 24; Pumps 24; Chemical Feed 12; Safety and Security 16.
- **D1:** Math 15; Operation and Maintenance 22; Rules 19; Pumps 13; Chemical Feed 15; Safety and Security 16.
- **D2:** Math 15; Operation and Maintenance 22; Rules 19; Pumps 13; Chemical Feed 15; Safety and Security 16.
- **D3:** Math 13; Operation and Maintenance 20; Rules 20; Pumps 15; Chemical Feed 15; Safety and Security 17.
- **D4:** Math 13; Operation and Maintenance 22; Rules 20; Pumps 13; Chemical Feed 15; Safety and Security 17. [2]

DDW also maps its official **Basic Distribution** study guide to SS, D1, and D2 and its **Advanced Distribution** study guide to D3 and D4. Those titles and links corroborate the basic/advanced split, but this dossier does not treat an inaccessible linked document’s unexamined contents as evidence for granular objectives. [2]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The supported learner is an adult candidate preparing for a Utah DDW-administered **Water Distribution Operator** examination at one selected grade: SS, D1, D2, D3, or D4. The suitable boundary is limited to the current, published distribution exam categories and the grade-specific question allocation. It does not authorize a combined treatment-and-distribution bank or a generic national distribution credential claim. [1] [2]

Future work under this route must exclude the following unless a later, current official source independently supports the exact objective and selected grade:

- Water Treatment Operator examinations and wastewater operator credentials.
- Direct Responsible Charge registration, unrestricted-status approval, reciprocity decisions, employment or experience determinations, and renewal administration. These are related programme processes, not the distribution exam knowledge boundary. [1] [3]
- Site-specific operating instructions, engineering design, emergency response plans, and system classification determinations.
- Any state-law, monitoring, reporting, or compliance claim not tied to the current Utah rule or another current Utah official source.
- Claims that the candidate package is exhaustive exam preparation, predicts a passing result, or substitutes for DDW’s current study materials.

## High-level objectives for a future review-only candidate dossier

These are planning objectives only. They are intentionally limited to DDW’s published category outline and the rule’s broad examination areas; they are not questions, answer keys, or instructional explanations.

1. Address the published distribution-exam math category at the selected grade.
2. Address distribution-system operation and maintenance within the selected grade’s published allocation.
3. Address pump-related knowledge within the selected grade’s published allocation.
4. Address chemical-feed knowledge within the selected grade’s published allocation.
5. Address Utah distribution rules, applicable requirements, water-quality standards, and recordkeeping only where an exact current official source supports the candidate claim.
6. Address safety and security concepts within the selected grade’s published allocation.

The rule’s examination framework also names general water-supply knowledge, distribution control processes, operation, maintenance, emergency procedures, recordkeeping, laws and requirements, and water-quality standards. Its list is expressly non-exhaustive; it should be used to constrain and corroborate a candidate claim, not to infer undocumented detailed test content. [1]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The controlling rule page identifies R309-300 as a **Current Rule**, with an effective date of November 9, 2017 and a February 10, 2025 notice of continuation. The programme web pages were retrieved from DDW on 2026-10-29 and directly link to DDW-hosted 2025 study resources. The rule’s older original effective date is therefore not treated as a superseded-rule defect, but the source set must be rechecked immediately before any candidate package is assembled. [1] [2] [3]

No material conflict was found. The rule’s five distribution grades match DDW’s SS plus D1–D4 presentation. The rule’s broad, non-exhaustive examination areas and DDW’s six test categories are complementary: the rule establishes the certification and examination framework, while DDW supplies the current grade-specific category weights. [1] [2]

A material limitation remains: the public outline is category-level rather than a detailed, published task-by-task blueprint. Consequently, future candidates may use only objectives with an exact traceable current official source and must not represent the package as complete coverage of Utah’s examinations.
