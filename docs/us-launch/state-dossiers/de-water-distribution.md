# Delaware water-distribution operator certification route

**Route ID:** `de-water-distribution`
**Matrix label:** State-specific
**State / stream:** Delaware (DE) / water distribution
**Research status:** Official-source readiness review only
**Retrieval date:** 2026-10-29

## Decision: Hold

Delaware has a real, separate **Distribution License** and a corresponding **distribution license written examination**. The current regulation therefore supports a credential boundary. It does not, however, publish a candidate handbook, examination blueprint, content outline, or official programme curriculum that identifies what the distribution examination tests. The state’s current public materials also conflict on the pressure threshold used to differentiate the distribution scope: the operative administrative code uses **20 psi**, while the state’s registration and application forms use **25 psi**. [1] [2] [3]

This is a **Hold**, not a future authoring authorization. The available official evidence is sufficient to identify a credential, but insufficient and internally inconsistent for source-bound learning objectives or a defensible candidate package. No questions, options, explanations, bank data, release, or learner content are authorized by this dossier.

## Credential and examination boundary

The bounded credential is the **Delaware Distribution License** under 16 DE Admin. Code 4463 §6.2.4. It is available to an operator responsible for oversight of a distribution system who has no treatment-plant operating responsibilities. Its prerequisites include either a high-school diploma or equivalent plus one year of acceptable operating experience, or three years of acceptable operating experience, and successful completion of the **distribution license written examination**. [1]

For this route, “distribution system” is limited to the network that delivers finished water: pipes, valves, hydrants, service lines, meters, storage tanks, finished-water reservoirs, and pumping stations. [1] The regulation identifies a distribution classification for operators whose responsibility is distribution oversight without treatment-plant responsibilities. It separately requires a base-level license and, at minimum, a distribution endorsement for systems capable of producing more than 500 gpm at 20 psi. [1]

The credential boundary must **not** be broadened to the base-level water-supply examination, operator-in-training credential, treatment endorsements, limited license, reciprocal license, approved sampler/tester endorsement, or wastewater operator credentials. The regulation establishes these as separate routes or elements. [1]

## Source freshness and conflict assessment

The legal source is the currently hosted Delaware Administrative Code page and records its most recent listed amendment in 23 DE Reg. 928, May 1, 2020. [1] The Office of Drinking Water programme page is currently available but carries no displayed revision date. [2] The current forms are revised through July 2023. [3] [4] These are official sources, but none is a recent candidate handbook or content outline.

A material conflict prevents scope normalization. Regulation 4463 defines the base-level license’s distribution coverage as systems below 500 gpm at **20 psi**, requires a distribution endorsement for systems over 500 gpm at **20 psi**, and lists distribution categories in Appendix A at **20 psi**. [1] In contrast, both the currently linked examination-registration form and license application label the relevant distribution threshold at **25 psi**. [3] [4] The form language cannot safely override the regulation, and the regulation does not state whether the form’s 25-psi references are a clerical legacy or an intended examination distinction. This must be resolved in an updated official examination outline or written clarification from the Delaware Office of Drinking Water before any question-authoring scope is set.

The regulation also states that examinations are confidential and may be prepared, administered, and graded by a third party. [1] Confidentiality is not itself a barrier to candidate preparation, but it makes an official public blueprint or curriculum especially important. The targeted review of the current Office of Drinking Water certification page and its linked official materials found no such document. [2] [3] [4]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The prospective learner is an applicant for the Delaware **Distribution License written examination** whose work is confined to distribution-system oversight and excludes treatment-plant operations. This is an experienced operator credential, not an entry-level general drinking-water course: applicants must meet the stated education/experience threshold in addition to passing the written examination. [1]

The only source-supported subject boundary is the legal definition of a distribution system and the role boundary between distribution-only oversight and treatment operations. It does **not** establish an item-level domain list or enough detail to determine what distribution operation, maintenance, sampling, hydraulic, emergency, safety, regulatory, or system-specific concepts belong on the examination. For this reason, no high-level objectives are proposed in this Hold dossier.

Excluded from any future route-specific package unless separately and directly supported are:

- base-level water-supply examination content, including its general water-system and hypochlorination components;
- treatment processes and all treatment endorsements or sub-endorsements;
- operator-in-training, limited-license, reciprocal-license, and approved sampler/tester requirements;
- wastewater treatment, collection, or other non-drinking-water credential content;
- site-specific standard operating procedures, design criteria, emergency response procedures, and unstated compliance duties;
- any threshold-based distinction using 20 psi or 25 psi until the Delaware Office of Drinking Water publishes a reconciled current specification.

## Bank recommendation

**No bank proposed.** Do not create a Delaware-specific distribution bank, do not map this route to a shared bank, and do not reuse base-level or treatment content as a substitute. A unique bank identity would not be evidence-supported until an official distribution examination blueprint, candidate handbook, approved curriculum, or written Office of Drinking Water clarification supplies an unambiguous content boundary and resolves the 20-psi/25-psi conflict.
