## Credential and examination boundary

California’s State Water Resources Control Board (State Water Board), through its Drinking Water Operator Certification Program (DWOCP), administers the potable drinking-water treatment operator pathway. California law defines a water treatment operator certificate as a certificate of competency for operating a water treatment plant at a specified classification and grade level; “operates a water treatment plant” concerns actions or decisions that control one or more drinking-water treatment processes. [1]

The State Water Board uses treatment grades **T1 through T5**. Examination eligibility and certificate issuance are not the same event. A person eligible for, and successful on, a grade exam is not necessarily certified at that grade: for T1, the certificate application must be made within three years after passing the T1 exam; the higher grades also require specified operator experience. [2] [3] The State Board’s October 2026 process notice independently confirms that T1 and T2 candidates apply for certification after passing, while grades T3–T5 require an application and supporting documentation. [4]

The only boundary supported for a future review-only candidate dossier is:

> **California DWOCP Water Treatment Grade T1 examination — foundational, written-exam scope only, limited to the official T1 objectives in the State Board’s currently posted “Expected Range of Knowledge for Drinking Water Treatment Exam.”** [5] [6]

This is intentionally an **exam-preparation boundary**, not a representation that an examinee holds, qualifies for, or may operate under a California water treatment operator certificate. T1 examination eligibility is a high-school diploma/GED, with two stated alternatives; the T1 certificate still requires passing the T1 examination and submitting a certification application within the regulatory three-year period. [2] [3]

## Readiness decision

**Narrow.** California has a current regulator, a current rule, a specific treatment examination path, and an official T1–T4 examination outline that lists both the T1 content-category weighting and individual T1 objectives. The State Board’s current October 20, 2026 operator-certification page still links the outline as the treatment operator “Expected Range of Knowledge,” and the related study-material document says exam questions are written to test subjects in that outline. [5] [6] That is enough authoritative, exam-specific evidence for a limited future **T1** review-only candidate dossier.

The route is not Suitable for an all-grades California treatment bank. The T1–T4 outline is marked “Rev. 4/2020,” and the State Board now separately publishes a January 2026 description of the **T5 oral examination**. T5 tests chief-operator administration and uses six confidential oral questions across regulatory, utility-management/emergency, and operational categories. [7] It is therefore not valid to treat T5 as part of the written T1–T4 outline, nor to infer a common all-grades blueprint.

[5] [6] The future candidate dossier must remain limited to the directly stated T1 objectives and must be rechecked against the State Board page before any drafting.

No scope conflict was found that changes the proposed T1 boundary. The 2026 emergency action amended only the operator certification fee schedule, effective August 31, 2026; it did not amend the treatment examination scope or grades. [9] The July 2026 treatment-exam application, the current rule, and the November 2024 State Board minimum-requirements document all separately identify T1–T5; for T1, each supports the high-school/GED eligibility condition and the exam-versus-certification distinction. [2] [3] [8] [10]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The learner is a prospective California **T1 treatment-exam** candidate. The scope is foundational treatment-operator knowledge and calculations expressly tagged T1 in the State Board’s outline. The official T1 exam weighting is: source water 25%; water treatment processes 25%; operation/maintenance 20%; laboratory procedures 15%; and safety, regulations, and administrative duties 15%. [5]

For a future review-only dossier, source-supported high-level objectives are limited to the following:

- Interpret foundational source-water conditions, make core flow/volume/pressure/detention-time and unit-conversion calculations, and identify common source-water risks or contamination indicators within the T1 objective list. [5]
- Apply foundational treatment-process knowledge for filtration, disinfection, corrosion control, taste and odor, iron and manganese, and the health effects or treatment context identified for T1. [5]
- Apply basic operation, maintenance, chemical-feed, pump, meter, pressure-gauge, and introductory SCADA knowledge expressly included at T1. [5]
- Apply T1-level sampling, basic laboratory, disinfectant, pH, turbidity, hardness, taste/odor, and microbiological-analysis knowledge expressly identified by the outline. [5]
- Recognize T1-listed safety, drinking-water regulation, monitoring/reporting, notification, recordkeeping, and water-quality-report interpretation concepts. [5]

**Excluded:**

- T2, T3, T4, and T5 objectives, eligibility requirements, certification experience, and supervisory or chief-operator competence.
- T5 oral-exam content, oral-examination format, confidential examination content, utility-management judgment, emergency decision-making, and any claim to prepare candidates for the T5 oral assessment. [7]
- Site-specific operating instructions, engineering design, chemical-feed setpoints, plant-specific procedures, legal interpretations beyond the cited California sources, and any federal or other-state rule not expressly incorporated by the current official California source record.
- Any conclusion that passing an examination alone authorizes plant operation or confers a certificate. [1] [3]

## Bank recommendation

**No new unique bank identity is proposed at this time.** The evidence supports only the bounded future dossier label **“California DWOCP Water Treatment Grade T1 examination”**; it does not establish that a new identity is non-overlapping with the existing dedicated U.S. Class I water package described in the readiness contract. That package must first be recovered and compared read-only, with its immutable package/checksum and authoritative target inventory preserved. A California-specific identity may be considered only after that reconciliation demonstrates a source-supported, non-duplicative California T1 gap.

This recommendation does not authorize authoring, database work, or release. The Narrow decision is sufficient only to retain the official source record and, after the required overlap review, consider a limited review-only candidate dossier tied one-to-one to the T1 objectives above.
