# Maryland water-treatment operator certification readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `md-water-treatment`
**Existing matrix label:** `wpi-customized`
**Research retrieval date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Defer**

## Exact state and stream

**Maryland (MD) — water treatment.** This dossier concerns Maryland’s Board of Waterworks and Waste Systems Operators credential category for operators of water-treatment plants. It does not cover water distribution, wastewater treatment, wastewater collection, industrial wastewater, or superintendent credentials.

## Credential and examination boundary

Maryland issues statewide Waterworks and Waste System Operator Certification through the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) Board of Waterworks and Waste Systems Operators. Certification applies to operators of water-treatment plants, among other operator categories. The legal framework classifies water-treatment facilities as Classes 1 through 5 and G; the operator’s authority and qualification requirements are tied to plant classification. [1] [2]

The currently available test-provider catalogue identifies separate **Maryland Water Treatment Class 1, Class 2, Class 3, Class 4, and Class 5** tests. [3] This establishes that Maryland has a live, class-specific treatment examination family. It does **not** establish the topic blueprint for any class, the mapping of Maryland’s facility classifications to each test, or the relationship of the G facility classification to a test title.

COMAR makes the missing boundary material: examinations must be based on the Board’s need-to-know criteria for **each specific classification**. [1] Neither MDE’s current public Board page nor the current PSI catalogue located in this review publishes those Maryland class-specific criteria, an official candidate handbook, a current Maryland examination outline, or an official crosswalk to WPI’s standardized material. [3] [4]

WPI publishes current 2025 *standardized* water-treatment need-to-know criteria for Classes I–IV, but its own page instructs users to consult the state or regional certifying authority to determine which materials are appropriate for the applicable jurisdiction and exam date. [5] That is not evidence that the WPI standard criteria are Maryland’s current customized test specification, particularly for Maryland Class 5 or facility Class G.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The operational programme is current: MDE’s Board page carries 2026 updates, the 2026 schedule is active, and the PSI catalogue shows current Maryland Water Treatment Class 1–5 test identities. [3] [4] [6] The governing regulation is an official current MDE-hosted COMAR compilation, but its extracted copy does not display a revision date; it should be rechecked against the official Division of State Documents before any later candidate work. [1]

No direct conflict exists between the MDE rule, facility classifications, and PSI’s active test catalogue. The material conflict is evidentiary: COMAR requires Board-determined need-to-know criteria by specific classification, while the public materials found do not disclose those criteria or connect Maryland’s tests to the WPI standardized 2025 criteria. [1] [5] The current Board minutes additionally describe potential Board-developed examinations, which prevents treating the inherited `wpi-customized` label or WPI standardized criteria as a confirmed, enduring Maryland content boundary. [8]

The assignment’s two older source hints are not relied on. The June 2019 Board minutes are historic, and the classification reference alone is not an examination blueprint. Similarly, an MDE training page that still links a 2022 schedule was not used to establish the present examination boundary.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The only defensible future audience is a Maryland operator-in-training or currently qualified applicant pursuing one identified Maryland **Water Treatment** operator class. The Board’s certification pathway states that the appropriate examination is part of operator certification after operator-in-training requirements and alongside education and experience requirements. [9]

A future package would need one expressly verified class boundary at a time, such as Maryland Water Treatment Class 1 or Class 4, and it would need an MDE/Board-approved objective list. Until then, all of the following are excluded:

- water distribution, wastewater treatment, wastewater collection, industrial wastewater, and superintendent content;
- cross-class material, including any inference that a topic in a T4 facility description is assessed on another Maryland class test;
- Maryland legal, reporting, permit, renewal, cybersecurity, site-specific operating, or emergency-response content unless the Board’s class-specific outline expressly includes it;
- T5 and TG subject matter, because the current sources do not publish a test-content boundary for those classifications;
- any assumption that WPI standardized Class I–IV need-to-know criteria apply unchanged to Maryland.

No high-level objectives are stated because a **Defer** decision does not support objective selection.

## Readiness decision

**Defer.** Maryland’s regulator, credential issuer, legal framework, facility classes, active examination programme, and active test titles are verified. The route is nevertheless not ready for a future review-only candidate question-bank package because the critical source required by COMAR—the Board’s current need-to-know criteria for the specific Maryland class—is not publicly evidenced. The available WPI criteria are explicitly standardized and require jurisdictional confirmation; the State has not published that confirmation or a current Maryland candidate handbook/outline. [1] [5]

The decision is not a finding that Maryland lacks water-treatment certification or tests. It is a finding that the present public evidence does not safely identify a single testable scope for a limited candidate dossier.

## Bank recommendation

**No unique bank proposed.** A Maryland-specific bank identity would be premature and could duplicate or misrepresent the active Maryland Class 1–5 examination family. Do not infer that a shared WPI bank, a Maryland addendum, or a new `md-water-treatment` bank is supported by the current evidence.

Reconsider only after MDE/the Board supplies or publicly posts all of the following for one named Maryland Water Treatment class: a current candidate handbook or Board-approved need-to-know/examination outline; confirmation of the current test owner and whether it uses, modifies, or replaces WPI standardized criteria; and a class mapping that resolves facility Class G and any Class 5 scope. The resulting dossier must then be re-reviewed for overlap with existing U.S. or WPI material before any identity is proposed.

## Candidate-only and learner-access boundary

This is a research-only readiness dossier. It authorizes **no candidate package, no bank data, no database action, and no content release**. If the missing official boundary is later obtained and independently verified, any future work remains **candidate-only / `in_review` / no learner access** until the separate evidence, structural validation, independent-review, and approval controls in the readiness contract are satisfied.
