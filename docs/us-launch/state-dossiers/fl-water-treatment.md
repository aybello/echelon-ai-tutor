# Florida Drinking-Water Treatment Operator Readiness Dossier

**Route ID:** `fl-water-treatment`
**Existing matrix label:** State-specific
**Research status:** Official-source readiness review only. This dossier creates no questions, no candidate package, no database data, and no learner-facing material.

## Exact state and stream

**State:** Florida (FL)
**Stream:** Drinking-water treatment, not domestic-wastewater treatment and not water-distribution operations.
**Regulator and certification body:** Florida Department of Environmental Protection (DEP), Operator Certification Program (OCP). DEP states that a person operating a drinking-water treatment plant must be licensed by DEP and that the program uses an examination for the desired license type and level. [1]

## Credential and examination boundary

The supported boundary is the **Florida DEP Drinking Water Treatment Plant Operator — Class C examination** only.

This is an examination-only, pre-licensure boundary. DEP’s current treatment-examination form separately selects **Water Treatment** and **Class C**, and states that the application is for examination rather than licensure. [6] Rule 62-602.270 requires a high-school diploma or equivalent and completion of the relevant Department-approved course within five years for a Class C treatment-operator examination. [3] Passing the exam does not by itself establish a Florida Class C license: Rule 62-602.300 separately requires the diploma or equivalent, 2,080 hours of eligible experience, and a passing Class C examination score for licensure. [4]

The current DEP handbook describes Class A, B, C, and D as distinct treatment-examination classes. For Class A, B, and C it specifies a three-hour, 100-item multiple-choice examination. Its Class C drinking-water-treatment subject list covers aeration; source-water characteristics; clarification/sedimentation; coagulation/flocculation; corrosion control; disinfection and disinfectant byproducts; distribution; filtration; facility operation and maintenance; equipment evaluation and operation; iron and manganese control; laboratory sampling, analysis, and interpretation; mathematics; safety and security; regulations; reservoir and well-field management; softening; taste and odor control; waste handling and disposal; and water process. [2]

**Boundary statement:** A future package may address only knowledge traceable to the published **Florida Drinking Water Treatment Plant Operator Class C examination subject areas**. It must not represent itself as training for licensure, a substitute for the required approved course, proof of operating competence, or coverage of the Class A, Class B, Class D, wastewater-treatment, or distribution-operator examinations.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The controlling content source is the DEP handbook revised **June 2026**. The separate PDF linked from DEP's current subject-area-overview page bears an April 2011 date. It is not used as evidence for this dossier or for future candidate scope; the June 2026 handbook is the sole official content source of record. The current DEP landing page remains active and was last modified July 30, 2025. [2] [7]

The applicable licensure and examination-application rules were amended effective **November 6, 2025**. Rule 62-602.270 has an older effective date of October 15, 2007 but is presented by Florida’s official rule system as the current final adopted examination-eligibility rule. [3] [4] [5] The November 2025 examination form is incorporated by Rule 62-602.410 and is consistent with the Class C route defined in the handbook and eligibility rule. [5] [6]

The assignment manifest pointed to a July 2026 handbook filename. DEP’s active handbook resource retrieved for this review is the **June 2026** revision, linked from DEP’s current program materials. This dossier relies on that active official resource. No material conflict affects the Class C boundary. A course-completion distinction for Class D shown in the handbook and form does not affect Class C, for which the rule, handbook, and form all require the corresponding approved course. [2] [3] [6]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The appropriate learner is a prospective **Florida Drinking Water Treatment Plant Operator Class C examination candidate** who has completed, or is within the valid five-year window after completing, the corresponding DEP-approved training course. This is an examination-preparation audience, not an untrained public audience and not a licensee-equivalency audience. [3] [6]

The limited future candidate scope may use only high-level objectives that are directly traceable to the current Class C subject list:

- Relate source-water characteristics to the Class C treatment-process topics named by DEP.
- Distinguish the conceptual roles of aeration, coagulation/flocculation, clarification/sedimentation, filtration, softening, and iron-and-manganese control.
- Recognize the Class C scope of disinfection, disinfectant byproducts, corrosion control, taste-and-odor control, and water-process concepts.
- Identify the purpose of laboratory sampling, analysis, interpretation, basic treatment mathematics, equipment evaluation, and facility operation and maintenance within the published examination scope.
- Recognize the examination-level relevance of safety and security, regulations, reservoir and well-field management, distribution, and waste handling and disposal when a current official source supports the specific claim.

Explicit exclusions are Class A, B, and D treatment examinations; wastewater-treatment examinations; water-distribution examinations; course approval; candidate eligibility determinations; licensure experience verification; reciprocity; continuing education; test scheduling; test-vendor procedures; detailed facility design; site-specific operating instructions; procedural safety direction; legal or numeric compliance requirements not supported by a current Florida primary source; and any assertion that an examination result confers a license.

## Decision

**Decision: Narrow.**

Florida has a current, official, state-specific Class C drinking-water-treatment examination boundary. The June 2026 DEP handbook supplies a sufficiently specific current subject-area list for a limited future candidate dossier, while the current form and rules verify that this is a real, examination-only Florida path. [2] [3] [5] [6]

The broader `fl-water-treatment` route should not be treated as one all-level bank. Florida publishes separate Class A, B, C, and D treatment examinations with different eligibility and subject scope. The evidence supports only the bounded **Class C** subset for the next review-only stage. [2] [3]

## Bank recommendation

If, and only if, a read-only reconciliation confirms that no existing reviewed package already covers this exact Florida-specific boundary, the evidence supports the provisional unique identity **`fl-drinking-water-treatment-class-c`**.

Do not create a generic Florida water-treatment bank and do not use this identity for Class A, B, D, wastewater-treatment, water-distribution, licensure, or course-completion material. The proposed identity is a dossier recommendation only; it does not create a bank or candidate data.
