## Decision

**Narrow.** Louisiana Department of Health (LDH) currently operates a state-specific Operator Certification Program, publishes a 2026 examination schedule, provides a Water Treatment examination application that separately names Classes 1–4, and links an official Water Treatment “Need to Know” document under its current examination resources. Those sources establish a specific, usable boundary for a limited future dossier: the **Louisiana Water Treatment Class 1 certification examination**. [1] [2] [3] [5]

The route should not be treated as one undifferentiated Louisiana water-treatment examination or as a complete four-class product. Water Treatment Classes 1–4 are separate, sequential examinations with cumulative scope. The published outline has no revision date, test specification, percentage weighting, item count, or stated source version; the formal rule linked by LDH is a 2002 republication. These limits preclude a full-route or advanced-class candidate package at this time, but do not prevent a tightly bounded Class 1 technical-scope dossier. [2] [6]

## Exact credential and examination boundary

The supported boundary is **Louisiana Department of Health, Office of Public Health, Operator Certification Program — Water Treatment Class 1 certification examination**.

The current LDH exam application offers Water Treatment Class 1, Class 2, Class 3, and Class 4 as individually selected exams. The current program FAQ states that an applicant must apply for an examination, pass it, and then apply and pay for the license; exam results are valid for two years. The rule available from the current LDH program page says classes are taken and passed in sequence from Class 1 through Class 4 within each category. [3] [4] [6]

For this narrow decision, “Class 1” means the first technical examination in Louisiana’s Water Treatment category. It does **not** mean that passing the examination alone makes a person a professional operator. The current FAQ and the regulator-linked rule distinguish passing an examination from obtaining a certificate, which also depends on the applicable education, experience, application, and employment conditions. [4] [6]

### Included candidate-level scope

A future review-only candidate dossier may be limited to the Class 1 “Need to Know” technical and calculation scope expressly listed by LDH:

- foundational reasons, materials, testing concepts, and safe-handling concepts for fluoridation, pH adjustment, and chlorination;
- basic treatment-process concepts named in the outline, including aeration, coagulation/flocculation, sedimentation, iron and manganese removal, softening, filtration, corrosion control, taste-and-odor control, pumps, motors, electricity, and cross-connection control;
- basic feeder type, calibration-check, and maintenance concepts at the non-site-specific level; and
- the Class 1 mathematics headings of standards conversion and area calculations. [2]

The current LDH exam math sheet is a supporting reference for general equivalents and area formulas, but it does not expand the Class 1 scope beyond the Class 1 headings in the Water Treatment outline. [7]

### Exclusions

Do not include any of the following in this future Class 1 candidate dossier:

- Water Treatment Classes 2, 3, or 4, including their cumulative or advanced treatment, laboratory, management, cost, instrumentation, SCADA, sludge-disposal, reverse-osmosis, or electrodialysis material. [2]
- Water Production, Water Distribution, Wastewater Collection, or Wastewater Treatment examinations; LDH lists these as separate categories or examination selections. [1] [3] [6]
- the separate Certification Law and Rules Examination, certificate application rules, renewal requirements, fees, examination scheduling, eligibility, point calculations, or legal/regulatory requirements. These are not a class-specific Water Treatment technical blueprint and should be separately verified against a current codified authority before any candidate work. [1] [4] [6]
- facility-specific operating instructions, chemical-feed settings, laboratory procedures, emergency actions, design decisions, or local operating policies.
- any assertion that a Class 1 examination pass independently grants professional certification.

## High-level objectives for a future review-only candidate dossier

These objectives are intentionally high level and are not candidate items.

1. Describe the foundational purpose of common Class 1 water-treatment chemical applications and associated monitoring concepts named by LDH.
2. Recognize the basic function of the treatment processes and supporting equipment explicitly named for Water Treatment Class 1.
3. Apply the Class 1 calculation domains limited to standards conversion and area calculation.
4. Distinguish the Class 1 technical water-treatment boundary from separate Louisiana water categories and higher Water Treatment classes.

## Source freshness, alignment, and limitations

The current 2026 LDH program page, current application, current FAQ, 2026 schedule, and 2026-dated math sheet align on an active Louisiana operator-certification program with distinct water-treatment class examinations. The Water Treatment Need-to-Know document aligns with the application’s Classes 1–4 and the regulator-linked rule’s category and class structure. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [7]

No material conflict affects the narrow technical Class 1 boundary. The current schedule uses a 32-hour-course condition for closed exams, while the older linked rule describes a 40-hour course after three failed attempts; these statements concern different testing circumstances and are excluded from scope. [5] [6]

There are material limitations. The technical outline has no document date, version identifier, test weights, or form details. The available rule text is dated 2002, and no current official codified consolidation or amendment history was identified in the sources reviewed. Accordingly, current legal, administrative, and examination-administration assertions must not be transformed into candidate content from this dossier. Recheck the current LDH program page, Water Treatment outline, application, and an official current codified source immediately before any future candidate drafting.
