## Decision

**Suitable** for a *limited, future, review-only* candidate package tied only to the New Mexico Water Supply – Level 3 (WS3) examination. The New Mexico Environment Department (NMED) publishes a March 2026, level-specific operator guidebook that identifies the credential, eligibility, exam format, passing threshold, weighted content areas, and need-to-know topics. That guidebook is current programme material and states that it was reviewed by the New Mexico Utility Operators Certification Advisory Board in January and February 2026. [1]

The supporting official rule identifies the public-water-supply treatment processes and population bands for which WS3 is the required certification, and requires a written examination for the requested certification level. [2] The programme's current certification page independently lists WS3 as a water certification requiring four years of experience and 50 training credits, and describes a candidate guidebook for each certification type and level. [3] This is a discrete, state-specific credential/exam boundary rather than a generic water-treatment route.

## Credential and examination boundary

The applicable route is **New Mexico Water Supply – Level 3 (WS3)**, administered by NMED's Utility Operator Certification Program (UOCP) for public water-system utilities in New Mexico. UOCP states that New Mexico certification requires meeting the applicable certification requirements and passing New Mexico certification examinations, which it administers through computer-based testing at Workforce Connections Centers. [4]

The candidate guidebook identifies the WS3 exam as a **120-question multiple-choice** examination, with a **70% minimum passing score (84/120)**. It allocates questions across 20 named content areas: administration; chemical stabilization; coagulation and flocculation; cross-connection control; disinfection; distribution; filtration; fluoridation; general; ion-exchange softening; laboratory procedures; mechanical systems; regulations; safety; sampling and reporting; sedimentation; storage; taste and odor; water characteristics; and wells. [1]

The legal operating boundary is the treatment-process and population-served matrix in 20.7.4.12(A) NMAC. It assigns WS3 to specified public-water-supply treatment scenarios, including several filtration, conventional-treatment, aeration, activated-carbon, chemical-addition, pressure-filtration, ion-exchange, disinfection, fluoridation, arsenic-removal, radionuclide-removal, and groundwater-production classifications; WS4 remains required for specified larger or special/desalination scenarios. [2] A WS3 holder also has the lesser included authority stated in 20.7.4.14(E), but those lower credentials remain out of scope as separately marketed or separately authored examinations. [2]

The baseline examination-eligibility boundary is applicant age of majority, high-school diploma or GED, four years of qualifying experience, 50 approved training credits, a complete application and fee, and passage of the WS3 examination, subject to the rule's express substitution provisions and NMED's determinations. [1] [2] Eligibility adjudication, equivalency, and substitution decisions are not candidate-package content.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The intended future learner is an experienced operator who is preparing specifically for the **New Mexico WS3 written examination**, not an entry-level water operator or a general water-treatment learner. The standard experience and training prerequisites indicate an advanced operational level relative to WS1 and WS2. [2] [3]

A future review-only package may cover only the published WS3 content areas and only to the depth supported by the March 2026 need-to-know criteria. This includes water characteristics and wells; conventional and specified advanced treatment concepts; disinfection and fluoridation; distribution, storage, cross-connection, and mechanical-system concepts where they are explicitly examined; laboratory, sampling, reporting, regulatory, administrative, and safety knowledge within the published outline. [1]

The following are excluded:

- WS4 and its higher/special-system boundary, including content that assumes the credential is sufficient for systems that the rule assigns to WS4, such as special/desalination treatment scenarios. [2]
- Separate Small Water, Small Water Advanced, WS1, WS2, Water Sampling Technician, and Distribution System certification examinations. WS3's lesser-included authority does not make those separate exam blueprints part of this route. [2]
- Wastewater treatment, wastewater laboratory, and collection-system certification content.
- Site-specific standard operating procedures, engineering design, facility-specific set points, vendor-specific equipment instructions, and legal/compliance assertions not directly traceable to current New Mexico authority.
- Individual application, education/experience substitution, reciprocity/equivalency, renewal, or enforcement determinations, which remain NMED functions under the rule and programme.

## High-level source-bound objectives for a future candidate dossier

These are objective domains only. They are not questions, answer choices, explanations, candidate records, or authoring instructions.

1. **Water sources and characteristics:** Recognize the physical, chemical, and microbiological water-characteristic concepts and well-system concepts identified for WS3 examination coverage. [1]
2. **Treatment-process concepts:** Apply the published WS3 framework for coagulation/flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, chemical stabilization, ion exchange, taste-and-odor control, and fluoridation at a conceptual operator-exam level. [1]
3. **Disinfection and process support:** Interpret the guidebook's published disinfection, chemical-handling, mechanical-system, measurement, and calculation domains within the WS3 exam boundary. [1]
4. **System integrity and operations:** Recognize the WS3 outline's distribution, storage, cross-connection-control, record, and process-control domains, without treating distribution as a separate credential examination. [1] [2]
5. **Compliance-facing knowledge:** Recognize the published WS3 domains for sampling and reporting, regulations, laboratory procedures, administration, and safety, while keeping any state-specific requirement tied to current New Mexico primary authority. [1] [2]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The controlling exam source is the NMED WS3 guidebook dated **March 2026**, with a stated Advisory Board review in January and February 2026. NMED's live programme and certification pages were retrieved on 2026-10-29 and link candidates to the current guidebook pathway. [1] [3] [4]

The official NMAC text has older section amendment histories, but it remains the current official rule page and is the authority for legal certification classifications and minimum requirements. [2] Its WS3 classification, lower-level inclusion, written-exam requirement, and standard qualifications align with the current NMED guidebook and programme page. No material conflict was found. For future authoring, the guidebook should remain the content/blueprint authority and the official current NMAC publication should be rechecked for amendments before any candidate dossier is assembled.

## Bank recommendation and release boundary

**Recommendation:** Propose one unique, state-specific future candidate-bank identity: **`nm-ws3-water-treatment`**, limited to the New Mexico Water Supply – Level 3 written-exam family. The exact state, credential, official exam outline, and legal process boundary support a unique bank rather than reuse of a generic or lower-level bank. This proposal does not create a bank, database record, candidate package, or release.

**Release boundary:** Any future work is **candidate-only and review-only**. It must remain inaccessible to learners and must not be published, activated, sold, assigned, imported, or otherwise released. A future candidate package would still require source-by-source evidence records, structural validation, and an independent reviewer decision before any later release consideration.
