# Nebraska water-treatment operator route — readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `ne-water-treatment`
**Exact state / stream:** Nebraska (NE) — water-treatment
**Existing matrix label:** state-specific
**Research retrieval date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Defer**

## Credential and examination boundary

Nebraska’s current rule creates a single **public water-system operator** licensing structure, not a separate water-treatment operator credential. A licensed operator is authorized to make process-control or system-integrity decisions about water quality or quantity in public water systems. The operator in responsible charge may make daily decisions for a public water system, water-treatment facility, and/or distribution system. Public water systems must have an operator licensed at or above the system classification. [1]

The rule defines Grades I–V for public water-system operators and Grade VI for authorized backflow-preventer testing and repair technicians. Grades I–IV require validated examinations for the requested grade; Grade I additionally requires prior successful completion of the Grade II examination, and Grade II requires prior successful completion of the Grade III examination. Grade V has a distinct, narrowly stated examination on sampling, interpreting biological-examination results, and maintaining required records. Grade VI is a backflow credential with written and hands-on examination components. [1]

For this route, the closest treatment-associated boundary is **the Grade I–II public-water operator training/examination path**, because the current DWEE application identifies *Water Treatment Plant Operation*, Volumes 1 and 2, as the required books for the combined Grade I and II course. However, the same application offers individual examinations by grade only (I–VI), with no separately named treatment examination, and it does not describe the tested domains. [2]

The rule’s system classification is also not treatment-only. Grades I–III can correspond to systems using filtration, but classifications can also derive from distribution-system population and other system characteristics. A Grade I–IV license therefore reaches integrated public-water-system operation rather than a discrete treatment discipline. [1]

**Defensible boundary for future review:** Nebraska public-water-system operator licensure exams by grade. This dossier does **not** establish a treatment-only examination, a Grade I–II treatment-only blueprint, or a valid content boundary for a `ne-water-treatment` question bank.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The governing rule is explicitly effective March 18, 2025, and the application is explicitly revised July 8, 2025. The 2026 DWEE training calendar confirms that the grade-based courses remain current for the 2026 schedule. The current program page is live and was retrieved on the dossier date, although it does not display a publication or revision date. These current official sources align on the existence of a grade-based water-operator licensure program.

No material conflict was found. The rule carries the Department of Health and Human Services title, while DWEE currently hosts the rule and administers the current program forms and training page; this is an administrative publication context, not conflicting credential scope. A 2019 water-operator licensing brochure was located during research but was not used as decision evidence because it predates the current rule and current application. No current official candidate handbook, examination blueprint, detailed content outline, or treatment-specific examination title was located on the regulator’s public materials reviewed.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The only verified learner population is an eligible applicant for a Nebraska **public water-system operator grade**. Eligibility is not entry-level in a uniform sense: Grades I–III have sequential exam and education/experience requirements, Grade IV has its own eligibility path, Grade V is restricted to transient-system operators, and Grade VI concerns backflow prevention. [1]

This water-treatment route excludes all of the following until DWEE supplies a current, official, exam-specific scope:

- a claim that Nebraska administers a separate water-treatment operator examination;
- treatment-process objectives inferred from commercial preparation products, textbooks, or out-of-state outlines;
- distribution, sampling, recordkeeping, backflow, renewal, enforcement, or general public-water-system content merely because it may appear in a grade-level examination;
- level-specific objectives for Grades I, II, III, or IV; and
- state-law, compliance, or site-operating content beyond the cited rule.

## Readiness decision and bank recommendation

**Decision: Defer.** Nebraska has a current, official credentialing rule and current regulator-administered applications, but the evidence does not establish a treatment-only credential/exam boundary or an official Grade I–II examination blueprint. The treatment books named for the combined Grade I–II course identify course materials, not the content domains, weighting, or limits of a separate treatment exam. A limited future candidate dossier cannot therefore be traced to a sufficiently specific official treatment-exam scope.

**Bank recommendation:** **Do not propose a unique bank.** Do not create `ne-water-treatment` or a Grade I–II treatment bank from the available evidence. Reassess only if DWEE publishes or directly provides a current official examination outline, candidate handbook, test specification, or other authoritative document that identifies the grade-specific examination content and distinguishes any treatment-only scope from integrated public-water-system operation.

**High-level objectives:** Not proposed. Under the readiness contract, objectives are reserved for a Suitable or Narrow route; this route is Defer.
