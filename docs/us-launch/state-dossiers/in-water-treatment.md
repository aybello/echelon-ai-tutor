## Credential and examination boundary

Indiana's drinking-water treatment route is not one generic treatment examination. IDEM states that Indiana has **five water-treatment examinations with corresponding certifications** and publishes separate WT1, WT2, WT3, and combined WT4/WT5 study guides. A generic `in-water-treatment` bank would therefore combine distinct credentials and is not supported. [1]

The only boundary supported for a limited future dossier in this review is:

> **Indiana Department of Environmental Management (IDEM) WT1 Drinking Water Operator certification examination — groundwater or purchased-water treatment systems serving no more than 500 people and using ion-exchange softening for cation removal and/or inline filtration with no chemical treatment.**

IDEM describes WT1 operators as limited to operating WT1 systems. Its current examination page independently describes the WT1 facility category and specifies one year of hands-on operational experience for WT1. [2] [3] Applicants select one grade of water-treatment exam on the certification application, which further supports a grade-specific rather than stream-wide examination boundary. [4]

This dossier does **not** conclude that WT2, WT3, WT4, WT5, or an undifferentiated Indiana water-treatment bank is ready. WT2 and WT3 are separate groundwater/purchased-water classes with broader treatment conditions, while WT4/WT5 are surface-water or groundwater-under-the-direct-influence-of-surface-water classes. [3] [5]

## Readiness decision

**Narrow.** There is a current, official, state-specific certification path and a separately published WT1 study guide with an identifiable entry-level treatment-system boundary. This is enough evidence for a limited future *WT1 candidate dossier*, but not for the full Indiana water-treatment stream.

The boundary remains narrow for two reasons. First, the parent stream contains five distinct treatment examinations, each tied to a different facility classification. Second, IDEM's WT1 document is a study guide with a contents-based scope; the official material reviewed does not provide a detailed scored examination blueprint, domain weights, or an explicit item-by-item test specification. Future work must not infer the scope of another WT grade from WT1.

## Learner level, supported scope, and exclusions

**Learner level.** Entry-level Indiana WT1 candidate: a person pursuing a grade-specific drinking-water treatment operator examination. This is not a general public-water-system course, a distribution-operator course, or an experienced surface-water-treatment credential.

**Supported high-level objectives for a future WT1-only candidate dossier.** Any later objectives must be confirmed against the cited pages of the current WT1 guide before candidate drafting.

1. Recognize the WT1 facility boundary: small community groundwater or purchased-water systems, with the limited WT1 softening and/or no-chemical inline-filtration configuration.
2. Identify foundational groundwater and well-system concepts within the WT1 guide's stated scope.
3. Recognize basic operator responsibilities, records, and public-water-system regulatory context as presented in the WT1 guide.
4. Apply foundational treatment-system operations concepts within the guide's stated coverage, including meters, valves, pumps, maintenance, water quality, ion exchange, and basic calculations.
5. Recognize the guide's high-level coverage of sampling, laboratory context, safety, and backflow/cross-connection topics, without converting the guide into site-specific operating directions.

**Excluded.**

- WT2, WT3, WT4, WT5, and any potential WT6 technology-specific certification.
- Surface-water and groundwater-under-the-direct-influence-of-surface-water treatment, including coagulation/flocculation, sedimentation, surface-water filtration, and the surface-water credential pathway.
- Chemical-feed treatment, disinfection operations, reverse osmosis, lime-soda softening, pressure/gravity filtration, and other higher-class treatment processes except where a topic is unambiguously within the WT1 guide.
- Water distribution certification, wastewater operations, reciprocity, facility-specific operator status, renewal/CEU administration, and employment/experience eligibility adjudication.
- Site-specific procedures, operational setpoints, emergency response, compliance determinations, legal advice, and any numerical/regulatory claim not rechecked against the current controlling rule or current IDEM material at authoring time.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The key scope evidence is current IDEM web content retrieved October 29, 2026, plus a WT1 PDF that IDEM currently hosts. The PDF's document metadata shows a December 2024 creation/modification date. The 2026 IDEM exam-dates page shows continued state administration of grade-specific examinations and lists current WT1 requirements and classifications. [2] [3]

No material conflict was found among the current IDEM program page, 2026 exam-dates page, application instructions, and WT1 guide regarding the need for a grade-specific treatment examination. The WT1 guide says systems serving **less than or equal to 500** people; the exam-dates page says **fewer than 501** people. These are equivalent thresholds. [2] [3]

The live Indiana Administrative Code index identifies 327 IAC 8-12 as the controlling rule, titled “Classification of Community Public Water System and Nontransient Noncommunity Public Water System Treatment Plants and Distribution Systems; Examination and Certification of Operators.” The older `iac.iga.in.gov` PDF link used by IDEM now returns a 404, so it must not be treated as a current source file. Use the live Indiana Administrative Rules and Policies (IARP) rule page for any later legal or regulatory claim. [6]
