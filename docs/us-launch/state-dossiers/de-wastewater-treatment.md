# Delaware wastewater-treatment operator route — readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `de-wastewater-treatment`
**State / stream:** Delaware (DE) / wastewater treatment
**Existing matrix label:** state-specific
**Research retrieval date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Defer**

## Exact credential and examination boundary

This route concerns the Delaware Department of Natural Resources and Environmental Control (DNREC) **Delaware Licensed Wastewater Operator** program for wastewater-treatment facilities. The controlling regulation establishes four regular operator levels, **Level I, II, III, and IV**, and requires separate written examinations for each level. DNREC’s current program page states that those level-specific examinations use multiple-choice and true/false questions and require a 75 percent passing score. Passing an examination alone is not the license: licensing also depends on the applicable education, operating-experience, employment, application, and Board/Department determinations. [1] [2]

The bounded examination family is therefore: **Delaware Licensed Wastewater Operator written examinations, Level I through Level IV, for wastewater-treatment-facility operators.** It is not a generic wastewater-treatment exam, a drinking-water credential, an on-site wastewater credential, a continuing-education course, or a facility-specific specialty license. The regulation permits a specialty license only for a highly unusual facility and makes it valid only for that facility; it is outside this route. [1]

The evidence does **not** identify official tested domains, objectives, task statements, item weights, references, permitted-resource rules, or a separate content outline for any Level I–IV examination. The rule says that the Board prepares examinations to assess knowledge, ability, and judgment and that separate examinations cover each level, but does not define their subject-matter blueprint. [1]

## Decision and bank recommendation

**Decision: Defer.** Delaware has a current and specific state credential/examination boundary, but DNREC has not published a current official examination outline, candidate handbook, or other level-specific tested-content specification sufficient to delimit even a small, defensible review-only question-bank candidate package. The facility-classification rule includes wastewater-process definitions, but it classifies facilities; it does not state that those definitions or processes are tested at a particular operator level. It must not be converted into presumed examination objectives. [1]

**Bank recommendation: none.** Do not propose a unique Delaware wastewater-treatment bank, an all-level bank, or a Level I bank at this stage. A future bank identity may be considered only after DNREC or the Board supplies a current, official, level-specific examination blueprint, outline, candidate handbook, or expressly designated examination preparation material.

Because the decision is Defer, this dossier intentionally provides **no high-level objectives**. Creating objectives would require an unsupported inference about what a Delaware level-specific examination tests.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The intended learners would be candidates for one specified Delaware Licensed Wastewater Operator examination level, not all wastewater personnel generally. The available sources establish eligibility and level distinctions, but not the knowledge boundary of any one exam.

Excluded from any future candidate package unless a current official Delaware source expressly supports them are:

- cross-level content or a combined Level I–IV bank;
- specialty-license content and facility-specific requirements;
- site-specific operating instructions, NPDES permit limits, reporting procedures, or emergency actions;
- non-Delaware certification rules, commercial preparation material, blogs, or third-party practice tests;
- continuing-education course material unless DNREC identifies it as examination content;
- drinking-water, collection-system, septic/on-site, biosolids, laboratory, engineering-design, and other adjacent content unless an official level-specific examination source includes it.

The statutory/regulatory concepts in the licensing rule may support a later compliance-oriented candidate only where an official source also establishes that the concept belongs in the particular examination boundary. They are not a substitute for a test blueprint.

Code 7204, Regulations for Licensing Operators of Wastewater Facilities** — **Issuer:** Delaware Department of Natural Resources and Environmental Control; Delaware Administrative Code. **URL:** https://regulations.delaware.gov/AdminCode/title7/7204. **Retrieved:** October 29, 2026. **Claim supported:** This is the controlling licensing regulation. It defines a Delaware Licensed Wastewater Operator and a wastewater treatment facility; establishes four operator levels in §6.2; requires a qualifying written examination within the prior three years in §6.1; requires the Board or designee to prepare written examinations assessing knowledge, ability, and judgment in §7.1; and requires separate examinations for each level in §7.4. It also frames specialty licenses as case-specific in §6.3. The rule provides no level-specific examination content outline, blueprint, task list, or weights. [1]

2. **Wastewater Operator Certification** — **Issuer:** DNREC, Division of Water. **URL:** https://dnrec.delaware.gov/water/commercial-government/wastewater-facilities/operators/. **Retrieved:** October 29, 2026. **Claim supported:** DNREC administers the certification program with the Board’s advice and assistance; applicants must pass an examination before applying for a license; the current page publishes 2026 quarterly exam dates; and it states that there is a separate written exam for each license level, using multiple-choice and true/false questions, with a 75 percent passing score. The page does not link to an official test blueprint, content outline, or candidate handbook. [2]

## Source freshness and conflicts

The current DNREC certification page publishes a 2026 quarterly examination schedule and is the best available operational source for current exam cadence. The September 2024 brochure is relatively recent but is not a current content outline. The Administrative Code page is the controlling legal source even though its displayed amendment history is 2018; it remains DNREC’s current online regulation and controls where informational materials conflict. [1] [2] [3]

Two material conflicts require the rule to prevail:

- **Retake threshold:** Section 7.5 of the regulation requires an approved educational examination-review course after two consecutive failures for the same level. The 2024 brochure says after three consecutive failures. The current DNREC program page does not resolve this discrepancy. A future package must not state a retake threshold without DNREC confirmation or a corrected current publication; the controlling rule is two consecutive failures. [1] [3]
- **Level III and IV qualifications:** The current Board FAQ describes Level III and IV educational qualifications differently from §6.2 of the regulation and from the 2024 brochure. The rule requires an associate degree or equivalent approved credit hours for Level III and a bachelor’s degree or equivalent approved credit hours for Level IV, with stated experience/DRC requirements. The FAQ is not a reliable source for those criteria. [1] [3] [4]

The official registration form is stale operationally because it states semiannual January/June administration, while the current DNREC program page gives four 2026 dates. This conflict further confirms that dated materials cannot supply a current candidate boundary. [2] [5]

No conflict changes the Defer result. The decisive limitation is the lack of an official, level-specific subject-matter outline, not uncertainty that the credential exists.
