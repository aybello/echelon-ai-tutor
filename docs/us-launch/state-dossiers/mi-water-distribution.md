# Michigan Water Distribution — State-Specific Readiness Dossier

**Decision:** **Narrow**

## Credential and examination boundary

This dossier concerns the **Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) Type I S — Distribution operator certification examinations for Levels S-1, S-2, S-3, and S-4 only**. Michigan’s current certification requirements identify S as the Distribution category, describe Level 1 as the highest level and Level 4 as the entry level for community-water-supply certification, and state that Levels 1–4 are administered twice yearly. [1]

The controlling Part 19 rules assign S-1 through S-4 to distribution systems at community supplies, using population bands for those classifications. The rules require a written examination for each public-water-supply classification except the Level 5 classifications; they also require the advisory board to schedule at least one annual examination for S-1 through S-4. [2] Michigan’s current 2026 calendar confirms active distribution examinations in May and November. [3]

This is an **examination-content boundary, not an independent authorization-to-operate or certification-eligibility determination**. EGLE evaluates application, education, and experience information before examination admission, and certification involves the department’s review and action after examination. [1] [2]

**Excluded credential boundaries:** S-5 noncommunity certification; F complete-treatment certification; D limited-treatment certification; wastewater or collection-system credentials; restricted, site-specific certificates; reciprocal certificates; and credentials issued outside Michigan. S-5 is expressly a different rule classification and may use a different examination format. [2]

## Readiness finding

**Narrow** is appropriate. EGLE currently publishes a regulator-issued, Type I S Distribution (Levels 1–4) examination family; current 2026 requirements divide the published S examination domains into Levels 1–2 and Levels 3–4; and current program pages, applications, study materials, and a 2026 calendar show that the program is active. [1] [3] [4] [5]

The available current public content boundary is not specific enough to support a separate S-1, S-2, S-3, or S-4 bank, or a comprehensive combined bank. EGLE’s Rev. 2/2026 requirements group S content as **Levels 1–2** and **Levels 3–4**, state that subjects are typically covered, and state that subjects may change without notification. They do not publish content weights, task statements, a level-by-level distinction within either pair, or an official depth specification. [1]

Accordingly, only a small future review-only candidate dossier for source-supported, common Type I S distribution domains is within scope. It must not infer individual-level coverage, examination weighting, detailed compliance duties, system-specific operating actions, construction criteria, calculation methods, or safety procedures from domain headings.

## Learner level, permitted scope, and exclusions

The intended learner is an EGLE-approved applicant preparing for a Michigan **Type I S Distribution** certification examination at Level S-1, S-2, S-3, or S-4. This designation does not establish that a learner meets Michigan’s application, education, experience, classification, operator-in-charge, or other certification conditions. [1] [2]

For a future candidate-only dossier, the following **high-level objectives only** are source-supported by EGLE’s current Level 1–2 and Level 3–4 S domain lists:

- Recognize the broad distribution-system operation and maintenance domains identified by EGLE, including system components, storage, valves, hydrants, meters, pipes and joints, pumps and motors, and water-main installation.
- Recognize the broad water-quality domains identified by EGLE for the applicable level group, including chlorination, corrosion or corrosion control, sampling, and water quality; microbiology is limited to the published S-3/S-4 group.
- Recognize EGLE’s identified distribution hydraulics and instrumentation domains at a high level.
- Recognize the cross-connection-control, safety, and contingencies or emergencies domains identified by EGLE.
- Recognize the Michigan Safe Drinking Water Act, recordkeeping, management, and customer-relations domains identified by EGLE, without making unsupported legal or operational assertions. [1]

The following are outside the permitted scope:

- Content asserted to be unique to S-1, S-2, S-3, or S-4, unless later supported by a current official individual-level outline or equivalent authoritative source.
- S-5, noncommunity, treatment, wastewater, collection, or non-Michigan material.
- Detailed regulatory thresholds, sampling schedules, notification or reporting requirements, construction specifications, disinfection parameters, cross-connection-device selection, engineering design, or calculation procedures unless a current official source supports the exact claim.
- Site-specific operating directions, emergency response directions, field work methods, safety procedures, or determinations about a particular water system.
- Any claim that an examination result alone confers certification, employment qualification, operator-in-charge authority, or authority to operate a facility.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The principal scope source is EGLE’s **Rev. 2/2026** certification-requirements document, which is current and gives the only located current public division of S examination domains: Levels 1–2 and Levels 3–4. EGLE’s October 2026 calendar and its 2026 examination-application page corroborate that the corresponding distribution examinations are actively administered. [1] [3] [4]

Michigan’s controlling statutory and Part 19 rule sources align with EGLE’s current program materials: the statute authorizes classification and examination of distribution operators, and Part 19 defines S-1 through S-5, requires appropriately classified supervision, and sets the examination framework for S-1 through S-4. No unresolved conflict was identified among those sources. [2] [6]

EGLE currently links a separate *Study Guide of Typical Exam Content for Waterworks Operator Certification Examinations — Distribution S Classifications*, but the document is revised July 2019. It separates S-1 through S-4 and says its subjects may change without notice. Its published availability does not cure its document-age limitation. It is therefore not used to establish a current individual-level boundary, objectives, or bank identity. [7]

The current Type I S distribution page and current practice-materials page confirm that EGLE supplies study materials for Levels 1–4, but neither page supplies a newer individual-level blueprint, weighting, or task-level mapping. [5] [8]

## Bank recommendation and release boundary

**Bank recommendation:** **No unique bank identity is recommended at this time.** The evidence supports only a narrow, review-only common-content candidate dossier for the Type I S-1–S-4 examination family. It does not support a separate bank for any individual S level, a comprehensive combined S-1–S-4 bank, or a shared bank with another jurisdiction. A later decision may name a bank only after EGLE publishes, or otherwise provides, a current official level-specific examination outline or a sufficiently detailed current official scope.

**Release boundary: candidate-only/no learner access.** This dossier authorizes no content creation, database change, activation, publication, pricing, purchase flow, entitlement, learner visibility, or release. Any future candidate material must remain review-only and must have atomic current-source support, structural validation, and independent review before a separate approval decision.
