## Credential and examination boundary

Louisiana’s governing program is the Louisiana Department of Health (LDH), Office of Public Health, Bureau of Engineering Services, Operator Certification Program, advised by the Louisiana Committee of Certification. The state’s current program page provides registration, a 2026 schedule, a Water Distribution “Need to Know,” the certification rule, and the official examination application. [1] [2]

The credential family is **Louisiana Water Distribution operator certification**, with **separate Class 1, Class 2, Class 3, and Class 4 examinations**. The official application lists each of these four Water Distribution exams distinctly. [3] The applicable occupational boundary is the portion of a water supply system that conveys water from the water-treatment plant or other supply point to consumers’ premises. [4]

The official rule identifies water distribution as a distinct certification category, requires its certification for the distribution portion of a water-supply system, and requires examinations to be passed sequentially from Class 1 through Class 4 within each category. [5] The current official examination outline separately states knowledge and mathematics expectations for each Water Distribution class; higher classes expressly include lower-class skills. [2]

This dossier supports a limited future candidate package only for the **Louisiana Water Distribution Class 1–4 examination family**, with every candidate labeled to one class. It does not establish eligibility for certification, replace required education or experience, or cover the separate post-exam Certification Law and Rules Examination.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The strongest current evidence is LDH’s live October 2026 program page, its linked active 2026 exam schedule, its current application, and the class-specific Water Distribution Need-to-Know outline. These sources agree on an active Louisiana administered Water Distribution exam route and four class levels. [1] [2] [3] [6]

The linked certification rule was promulgated in 2002 and uses former statutory citations, including R.S. 40:1141–1151 and the former agency name, Department of Health and Hospitals. The official Legislature now presents the relevant statutory provisions as R.S. 40:1281.1 et seq.; the definition page records a 2024 amendment. [4] The statutory renumbering and agency-name change do not conflict with the live LDH program materials, which still link the rule as the Operator Certification Rule. ## Learner-level scope and exclusions

**Learner level.** Candidates preparing for Louisiana Water Distribution Class 1, 2, 3, or 4 examinations. Class 1 is the foundational boundary. Class 2 adds applied calculation, hydraulics, pumps, storage, sampling, and disinfection topics. Class 3 adds system planning, emergency-continuity, preventive maintenance, loss control, instrumentation, pump-curve interpretation, legal responsibilities, and safety. Class 4 adds advanced pump-performance interpretation, economical replacement planning, costs, meter-shop functions, safety-program planning, public relations, flow-formula applications, and complex friction-loss calculations. [2]

**Included future candidate boundary.** High-level outcomes must be mapped to the matching official class and only to the listed Water Distribution Need-to-Know scope. A Class 2–4 outcome must account for the outline’s stated carry-forward of lower-class skills. [2]

## High-level, source-supported objectives

These are only objective labels for a future review-only candidate dossier. They are not questions, explanations, options, or bank data.

### Class 1

- Describe foundational distribution-system concepts: sources and characteristics of water, potable-water quality, appurtenances, construction materials, and installation methods. [2]
- Identify foundational distribution practices involving disinfection, residual chlorination, sampling, flushing, metering, pumps, cross-connections, safety, and applicable water-quality standards. [2]
- Perform the outline’s listed foundational math functions: unit conversions and area calculations. [2]

### Class 2

- Apply Class 1 concepts and calculate foundational distribution capacity, flow, volume, velocity, tank capacity, pressure, simple chemical dosage, detention/retention, and simple pipe friction loss. [2]
- Explain distribution-system sampling selection and common bacteriological-test meaning; identify cross-connections and basic corrective approaches. [2]
- Explain storage-tank sanitation and maintenance, basic distribution hydraulics, meter error, construction-material tradeoffs, and pump types and components. [2]

### Class 3

- Apply the lower-class scope to production-and-storage adequacy, sampling and cross-connection program planning, service continuity, preventive maintenance, water-loss control, and flow/pump-rate measurement. [2]
- Interpret basic distribution instrumentation and controls, pump capacity-head and performance curves, and main carrying-capacity loss causes and corrections. [2]
- Apply the listed Class 3 mathematics: pump capacity, horsepower and cost, pump and motor efficiency, water loss, chemical dosage, and equipment-capacity calculations. [2]

### Class 4

- Apply the lower-class scope to preparing and interpreting pump-performance curves from test data and to complex pipe friction-loss calculations. [2]
- Explain high-level planning concepts for pump replacement economics, cost reports, meter-shop functions, safety programs, public relations, and flow-formula applications. [2]
