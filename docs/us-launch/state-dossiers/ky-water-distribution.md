# Kentucky water-distribution operator certification readiness dossier

**Route ID:** `ky-water-distribution`
**State and stream:** Kentucky — drinking-water distribution
**Existing matrix label:** state-specific
**Research date:** October 29, 2026
**Decision:** **Narrow**

Kentucky has an active, state-administered drinking-water distribution certification route with four distribution classifications and active 2026 distribution testing events. A limited future **review-only** candidate dossier is supportable only for cross-class distribution quantitative competencies expressly supported by the currently posted official Distribution Math Study Guide. The evidence does **not** support a full Kentucky distribution question bank, a separately scoped Class I, II, III, or IV bank, or questions framed as current Kentucky regulatory duties. No current public official candidate handbook, examination blueprint, domain weighting, question count, or class-specific examination outline was located in the official materials reviewed. [1] [2] [5] [6] [7]

## Credential and examination boundary

The credential family is **Kentucky water distribution system operator certification**. The governing classification rule identifies **Class ID, Class IID, Class IIID, and Class IVD distribution certifications**. A certificate holder may be in direct responsible charge only within the class authority described by the rule; the Class IVD distribution credential may be in direct responsible charge of any water distribution system. [2]

For initial certification, the Cabinet requires the applicant to satisfy the applicable education and experience requirements and achieve at least 70 percent on a Cabinet-administered examination. A successful certificate designates the classification in which competency was demonstrated. [3] The Kentucky Operator Certification Program's 2026 schedule separately identifies **Distribution — Drinking Water** classes and associated Cabinet exam-only events. [5]

This dossier does not assume that a single unlabelled distribution examination has identical content or difficulty at every class. The public math guide groups its material as **Class I & II** and **Class III & IV**, but it does not publish an examination blueprint, scored-domain allocation, class-specific objective list, or statement that every listed topic is tested at a stated class. [7] Therefore, the controlled boundary is a narrow, cross-class quantitative preparation subset, not a representation of the full examination content for any individual credential class.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The permitted future learner level is an eligible Kentucky distribution-certification applicant or operator-in-training candidate preparing for the distribution examination, limited to quantitative concepts expressly covered in the active program's posted Distribution Math Study Guide. The classification rule makes the credential ladder experienced-operator oriented: standard Class ID, IID, and IIID applicants must meet high-school/GED and progressive distribution-operating-experience requirements, while Class IVD requires a qualifying baccalaureate degree and qualifying distribution experience. Entry-level Class ID Operator-in-Training applicants are an exception to the experience requirement. [2]

The permitted scope is deliberately narrower than the credential family:

- Cross-class distribution calculations and basic quantitative relationships that the official guide expressly associates with the distribution manual and examination. [7]
- General mathematical concepts that can be stated without asserting a current Kentucky legal threshold, site operating procedure, or undisclosed class-specific test allocation.

The following are excluded from the future candidate dossier: water-treatment, groundwater-treatment, bottled-water, wastewater, and collection content; credential eligibility, fees, application workflow, renewal, reciprocity, disciplinary action, and continuing-education requirements; direct-responsible-charge or staffing determinations; population-classification thresholds; equipment-specific operating instructions; chemical-handling procedures; cross-connection compliance obligations; and any representation of current test weights, question counts, passing strategy, or class-specific blueprint. The 2013 manual may not be used to establish a current legal or regulatory requirement. [3] [4] [8]

## High-level objectives permitted for a Narrow future candidate dossier

These objectives are planning boundaries only. They are not questions, item specifications, answer keys, or released learner content.

1. Apply fundamental distribution-system calculations involving flow, volume, demand, and unit conversion.
2. Apply basic hydraulic quantitative relationships relevant to distribution systems, including velocity, pressure/head, and friction-loss relationships.
3. Apply calculation methods for basic storage, pumping, and disinfectant-quantity scenarios that are expressly represented in the official Distribution Math Study Guide.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The regulatory foundation is current enough for a limited readiness decision: the current 401 KAR 11:040 page records a certification to amend due in 2028, and the program's live 2026 schedule demonstrates current distribution testing activity. [2] [5] The LRC rule pages are state-legislature sources, but the Cabinet expressly advises that the LRC web versions are not the official versions. Before any future candidate batch is prepared, the cited rule text must be reconfirmed against the Kentucky Administrative Regulations Service or current Administrative Register, particularly if the effective text changes. [1]

There are three material limitations. First, 401 KAR 8:030 displays a November 1, 2026 expiration date, only days after this retrieval. Its population and staffing details must be rechecked and are excluded from candidate authoring. [4] Second, the only public topical manual is revised February 12, 2013. Its continuing presence on the current test-preparation page supports its use as an official study resource, not an inference that it is a current examination blueprint or source of legal duties. [6] [8] Third, the undated fact sheet states Class IID/IIID experience differently from current 401 KAR 11:040. The current rule controls; the fact sheet is excluded from detailed eligibility and all authoring use. [2] [9]

No current official public candidate handbook, examination outline, content-domain weighting, number-of-questions statement, or class-specific exam blueprint was found in the current official program pages, rules, schedule, and downloadable study materials reviewed. This is a limitation of the evidence reviewed, not a claim that such material cannot exist elsewhere in the Cabinet's controlled records.

## Decision and bank recommendation

**Decision: Narrow.** Kentucky satisfies the threshold for a bounded future review-only candidate dossier because the state has a specific, active distribution credential ladder, Cabinet-administered examinations, current 2026 distribution exam events, and a current program page that hosts a distribution math guide expressly tied to the manual and examination. [2] [3] [5] [6] [7]

The restriction is material. The available official evidence supports only a tightly limited mathematical subset across the distribution credential family. It does not establish enough current, class-specific examination scope to author a full bank, claim alignment with a particular class examination, or use the 2013 manual as a complete current blueprint.

**Bank recommendation:** Do **not** propose or create a new distinct Kentucky bank identity at this stage. A future review-only candidate dossier may be considered only as a temporary, non-public Kentucky water-distribution quantitative subset after: (1) reconfirming the effective regulatory text, (2) confirming the still-current status of the official math guide, (3) checking the recovered immutable inventory for overlap with `us-class1-water-dist`, and (4) obtaining a current Cabinet-issued distribution exam outline or written confirmation of the subset's continuing applicability. A unique bank can be considered only if those controls demonstrate a non-overlapping Kentucky-specific examination scope.
