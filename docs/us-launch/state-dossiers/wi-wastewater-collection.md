## Decision

**Suitable** for a limited, future review-only candidate dossier at one exact boundary: the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources (DNR) **Wastewater — Subclass SS, Sanitary Sewer Collection System, basic certification examination**. This is a distinct Wisconsin DNR-administered, 50-question, closed-book multiple-choice examination. The current DNR wastewater-certification page identifies SS as the collection-system subclass, says that collection certification requires the 50-question collection-systems examination without the Basic General Wastewater examination, and states that basic certification is the only level required for collection systems. [1] [2]

The Wisconsin Administrative Code independently establishes the operative collection-system setting and the applicable exception: a sanitary sewage collection system consists of common sanitary sewers, interceptor sewers, pump stations, and supporting equipment that convey wastewater to a treatment plant; owners of treatment plants that include such a system must have a designated collection-system operator-in-charge certified in the SS subclass, while satellite-system certification is voluntary. The rule also provides that the general wastewater examination is not required for an SS basic certification. [3]

The boundary is sufficiently specific and the official DNR study guide is sufficiently organized for a limited future candidate dossier. The guide expressly states that its chapters and key knowledges prepare candidates for the SS certification examination and that every test question and answer is drawn from those key knowledges. [4]

## Credential and examination boundary

The only eligible future package boundary is:

> **Wisconsin DNR Wastewater Operator Certification — Subclass SS: Sanitary Sewer Collection System, basic certification examination.**

This is not a general wastewater-treatment examination. The SS examination is separately listed on the current DNR 2026 wastewater application as “Subclass SS — Sanitary Sewer Collection System,” and the current DNR exams page lists it as a 50-question wastewater examination. The DNR states that Wisconsin wastewater examinations are closed book and are administered in person on paper in 2026. [2] [5]

Passing the SS examination is not, by itself, the whole credential determination. The applicable rule requires a passing score of at least 75 percent on an examination, and it ties basic-level operator qualification generally to the specific subclass examination and one year of satisfactory subclass-specific experience. The special SS exception removes the Basic General Wastewater examination from the basic-certification requirements; it does not eliminate DNR’s certification, application, or experience determinations. [3]

The future package must not combine this route with Wisconsin Wastewater — General; treatment subclasses A1 through U; the wastewater advanced examination; waterworks, small-water-system, septage, landfill, or private-water credentials; or any other state’s collection credential. The Wisconsin DNR identifies SS as its own wastewater subclass and its current application presents it as a distinct requested examination. [1] [5]

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The future learner is a candidate for the Wisconsin DNR **basic SS collection-system certification examination**, not a general wastewater-treatment or advanced-certification candidate. The source-supported scope is foundational collection-system knowledge identified in the DNR SS guide, not independent field authorization or proof of competence. [1] [4]

High-level objectives for the suitable boundary are limited to the following official guide categories:

- Recognize collection-system terminology and the high-level function of collection assets, including pipes, manholes, lift stations, flow-monitoring stations, sampling, and SCADA.
- Recognize the safety subject areas identified for the SS examination, including confined-space and atmospheric hazards, electrical hazards, excavation and trenching, hazardous energy, personal protective equipment, and traffic or work-zone hazards.
- Recognize foundational operation-and-maintenance concepts for wastewater conveyance, cleaning, fats, oils, and grease, inspections, and lift-station maintenance.
- Recognize the guide’s high-level coverage of rehabilitation or replacement, records and mapping, and ordinances or regulations relevant to collection systems.
- Apply only the foundational calculation categories named by the guide: sampling, flow conversion and flow rate, tank area and volume, pounds formula, pump rate, detention time, and sewer grading.

These are future objective labels only. They are not question prompts, answers, explanations, procedures, calculations, or learner material. [4]

The following are excluded from any future candidate dossier unless a then-current official source directly supports the narrower claim:

- Basic General Wastewater, all other wastewater subclasses, wastewater treatment-plant process operation, and advanced wastewater certification.
- Certification eligibility decisions, experience verification, operator-in-charge designation, reciprocity, renewal, continuing education, application processing, fee handling, or exam scheduling beyond factual candidate metadata required for a future controlled review.
- Facility-specific operations, lift-station settings, work orders, emergency-response plans, local sewer-use ordinances, WPDES permit conditions, manufacturer instructions, and site design or rehabilitation decisions.
- Safety-critical procedural instruction; confined-space, excavation, traffic-control, electrical, lockout/tagout, or hazardous-material steps; and federal, state, local, or permit-specific legal duties, thresholds, or reporting timeframes unless separately sourced to a current authority and independently reviewed by an appropriately qualified reviewer.
- Satellite-system certification status as a generic eligibility claim. The rule makes it voluntary for satellite systems, whereas the DNR recommends certification; neither statement changes the SS examination boundary. [1] [3] [4]

## Source freshness and conflict assessment

The credential and examination boundary is current and specific. The current DNR program page is actively publishing 2026 in-person paper examination administration, and the current DNR application was revised in October 2025 for the 2026 cycle. The Legislature’s Chapter NR 114 page identifies the code as updated on the first day of each month and as the current code. [2] [3] [5]

The detailed content source is older: the official SS guide is labelled **August 2018 Edition**. It remains the guide that DNR currently links from its wastewater-certification page, and the current 2026 application directs candidates to that page for study guides. This supports a limited future review-only dossier, but it is a material freshness limitation. Before any candidate authoring, the owner must recheck that DNR still posts this guide for SS and must identify any DNR replacement, amendment, or superseding official examination outline. A changed guide, changed examination format, or changed regulatory scope requires a new readiness decision. [1] [4] [5]

No material conflict was found. Chapter NR 114 states the general operator-in-training and basic-level framework in terms of the general wastewater and subclass examinations, while the more specific NR 114.57(4) exception removes the general examination from SS basic certification. The current DNR program page states the same SS exception. The sources therefore align when the specific SS provision controls the general framework. [1] [3]

The guide cites other authorities and resources, including state code, OSHA, and Water Environment Federation material. They are not treated as independent examination sources in this dossier and must not be used to extend the future bank beyond the SS guide’s own key-knowledge boundary without current, direct official support. [4]
