## Credential and examination boundary

The verified Pennsylvania route is the **wastewater Class E, Subclassification 4 (E4) standalone certificate**, identified by DEP as **WWE4 — Wastewater Collections**. It is the collection-system examination route for satellite collection systems and single-entity collection systems. It is not the wastewater Part I General Examination, a wastewater treatment Part II technology examination, or a treatment-system Class A, B, C, or D credential. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Pennsylvania’s current regulation requires a separate stand-alone examination for wastewater collection systems, either satellite or single entity. For wastewater certification, the collection-system examination is an alternative named in the examination requirements. The current regulation also provides that Class E combined with Subclassification 4 is a standalone certificate for collection-system operators who meet certification requirements. [1] [2] [3]

DEP’s current examination pages identify the E4 route as a stand-alone wastewater collection certification. DEP states that WWE4 may later be combined with wastewater A, B, C, or D certifications and other subclasses, but that possible combination does not broaden the scope of this dossier beyond the E4 collection examination. [4] [5]

Initial certification remains conditional on the Board’s requirements beyond exam passage, including the applicable education, experience, application, and Board certification requirements. Under the current regulation, the Track I minimum for wastewater Class E is one year of experience; the education-adjusted Track II table lists six months for Class E. These admission and certification conditions are not proposed as bank content. [6] [7]

## Decision and bank recommendation

**Suitable.** The current Pennsylvania Code establishes a distinct, regulated collection-system examination and certificate boundary. Current DEP examination pages identify the same WWE4 route as stand-alone, and current DEP study-preparation materials map collection-specific modules to **Class E, Subclass 4**. Those materials support a limited future candidate dossier without treating wastewater-treatment content as collection-exam content. [1] [3] [4] [8] [9]

**Proposed unique bank identity, if a future candidate package is authorized:** `pa-wastewater-collection-e4`.

The proposed identity is evidence-supported because it maps to one distinct Pennsylvania credential/exam family: the wastewater E4 standalone collection-systems examination. It must not be represented as a bank for wastewater treatment classes A–D, wastewater Part I General, wastewater treatment subclasses 1–3 or 5, drinking-water distribution, or a multistate collection credential. [2] [3] [4] [5]

## Candidate learner level, scope, and exclusions

The intended learner is an applicant preparing for the Pennsylvania **WWE4 / wastewater Class E, Subclassification 4** collection-systems examination. The scope is entry-to-practitioner collection-system operations knowledge appropriate to the state’s stand-alone E4 exam route, not independent authority to make process-control decisions or a substitute for supervised field training, owner procedures, permits, or regulator direction. [1] [5] [7]

A future limited candidate dossier may use only the following high-level objectives, subject to item-level evidence records and independent review:

- Recognize wastewater collection-system types, principal components, appurtenances, and collection versus conveyance context.
- Distinguish the operational significance of sanitary and combined collection systems, infiltration and inflow, inspection, cleaning, and rehabilitation concepts.
- Recognize collection pumping-station types, major components, and routine operations-and-maintenance recordkeeping concepts.
- Recognize the role of industrial pretreatment fundamentals in protecting collection and publicly owned treatment works systems.
- Recognize collection-system work hazards and the applicable high-level safety domains: traffic control, confined spaces, excavation, and electrical hazards.
- Apply basic collection-system flow, pump/hydraulics, maintenance, flowmeter, and math concepts only where the official E4 preparation materials support the objective.

These objectives are bounded by DEP’s currently linked E4 preparation mapping and its collection-system and pretreatment modules. [9] [10] [11] [12]

Excluded from the future E4 candidate scope are: wastewater-treatment process control for subclasses 1–3; wastewater laboratory-supervisor content; water-system exams; treatment-system Part I General content except where DEP explicitly maps a basic topic to E4; site-specific design, construction approval, permit interpretation, or emergency response instructions; numerical permit limits; local sewer-use ordinances; and professional engineering decisions. The curriculum must not derive a universal Pennsylvania legal duty from a general training module.

## Freshness and conflict handling

The governing regulatory sources were retrieved from the current Pennsylvania Code, which reports changes effective through August 8, 2026. DEP’s current program pages were retrieved on October 29, 2026, and the linked 2026 examination schedule is revised October 8, 2026. The study modules are labelled revised or updated in 2020 and the wastewater training-advice document is dated 2024. DEP’s current preparation and training-module pages continue to direct candidates to these materials and map them to E4; they are used only as scope evidence, not as current legal authority. [1] [5] [8] [9] [10] [11] [12] [13]

An older DEP examination-registration form, revised December 2021, contains wording that appears to invert the ownership relationship in its narrative definition of Subclassification 4 while elsewhere identifying the E4 exam as covering satellite and single-entity-owner collection systems. It is not used to establish the facility-ownership definition, legal boundary, or candidate objective. The current Code and current DEP definitions page control the terms used in this dossier. This is a resolved terminology limitation, not a reason to expand the proposed scope. [1] [3] [4]

No current official source reviewed publishes a numerical test blueprint, item weights, released questions, or examination content. The future candidate package therefore must remain limited to the official E4-mapped high-level objectives above and must not infer exam weighting, item counts, or tested legal thresholds.
