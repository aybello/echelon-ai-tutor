## Credential and examination boundary

The evaluated boundary is only:

> **New Mexico Environment Department Utility Operator Certification Program — Wastewater Collection System – Level 1 (CS1) written certification examination, as described in the March 2026 CS1 Operator Guidebook.**

The current rule establishes two wastewater-collection certification levels, CS1 and CS2. It defines a collection system as the pipelines or conduits, pumping stations, force mains, and associated devices or facilities used to collect and conduct waste to treatment and disposal. It also requires a written examination for the level of certification sought. [1]

For CS1, the official guidebook states that the examination contains 100 multiple-choice questions, has a minimum passing score of 70%, and covers the following content areas: collection systems (32 questions); cross-connection control (3); general (23); mechanical systems (20); regulations (3); safety (17); and sampling and reporting (2). [3]

The standard eligibility pathway is separate from the examination-content boundary: high-school graduation or a GED, one year of qualifying experience, and ten approved training credits. NMED may evaluate the rule’s stated substitutions case by case. Eligibility assessment, application acceptance, experience substitution, fees, and certification issuance remain solely NMED functions and are not candidate-package content. [1] [2] [4]

The boundary does **not** include CS2; Small Wastewater or Advanced Small Wastewater; public wastewater facility treatment credentials; wastewater laboratory credentials; drinking-water credentials; certification from another jurisdiction; equivalency determinations; temporary certification; or site-specific authorization to operate. It also does not make a legal determination about the certification level required at a given facility population.

## Learner-level scope and exclusions

The future audience is a CS1 examination candidate in New Mexico. CS1 is a distinct first-level collection-system credential, but NMED’s standard application pathway presupposes secondary education or equivalent, qualifying experience, and approved training. Material must therefore be framed for an adult operator candidate, not as unsupervised field instruction. [1] [3] [4]

If future source-linked candidates are authorized, the supported high-level objectives are limited to the current CS1 technical outline:

- Recognize foundational collection-system components, layouts, maps, service connections, cleaning, preventive maintenance, stoppage response concepts, and lift-station concepts named in the CS1 outline.
- Apply the guidebook’s introductory collection-system math, flow, hydraulics, measurement-unit, wastewater-characteristic, and removal-efficiency knowledge areas at the CS1 examination level.
- Recognize the CS1 mechanical-system categories and their stated operations-and-maintenance concepts for chemical feeders, metering equipment, motors, pumps, and valves.
- Recognize the listed cross-connection-control concepts and the safety subjects within the CS1 outline, including chemical, confined-space, electrical, excavation, traffic, hazardous-gas, SDS, PPE, and rotating-machinery awareness.
- Distinguish the guidebook’s listed sampling-and-reporting categories at a high level, including grab and composite sampling and the named gas and toxicity topics.

The following are excluded from any future CS1 candidate dossier:

- Any legal classification claim keyed to facility population served, including whether CS1, CS2, or Small Wastewater certification is required for a given population band. This exclusion is mandatory until NMED reconciles its guidebook with the published NMAC rule. [1] [3]
- All regulatory questions or explanations that depend on an unresolved version, population threshold, permit-specific duty, reporting timeframe, or site-specific permit condition. The guidebook’s three-question Regulations content area is not a candidate-authoring basis without a then-current, clause-specific primary source and independent qualified review. [1] [3]
- CS2 and every other NMED credential or level, including wastewater treatment, laboratory, drinking-water, distribution, sampling, and small-system routes.
- Site-specific operating instructions; employer policies; emergency-response, confined-space-entry, excavation, electrical, chemical, traffic-control, or other safety-critical procedures; local permits; and hands-on competence assessments.
- Any assertion that review-only material qualifies a person to operate, substitutes for NMED experience or approved training, or assures examination eligibility or certification.

## Source freshness and conflict assessment

The principal examination-outline source is current and specific. NMED’s March 2026 CS1 guidebook was reviewed by the advisory board in January and February 2026; NMED’s live certification page still links it as the CS1 guidebook at retrieval. The April 2026 eligibility pamphlet is supplementary and current enough to confirm that individual eligibility is not a fixed content claim. [2] [3] [4]

The controlling rule is published on the current official New Mexico Administrative Code site. Its section histories show that the collection-system certification table was amended on January 15, 2011, and its CS1 application and minimum-requirement sections were amended on November 15, 2006. Those dates do not make the rule inoperative; they require it to be treated as the binding legal text unless and until NMED publishes a superseding amendment. [1]

There is a material, localized conflict. The NMAC table at 20.7.4.13(B) assigns **SWW** to collection systems serving 25–500, **CS1** to 501–5,000 and 5,001–10,000, and **CS2** to 10,001–20,000 and 20,000+. By contrast, the March 2026 CS1 guidebook’s page-one table depicts CS1 for 25–500 and 501–5,000 and states that its left-pointing arrow signifies CS1 also covers lower categories. Because the guidebook expressly attributes that display to 20.7.4.13(B), the discrepancy cannot safely be resolved by inference. [1] [3]

This discrepancy does not prevent a limited CS1 technical-exam dossier: the credential identity, written-exam boundary, question count, passing score, and technical content outline are clear and current. It does prohibit using either source as a standalone basis for a population-band certification claim or a CS1/SWW/CS2 legal-comparison candidate. Before authoring any regulatory content, a future owner must obtain an NMED clarification or a current amended rule and rerun this conflict check. Any material new edition, revised blueprint, rule amendment, or changed CS1 test format also requires a new readiness review.
