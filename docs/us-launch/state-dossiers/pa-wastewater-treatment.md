- **Readiness outcome:** The full wastewater-treatment route is not one examination. Pennsylvania requires the wastewater Part I General Examination plus one or more Part II technology-specific examinations for wastewater certification. The defensibly bounded future package is the common **Wastewater Part I General Examination** only. It is preparation for one required examination component, not a certificate and not complete preparation for any technology-specific treatment credential. [1] [2] [5]

## Credential and examination boundary

The credentialing authority is the **Pennsylvania State Board for Certification of Water and Wastewater Systems Operators**, with examination administration by the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP). The Board administers DEP examinations, reviews applications, and issues certification; DEP’s current initial-certification page confirms that certification also requires education, operating experience, application, criminal-history documentation, and Board approval. Passing examinations alone does not confer a certificate. [8] [9]

For a Pennsylvania wastewater treatment operator seeking an A, B, C, or D wastewater certificate, the current examination boundary is **Part I General Examination plus one or more Part II Treatment Technology Specific Examinations**. The current rule also recognizes the wastewater master examination and the separate collection-system examination, but neither is within this narrowed treatment-package boundary. [1]

The common Part I examination applies across wastewater treatment technologies. The applicable Part II examination depends on the facility’s treatment technology. Pennsylvania’s current categories are: Subclassification 1, Activated Sludge; Subclassification 2, Fixed Film; and Subclassification 3, Treatment Ponds and Lagoons. A wastewater Class E / Subclassification 4 collection credential is a stand-alone collection-system certificate and belongs to the separate wastewater-collection route, not this dossier. Laboratory Supervisor Subclassification 5 is also distinct. [3] [4] [5]

The A–D class is tied to system flow classification, while the Part II technology examination is tied to the treatment technology used at the facility. This dossier does not represent that one common Part I examination gives an operator authority to work at all classes or technologies. [3] [4] [5]

## Why the decision is Narrow

The exact state credential and examination structure are current, official, and specific. DEP’s current 2026 exam page says wastewater Class A–D examinees take both a Part I General Exam and a Part II technology-specific exam. DEP’s current preparation page links the Part I and Part II wastewater preparation resources; the current 2024 Part II resource separately maps active exam components to DEP materials. [5] [6] [7]

A full, undifferentiated Pennsylvania wastewater-treatment bank would be overbroad because an examinee needs the technology-specific Part II examination or examinations that match the plant. The route therefore supports a bounded future dossier for the shared Part I General Examination, but does not support one bank presented as complete preparation for all Pennsylvania wastewater-treatment certifications. The activated-sludge, fixed-film, treatment-ponds-and-lagoons, laboratory-supervisor, collection, and master boundaries require separate scope decisions before any future candidate work.

## Conditional bank recommendation

**Propose one limited, unique future bank identity only:** **PA Wastewater Treatment — Part I General Examination** (proposed identity: **pa-wastewater-treatment-part1-general**).

This recommendation is conditional and does not authorize authoring, data creation, or release. The identity is justified because Part I is an expressly named common examination component in the current rule and current DEP examination guidance. It must be labeled as a Part I component only, with no claim that it independently prepares a learner for certification. [1] [5]

Do **not** propose a single whole-route bank named simply “Pennsylvania Wastewater Treatment Operator Certification,” a wastewater master bank, a collection-system bank, or any Part II technology-specific bank from this dossier. Those products would have a different examination boundary.

## High-level objectives for the narrow future scope

If a later controlled review authorizes a candidate dossier for the proposed Part I bank, its objectives must remain high level and traceable to the current DEP Part I module mapping and current preparation guidance:

1. Recognize the general operational context of a wastewater treatment system, including introductory and fundamental treatment concepts.
2. Identify the general purpose and operational role of disinfection and chlorination, solids handling and disposal, and chemical-feed systems.
3. Recognize foundational equipment and support-system concepts associated with pumps, hydraulics, maintenance, and flow measurement.
4. Recognize the general purpose of wastewater laboratory overview, treatment-plant administration, industrial pretreatment, basic math, general chemistry, and safety as Part I preparation areas.

These are topic-bound objectives only. They are not permission to infer process-control thresholds, permit requirements, equipment settings, calculations, laboratory methods, safety procedures, or operational responses. DEP’s current module index maps these named wastewater modules to the Part I General Exam, and DEP’s 2024 preparation resource lists the corresponding Part I module set. [7] [8]

## Learner level and exclusions

**Learner level.** The potential learner is an applicant preparing for Pennsylvania’s common wastewater Part I General Examination. The learner may be pre-certification; DEP states that an applicant may take an examination before meeting experience requirements, but certification requires all additional statutory and Board requirements. [9]

**Included only.** General wastewater-treatment examination preparation that is common to the Part I General Examination and supported by current DEP mappings.

**Excluded.**

- Part II Activated Sludge, Fixed Film, Treatment Ponds and Lagoons, and Laboratory Supervisor examinations.
- The wastewater master examination.
- Wastewater Class E / Subclassification 4 collection-system examination, including satellite and single-entity collection-system content.
- Drinking-water certifications or examinations.
- Site-specific permit requirements, NPDES limits, local conditions, facility-specific standard operating procedures, operating setpoints, process-control directions, emergency response, detailed laboratory methods, design engineering, or unsourced calculation rules.
- Any implication that a Part I score independently grants a Pennsylvania operator certificate or authority to make process-control decisions.

## Source freshness and conflicts

The controlling legal sources are current: the official Pennsylvania Code pages state that the Code reflects changes effective through August 8, 2026. DEP’s active examination page links a 2026 schedule revised October 8, 2026. These sources control the credential, examination, class, and subclass boundary. [1] [2] [3] [5] [10]

DEP’s 2024 wastewater reference-material document and live module index provide current preparation mapping, but neither is treated as a current question-distribution blueprint. They support only the narrow, high-level Part I topic boundary in this dossier. [7] [8]

DEP still links an official *Operator Certification Program Handbook* issued January 26, 2013. Its Appendix A contains detailed need-to-know criteria, but it is not used as a current source for numerical item counts, passing counts, or a current blueprint. A legacy 2016 DEP Module 1 PDF also reverses the labels of wastewater Subclassifications 2 and 3 when compared with the current Pennsylvania Code, current DEP Definitions page, current 2024 preparation document, and live module index. That legacy conflict is material: any later candidate work must use the current labels—Subclass 2 Fixed Film and Subclass 3 Treatment Ponds and Lagoons—and must not use the legacy module’s classification table. [3] [4] [7] [8]

No conflict was found among the current rule, current DEP class definitions, current 2026 examination page, current 2024 training-reference document, and current module mapping on the Part I-plus-Part II structure or on the active Subclass 1–3 treatment names.
