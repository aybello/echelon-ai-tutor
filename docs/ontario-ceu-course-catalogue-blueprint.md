# Ontario CEU Course Catalogue Blueprint

## Purpose

Echelon Institute is preparing a three-course professional-learning catalogue for Ontario water and wastewater operators. The catalogue is designed to create a new revenue line beside exam-preparation passes. It is **not** currently advertised as Director approved, accredited, assigned a CEU value, or accepted for any operator renewal requirement.

The first commercial release should be an approval-ready catalogue page and a lead list. The paid delivery release should follow only after each course has the applicable review outcome, final instructor and designer evidence, assessed course status, and a confirmed commercial offer.

## Ontario approval paths

Ontario drinking-water continuing-education training requires Director approval before it can be used to meet the drinking-water continuing-education requirement. The provider submits a course-assessment application through WWOCS with the required supporting materials. The Director determines the assessed CEU value, Course ID, and renewal date. A Director-approved course is measured in contact time. One CEU represents ten contact hours, course hours are rounded down to a full hour, and no more than seven course hours are credited in one day.[1]

Wastewater-only courses cannot be Director approved for drinking-water renewal. They can be submitted to OWWCO for wastewater-course review to determine course length or CEU value. After assessment, the course can be made available in WWOCS for operator records.[2]

> The catalogue must not use terms such as **Director approved**, **MECP approved**, **OWWCO approved**, **accredited**, or an assessed CEU amount until the relevant written outcome is received. The ministry and OWWCO also do not endorse the provider or course content.[1] [2]

## Course 1: Drinking Water Operations and Regulatory Compliance

This course is intended for experienced Ontario drinking-water operators who are responsible for operational decisions, compliance records, incident response, and shift oversight. Learners will map operator duties and escalation responsibilities, evaluate control-limit deviations and results, document defensible corrective action, and assemble traceable operational evidence.

The course includes six modules: Ontario drinking-water compliance map and operator accountability; operating within control limits; sampling, testing, and result review; adverse results and corrective-action decision-making; records and audit-ready evidence; and an integrated compliance practicum. The planned timetable totals seven contact hours. Breaks are excluded.

The course must use fictional scenarios and general operating principles. It must not replace site-specific procedures, legal advice, emergency direction, operational plans, approvals, licences, or an employer's instructions.

## Course 2: Water Treatment Process Control and Optimization

This course is intended for experienced Ontario water treatment operators who interpret process data, troubleshoot treatment performance, and evaluate operational adjustments. Learners will interpret raw-water changes and treatment trends, evaluate treatment-process adjustments within defined limits, validate instrumentation and sampling data, and develop evidence-based optimization responses.

The six modules are: process-control foundations and Ontario operating context; raw-water characterization and treatment-train response; coagulation, flocculation, clarification, and filtration optimization; disinfection process control and treated-water protection; instrumentation, SCADA, sampling, and data integrity; and optimization decisions, upset response, and continuous improvement. The planned timetable totals seven contact hours. Breaks are excluded.

The course must remain general, non-promotional, and non-site-specific. It must never be positioned as a substitute for local operating procedures or professional engineering support.

## Course 3: Wastewater Treatment Operations and Process Control

This course is intended for experienced Ontario wastewater treatment operators who are responsible for biological process control, clarification, solids management, and effluent performance. Learners will interpret monitoring data, diagnose biological and clarification performance, evaluate nutrient and solids-management interactions, and defend operational priorities in an integrated virtual shift.

The six modules are: Ontario operator context and treatment-process overview; monitoring, sampling awareness, data quality, and records; biological treatment and secondary clarification control; process-control calculations and troubleshooting; nutrients, final treatment, solids, and effluent protection; and an integrated virtual shift with instructor debrief. The planned timetable totals seven contact hours. Breaks are excluded.

The course should be submitted for OWWCO wastewater-course review. It cannot be advertised as Director approved for drinking-water renewal.[2]

## Required delivery evidence

Each course should be built as structured learning rather than passive video. The provider guide requires distance learning to include learner-provider contact, engagement, and attendance verification. Supporting evidence should include knowledge checks, interactive scenarios, assignments or case work, scheduled instructor Q&A, participation records, and a final assessment. A completion certificate should be issued only after verified attendance and the documented successful-completion standard are met.[1]

The submission package for each course should include documented learning objectives, training materials, a timetable, learner-verification methods, engagement and attendance methods, a sample completion record, an anonymous course and instructor evaluation, and designer and instructor qualifications.[1]

## Implementation sequence

The current public catalogue is a safe market-validation stage. It publishes learning-path titles, professional-development scope, planned contact time, and a course-update request form. It intentionally does not show a price, checkout, Course ID, completion certificate, CEU value, or accreditation claim.

The next operational stage is to finalize the course materials, assessments, instructor plan, attendance controls, evaluation form, completion policy, and supporting evidence. Echelon should then establish or use its WWOCS training-provider account, submit each drinking-water course for Director approval, and submit the wastewater course for OWWCO review.[2]

After written review outcomes are received, the product team can add the assessed course status, approved wording where permitted, course record format, pricing, checkout, entitlements, progress tracking, completion controls, and any required WWOCS completion uploads. Director approval is valid for three years and must be reassessed before the renewal date.[2]
