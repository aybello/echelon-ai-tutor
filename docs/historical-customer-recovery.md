# Historical Customer Recovery Controls

The recovery process begins with a private Stripe export. The export must never be placed in the repository, attached to public assets, or pasted into chat. The normalizer accepts a file path outside the repository, produces a non-sensitive manifest by default, and does not write evidence rows unless a one-time operator approval variable is present.

Evidence staging is not entitlement recovery. Staged rows have no product mapping, no claimant verification, no approval, and no ability to create purchases, subscriptions, accounts, access tokens, or course access. A later recovery operation must require a separate written authorization, a reviewed product mapping, verified control of the original purchase email, and an approved evidence row. Refunded, disputed, duplicate, or already-imported evidence must fail closed.

The live application must use the Echelon Institute Stripe merchant account. Do not use a stale sandbox credential to retrieve or stage evidence. Use the owner-controlled Stripe dashboard export and record only its private archive reference and SHA-256 manifest in recovery operations.

The optional `--summary` output is a category-level recovery review only. It deliberately excludes customer identifiers, raw descriptions, payment IDs, and customer-level amounts. Its product labels are conservative candidates, not entitlement decisions. Every candidate still requires claimant verification and explicit approval before any import.

## September 18, 2026 controlled recovery record

Following explicit owner authorization to restore customer access, the protected pre-reset archive and protected Stripe evidence were reconciled through bounded, idempotent recovery operations. The operations restored verified paid permanent purchase entitlements, exact active individual subscription terms, and a small set of payment-and-correspondence-verified records. The source export, customer identities, payment references, backup artifacts, and detailed reconciliation manifests remain outside the repository.

The recovery operations are recorded privately under the `2026-09-18` recovery runbooks. They are bound to the protected archive SHA-256 `eeebf74d564065c8d931043fa76adcb1a410ff6da5043f302ab2af31c5d17646` and the reviewed five-record plan digest `b68935d7b06c03b558f5a51a57ed2c9a69797a5ffefcc1365288c0ebb7fe824d`. The protected before-images and separately escrowed keys are retained for a minimum of 365 days after recovery closure by the owner or a designated recovery custodian. A recovery reversal requires explicit owner approval and a record-by-record operation based on the protected before-image. After an ambiguous result, operators must halt, make no further mutations, and escalate to the owner for record-by-record reconciliation against that before-image. Operators must not rerun an importer after an ambiguous result.

Verification standards differed by source and are recorded deliberately. Exact archive purchases and active subscription rows were restored by record parity against the protected pre-reset archive. The five ambiguous payment records required successful payment evidence plus direct course correspondence. The owner authorized this archive-led recovery exception in place of separate claimant submissions. This supersedes the default claimant-submission requirement above for the September 18, 2026 event only; claimant verification remains the default for every future import. Twenty-seven active zero-value archive rows were excluded because their archive `amountCAD` value in the pre-reset archive schema captured on September 18, 2026 was zero and they did not establish a paid customer purchase. Those rows remain preserved privately for case-by-case claimant review. No user accounts, Stripe objects, organization seats, or outbound messages were created by the individual-access recovery operations. Every restored entitlement was verified against the live entitlement resolver.
