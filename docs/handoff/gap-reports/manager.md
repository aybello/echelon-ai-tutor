# Required topic checklist: So You Want to be a Utility Manager vs Royce U `ump` (400 slides, read in full 2026-11-17)

Teach in our own words, with our own scenarios. Do not copy Royce U wording, questions, case stories or images. Financial figures, rate values and ERC examples are illustrative only: use our own numbers and say each is an example. Do not state any tax, rate, interest or regulatory figure as a universal rule.

## Manager's responsibilities
- Overview: the manager is responsible for properly using and protecting the utility's resources (people, money, equipment, compliance). Why utilities exist in public health terms; passive protection against health hazards (purpose only).

## Compliance
- Compliance with local, state and federal rules; facility design versus operation (the operating permit governs); permit compliance as top priority; non-compliance consequences (consent orders, public notice, legal exposure); chain of custody and records; trade associations and periodicals; relationships with regulators (be known as trustworthy and competent); learning the rules and staying current.
- Safety of employees and the public; accidents and liability; insurance and risk reduction (purpose).

## Financial resources: revenues
- Funding sources: general fund (tax-funded, competes with other services) versus enterprise fund (utility pays its own way through rates); why utilities usually use enterprise funds.
- Rate structures: fixed charges, volume charges, block rates (conservation effect), capital cost recovery, operating cost recovery, master-metered multifamily and how to allocate, equivalent residential units (ERCs) as a concept.
- Impact fees: what they cover and why they are charged for growth.
- Capital improvement plan (CIP) as a planning tool.

## Financial resources: capital budget requests
- Capital projects driven by regulation, growth, replacement and safety; equipment life and replacement planning; efficiency and payback concept; SCADA or automation as a capital case; risk ranking; how to justify a project to elected officials; building a case with data, not ego.

## Financial resources: commodity (operating) budget requests
- Operating budgets: forecasting chemicals, electricity, materials, contract services; units consumed; fees; outsourcing decisions; federal and state funding (no figures); using records to build the next year's budget; line items.

## Utilisation of people
- Management theories and their uses: scientific management (Taylor), process approach and Fayol's principles, Hawthorne effect (attention and motivation), Maslow's hierarchy of needs, management by objectives (MBO), management styles (authoritarian, consultative, laissez-faire, democratic) and the management-by-approach list (coaching, consensus, exception, information, objectives, performance, etc.) at recognition level.
- Communication, delegation, coaching and decision-making.
