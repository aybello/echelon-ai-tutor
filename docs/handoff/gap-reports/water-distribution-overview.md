# Required topic checklist: Water Distribution Overview vs Royce U `wdo1p` (183 slides) and `wdo2p` (160 slides), read in full 2026-11-17

Teach each item in our own words, with our own scenarios and numbers. Do not copy Royce U wording, questions, examples, images or numeric values. Where they give a regulatory or engineering number (minimum pressures, velocities, sizing, flow classes, service-line sizes, testing intervals, residual or pressure requirements), say the applicable rule, the utility standard or the design engineer sets it; do not restate it.

## Module 1: system components and storage
- Purpose of a distribution system; waterborne disease context (purpose only, no list to memorise).
- Storage roles: equalising demand over the day, fire-flow reserve, pressure stability, and how tank level changes with demand (demand cycle and fill and drain).
- Tank types and how each works: clear well, elevated tank (pressure from the water column: 0.433 psi per foot), standpipe, ground storage tank, hydropneumatic tank (air to water ratio and short cycling), surge tank (limited role against surge).
- Water age in tanks and why tanks are covered and accessible for cleaning.
- Pressure zones and minimum pressure in principle; backsiphonage and backpressure as backflow causes; cross-connections; ISO fire-protection grading and why it matters.
- Pipe materials: gray cast iron (joints, break types), ductile iron (replaced cast iron, joints), steel (large diameter, coatings, welding), asbestos cement (handling risk and work practices), PVC (common material, pressure class, sunlight, sagging), concrete (prestressed and embedded cylinder types), water-main selection factors.
- Corrosion and electrical: why corrosion happens; cathodic protection with sacrificial anodes and rectifiers (purpose); polyethylene encasement; electrical continuity across joints; bonding at meters; why service pipes must not be used as electrical grounds.
- Layouts: tree or branch (dead ends and water age), loop, grid (best redundancy); why a single failure affects more customers in a tree layout.
- Valves: gate (fully open or closed, rising stem shows position), butterfly (disc stays in flow), check (one-way, surge), pressure reducing (purpose, the downstream setting, and why it matters), altitude valve (diaphragm stops flow into an elevated tank), air release valves (entrapped air acts like a closed valve).
- Water hammer as a sudden change in velocity and pressure; why valves should not be closed fast (purpose only, no closing times).

## Module 2: hydrants, meters and backflow
- Fire hydrants: purpose; dry barrel (operating nut at top, drains) and wet barrel (freezing risk); parts (cap, stem nut, flange, riser, thrust blocks, lateral line with isolation valve, service line sizing principle); flush hydrants for airports and bridges.
- Hydrant colour and capacity classes (purpose: the colour tells the rated flow class; no class numbers).
- Hydrant cap and valve operation (operate fully open; drains open only when fully open; close without over-tightening; leave the cap off while flowing); temporary out-of-service marking; inspection documentation; NFPA 291 flow-test purpose.
- Lubricants and materials in contact with drinking water must be certified for that use (name the standard type, no number beyond what the course gives as a concept).
- Meters: why meters exist (revenue, leak detection, demand tracking); velocity meters (propeller/multi-jet); positive displacement (piston, nutating disc, head loss); turbine meters for large commercial and master meters; venturi (restricted throat, entrained air effect); proportional and orifice meters (concept); magnetic meters; ultrasonic meters (transducers, transit time); compound meters (two chambers, low-flow and high-flow); AMR and AMI (radio-read and real-time data).
- Meter testing and replacement programme purpose (accuracy degrades; larger meters tested more often; replacement interval set by the utility or standard).
- Backflow: contamination versus pollution (purpose), backsiphonage and backpressure; air gap (most effective, physical separation); reduced pressure zone device (best mechanical device for high hazard, relief port and pressure differential purpose); double check valve (containment versus isolation, unsuitable for high hazard); residential dual check; atmospheric vacuum breaker (isolation, no continuous pressure); pressure vacuum breaker (backsiphonage, not for backpressure); containment at the meter or property line versus isolation at the fixture.
- Testing purpose for backflow assemblies by a qualified tester on a regular schedule.
