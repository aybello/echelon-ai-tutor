## Chlorine properties and materials
- Gas heavier than air, liquid vaporises readily, volume change liquid to gas, temperature and volume relationship.
- Materials compatibility: dry gas and liquid with steel and black iron; wet gas corrosive; plastics and elastomers that resist it; what attacks PVC and rubber.

## Health effects and first aid (purpose and recognition)
- Liquid contact (burn-like injury), gas as an irritant, dry versus wet gassing, delayed pulmonary effects, why exertion changes exposure, why duration matters, first aid purpose (fresh air, flushing, medical help), no step-by-step treatment.

## Hazards, leak response principles and detection
- Leaks worsen unless corrected; never ignore an alarm; keep upwind; ammonia-fume leak detection (white cloud) at recognition level; never spray water on a leak; emergency kit purpose by container type; fire and heat (remove containers from heat, cooling purpose); two-person rule as a principle; who to notify and what information to give (purpose only).

## Containers and handling (recognition level)
- 150 lb cylinder, one-ton container, tank car and truck: what each is, typical use by flow, approximate content, valve arrangement (liquid versus gas withdrawal), labelling and stamped-number tracking, full and empty segregation, scale use, why replacement is a team task with specific equipment (purpose only, no step list).

## Equipment and process
- Scales, evaporator, pressure reducing valve, manifold and isolation, heat strips, leak detector sampling near the floor, vacuum-operated chlorinator and injector (water flow creates the vacuum), residual analyzer and recorder, compound loop control (flow pacing and residual trim), expansion chamber, automatic changeover and why it matters, hypochlorite (bleach) feed and metering purpose, why gas and liquid feeds differ.

## Chemistry
- Chlorine in water to hypochlorous acid and hypochlorite; why hypochlorous acid is the stronger disinfectant; free versus combined versus total residual.
- Chloramines: monochloramine, dichloramine, trichloramine; effect of pH; why ammonia changes the picture.
- Breakpoint chlorination curve: demand, combined residual, the dip, breakpoint, free residual after breakpoint, why adding more chlorine can lower the reading.
- Demand concepts: chlorine demand definition; nitrification and denitrification effects on demand; nitrite demand (concept).

## Contact time and calculations (worked, with OUR numbers)
- Detention time from chamber volume and flow (volume in ft3, flow converted with 1 MGD = 133,680 ft3 per day, about 92.84 cfm, stated in the question).
- Chlorine dose and residual, demand as dose minus residual, pounds per day from mg/L and MGD (8.34).
- Injection point and mixing purpose (diffuser or flash mixer), why mixing matters.

## Monitoring and compliance awareness
- Coliform testing purpose: total and fecal groups as indicators; why pathogens are not tested directly; sampling purpose (no counts or limits as numbers).
- SDS sections for chlorine (identification, hazards, physical data, reactivity, first aid, spill response purpose, special protection).
- Regulatory awareness: community right-to-know reporting, training obligations, notifying neighbours and emergency services (purpose only).

## Operating problems and preventive maintenance
- Operating problems and likely causes: high usage, erratic scale readings, leaks, iced containers or pigtails (high feed rate), gas pressure and rotameter bounce, low vacuum from low water flow, water in rotameter, faulty heater or injector, container running out without changeover, low or high readings in flow pacing.
- Preventive maintenance purpose: scale inspection, changeover testing, rotameter cleaning, leak detector testing and calibration, record keeping.
