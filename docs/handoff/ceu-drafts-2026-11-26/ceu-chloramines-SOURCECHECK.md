# Source check: ceu-chloramines vs textbooks and EPA pages

Checked 2026-11-26 against ceusrc/sp6.txt (Spellman Handbook 2009), sp2.txt and sp3.txt (Spellman waterworks operator handbooks), wqc.txt (Water Quality Control Handbook) and the EPA pages (curl returns 200 for each URL). Verdicts: SUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED, NOT COVERED. The textbooks say little about chloramine operation, so most operating claims are NOT COVERED by them; they are kept as general operator knowledge at concept level, with no numeric operating ranges or regulatory limits stated. Claims rely on EPA where EPA supports them. Page markers are as printed in the extractions; where none is printed the section or table is named.

## Findings
- No lesson claim is contradicted by the sources.
- The WQC handbook gives pH boundaries for chloramine types (monochloramine above 8.5, dichloramine 4.8 to 8.5, trichloramine below 4.8) in a wastewater setting. They differ from usual drinking-water practice, so the course teaches the direction only and states no pH numbers.
- The Royce slides give numeric operating ranges (ratio ranges, free ammonia targets, residual goals, pH and temperature ranges for nitrifiers, tank turnover rates, storage pressures). None are in the textbooks, so none are stated as facts here. Every question carries its own constants.
