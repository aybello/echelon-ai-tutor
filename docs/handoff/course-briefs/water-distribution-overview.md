## Modules
1. **System components and layout:** source to tap; pump stations; storage tanks (elevated, ground, standpipe) and their purposes; mains, valves, hydrants, service lines, meters; pressure zones and why they exist; grid versus branched layouts and dead ends; pipe materials (ductile iron, PVC, HDPE, steel, concrete, asbestos-cement awareness, lead service line awareness) and how each tends to fail; reading a distribution map.
2. **Operating the system:** demand patterns through the day; how tanks float on the system; pressure and flow behaviour at foundation level with a few worked calculations (pressure from elevation, tank fill and draw-down, velocity in a main, volume of a main in gallons) that cross-refer to the hydraulics course; valve operation and exercising (purpose); hydrant types (dry-barrel, wet-barrel); flushing programs and flow tests (purpose and recognition).
3. **Maintenance and repair awareness:** main breaks and leak causes; leak detection methods at recognition level; main repair and disinfection of repaired or new mains at concept level (purpose, no step lists or exact dose numbers); service connections and meter basics; cross-connections and backflow prevention (assemblies, air gap, test purpose); inspection and record keeping; trench and traffic safety awareness at recognition level.
4. **Water quality, customers and compliance awareness:** why a disinfectant residual is kept; water age and where it causes problems; taste, odor, colour and pressure complaints and how to respond and record them; coliform sampling purpose and sample site idea (no counts or limits); lead and copper awareness at concept level; public notification purpose; operator certification and continuing education purpose; working with the regulator and the public.
