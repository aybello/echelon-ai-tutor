# Course brief: Supervisor Essentials for Water and Wastewater Operators

Rules: `docs/handoff/course-briefs/COMMON.md`. Checklist: `docs/handoff/gap-reports/supervisor.md`. File: `content/ceu-drafts/ceu-supervisor-essentials.json`. Key `ceu-supervisor-essentials`, version `2026-11-17.1`, `plannedMinutes` 210, shortTitle "Supervisor Essentials", stream `drinking_water` (shared with wastewater; say so in NOTES). Diagrams folder: `scratchpad/diagrams-supv/`.

Scope: general supervision principles for operators stepping into a lead role. Florida's classification tables and "licensed operator in responsible charge" wording are NOT taught as rules; each state's or province's certification rule is marked "check your state's rules".

## Modules
1. **Stepping into the role:** what a supervisor is and is not, the change from operator to supervisor, relationships with former peers, consistency, the difference between a supervisor, a manager and a lead operator, what authority a supervisor usually has (discipline, scheduling, assignments; hiring and firing vary).
2. **Responsibility and accountability:** the supervisor's job (guide the group, see that every member is productive), documentation and checklists as accountability tools, why a signed task must actually be done, responsibility for decisions made on shift, certification and "in responsible charge" as a concept, public-health stakes.
3. **Management and organisational styles:** autocratic, paternalistic, democratic, laissez-faire and bureaucratic; a worked scenario showing how each style handles the same task; advantages and disadvantages; which style suits which utility situation; organisation charts and span of control.
4. **Leading people through change and problems:** communicating expectations, delegating, coaching versus directing, handling a poor performer fairly, documentation of events, team troubleshooting, the operator-to-supervisor transition through a worked example.

## Diagrams (at least five)
Operator-to-supervisor role map; the five management styles as a spectrum; a flat versus layered organisation chart; a documentation loop; a decision flow for escalation.

## Question mix
At least 25 percent of final questions are scenario judgements with a clear best answer; at least 20 percent are calculations (for example span of control, shift-coverage hours, or a simple budget share). No Florida tables or staffing numbers.
