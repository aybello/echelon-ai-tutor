## Modules
1. **Chlorine forms and chemistry:** chlorine gas, sodium hypochlorite, calcium hypochlorite and how each is delivered; what happens when chlorine enters water (hypochlorous acid and hypochlorite ion, effect of pH on disinfecting power); free, combined and total chlorine; chloramine formation with ammonia and why utilities use it; breakpoint chlorination with the breakpoint curve (concept only).
2. **Dose, demand and residual calculations:** dose = demand + residual; the pounds formula (mg/L x MGD x 8.34 = lb/day); feed rate for gas and for hypochlorite of stated strength (percent available chlorine, density, gallons per day); converting between lb/day and gal/day; demand from dose and residual; unit conversions; check-your-answer habits.
3. **Contact time, residual monitoring and by-products:** why contact time and residual together matter, detention time and baffling at foundation level (no CT verification), where and how residual is measured (DPD colorimetric and amperometric analyzers at recognition level), residual in the distribution system, residual loss and water age, disinfection by-product awareness, dechlorination purpose.
4. **Feed equipment and operating problems:** gas chlorinator parts at recognition level (cylinder, vacuum regulator, ejector, rotameter, feed line), hypochlorite feed pumps and day tanks, off-gassing and loss of strength, scale and air binding, residual too high or too low and common causes, alarms and automatic control (flow pacing, residual trim) at concept level, storage and incompatibility recognition, leak awareness (who to notify; no step-by-step response).

## Question mix
At least half of the final questions are calculations. Constants stated in each question: 8.34 lb per gal, 1 MGD = 1,000,000 gal per day, hypochlorite strength as percent available chlorine and density as lb per gal. Wrong values come from real mistakes (forgot 8.34, percent used wrongly, mixed lb and gal, total used instead of free residual).
